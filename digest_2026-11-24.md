### Gut bacterial amyloid curli translocation as a major link in central Aβ progression and cognitive decline
**Tushar K Das & Bhanu P Ganesh | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** This work moves the microbiota–gut–brain axis in Alzheimer's from correlation toward a defined molecular conduit. In Tg2576 and huAPP knock-in mice, inducing colitis (DSS) or infection with curli-producing *E. coli* raised luminal curli amyloid and increased its translocation into plasma-derived extracellular vesicles (pEV-curli), and pEV-curli burden tracked with cognitive impairment. Critically, MCI patients who later progressed to AD showed elevated pEV-curli, nominating a peripherally accessible early biomarker. The claim of a "causal role" rests on mouse manipulations; the human data are associative.

**Model/system & primary method:** Tg2576 and hAβ-KI AD mice with induced gut inflammation/infection; 16S rRNA microbiome profiling; human and mouse plasma EV proteomics; behavioral testing (NOR, Y-maze).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.72003

### Gammaherpesvirus infection drives genotype-dependent virus-specific T cell responses in the CNS prior to autoimmune neuroinflammation
**Katherine J Sessions & Dimitry N Krementsov | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Why it matters:** EBV is the strongest environmental risk factor for MS, but how poor genetic control of the virus translates into CNS pathology is unresolved. Using the B-cell-tropic murine homolog MHV-68, the authors show that chronic infection alone drives accumulation of CX3CR1⁺KLRG1⁺ cytotoxic CD8 T cells — tetramer-confirmed as virus-specific, with viral DNA in CNS leukocytes — at brain borders and in parenchyma. CNS infiltration was strongly attenuated in genetically divergent PWD/PhJ mice that control the virus well, directly linking host genotype to viral control and T-cell seeding. Unexpectedly, chronic infection modestly delayed EAE onset in a B-cell-dependent manner, underscoring that the virus–autoimmunity relationship is not simply additive.

**Model/system & primary method:** MHV-68 infection across three mouse genotypes (C57BL/6, PWD/PhJ, 129S1); EAE induction; MHC tetramer analysis, flow cytometry, viral DNA detection, B-cell depletion.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2606486123

### A shared meaning component across languages in the human brain
**Andrea Gregor de Varda & Evelina Fedorenko | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Combining existing and newly collected fMRI across 21 languages from 4 families, the authors used multilingual neural-network embeddings to build encoding models of the language network. Models reliably predicted brain responses within each language, and — the key result — models trained on one set of languages generalized zero-shot to unseen languages, predicting neural responses without any exposure to the target language. Generalization held across language family, stimulus type, and presentation modality, pointing to a language-independent neural representation of meaning. A strong demonstration that semantic representation in the language network abstracts away from the specific linguistic surface form.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human fMRI (21 languages); voxelwise encoding models using multilingual LLM representations; cross-lingual zero-shot generalization testing.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02523-6

### A three-axis structural framework for state-dependent spectral gradients in macaque cortex
**Ao Ma & Yuhan Chen | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** This study connects large-scale electrophysiological dynamics to cortical architecture. From cortex-wide macaque ECoG, the authors identified a dominant cross-frequency spectral gradient that reorganizes between wakefulness and anesthesia, then distilled 29 anatomical, molecular, and connectivity maps into three structural axes — laminar architecture, local microstructure, and afferent wiring range. The awake gradient required the joint contribution of all three axes, whereas anesthesia unbalanced them: ketamine selectively reduced the long-range afferent contribution, while propofol implicated stronger local inhibitory control. It offers a principled, structure-grounded account of how brain state reshapes the spatial organization of cortical rhythms.

**Model/system & primary method:** Cortex-wide macaque electrocorticography under wakefulness, ketamine, and propofol; biologically motivated structural models integrating multimodal cortical maps.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.018

### Neural correlates of facial recognition in a social wasp
**Christopher M Jernigan & Michael J Sheehan | Cell**

**Why it matters:** *Polistes fuscatus* wasps evolved individual facial recognition independently of vertebrates, making them a natural test of whether specialized face-encoding circuits are a convergent solution. Surveying brain-wide neural responses, the authors found a localized population of "wasp cells" that selectively fire to front-facing wasp images, with some encoding facial-pattern information. This is the first demonstration of high-level, feature-selective face tuning in an invertebrate, and it argues that dedicated circuits — rather than distributed general-purpose coding — are an adaptive answer to the demands of complex visual individual recognition, in brains separated from primates by hundreds of millions of years.

**Model/system & primary method:** *Polistes fuscatus* wasps; brain-wide neural recordings of responses to visual objects including conspecific faces.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.10.052

### Presynaptic functional organization by phase separation
**Tyler H Ogunmowo & Shigeki Watanabe | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** This review synthesizes evidence that the functional compartmentalization of the presynaptic terminal — the active-zone-adjacent readily releasable pool versus the reserve vesicle cluster, and spatially segregated recycling and proteolytic domains — is organized by liquid–liquid phase separation into non-membrane-bound condensates. It frames condensate formation as a mechanism for maintaining distinct molecular compositions and functional programs within densely packed terminals, with implications for the regulation of vesicle trafficking and synaptic transmission.

**Model/system & primary method:** Review of presynaptic phase-separation mechanisms (synaptic condensates, vesicle pool organization).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02517-4

### High-throughput screening of patient iPSC-derived neurons identifies gut microbial metabolites that attenuate Alzheimer's disease pathology
**Dhruv Gohel & Feixiong Cheng | Cell Rep**

**Key findings:** Screening 352 gut-derived metabolites in AD-patient iPSC-derived neurons for reduction of tau hyperphosphorylation identified agmatine as the lead hit. Agmatine lowered phospho-tau in neurons and cerebral organoids, and in 5xFAD mice (100 mg/kg/day) improved cognition, reduced amyloid plaques and neuroinflammation, and shifted gut microbiota toward *Agathobacter* and *Bacteroides*. Mechanistically, agmatine downregulated complement and inflammatory genes (C1QB, C2, C3AR1, CCL3, CXCL8, CXCL6) and reduced C3aR and pSTAT3 in vivo, linking the metabolite to tau pathology via complement/inflammatory signaling.

**Model/system & primary method:** AD-patient iPSC-derived neurons and cerebral organoids; 5xFAD mice; metabolite screen, transcriptomics, in vivo pharmacology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118333

### C16-ceramide exacerbates Tau pathology by interfering with Ppil2-mediated Grap2 degradation in Alzheimer's disease
**Xinhuang Lv & Jing Sun | Cell Rep**

**Key findings:** Plasma C16-ceramide was elevated in AD patients and correlated with cognitive impairment and p-Tau181. C16 supplementation aggravated tau phosphorylation in vitro and in vivo through GRAP2-mediated JNK activation. Mechanistically, PPIL2 normally drives K48-linked ubiquitination and degradation of GRAP2 (at Lys112); C16 binds PPIL2 and weakens the PPIL2–GRAP2 interaction, stabilizing GRAP2 and activating JNK. A binding-site mutation preserved basal PPIL2–GRAP2 association and blocked the C16 effect, and hippocampal *Grap2* knockout rescued C16-associated cognitive deficits and tau phosphorylation — defining a PPIL2–GRAP2–JNK axis linking ceramide metabolism to tau.

**Model/system & primary method:** AD patient plasma; cell and mouse models; ubiquitination/binding assays, hippocampal *Grap2* knockout, behavioral testing.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118339

### snRNA-seq of a Huntington's disease mouse model indicates cell type-specific transcriptional signatures and disease-relevant pathways
**Dennis Feigenbutz & Irina Dudanova | Cell Rep**

**Key findings:** Single-nucleus RNA-seq of motor cortex from an HD mouse model showed transcriptomic separation of HD from control within vulnerable glutamatergic — but not disease-resistant GABAergic — neuron clusters, consistent with selective vulnerability. Expression changes followed a two-stage early/late trajectory, with progressive loss of neuronal cell-type identity and upregulation of ER-phagy receptors. Co-expression network analysis flagged a candidate neuroprotective module, and cellular HD models confirmed increased ER-phagy, implicating ER-phagy in HD neuronal vulnerability.

**Model/system & primary method:** HD mouse model motor cortex; single-nucleus RNA-seq, gene co-expression network analysis; cellular validation of ER-phagy.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118332

### Holographic focused ultrasound enables multitarget EV and MSC delivery for neurovascular repair after ischemic stroke
**Jia-Ji Pan & Bingbing Cheng | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** A hologram-assisted multitarget BBB-opening platform (HaMBO) — low-cost 3D-printed acoustic holograms with a single-element transducer — produced spatially tailored fields that opened the BBB along infarct borders. In mice with transient focal ischemia, a single two-minute treatment enhanced peri-infarct delivery of extracellular vesicles and mesenchymal stromal cells, reduced brain atrophy by ~56%, roughly doubled angiogenesis and quadrupled neural progenitor proliferation, and improved long-term behavioral recovery. Spatial transcriptomics linked EV therapy to reduced macrophage infiltration and enhanced pro-angiogenic/neurogenic signaling; human skull simulations suggest translational adaptability. Evidence is preclinical (mouse) plus in silico.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse transient focal cerebral ischemia; 3D-printed holographic focused ultrasound; EV/MSC delivery, spatial transcriptomics, human skull acoustic simulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78847-5

### Effect of a theory-informed multicomponent exercise program on cognition in older adults with MCI: the MIND-STEP randomized controlled trial
**Chenlu Hong & Yanan Luo | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In a single-blind RCT of 156 nursing-home residents with MCI in Zhejiang, China, a 12-week theory-informed multicomponent exercise program (peer interaction + self-efficacy enhancement) improved MoCA-B versus unstructured usual care (mean difference 2.78 points, 95% CI 1.88–3.68; standardized mean difference 1.23), with benefits emerging at 6 weeks and maintained at 24 weeks. Intrinsic capacity, physical performance, depressive symptoms, and sleep quality also improved. The large effect size is notable but tempered by the comparator being unstructured usual care rather than an active control.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human two-arm single-blind RCT (n=156, nursing homes); 12-week multicomponent exercise intervention; MoCA-B primary outcome.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71984

### Cortical neurons accumulate point mutations at similar rates in mice and humans
**M. Saeidi & N. Spisak | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Using PacBio HiFi circular consensus sequencing of mouse cortical neurons, the authors estimated somatic point-mutation accumulation at ~25.9 mutations per diploid genome per year (95% CI 17.7–34.1). This rate is comparable to that reported for human neurons, implying that somatic mutation rates in postmitotic neurons do not scale with lifespan — contrary to the inter-species, age-inverse scaling seen for germline and some proliferative tissues. The work also shows single-cell-resolution somatic mutation calling is feasible with a widely available sequencing method. Interpretation is limited by cross-study comparison to human estimates rather than a matched within-study human cohort.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse cortical neurons across ages; PacBio HiFi single-cell somatic mutation estimation.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.21.768276

### Glial involvement in mitochondrial metabolic alterations in Alzheimer's disease
**K. Hirata & Y. Takado | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Integrating human posterior cingulate cortex proteomics and transcriptomics with magnetic resonance spectroscopy in AppNL-G-F knock-in mice, the authors found convergent reductions in mitochondrial oxidative-metabolism signatures associated with AD pathology and cognition. Spatial transcriptomics localized reduced mitochondrial pathway signatures to glia-enriched clusters, and hyperpolarized [1-¹³C]pyruvate spectroscopy showed reduced bicarbonate labeling in APP-KI mice, consistent with impaired oxidative pyruvate metabolism. The findings implicate glial cells, particularly astrocytes, in the early metabolic vulnerability of the PCC — though causal direction between glial metabolic failure and pathology is not established.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human AD PCC (proteomics/transcriptomics); AppNL-G-F knock-in mice; ¹H-MRS, hyperpolarized ¹³C spectroscopy, spatial transcriptomics.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.20.768184

### Chronic evoked seizures differentially worsen cognitive function and neuropsychiatric comorbidities across Alzheimer's disease-related genotypes
**K. Knox & M. Barker-Haliski | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Using corneal kindling to impose chronic seizures in two aged early-onset-AD mouse models (PSEN2-N141I, an amyloid-β-independent model, and APP/PS1, amyloid-β-dependent), the authors found genotype-specific behavioral consequences. Seizures worsened Barnes-maze spatial learning selectively in APP/PS1 females, and consistently disrupted home-cage circadian exploratory behavior in APP/PS1 males and females; PSEN2 and control males also showed seizure-related maze deficits. Saccharin-preference (anhedonia) was unaffected. The results argue that the cognitive and behavioral impact of seizures in AD is heterogeneous and depends on genetic background and sex, rather than being uniform. Findings are confined to behavior in mouse models.

**Model/system & primary method:** Aged PSEN2-N141I and APP/PS1 mice (both sexes); corneal kindling; Barnes maze, saccharin preference, circadian home-cage monitoring.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.20.768142

### Lipidomic profiling of L1CAM+ extracellular vesicles in Parkinson's disease
**M. A. Abdulsalam & B. Derkus | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In a small pilot (n=10 per group), untargeted LC-MS lipidomics of serum total EVs and L1CAM-enriched (neuron-associated) EVs separated PD from controls in both fractions (PLS-DA Q² ≈ 0.88) and distinguished L1CAM+ EVs from total EVs within PD. PD L1CAM+ EVs showed increased diacylglycerols, lysophosphatidic acid and phosphatidylserine with reduced lysophosphatidylcholine, and depletion of multiple sphingomyelin species relative to total EVs — a lipid signature qualitatively distinct from controls. The authors position L1CAM+ EV lipids as a candidate neuron-related biomarker source. Very small n and the known specificity caveats of L1CAM as a neuronal-EV marker warrant caution.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human serum (PD vs control, n=10/group); L1CAM-immunocapture EV isolation; untargeted LC-MS lipidomics, PLS-DA.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.21.768278

### Unsupervised tensor integration of hippocampal and colonic multi-omics reveals enteric neural signatures in cognitive aging
**Y.-h. Taguchi & T. Turki | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Applying tensor-decomposition-based unsupervised feature extraction to mouse hippocampal and colonic transcriptomic and promoter-methylation data across five ages, the authors recovered an age-monotonic component and 1,577 genes selected without using cognitive scores. The derived colonic methylation score correlated with cognition (r=0.589), though the association did not survive age adjustment. Selected genes were enriched for enteric neuronal and glial markers (~5-fold) and synaptic processes, nominating candidate genes for enteric neural involvement in cognitive aging. The authors are explicit that this establishes neither an age-independent association nor a causal gut-to-brain mechanism.

**Model/system & primary method:** Public mouse hippocampal + colonic transcriptomics and DNA methylation across ages; tensor-decomposition unsupervised feature extraction.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.20.768248
