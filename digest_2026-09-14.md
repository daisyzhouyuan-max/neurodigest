### Fibronectin mediates APOE4-driven blood-brain barrier dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease
**Prabesh Bhattarai & Caghan Kizil | Nat Aging**

**Why it matters:** APOE ε4 is the strongest common genetic risk factor for Alzheimer's, and blood-brain barrier (BBB) breakdown is one of its earliest correlates, but the connecting mechanism has been unclear. Here astrocyte-derived fibronectin (FN1) emerges as a proximal effector: APOE4, amyloid-β42 and inflammatory signals drive astrocytic FN1 upregulation and excessive perivascular deposition, which is sufficient to cause barrier leakage. Fibronectin disrupts VEGF/HB-EGF/IGF-1 signaling through integrin-mediated focal adhesion kinase activity, and reducing FN1 or restoring growth-factor signaling rescues barrier function. FN1 is nominated as a tractable target for the gliovascular arm of AD.

**Model/system & primary method:** Postmortem human brain tissue, human three-dimensional vascular models and in vivo mouse models; convergent evidence with clinical datasets.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-026-01204-0

### Human tau pathology is associated with lonely, nontraveling slow waves linked to memory impairment
**Omer Sharon & Matthew P. Walker | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** The link between tau pathology and memory decline is usually treated as direct, but this work interposes a sleep-physiology step. Frontal-lobe tau burden is associated with a loss of the en masse unity and cortical traveling propagation of NREM slow waves — "lonely," nontraveling slow-wave events — and that breakdown, rather than tau per se, tracks impaired overnight memory retention. The finding reframes tau-related memory deficits as partly indirect, and points to slow-wave traveling dynamics as a candidate intervention target.

**Model/system & primary method:** Humans; tau PET imaging with electrophysiological slow-wave analysis, replicated and extended in an independent clinical cohort using CSF AD biomarkers. Associational rather than causal.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02415-9

### Molecular programs in human locus coeruleus link APOE and neuromelanin to Alzheimer's vulnerability
**Bernard Mulvey & Keri Martinowich | Acta Neuropathol**

**Why it matters:** The locus coeruleus (LC) shows some of the earliest phospho-tau accumulation in AD and loses neuromelanin pigmentation as disease progresses, yet why these noradrenergic neurons are so selectively vulnerable is unresolved. Spatially resolved transcriptomics of human LC across neurotypical middle-aged donors, stratified by AD risk factors, defines molecular programs that connect APOE status and neuromelanin biology to LC neuron identity and vulnerability. The work offers a molecular substrate for the LC's early, selective involvement in AD.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem LC tissue from 33 neurotypical middle-aged donors; spatial transcriptomics stratified by AD risk. Cross-sectional and associational.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03073-8

### Joint impact of pathological burden and cognitive resilience on Alzheimer's disease risk
**Zhibo Wang & Jianping Jia | Nat Med**

**Why it matters:** AD is often framed purely as a consequence of accumulating amyloid and tau, yet outcome varies widely at any given pathological burden. In 3,119 older adults followed for a median of 13.7 years, longitudinal pathology (indexed by pTau181/Aβ42) and a separately derived cognitive resilience score behave as independent and synergistic dimensions of incident AD dementia. Treating resilience as a measurable, parallel determinant — not just noise around a pathology threshold — has direct implications for risk stratification and for trials that target reserve alongside pathology.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human prospective cohort (China Cognition and Aging Study, n=3,119; median 13.7-year follow-up); repeated-measure longitudinal indices. Observational.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04635-9

### Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease increases ALS risk while optimal cardiovascular health halves it
**Jiali Xie & Binbin Deng | Ann Neurol**

**Why it matters:** Whether vascular health is causally upstream of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis has been contentious. In 502,279 UK Biobank participants with linked records, prevalent atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (coronary artery disease, ischemic stroke, peripheral artery disease) was associated with an ~82% higher ALS risk, while high cardiovascular health (Life's Essential 8) roughly halved risk. Mendelian randomization alongside Cox and joint models supports a causal, and potentially modifiable, contribution rather than pure confounding — nominating cardiovascular prevention as a candidate lever on ALS risk.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human prospective cohort (UK Biobank, n=502,279; median ~13.7-year follow-up); Cox regression, joint analyses and Mendelian randomization. Observational with genetic-instrument support.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78321

### A hypothalamic-vagal pathway mediates stress-induced feeding suppression and gastric dysfunction
**Dan Dai & Shumin Duan | Neuron**

**Key findings:** A defined circuit couples acute stress to both anorexia and gut dysfunction. Paraventricular nucleus (PVN) neurons expressing the neuropeptide Y receptor Y1 (NPY1R) are rapidly engaged by diverse stressors; activating their projection to the dorsal vagal complex suppresses feeding, whereas inhibiting the pathway reverses stress-induced hypophagia. The same PVN→vagal route accounts for stress-associated gastric dysfunction, identifying a neural (rather than purely neuroendocrine) mechanism linking the stress response to feeding and gastrointestinal control.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mice; circuit-specific optogenetic/chemogenetic activation and inhibition with feeding and gastric readouts. Causal circuit manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.011

### ENT1 inhibition links oligodendrocyte lipid metabolism to connectivity in tauopathy
**Ching-Pang Chang & Yijuang Chern | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Myelinating oligodendrocytes are cast as a metabolically vulnerable hub connecting adenosine signaling, lipid handling and white-matter network integrity in tauopathy. Human tauopathy hippocampus and THY-Tau22 mice show oligodendrocyte lipidomic and metabolic disruption alongside diffusion-MRI connectivity deficits; treating symptomatic mice with the equilibrative nucleoside transporter 1 (ENT1) inhibitor J4 modulates these pathways therapeutically. The result ties a druggable adenosine-transport node to oligodendrocyte metabolism and circuit disconnection.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human hippocampal tissue plus THY-Tau22 mice; diffusion tensor imaging, metabolomics, lipidomics, single-nucleus RNA-seq, and pharmacological ENT1 inhibition. Mouse causal evidence.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71789

### Direct activation of SARM1 by dsDNA is not supported by biochemical and cellular evidence
**Allison H. Kao & Aaron DiAntonio | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** SARM1, the central NAD+-consuming executioner of axon degeneration, is canonically gated by the NMN/NAD+ ratio; a recent report proposed that cytosolic double-stranded DNA directly binds and activates its TIR domain. Using purified SARM1, biophysical assays, primary neurons and cellular pharmacology, this study finds no dsDNA-driven NADase activity, higher-order assembly, or activated-SARM1 metabolic signature — where effects appear, they are weak and off-pathway. A useful corrective that keeps the metabolic-sensor model of axon degeneration intact.

**Model/system & primary method:** Purified recombinant SARM1, biochemical/biophysical assays, and primary neuron pharmacology. Mechanistic, in vitro and cellular; a negative/rebuttal study.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2620112123

### Proteomic-guided targeted treatment of leukoencephalopathy with calcification and cysts
**Nils Briel & Tobias Weiss | Brain**

**Key findings:** Leukoencephalopathy with calcifications and cysts (LCC) is a currently incurable ribosomopathy (biallelic SNORD118 mutations) causing progressive white-matter degeneration and vascular proliferation. Spatially resolved proteomics of vasculature and white matter from patient tissue nominated actionable pathways, which the authors translated into a targeted treatment — the first proteomics-guided precision therapy reported for a monogenic leukoencephalopathy. A proof-of-concept for spatial proteomics as a route from molecular pathology to therapy in rare white-matter disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human LCC patient tissue; spatially resolved proteomics of defined vascular and white-matter regions, informing targeted treatment. Human, small-n and exploratory.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag299

### Cerebral oxygen extraction fraction as a physiological biomarker in vascular cognitive impairment
**Jie Song & Dengrong Jiang | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Validated functional biomarkers for vascular cognitive impairment and dementia (VCID) are scarce. In 81 older adults enriched for small-vessel disease and controlled for AD co-pathology, MRI-quantified oxygen extraction fraction (OEF) in major cerebral veins — a readout of small-vessel functional status — was assessed as a candidate physiological marker. The study positions OEF as a vascular-function biomarker distinct from, and complementary to, amyloid/tau measures in VCID, though the small cross-sectional sample tempers generalizability.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cross-sectional study (n=81) with cognitive, plasma and MRI data; MRI-based cerebral venous OEF quantification. Small-n, associational.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71828

### Microglia stabilize sleep homeostasis via adenosine A3 receptor signaling
**Zhong Zhao & Heping Cheng | Sci Adv**

**Key findings:** Sleep pressure is partly encoded by extracellular adenosine, but how microglia read it has been unclear. Using miniaturized two-photon imaging in freely behaving mice, microglial Ca²⁺ activity tracked brain-state transitions and was driven by adenosine A3 receptor (A3R) signaling; pharmacological manipulation of this pathway altered microglial calcium dynamics and sleep homeostasis. The work assigns microglia an active, receptor-defined role in sensing sleep pressure and stabilizing the sleep-wake balance.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mice; in vivo miniaturized two-photon microscopy of microglial Ca²⁺ in freely behaving animals with A3R pharmacology. Causal pharmacological manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aef3957

### Brain-resident myeloid cells promote rapid leukocyte adhesion in leptomeningeal vessels after anti-Aβ immunotherapy
**Mohammad Algamal & Brian J. Bacskai | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Anti-amyloid antibodies improve cognition but cause amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA) by unclear mechanisms. Longitudinal two-photon imaging in APP/PS1 mice shows that aducanumab and lecanemab, within 24 h, both reorganize plaque-associated microglia and trigger a transient cerebrovascular immune response: rolling and adhesion of monocytes, neutrophils and proliferative T cells along leptomeningeal vessels with endothelial activation. Depleting brain-resident immune cells reduced peripheral recruitment; high-dose dexamethasone cut adhesion without blocking microglial activation. A treated patient's postmortem tissue showed a matching proliferative-myeloid signature. The authors propose this immune-vascular interaction as a plausible ARIA-initiating step — mechanistic and hypothesis-generating.

**Model/system & primary method:** APP/PS1 mice; longitudinal in vivo two-photon imaging of microglia, immune cells and vasculature, with one postmortem human sample. Mouse-based, associational for ARIA.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.07.749938

### Structural heterogeneity of CTE Type I tau filaments with an interface-shifted polymorph
**Ryo Watanabe & Edward B. Lee | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Reanalyzing deposited cryo-EM data (EMPIAR-10313) from brain-derived chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) tau filaments, the authors resolve the canonical CTE Type I fold (2.78 Å) plus a previously unreported CTE Type I-like polymorph with a shifted protofilament interface (2.96 Å). Mapping particle states back onto micrographs shows the two packing states can coexist within locally contiguous regions of the same fibril. The finding refines the notion of one fold-per-disease, suggesting local interface heterogeneity along individual CTE filaments.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human brain-derived CTE tau filaments; cryo-EM reprocessing and per-particle state mapping of a public dataset. Structural, descriptive.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.10.750773

### USP15 regulates neuroinflammation and drives pathogenesis in synucleinopathies
**Nassima Fodil & Philippe Gros | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The deubiquitinase USP15 is nominated as a glial driver of synucleinopathy. Ablating Usp15 in astrocytes and microglia protected against lethal neuroinflammation in vivo; in a synucleinopathy mouse model, Usp15 deletion reduced brain α-synuclein deposits, slowed progression and extended survival, with downregulation of interferon-stimulated genes. Effects were reproduced in primary human microglia and astrocytes. In humans, microglial USP15 co-expressed with LRRK2 and SNCA, and a cis-eQTL raising myeloid USP15 was itself associated with increased PD risk, linking myeloid USP15 to genetic susceptibility. Convergent mouse-plus-human genetic evidence for a candidate target.

**Model/system & primary method:** Synucleinopathy mouse model with cell-type-specific Usp15 ablation; primary human glia; human eQTL/expression analyses. Mouse causal plus human genetic association.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.05.749595

### Soluble pathogenic tau transmission to astrocytes drives oxidative damage, senescence and neurovascular uncoupling
**Veronica Galvan & James Lechleiter | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Extending prior work on tau uptake by endothelium, this study shows soluble tau oligomers also enter astrocytes (via a heparin-sensitive route), causing microtubule destabilization, ATP depletion and mitochondrial ROS, followed by cellular senescence. Single-cell RNA-seq of hTau cortex flagged astrocytes as among the most transcriptionally altered cells, with coordinated electron-transport-chain downregulation and stress/inflammatory upregulation. Senescent astrocytes non-cell-autonomously reduced neuronal spine density and dendritic complexity; mitochondrial ROS scavenging blunted the SASP. Astrocyte-targeted SOD2 partly improved evoked cerebral blood flow, though that rescue did not reach significance — an appropriately hedged causal claim.

**Model/system & primary method:** hTau mouse cortex and primary human astrocytes; single-cell RNA-seq, mitochondrial/ROS assays, neuron-astrocyte coculture, and SOD2 overexpression. Mouse and in vitro mechanistic.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.08.750215

### The Parkinson's disease-associated BAP1/ASXL3 complex regulates internalization of α-synuclein fibrils
**Nicholas C. Karpilovsky & Edward A. Fon | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** A genome-wide CRISPR-activation screen for modifiers of α-synuclein fibril uptake identified BAP1, a PD risk gene, as a suppressor of fibril internalization. BAP1 acts as a transcriptional regulator of the cell-surface glycoproteome — downregulating heparan sulfate proteoglycans and upregulating O-linked glycoproteins — to reduce uptake. The effect is cell-type-specific (reducing uptake in iPSC-derived dopaminergic neurons but not microglia) and is mediated through ASXL3, another PD risk gene, in microglia-integrated human midbrain organoids. Higher BAP1/ASXL3 co-expression maps to brain regions less vulnerable to PD pathology, tying the complex to selective regional vulnerability.

**Model/system & primary method:** CRISPRa screen in cells; iPSC-derived dopaminergic neurons and microglia-integrated human midbrain organoids. Human cell/organoid mechanistic.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.09.26362627
