1Repetitive levodopa treatment drives cell-type-specific striatal adaptations associated with progressive dyskinesia in parkinsonian mice

Neuron  Rodrigo Manuel Paz & Alexandra B Nelson
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Levodopa-induced dyskinesia (LID) worsens with repeated dosing, but the drivers of its progression have been unclear. In the 6-OHDA mouse model, worsening LID tracked potentiation of levodopa-evoked responses in both D1- and D2-MSNs — independent of changes in dopamine release or MSN intrinsic excitability — with strengthening of glutamatergic synapses onto D1-MSNs emerging as the key signature. D2-MSN changes paralleled LID duration alongside a reduction in D2-receptor sensitivity. This reframes LID progression as a glutamatergic corticostriatal adaptation rather than a purely dopaminergic one, pointing to new synaptic targets.
Model & method
6-OHDA parkinsonian mice; combined in vivo and ex vivo electrophysiology of striatal D1-/D2-MSNs.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.028

2Motor neuron disease due to a novel PIK3R1 mutation

Brain  Brice Calco & Farinaz Safavi
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
A novel heterozygous PIK3R1 frameshift (c.1710dup) was found in a patient with common variable immunodeficiency who developed adult-onset, slowly progressive ALS-like motor neuron disease — the first link of this gene to nervous-system degeneration. Patient iPSC-derived motor neurons showed PIK3R1 haploinsufficiency with selective AKT activation, reduced electrical activity and markedly increased apoptosis; engineering the same variant into control iPSCs reproduced the phenotype, whereas canonical APDS2-associated PIK3R1 splice variants did not. This establishes a variant-class-specific mechanism linking PI3K-pathway dysregulation to human motor-neuron degeneration, though the clinical evidence rests on a single patient supported by isogenic cell models.
Model & method
Patient-derived and CRISPR-engineered iPSC motor neurons; single-cell RNA-seq and electrophysiology.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag403

3The niacin receptor HCAR2 is protective across early and advanced stages of amyloid pathology

Alzheimers Dement  Israel Coronel & Miguel Moutinho
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Niacin intake has been associated with lower Alzheimer's risk, and its high-affinity receptor HCAR2 (GPR109A) is expressed selectively by microglia in the brain. In 5xFAD mice, loss of HCAR2 exacerbated amyloid pathology at both early and advanced stages, while niacin treatment attenuated pathology even at advanced disease stages; complementary genetic analyses linked CSF Aβ42 levels to HCAR2 regulation. That a dietary agent can benefit late-stage amyloid pathology through a microglial receptor is therapeutically notable, though the causal evidence here is mouse-based.
Model & method
5xFAD mouse model; HCAR2 knockout and niacin treatment, with supporting human genetic association.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71988

4Glycosaminoglycans in the structures of Aβ42 amyloid fibrils purified from mouse brains

Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A  Dev Thacker & Sheena E Radford
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Ex vivo amyloid fibril structures often carry unassigned density adjacent to the protein core. Using cryo-EM, immunogold labeling and immunofluorescence on Aβ fibrils purified from AppNL-F and AppNL-G-F knock-in mouse brains, the authors identify this density as the glycosaminoglycans hyaluronic acid and chondroitin sulfate, which bind an exposed positively charged patch on the Aβ42 surface to form an intimate peptide–GAG copolymer. The glycan-binding site was present in Arctic (E22G) AppNL-G-F fibrils but required conformational rearrangement in a rare AppNL-F polymorph, the predominant ~85% of which were glycan-free. This provides a structural basis for GAG incorporation into brain-derived amyloid and nominates amyloid-associated ligands as therapeutic targets.
Model & method
Aβ fibrils purified from App knock-in mouse brains; cryo-EM with immunolabeling.
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2631665123

5Cancer Incidence After Parkinson Disease: A Nationwide Multi-Cohort Study With Genetic Analyses

Neurology  Rui Jian Chu & Valtteri Kaasinen
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Across three Finnish cohorts (FinnGen, FINPARK and a clinically validated Turku cohort; roughly 26,000 PD cases with matched controls), PD was associated with a 31–41% lower overall cancer incidence, strongest for respiratory and gastrointestinal cancers and persisting after smoking adjustment. Genetic analyses — Mendelian randomization, LD-score regression and polygenic risk scores — supported inverse associations between PD genetic liability and several cancers, with positive genetic correlations for melanoma and breast cancer, arguing for partly shared susceptibility rather than confounding alone. The authors caution that residual confounding and detection bias cannot be fully excluded and that generalizability beyond European populations is uncertain.
Model & method
Nationwide register and biobank cohorts; competing-risk/Cox models with MR, LDSC, PGS and GWAS.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218792

Highly selective visual circuits in mouse frontal cortex

Nat Neurosci  Anthony D Lien & Bilal Haider
Key findings
Frontal cortex neurons in awake mice showed highly selective visual spatial receptive fields resembling those of visual cortex, most sensitive to binocular visual space. These responses required retinotopically aligned V1 input, and the neurons sent functional axonal feedback throughout visual cortex, particularly binocular V1. The result reveals a cortex-wide circuit positioned for selective feedback control of spatial vision, challenging the assumption that elementary stimulus encoding is confined to sensory cortex.
Model & method
Awake mice; visual receptive-field mapping with axonal projection analysis and V1-dependent circuit tests.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02520-9

Blood proteome profiling of mild cognitive impairment identifies novel biomarkers for multi-pathway Alzheimer's assessment and cognitive decline prediction

Alzheimers Dement  Yuanbing Jiang & Nancy Y Ip
Key findings
Large-scale plasma proteomics across four independent MCI cohorts of Chinese and European descent identified 496 MCI-associated proteins enriched in inflammatory, angiogenic and protein-metabolism pathways. An 18-protein panel classified MCI (AUC 0.88–0.92), AD (0.95–0.97) and brain amyloid positivity (0.86–0.88) across populations, and panel-derived pathway scores captured neuronal, immune, vascular and metabolic dysfunction; longitudinal data showed the panel predicted cognitive decline over three years. The work extends blood biomarkers beyond the amyloid/tau/neurodegeneration axis toward multi-pathway disease assessment.
Model & method
Four human MCI cohorts; blood proteomic profiling with panel development and longitudinal validation.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.72031

Real-world comparison of lecanemab and donanemab in Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimers Dement  Moeko Noguchi-Shinohara & Kenjiro Ono
Key findings
In a retrospective comparison of 219 early-AD patients initiating amyloid-targeting therapy (134 lecanemab, 90 donanemab), six-month treatment persistence was higher with lecanemab (92.4% vs 78.1%), and ARIA occurred more often with lecanemab (11.2% vs 3.8%, predominantly isolated ARIA-H), while infusion-related reactions were the leading cause of donanemab discontinuation. Donanemab produced a significantly greater six-month reduction in CSF p-tau181, with a nonsignificant trend toward less MMSE decline. The agents showed distinct safety, persistence and biomarker profiles that may guide individualized selection; the retrospective, single-practice design limits causal inference.
Model & method
Real-world clinical cohort; comparison of ARIA, persistence, MMSE and CSF p-tau181.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71952

Beyond blood ammonia: Intestinal ammonia emerges as an early microbial driver of mild cognitive impairment

Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A  Hao Chen & Zheng Yu
Key findings
The study proposes gut microbiota-derived, urease-dependent intestinal ammonia as an early contributor to cognitive impairment. Human MCI-related cohorts showed increased fecal urease activity and ammonia-related microbial features; in mice, intestinal ammonia accumulation preceded detectable blood ammonia elevation, and ammonia suppressed colonic SMAD7 and activated brain JAK2 signaling to impair cognition independently of systemic hyperammonemia (supported in 5xFAD mice). Supplementation with an ammonia-assimilating bacterium (Limosilactobacillus mucosae) or L-ornithine-L-aspartate reduced intestinal ammonia and ameliorated early deficits, nominating an ammonia–SMAD7–JAK2 axis as a target. The causal mechanism rests primarily on mouse models.
Model & method
Human MCI cohorts with mouse models (incl. 5xFAD); microbiome, signaling and intervention studies.
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2606418123

Plasma Metabolic Proteomics Links Phenoconversion and Disease-Specific Brain 18F-FDG PET Pattern Expressions in Isolated REM Sleep Behavior Disorder

Ann Neurol  Ryul Kim & Jee-Young Lee
Key findings
Targeted plasma proteomics of 92 metabolism-related proteins across the REM-sleep-behavior-disorder–Lewy-body-disease continuum (116 patients, 30 controls) identified 16 differentially expressed proteins, enriched in neutrophil degranulation and concentrated in clinically manifest disease. In isolated RBD, only NT-proBNP was significantly upregulated, and higher NT-proBNP was associated with orthostatic intolerance, greater PD/DLB metabolic-pattern expression, and earlier phenoconversion over up to four years of follow-up. NT-proBNP is nominated as a candidate marker of short-term phenoconversion risk in this prodromal population.
Model & method
Prospective RBD–LBD cohort; proximity extension assay proteomics with FDG-PET and longitudinal follow-up.
https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78408

Circulating TIMP2 associates with brain health and lifestyle factors

Alzheimers Dement  Emily W Paolillo & Kaitlin B Casaletto
Key findings
Systemic TIMP2, previously shown to rejuvenate aged-mouse brain function, was examined in humans and mice. Higher plasma TIMP2 was associated with better global cognition and larger brain volumes across three human cohorts (n = 83, 31 and 213), and physical activity and multi-domain lifestyle changes were positively associated with plasma TIMP2. In mice, environmental enrichment raised plasma TIMP2, and enrichment-driven hippocampal neurogenesis was lost in TIMP2-null animals, tying a lifestyle-responsive circulating factor to neuroprotection. The human associations are cross-sectional.
Model & method
Three human cohorts with mouse environmental-enrichment models; plasma proteomics, neuroimaging and neurogenesis assays.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71963

Parkinson's disease-associated LRRK2 armadillo domain variants enhance kinase pathway activity through a Rab-binding site-dependent mechanism

medRxiv  Rashidova, N. & Alessi, D. R.
Key findings
Most pathogenic LRRK2 variants sit in the catalytic ROC-COR or kinase domains; this preprint characterizes N-terminal armadillo (ARM) domain variants. The Madrid haplotype variant L488P (but not L119P) roughly doubled LRRK2-mediated Rab10 phosphorylation in cells and in primary neutrophils and monocytes from carriers with and without PD, with coordinated increases across six Rab substrates. L488P lies adjacent to ARM Rab-binding site 1, and its activating effect was abolished by a site-1-disrupting substitution while activation by the ROC variant R1441C was retained; two of five additional rare ARM variants showed similar site-1-dependent activation. The data suggest an ARM-domain, membrane-recruitment–linked mechanism of LRRK2 activation distinct from catalytic-core variants, based on cellular and primary-cell assays.
Model & method
Cell lines and patient primary blood cells; Rab phosphorylation assays and PRM mass spectrometry.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.24.26368659

A proteomic signature for prognostic stratification in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis

medRxiv  Lester, D. G. & Thompson, A. G.
Key findings
High-depth serum and CSF proteomics from 426 people with ALS (851 serum and 244 CSF samples), replicated in a further 460 patients, identified survival-associated proteins including CSF TPM3 and serum NEFL and peripherin. A lasso-derived eight-protein serum panel (NEFL, peripherin, EDA2R, CALCA and others) improved survival prediction beyond clinical parameters and NEFL alone (external validation c-statistic 0.78 vs 0.74; 1-year AUC 0.89 vs 0.84), and serum EDA2R and CALCA trajectories tracked disease progression. The panel offers a route to stratify ALS prognostic heterogeneity, pending prospective validation.
Model & method
Longitudinal ALS serum/CSF cohorts; proximity extension assay proteomics with Cox and joint modeling.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.20.26358511

Disease-specific versus symptom-specific neuroimaging phenotypes across Alzheimer's Disease, Parkinson's Disease, and Small Vessel Disease

medRxiv  Lazari, A. & Mars, R. B.
Key findings
In 251 participants with Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, small vessel disease and age-matched controls scanned with a harmonized UK-Biobank MRI protocol, the authors found robust diagnosis-specific brain changes alongside substantial transdiagnostic overlap. Testing symptom–brain associations across diagnoses, lower medial frontal pole connectivity to primary motor cortex was preferentially associated with apathy, fatigue and depression, while cingulum microstructure was associated with cognitive deficits across disorders. The results suggest that shared symptoms across neurodegeneration partly reflect overlapping impact on common brain systems; the design is cross-sectional.
Model & method
Multimodal MRI across three neurodegenerative diseases and controls; case-control and transdiagnostic analyses.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.16.26368132

Plasma proteomic markers of cognitive and brain aging in a multi-ethnic cohort

medRxiv  Wood, B. & ADNI
Key findings
Plasma profiling of 220 neuro-related proteins (NULISAseq Neuro220) in 250 Chinese, South Asian and non-Hispanic White older adults linked proteins of neurodegeneration (including brain-derived phosphorylated tau), synaptic function, oxidative stress and inflammation to poorer cognition; phosphorylated Tau205 tracked reduced entorhinal thickness, and inflammatory proteins tracked small-vessel-disease imaging markers. Several associations (BDpTau species, NPTX2, BACE1, TREM1, CCL17, PRDX6, IGF1R) replicated in 1,381 ADNI participants, and cardiometabolic risk factors were linked to widespread proteomic alterations. The work extends brain-aging proteomics to populations historically under-represented in AD research; it is cross-sectional discovery with replication.
Model & method
Multi-ethnic older-adult cohort with ADNI replication; ultrasensitive plasma proteomics and neuroimaging.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.22.26368682

An AI agent guides a pipeline for selective protein interaction inhibitors from discovery across targets to preclinical evaluation

bioRxiv  Abdel-Rahman, S. & Gabr, M.
Key findings
An AI agent ("Bivexia") with decision rules fixed in advance and each selection cryptographically committed before testing guided discovery and de novo design of selective protein-interaction inhibitors. A selected compound bound LAG-3 with nanomolar affinity and disrupted its interactions with tau and amyloid-β while preserving binding to MHC class II and FGL1, and reduced tau and amyloid-β burden in two independent mouse models; the same framework produced hits against PrP and LRP1 but not in random controls. The paper is a proof-of-concept for programmable interaction selectivity with early AD-relevant in vivo efficacy; the disease relevance should be read cautiously pending peer review and independent replication.
Model & method
Computational design with biochemical binding assays and two amyloid/tau mouse models.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.23.768516
