### Human claustrum neurons encode uncertainty and prediction errors during aversive learning
**Mingyue Hu & Eyiyemisi C Damisah | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Single-neuron recordings from the human claustrum — a hub reciprocally connected to nearly all of neocortex but almost never recorded intracellularly in people — obtained during aversive active-avoidance learning, with simultaneous anterior cingulate and amygdala recordings. Claustrum neurons encoded model-derived latent variables (uncertainty and prediction error) that are not read out directly from sensory input, with a temporal profile dissociable from anterior cingulate: only cingulate carried uncertainty signals during the intertrial interval, whereas both regions encoded uncertainty and prediction error during active avoidance, while the amygdala showed minimal latent-variable tuning. The data provide direct human evidence that the claustrum tracks higher-order cognitive state rather than stimulus features.

**Model/system & primary method:** Intracranial single-neuron recordings in human patients; computational modeling of latent task variables during aversive learning.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02475-x

### Induced IL-10–expressing regulatory B cells reverse CNS autoimmunity by acting on microglia
**Benoît Manfroi & Simon Fillatreau | Immunity**

**Why it matters:** A compound screen identified N6, which drove stable IL-10 expression in >95% of B cells; transfer of these cells induced remission of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis. The cells accumulated in the CNS, differentiated into IL-10+ plasma cells, and reprogrammed CNS myeloid cells, and the therapeutic effect was abolished in mice lacking IL-10 receptor on long-lived myeloid cells — pointing to microglia as the critical target. An N6 analog corrected deficient IL-10 production in B cells from multiple sclerosis patients, suggesting a route to pharmacologically engineered regulatory-B-cell therapy. Efficacy evidence is causal but mouse-only; the human data are ex vivo.

**Model/system & primary method:** EAE in mice including IL-10R-conditional knockouts, compound screen and adoptive transfer; validation in MS patient–derived B cells.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2026.09.006

### Mitochondrial DNA damage and compensatory PINK1 upregulation in pontine cholinergic neurons in Parkinson's disease
**Huiwen Lu & Ilse S Pienaar | Brain**

**Why it matters:** Single-neuron ultra-deep whole-genome sequencing of pedunculopontine cholinergic neurons — a population that degenerates extensively in PD — from patient and control post-mortem brain shows these neurons accumulate large-scale mtDNA deletions (mostly >1 kb, concentrated in the major arc). The near-absence of small (<50 bp) deletions argues against free-radical strand breakage and for replication-associated error as the dominant mechanism; point-mutation burden matched controls but fell disproportionately on complex-I subunits. mtDNA damage was accompanied by marked PINK1 upregulation specifically in longer-surviving patients, suggesting mitophagy activation as an adaptive cytoprotective response rather than a driver of loss.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem single-neuron whole-genome sequencing plus multiplexed single-cell gene expression; thermodynamic modeling of deletion breakpoints.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag348

### Plasma pTau217 quantitatively tracks cortical pTau217 at autopsy
**Steffi De Meyer & Rik Vandenberghe | Neurology**

**Why it matters:** A post-mortem validation pairing antemortem plasma with temporal-cortex tissue (n = 32 dementia cases; 19 AD, 13 FTLD) shows plasma pTau217 and the pTau217/Aβ42 ratio correlate strongly with pTau217 measured directly in cortex (partial Spearman ρ ≈ 0.73–0.78) and separate AD from non-AD controls (AUC 0.88–0.94). After mutual adjustment, plasma markers remained associated with tau-aggregate density but not independently with amyloid-plaque density, grounding plasma pTau217 as a quantitative readout of brain tau burden rather than amyloid. Single memory-clinic cohort, modest n.

**Model/system & primary method:** Retrospective human paired antemortem-plasma / post-mortem-brain study; Lumipulse G immunoassays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218598

### Cognitive impairment in Parkinson's disease tracks cortical cholinergic axon loss at autopsy
**Rebecca Miller & Paul Kotzbauer | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** In 37 PD autopsy cases, neocortical choline acetyltransferase (ChAT) levels — an index of the integrity of cholinergic projections from the nucleus basalis of Meynert — correlated positively with MMSE and a composite global-cognition z-score, with a trend toward inverse correlation with CDR sum of boxes. The findings give direct human neuropathological support for degeneration of cortical cholinergic projections as a substrate of cognitive decline in PD, reinforcing the rationale for cholinergic-targeted approaches. Cross-sectional and correlational.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human autopsy cohort; ChAT sandwich ELISA across eight brain regions.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71868

### Clusterin is enriched in plaque-associated astrocytes and localizes to synapses in Alzheimer's disease
**Robert A J Byrne & Nikoleta Daskoulidou | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Using newly generated monoclonal antibodies that distinguish secreted from intracellular clusterin (CLU is a top AD GWAS risk locus), the authors show intracellular clusterin is astrocyte-specific and selectively enriched in plaque-associated astrocytes, particularly those surrounding larger amyloid plaques, while clusterin also localizes to synapses in both healthy and AD cortex. The work resolves the cellular source and subcellular distribution of a long-studied AD biomarker and candidate therapeutic target.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human plasma/serum/CSF and brain tissue plus iPSC-derived astrocytes; validated monoclonal antibodies, immunostaining and quantification.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71885

### SOD1 co-pathologies in the brain: neuropathological evidence and implications
**Evellyn Mayla de Azevedo & Tiago Fleming Outeiro | Acta Neuropathol**

**Key findings:** A review of neuropathological evidence that misfolded and aggregated SOD1 co-occurs with TDP-43, tau, α-synuclein and amyloid-β beyond amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, across multiple neurodegenerative diseases. The authors propose SOD1 as a participant in interconnected proteopathic networks via oxidative stress, impaired proteostasis, aberrant phase separation and cross-seeding, arguing for a shift from viewing SOD1 as a disease-specific protein to a broadly relevant aggregation-prone one with biomarker and therapeutic implications.

**Model/system & primary method:** Review of human neuropathology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03090-7

### The locus coeruleus in sleep, cerebrovascular control, and glymphatic clearance
**Pablo R Castillo & Eduardo Benarroch | Neurology**

**Key findings:** A review synthesizing evidence that locus coeruleus noradrenergic activity shapes NREM sleep architecture — ultraslow LC oscillations reduce N2 spindle density and phase-lock to N3 slow waves, while LC silencing promotes the NREM-to-REM transition — and drives tightly synchronized vasoconstriction/vasodilation cycles that pump CSF through periarterial spaces to support glymphatic clearance. Links LC function to brain waste clearance with implications for neurodegenerative disease prevention.

**Model/system & primary method:** Review.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218662

### Serum chitotriosidase links to activated glia at chronic active lesion rims in multiple sclerosis
**Venla Ahola & Laura Airas | Ann Neurol**

**Key findings:** In 89 people with MS imaged with [11C]PK11195 TSPO-PET, higher serum chitotriosidase (CHIT1) correlated with the number, volume and percentage of rim-active (chronic active) lesions and with perilesional TSPO binding, as well as with GFAP and neurofilament light. Spatial transcriptomics confirmed elevated CHIT1 expression at the edges of chronic active lesions. The data nominate serum CHIT1 as a candidate blood marker of smouldering glial activation. Cross-sectional, exploratory median-split cohort requiring validation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human TSPO-PET cross-sectional study plus post-mortem immunohistochemistry and re-analysis of spatial/single-nucleus transcriptomics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78386

### Neuropsychiatric and cognitive symptom evolution over the first decade of biomarker-positive Parkinson's disease
**Izabelle Schoen & Daniel Weintraub | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** In dual biomarker-positive sporadic PD (CSF α-synuclein seed amplification–positive and abnormal dopamine-transporter SPECT; n = 846) versus controls (n = 282), eight of nine neuropsychiatric symptoms rose in prevalence over 10 years (6.6%→23.5%), with the steepest increases for psychosis and apathy, while anxiety declined slightly; mean symptom count grew from 1.39 to 2.39 as controls remained flat. Low- and high-burden phenotypes identifiable at diagnosis persisted at 5 and 10 years, underscoring progressive, heterogeneous neuropsychiatric trajectories.

**Model/system & primary method:** Longitudinal biomarker-defined human PD cohort; generalized estimating equations, PCA and cluster analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70557

### Loss of the age-associated increase in m6A-modified RNA contributes to GABAergic dysregulation in Alzheimer's disease
**J. L. Libera & B. Wolozin | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Nucleotide-resolution m6A mapping (DART-seq) in human dorsolateral prefrontal cortex finds that m6A sites normally increase with age, predominantly in the 3'UTRs of transcripts encoding tripartite-synapse proteins, but AD brains lose this age-associated gain and show global hypomethylation including of MAPT. Hypomethylated, down-regulated genes clustered in GABAergic signaling, glutamate transport and ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis, and the normal coupling between GABRA1 m6A and its expression was abolished in AD — tying an epitranscriptomic aging signature to synaptic excitotoxicity and proteostasis failure. Human post-mortem, correlational. v4 revision of a preprint first posted May 2025.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem dorsolateral prefrontal cortex; DART-seq m6A profiling.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.05.02.651974

### Pathological tau drives network hyperexcitability through neuronal-subtype-specific functional impairment
**C. Ji & E. Sigurdsson | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In human iPSC-derived neurons, the familial MAPT P301L mutation produced network hyperexcitability arising from an excitation/inhibition imbalance traced to subtype-specific tau pathology and distinct tau interactomes in excitatory versus inhibitory neurons. Suppressing enhanced excitatory synaptic transmission, or blocking the Ca2+ influx driven by an enhanced P301L-tau/AHNAK interaction in inhibitory neurons, rescued the hyperexcitability — nominating AHNAK-dependent Ca2+ entry as a mechanistic target. In vitro human model.

**Model/system & primary method:** iPSC-derived excitatory and inhibitory neurons; network electrophysiology and tau interactome analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.05.756807

### Axonal dysfunction precedes axonal degeneration in a mouse model of progressive parkinsonism
**A. M. Lipkin & Z. M. Khaliq | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In the MitoPark model, substantia nigra dopaminergic axons showed a pronounced reduction in action-potential-evoked calcium signals, impaired AP propagation and an altered AP waveform well before dopaminergic fiber loss or motor deficits appeared. The results define a window of reversible axonal physiological dysfunction preceding "die-back," pointing to early axonal excitability deficits as a potential therapeutic window. Mouse model.

**Model/system & primary method:** MitoPark mouse; axonal electrophysiology and calcium imaging versus age-matched controls.

### Metabolic commitment to human motor neurons is characterized by mitochondrial depletion and pyruvate rewiring
**R. Torregrosa-Munumer & H. Tyynismaa | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** A temporal multi-omic resource across human iPSC-to-motor-neuron differentiation reveals prolonged mitochondrial depletion — reduced content, fragmentation and lysosome-dependent turnover — through the neural progenitor stage, followed by recovery of mitochondrial content during maturation. The transition is accompanied by a shift from pyruvate carboxylation toward pyruvate-dehydrogenase-dependent oxidation, and pharmacologically forcing this switch was required for neuronal differentiation. Establishes metabolic control points for human motor-neuron generation relevant to ALS modeling. In vitro human.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-to-motor-neuron differentiation; integrated proteomics, metabolomics and mitochondrial characterization with pharmacological perturbation.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.05.756667

### Blood biomarkers of Alzheimer's disease and response to a multidomain lifestyle intervention: secondary analysis of the FINGER trial
**A. Rosenberg & M. Kivipelto | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In the 2-year FINGER multidomain-lifestyle RCT (N = 1,260 at-risk older adults), 85% had baseline plasma p-tau217 below the pathological threshold. Higher baseline biomarkers (Aβ, p-tau181/217/231, NfL, GFAP) predicted less favorable cognitive trajectories but only modestly predicted amyloid-PET positivity (p-tau217 AUC 0.72); baseline levels did not modify the intervention's cognitive benefit, and biomarker changes over two years were small and similar across arms. This suggests the intervention's cognitive benefit in a largely biomarker-negative population acts mainly through pathways not captured by AD blood biomarkers. Secondary analysis.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human RCT secondary analysis; Lumipulse plasma AD biomarkers.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.02.26364486
