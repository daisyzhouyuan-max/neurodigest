### Blood-brain barrier opening with focused ultrasound reduces neuromelanin in a non-human primate model
**Noelia Esteban-García & Javier Blesa | Brain**

**Why it matters:** Neuromelanin accumulation is thought to sensitize nigral catecholaminergic neurons in Parkinson's disease, yet it has been essentially untargetable in vivo. In a macaque engineered to overexpress human tyrosinase — which drives NM build-up in the substantia nigra pars compacta alongside extracellular NM release, iron loading, nigral inflammation and intracellular inclusions, but without substantial dopaminergic loss or motor deficits — repeated low-intensity focused ultrasound with microbubbles (LIFU-MB) to open the blood-brain barrier reduced NM accumulation. This nominates a non-invasive, spatially targeted route to modulate a candidate upstream driver of nigral vulnerability, in a primate brain, before overt degeneration.

**Model/system & primary method:** Tyrosinase-overexpressing non-human primate model of early PD; repeated LIFU-MB BBB opening over the SNpc with histological and imaging characterization. Causal evidence is primate but pre-degenerative; durability and effect on eventual cell loss remain open.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag343

### CRISPR screen reveals FTO-mediated m6A modification of ATF3 as protective in ischaemic stroke
**Jiangtao Yu & Xiaoqing Jin | Brain**

**Why it matters:** A CRISPR-Cas9 loss-of-function screen across 118 RNA-modification genes in neurons under oxygen-glucose deprivation/reoxygenation identified the m6A demethylase Fto as the top negatively selected (protective) gene; Fto fell and global m6A rose during ischaemic injury. Neuron-specific Fto knockout enlarged infarcts and worsened neurological and cognitive deficits and apoptosis, while Fto overexpression was protective in vivo. Integrated m6A-seq/RNA-seq placed Atf3 as a hypermethylated, upregulated downstream hub, defining an Fto/m6A/ATF3 axis as a tractable mechanism of ischaemic neuronal death.

**Model/system & primary method:** Neuronal CRISPR screen under OGD/R; mouse middle cerebral artery occlusion/reperfusion with neuron-specific Fto knockout and overexpression; m6A-seq and RNA-seq. Causal in mouse/cell; human relevance not yet tested.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag342

### Astrocytic PI3Kα controls synaptic plasticity and cognitive function via serine metabolism
**Alba Fernández-Rodrigo & José A Esteban | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** The work ties astrocyte metabolic state directly to synaptic function. Deleting the p110α isoform of PI3K in hippocampal astrocytes rewired their metabolism — reducing glycolytic flux and raising mitochondrial respiration — which curtailed L-serine biosynthesis and, in turn, impaired NMDA-receptor activation during LTP induction. The LTP defect was rescued by the NMDAR co-agonist D-serine or its precursor L-serine, and the resulting memory deficits were reversed by in vivo L-serine administration, establishing astrocytic PI3K–serine supply as a causal gate on plasticity and cognition relevant to brain aging.

**Model/system & primary method:** Hippocampal astrocyte-specific p110α knockout mice; LTP electrophysiology, metabolic flux analysis, behavioral memory tasks with D-/L-serine rescue. Mouse causal evidence.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77319-0

### Structural compression and entorhinal vulnerability: Linking tentorial adjacency to tau burden and dementia progression
**Luyue Zhang & Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** The authors propose a biomechanical contribution to the entorhinal cortex's early tau vulnerability in sporadic AD. Using a neuroanatomical contact coefficient to quantify EC proximity to the tentorial incisura in ADNI multimodal imaging (n = 47), entorhinal tau-PET signal predicted conversion from MCI to AD only in the high-adjacency group (P = 0.036), after controlling for standard risk factors. The claim is anatomical and correlational — a small, cross-sectional stratification rather than mechanical proof — but offers a testable, geometry-based risk marker.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cross-sectional ADNI cohort (n = 47); tau-PET, structural MRI, custom adjacency metric. Observational.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71827

### Brain weight across the adult lifespan in great apes
**Branka Hrvoj-Mihic & Chet C Sherwood | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** Across chimpanzees, bonobos, gorillas and orangutans spanning the full adult age range, brain weight showed no significant decline into advanced age, including when body weight was accounted for in a chimpanzee subsample. In contrast to the pronounced age-related brain atrophy of humans, this suggests marked late-life brain weight loss is largely human-specific rather than a shared primate aging process — framing human brain shrinkage, and its exacerbation in AD and other dementias, as a lineage-specific susceptibility. The evidence is cross-sectional postmortem weight, not longitudinal volumetry.

**Model/system & primary method:** Comparative postmortem brain-weight analysis across four great ape species spanning adult lifespan. Observational cross-species comparison.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2609260123

### NKCC1 coordinates SPAK-PP1 signalling to regulate KCC2 membrane stability and seizure susceptibility
**Erwan Pol & Sabine Lévi | Brain**

**Key findings:** Chloride homeostasis — and thus GABAA inhibition — hinges on the NKCC1/KCC2 balance. The authors show KCC2 physically co-clusters with NKCC1 and SPAK in hippocampal neuronal membranes, with freely diffusing KCC2 becoming trapped in NKCC1 clusters, and that NKCC1 coordinates SPAK/PP1-dependent control of KCC2 surface stability. Rationally designed peptides tuned this interaction in opposite directions: a SPAK-inhibiting peptide that blocks SPAK recruitment stabilized surface KCC2 and enhanced chloride extrusion, with an optimized analog suitable for in vivo use — a candidate strategy for restoring inhibition in seizure disorders.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse brain co-immunoprecipitation, single-molecule membrane imaging in hippocampal neurons, structure-guided peptide design with in vivo-compatible analog. Mechanistic, mouse/cellular.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag341

### Directional Adaptive Deep Brain Stimulation: An Initial Report of Safety, Tolerability, and Effectiveness
**Martijn Beudel & ADAPT‐PD Trial Investigators | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** From a supplemental cohort of the ADAPT-PD trial, 17 people with Parkinson's (15 completing) switched from stable continuous directional DBS to chronic directional adaptive DBS. Both directional aDBS modes met the prespecified primary endpoint; on-time without troublesome dyskinesia increased by a median of 1.2 hours/day in the dual-threshold mode, with stimulation-related events comparable to continuous DBS. All 15 completers elected to continue directional aDBS chronically. This is a small, open-label feasibility report — encouraging tolerability and preliminary efficacy signals rather than controlled effectiveness.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human open-label supplemental cohort (n = 17 enrolled); within-subject comparison of directional continuous vs adaptive DBS. Early-phase clinical.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70556

### Ofatumumab vs Ocrelizumab in Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis: A Nationwide Cohort Study
**Sahla El Mahdaoui & DMSG Study Group | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In 1,870 patients with RRMS from the Danish MS Registry (485 ocrelizumab, 1,385 ofatumumab; 2022–2025), propensity-weighted comparison found the two anti-CD20 therapies broadly comparable on annualized relapse rate and time to first relapse, with secondary outcomes spanning confirmed disability worsening, PIRA and MRI lesion activity. The observational design and registry confounding temper causal interpretation, but the nationwide scale provides real-world comparative-effectiveness evidence between a subcutaneous and an intravenous anti-CD20 option.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human nationwide observational cohort (n = 1,870) with inverse-probability-of-treatment weighting; ITT and per-protocol analyses. Observational comparative effectiveness.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218545

### Plasma eMTBR-tau as a robust biomarker for Alzheimer's disease tangle pathology up to 10 years before death
**Shi, R. & Karikari, T. K. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** A blood immunoassay for an endogenously-cleaved eMTBR-tau fragment tracked autopsy-diagnosed Alzheimer neuropathologic change and, distinctively, reflected tangle burden and Braak-stage anatomical progression rather than amyloid plaque load. Plasma eMTBR-tau outperformed p-tau217 at classifying Braak stage and ADNC, holding accuracy (AUC 0.91–0.95) even when blood was drawn up to a decade before death, and higher baseline levels predicted faster cognitive decline; findings replicated against in vivo tau/amyloid imaging (up to 94% concordance). If it holds, this is a scalable tangle-specific blood marker — but it remains an unreviewed preprint pending independent validation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human autopsy-linked plasma cohorts with ADNC/Braak staging, replicated in a living cohort with tau/amyloid PET; blood immunoassay vs p-tau217. Observational, correlative with pathology.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.29.26363951

### Templated seeding induces the formation of Alzheimer paired helical tau filaments in human brain organoids
**Bacioglu, M. & Spillantini, M. G. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** AD frontal-cortex tau seeds added to human iPSC-derived cortical organoids expressing endogenous three-repeat tau induced three-repeat tau filaments whose formation required endogenous tau — and which were, by cryo-EM, structurally identical to the paired helical filaments of the seeds. This is a stringent demonstration of faithful prion-like templating in human neural tissue at physiological tau levels, providing a genetically tractable human system to dissect PHF assembly and downstream toxicity rather than relying on overexpression or non-human cells.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC cortical organoids seeded with AD brain-derived tau; cryo-EM structural comparison of induced vs seed filaments. In vitro human-tissue model.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.30.755295

### Monomeric TDP-43 binds GU-rich RNA via its NTD but only its oligomers block cryptic exon inclusion
**Polymenidou, M. & Leitner, A. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The N-terminal domain of TDP-43 preferentially bound UG-rich RNA through residues at its oligomerization interface, but only on monomeric protein — RNA binding and oligomerization compete for the same surface. By separating these activities, the authors show that oligomerization, not NTD–RNA interaction, is what is required for TDP-43-dependent alternative splicing and repression of cryptic exons. This refines the mechanistic link between TDP-43 self-assembly and its nuclear loss-of-function in ALS/FTD, positioning oligomeric state as the determinant of cryptic-exon repression.

**Model/system & primary method:** In vitro biochemistry/biophysics of recombinant TDP-43 NTD (RNA binding, oligomerization) with cellular splicing and cryptic-exon readouts. Mechanistic, reductionist.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.01.755875

### Structural integrity moderates the cognitive bearing of dopamine clearance in aging
**Esmaeili, A. & Jirsa, V. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** An "amortized dopaminergic aging model" coupled personalized dopamine-clearance estimates with individual structural connectomes and fMRI to constrain whole-brain dynamics across 3,650 adults aged 36–95 from five cohorts. Dopamine clearance timescales rose with age; critically, elevated clearance tracked poorer cognitive performance only in individuals with marked structural degradation, and only within age windows centred between roughly 56 and 76 years. The framework attempts to disentangle neuromodulatory from structural contributions per individual — a modeling/inference result on observational data rather than direct neurochemical measurement.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multi-cohort modeling (n = 3,650); structural connectome embeddings updated with fMRI to infer dopamine clearance timescales. Computational/observational.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.29.753541

### Hyperexcitable primary somatosensory cortex in the MitoPark mouse model of Parkinson's disease
**Zhang, R. & Emanuel, A. J. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** To probe the somatosensory deficits common in PD, the authors recorded primary somatosensory cortex in MitoPark mice, which undergo progressive, α-synuclein-independent dopamine-neuron degeneration. At late symptomatic stages, layer V regular-spiking (largely pyramidal) neurons — but not fast-spiking inhibitory neurons — showed elevated spontaneous firing and became hyperresponsive to tactile stimuli, recruiting more neurons at higher rates; fast-spiking cells instead desynchronized. These changes coincided with pronounced dopaminergic degeneration in midbrain, striatum and S1, linking progressive dopamine loss to cell-type-specific cortical hyperexcitability underlying altered somatosensation.

**Model/system & primary method:** MitoPark mouse model of PD; in vivo S1 electrophysiology with tactile stimulation and tyrosine-hydroxylase histology. Mouse, correlative with degeneration stage.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.29.755142
