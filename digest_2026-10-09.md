### Alzheimer's disease target and drug discovery by leveraging multiomics and electronic health data
**Yuan Hou & Feixiong Cheng | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Mendelian randomization across multiple AD GWAS and functional multiomics datasets nominated druggable targets — 19 for European-ancestry and 7 for African-ancestry individuals — including epoxide hydrolase 2 (EPHX2), where a protective missense variant (p.Arg287Gln) acted via reduced EPHX2 and phosphorylated tau (pTau-181) in patient iPSC-derived neurons and cerebral organoids. Pharmacological EPHX2 inhibition improved cognition in 5xFAD mice, and mining electronic health records from 111,680 patients with mild cognitive impairment flagged 12 drugs associated with lower AD incidence, including trazodone (ADRA1A) and baclofen (GABBR1). The value here is the chaining of genetic causal inference, functional validation and real-world repurposing signals in one pipeline.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human GWAS/multiomics Mendelian randomization; iPSC-derived neurons and cerebral organoids; 5xFAD mice; EHR pharmacoepidemiology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02472-0

### Microglia-mediated extracellular matrix disruption drives neuronal dysfunction, motor impairments, and sedation following alcohol abuse
**Evi Paouri & Dimitrios Davalos | Immunity**

**Why it matters:** Using binge and high-intensity ethanol models with longitudinal in vivo imaging, microglia showed acute and progressive morphological responses that preceded and paralleled ethanol-induced sedation, and drove extracellular matrix (ECM) disruption, synapse elimination, and reduced neuronal activity and density. Microglia-specific deletion of Myd88 prevented ECM disruption and protected against neuronal damage, intoxication and motor impairment, placing microglial innate-immune signalling causally upstream of the acute neural effects of alcohol. Frontal-cortex ECM was similarly disrupted in human AUD brain tissue, supporting translational relevance, though the causal manipulations are mouse-only.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse binge/high-intensity ethanol with microglial Myd88 deletion and in vivo two-photon imaging; human AUD cortical tissue.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2026.09.011

### Associations between plasma biomarker levels and quantitative postmortem neuropathologic measures in Alzheimer's disease and related dementias
**Celia R Bianco & Alberto Serrano-Pozo | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** In 124 MADRC autopsy participants, plasma p-tau217 associated strongly with amyloid plaques (especially neuritic) and AT8+ phospho-tau neurons, while p-tau181 and total tau were weaker; NfL related only to lower cortical thickness, and GFAP tracked plaques, tau and astrogliosis. Plasma p-tau217 and GFAP statistically mediated the plaque-to-tangle association. Because the comparison is against quantitative, region-resolved neuropathology rather than PET surrogates, it anchors the biological specificity of each blood marker to distinct downstream processes. The mediation is cross-sectional, so it indexes biological alignment rather than temporal sequence.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; plasma immunoassays (MSD) correlated with quantitative postmortem neuropathology across temporal, frontal and occipital lobes.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71883

### White matter hyperintensity burden in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis: motor and survival correlates
**Alberto De Lorenzo & Nicola Ticozzi | Brain**

**Why it matters:** Across 399 ALS patients, MRI white-matter hyperintensity (WMH) burden — a marker of small-vessel disease — scaled with more advanced King's and MiToS staging, greater ALSFRS-R impairment, and cognitive deficits on the ECAS, and correlated specifically with lower (not upper) motor-neuron impairment. High periventricular WMH burden independently predicted shorter survival (adjusted hazard ratio ≈ 3.5) after accounting for established prognostic factors, suggesting a diffuse neurovascular component to ALS that could aid stratification. The design is retrospective and the associations observational, so causal direction between microvascular pathology and neurodegeneration remains open.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; retrospective MRI cohort with visual grading and semi-automated volumetric WMH quantification, survival analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag347

### Simultaneous awake replay of multiple spatial representations
**Xiang Mou & Daoyun Ji | Neuron**

**Key findings:** In a rat observational navigation task, hippocampal place-cell sequences representing multiple distinct T-maze trajectories were frequently co-replayed within single sharp-wave-ripple population bursts, rather than one trajectory at a time. These simultaneous replay events respected the maze's spatial layout, preferentially converged on reward sites, distinguished upcoming behavioral choices, and were coupled to choice-selective activity in anterior cingulate cortex; single-trajectory replay showed weaker behavioral and cortical coupling. The work establishes parallel, rather than strictly serial, hippocampal replay during reward-directed decision-making.

**Model/system & primary method:** Rat; hippocampal ensemble recording during an observational navigation task with simultaneous anterior cingulate recording.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.014

## NEURODEGENERATION & AGING — TOP 5

### Plasma eMTBR-tau243 as a disease biomarker across Alzheimer's disease clinical phenotypes
**Jennifer L Whitwell & Keith A Josephs | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In 20 amyloid-PET-positive AD patients spanning four clinical phenotypes and 10 controls, plasma eMTBR-tau243 (a microtubule-binding-region tau species), p-tau217 and %p-tau217 were all elevated in AD, with the highest levels in motor-predominant AD. eMTBR-tau243 correlated most strongly with cortical flortaucipir-PET uptake and was the only marker correlating with cognition, but was undetectable in 45% of patients with mild/focal tau-PET — consistent with a marker that rises with established tangle burden. The cohort is small, so phenotype-level claims are preliminary.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; plasma mass-spectrometry/immunoassay tau species correlated with tau- and amyloid-PET and cognition.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71898

### Relationship Between Braak and Clinical Stage in Autopsy-Confirmed Parkinson Disease: A UK Brain Bank Study
**Lazzaro di Biase & Alberto J Espay | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In 61 autopsy-confirmed Parkinson's cases, α-synuclein Braak stage was not correlated with Hoehn & Yahr stage at death, disease duration, age at death, or brain weight, and copathologies (allocortical tau 82%, small-vessel disease 60%, β-amyloid 58%) did not differ across Braak stages. The finding challenges the assumption that pathologic α-synuclein Braak staging indexes clinical progression, implying that spread of Lewy pathology alone is an incomplete model of PD course. The sample is modest and single-center, and staging relied on categorical pathology rather than quantified synuclein load.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; retrospective clinicopathologic analysis of an autopsy-proven PD brain bank cohort.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218469

### Proteomic dysregulation associated with cerebral amyloid angiopathy
**Sang-Won Han & Kwangsik Nho | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Integrating postmortem brain proteomics (ROS/MAP) with CSF proteomics (ADNI), 30 proteins associated with cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) severity along pathways distinct from parenchymal AD, with 11 replicated in CSF. Two network modules implicated microglial vesicle transport and astrocytic potassium homeostasis, and three proteins (RCSD1, FRZB, AKAP6) improved CAA classification beyond demographics and APOE ε4. The signatures offer candidate biomarkers for detecting CAA and gauging risk of therapy-related amyloid imaging abnormalities, relevant as anti-amyloid immunotherapy expands. Associations are cross-sectional and derive from pseudotime ordering rather than longitudinal data.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; postmortem brain and CSF proteomics with network and pseudotime analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71901

### Longitudinal associations of rapid eye movement (REM) sleep and slow-wave sleep with plasma Alzheimer's disease biomarkers
**Jeongyeon Hwang & Andrew W Varga | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In 64 cognitively unimpaired older adults with polysomnography and plasma amyloid sampled ~2.6 years apart, frontal slow-wave activity was inversely associated with cross-sectional plasma Aβ42/40, whereas low REM-sleep percentage predicted a larger longitudinal decline in Aβ42/40 — an effect stronger in amyloid-positive participants. The dissociation suggests non-REM slow-wave activity indexes current amyloid load while REM sleep may track accelerating amyloid accumulation in preclinical stages, nominating both as early, potentially modifiable targets. The cohort is small and amyloid is measured in plasma rather than CSF/PET.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; polysomnography with longitudinal plasma Aβ42/40 by mass spectrometry.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71910

### KCNA2-Associated Hereditary Spastic Paraplegia: Clinical Characteristics and Rationale for Targeted Treatment
**Christian M Boßelmann & Holger Lerche | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** Across 23 individuals from 17 families, the recurrent KCNA2 p.Arg294His variant produced a recognizable hereditary spastic paraplegia with fully penetrant abnormal evoked potentials, cerebellar ataxia (83%) and lower-limb spasticity (70%). Off-label eplerenone, a potassium-sparing diuretic, was associated with improvement on the ataxia rating scale (Cohen's d = −1.8) but not the spasticity scale, with worsening on withdrawal and 4/5 patients choosing to continue at 2 years. This provides a variant-specific phenotype and preliminary rationale for a mechanism-matched therapy in a rare channelopathy. Evidence is retrospective, off-label and uncontrolled, so the treatment signal is exploratory.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; retrospective rare-disease case series with off-label eplerenone outcomes.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70555

### Engineered Chimeric Proteins Functionally Replace ALS-causing Endogenous FUS and TDP-43 to Improve Neuronal Health
**Hayashi & Guo | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The authors append optimized anti-aggregation motifs to FUS and TDP-43 to create engineered chimeric versions with enhanced nuclear localization and solubility, intended to functionally replace the aggregation-prone endogenous proteins central to ALS/FTD. The chimeras reportedly lower endogenous FUS/TDP-43 via autoregulation while preserving nuclear functions: chimeric FUS restored neurite outgrowth in patient iPSC-derived neurons, and chimeric TDP-43 suppressed cryptic exon inclusion in patient fibroblasts. If it holds, this reframes RNA-binding-protein proteinopathies as potentially addressable by replacing, rather than only clearing, the toxic protein — but the evidence is in vitro and in patient-derived cells, without in vivo or longevity data.

**Model/system & primary method:** Patient iPSC-derived neurons and fibroblasts; engineered chimeric RBP expression with aggregation, localization and splicing readouts.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.28.755047

### Targeting microglial P2Y12-receptor signalling attenuates neurodegeneration in experimental Parkinson's disease
**Iring & Sperlagh | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Tamoxifen-inducible, microglia-specific deletion of P2ry12 in acute and subchronic MPTP models reduced nigrostriatal dopaminergic neuron loss, preserved dopamine synthesis, attenuated neuroinflammation, and improved motor deficits — and remained protective when deletion was induced after neurodegeneration had begun, arguing P2Y12R contributes to ongoing progression. Novel structure-based P2Y12R antagonists inhibited the receptor in vitro, and central administration of the lead compound recapitulated the genetic neuroprotection, giving convergent genetic and pharmacological proof-of-concept. Causal evidence is mouse-only and confined to toxin-based Parkinsonism models.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse MPTP Parkinsonism with inducible microglial P2ry12 deletion and central administration of novel P2Y12R antagonists.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.02.756181

### Disease stage-sensitive MFSD2A-caveolae axis instigates hippocampal blood-brain barrier disruption in Alzheimer's disease
**Zhu & Yi | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Loss of endothelial MFSD2A, a suppressor of caveolae-mediated transcytosis, is reported as an early, hippocampus-specific feature of AD vascular impairment: reduced in post-mortem human AD hippocampus alongside raised caveolin-1 and perivascular albumin leakage, and declining across the MCI-to-AD transition in single-nucleus data. In APP/PS1 mice, early MFSD2A loss coincided with disproportionate hippocampal BBB leakage and reduced GLUT1/claudin-5. AAV-BR1 restoration of endothelial MFSD2A during early — but not established — amyloid pathology preserved BBB integrity, reduced plaque burden and neuritic dystrophy, and improved LTP and spatial memory, defining a stage-dependent therapeutic window. Evidence rests on a mouse model plus human correlative data.

**Model/system & primary method:** APP/PS1 mice with AAV-BR1 endothelial MFSD2A restoration; human post-mortem and single-nucleus transcriptomic data.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.30.755839

### Large-scale cerebrospinal fluid proteomics identifies diagnostic biomarker candidates across the clinico-pathological continuum of frontotemporal lobar degeneration
**Saloner & Boxer | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Multi-platform CSF proteomics in 566 individuals (including 48 autopsy-confirmed FTLD-TDP and 90 FTLD-tau) identified elevated MMP10 as a near-perfect classifier of Pick's disease versus other FTLD subtypes, validated by targeted immunoassay in an independent autopsy-confirmed cohort. Post-synaptic HOMER1 and DLG4 were elevated across FTLD-TDP subtypes versus FTLD-tau, and network analysis separated RNA-metabolism/pro-inflammatory signatures (FTLD-TDP) from cytoskeletal/heat-shock signatures (PSP). These are candidate antemortem markers for a group where in vivo molecular diagnosis is currently limited. Findings await peer review and prospective validation against future autopsy confirmation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human CSF; multi-platform proteomics with targeted immunoassay validation in autopsy-confirmed FTLD cohorts.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.29.755483

### CCT2 expression marks a translation-activated state in sporadic ALS motor neurons and is associated with functional decline
**Bhandary & Wang | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Across iPSC-derived motor neurons from 619 AnswerALS participants profiled by transcriptomics, proteomics and whole-genome sequencing, scoring 391 sporadic patients along bioenergetic-stress, cell-cycle and inflammation axes resolved three molecular states. One, marked by the TRiC chaperonin subunit CCT2, separated from the quiescent state (AUC 0.892), was elevated at the protein level, and tracked the rate of ALSFRS-R decline — though that association weakened after adjusting for culture composition. The state sat downstream of MYC/mTORC1 activation, was not explained by germline variation, and the CCT2–cell-cycle link replicated in postmortem layer-5b-enriched cortex, offering a candidate biology-based stratifier for sporadic ALS trials. Preprint; the clinical association is correlative and partly confounded by culture composition.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived motor neurons (multi-omics) with postmortem cortex validation.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.06.757033
