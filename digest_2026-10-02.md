### Different functional connectivity gradients reflect aging and Alzheimer's disease
**Jonathan Rittmo & Jacob W Vogel | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** This resolves a long-standing debate over whether AD-related connectivity change is simply accelerated aging. Across two independent cohorts, the two processes load onto orthogonal axes of cortical hierarchy: early AD pathology and subsequent cognitive decline track change along the sensory–association gradient, whereas normal aging shifts connectivity along a representation–executive gradient consistently across the adult lifespan. The dissociation argues the two are mechanistically distinct and offers a whole-cortex gradient framework for separating them in vivo.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human resting-state fMRI connectivity gradients; BioFINDER-2 (N = 973) and ADNI (N = 129) with amyloid/tau measures. Cross-sectional/observational.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02402-0

### Lateral hypothalamic control of the striatal dopamine landscape during consummatory behavior
**Adam G Gordon & Garret D Stuber | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** The lateral hypothalamus is shown to actively sculpt a spatially organized dopamine "landscape" across the striatum during consumption, not merely gate feeding. Opposing LHA GABAergic and glutamatergic populations scale their activity with rewarding and aversive solutions, and their balance sets an anterior–posterior dopamine gradient — anterior tracking value and recent history, posterior encoding sensorimotor features — with release controlled locally rather than propagated. Manipulation establishes the LHA dependence causally and links it to licking initiation, giving a circuit-level account of how the hypothalamus shapes motivated behavior.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mice; cell-type-specific recording and optogenetic manipulation of LHA neurons with spatially resolved striatal dopamine-sensor imaging during consummatory behavior.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.002

### A human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived spheroid model to investigate myelin injury and repair
**Shwathy Ramesan & Samantha K Barton | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Human remyelination has been hard to model, slowing therapeutic discovery for demyelinating disease. This engineered iPSC spheroid combines mature myelinating oligodendrocytes with functionally reactive microglia and reproduces the full injury–repair cycle: myelin fragmentation, microglial phagocytosis of debris, new oligodendrocyte generation and axonal re-ensheathment. Newly formed myelin is thinner than the original by ultrastructural quantification — a faithful feature of genuine remyelination — making this a tractable human platform for mechanistic study and drug screening in multiple sclerosis.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived 3D spheroids with oligodendrocytes and microglia; demyelinating insult and electron-microscopy g-ratio quantification. In vitro model.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02457-z

### Dual effect of α-synuclein disease variants on condensate formation
**Aswathy Chandran & Janin Lautenschläger | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** This challenges the assumption that all PD-linked α-synuclein mutations act through the same condensate/aggregation pathway. Only E46K and E83Q enhanced biomolecular condensate formation, whereas A30G, G51D and A53E failed to form condensates in cells, and synaptic enrichment of each variant tracked its propensity for VAMP2-mediated phase separation. α-Synuclein dysfunction can therefore diverge — both increased and decreased condensate formation may be pathogenic — which reframes how variant-specific mechanisms and candidate condensate-targeting therapies should be interpreted.

**Model/system & primary method:** In vitro and cell-based condensate assays plus iPSC-derived human neurons; disease-variant comparison.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77002-4

### Clinical factors associated with discordant plasma p-tau217 and established Alzheimer disease biomarkers
**Dror Shir & Gregory S Day | Neurology**

**Why it matters:** As plasma p-tau217 is adopted for AD diagnosis and anti-amyloid treatment decisions, knowing when it disagrees with reference biomarkers is clinically pressing. In 706 participants, 8.5% were discordant with amyloid-PET/CSF, with false negatives (5.6%) more common than false positives (2.8%). Discordance tracked factors plausibly affecting amyloid or marker clearance and distribution — older age, renal function (eGFR), BMI, obstructive sleep apnea, thyroid disease — and clinical stage (false negatives at early CDR ≤ 0.5, false positives at CDR ≥ 1), flagging which patients warrant confirmatory testing.

**Model/system & primary method:** Retrospective human cohort (Mayo Clinic memory/aging studies); plasma p-tau217 versus amyloid-PET and CSF p-tau181/Aβ42, multivariable logistic regression. Observational.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218591

### Circuit mechanisms of psychotic symptoms across disorders
**Urs Braun & Andreas Meyer-Lindenberg | Neuron**

**Key findings:** A transdiagnostic, multiscale framework for psychotic symptoms across psychiatric and neurological disorders. Imbalances in dopamine, glutamate, GABA, serotonin and acetylcholine alter neuronal gain and synaptic efficacy, degrading the precision-weighting of sensory evidence and prediction errors; these local disturbances propagate through cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical loops into abnormal integration–segregation dynamics and unstable attractor states. The model frames hallucinations and delusions as convergent computational vulnerabilities reachable by distinct biological routes.

**Model/system & primary method:** Review / theoretical synthesis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.011

### Precise length and charge segmentation of billion-dalton supramolecular polymers
**Michael D Dore & Samuel I Stupp | Science**

**Key findings:** A living supramolecular polymerization yields fibrillar peptide assemblies with precisely segmented charge — a central positive segment flanked by negative ones — reaching collective masses on the order of billions of daltons, enabled by an untwisted β-sheet core and self-capping dormant ends. Anchored to neuronal membranes through their positive domains, with the connected negative segments kept highly dynamic by charge repulsion, the assemblies promoted extensive neurite outgrowth and synaptic activity, pointing toward designer biomaterials for interfacing with neurons.

**Model/system & primary method:** Synthetic peptide self-assembly (in vitro supramolecular chemistry) with cultured neurons as the biological readout.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aeg5583

## NEURODEGENERATION & AGING — TOP 5

### Association of skin denervation with brain atrophy and cognitive impairment in patients with Parkinson disease
**Minglei Liu & Jing Yang | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In 155 PD patients and 50 controls, intraepidermal nerve fiber density (IENFD) declined stepwise across the cognitive spectrum (PD-NC 5.89 → PD-MCI 4.65 → PDD 3.40 fibers/mm) and correlated with global and domain-specific cognition. Lower IENFD independently predicted cognitive impairment (OR 0.54) and discriminated PD-MCI/PDD from PD-NC (AUC 0.77); frontal and insular gray-matter volumes statistically mediated the IENFD–cognition association (~8–10% mediated). The data support a peripheral–central axis in which small-fiber pathology mirrors central neurodegeneration, though the design is cross-sectional and the mediation modest.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cross-sectional study; skin-biopsy IENFD, neuropsychological testing, structural MRI, mediation analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218583

### Programmable DNA protonuclei reveal environmental context on protein phase separation
**Johann Fritzen & Andreas Walther | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Programmable DNA-based "protonuclei" act as tunable nucleus-mimicking compartments for studying phase separation of the ALS/FTD-linked protein FUS. FUS partitioning and condensate dynamics depended strongly on nucleic-acid sequence, spatial confinement and the viscoelasticity of the compartment core, and classical dilute-solution affinity assays failed to predict behavior in the crowded, multivalent environment; tuning DNA crosslinking suppressed the liquid-to-solid transitions that are a disease hallmark. The platform sits between test tube and cell for dissecting pathological FUS condensation under controlled conditions.

**Model/system & primary method:** In vitro engineered DNA compartments with recombinant FUS; biophysical imaging. Reductionist system.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78144-1

### Timing of deep brain stimulation and cognitive trajectories in GBA1-associated Parkinson's disease
**Evridiki Asimakidou & PARKNET Study Group | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** A multicenter cohort (91 GBA-PD, 252 non-GBA-PD) undergoing bilateral subthalamic-nucleus DBS. GBA-PD patients treated later showed significantly faster cognitive decline (Mattis Dementia Rating Scale) independent of age, with disease-onset-to-DBS cutoffs of 7–8 years marking the divergence, and MDRS declined with longer onset-to-DBS interval in GBA-PD but not non-GBA-PD. Cognitive trajectories were worse in GBA-PD regardless of variant class, suggesting earlier STN-DBS and earlier genetic testing may preserve the motor-benefit window before cognitive decline — though the observational design means timing effects could partly reflect patient selection.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multicenter cohort (10 sites); STN-DBS with linear mixed-model analysis of MDRS.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70520

### Pharmacological targeting of bunyavirus-induced synaptic impairment prevents neuronal hyperexcitability
**Barbara Gorda & Raphael Gaudin | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** The neglected orthobunyavirus Ťahyňa virus disrupted neuronal integrity and proteostasis in human cerebral organoids, lowering synaptic protein expression and density; in human post-mortem brain-explant cultures it perturbed local field potentials, and in mice it raised metabotropic glutamate receptor 1 without overt encephalitis or neuroinflammation. Proteome-based network medicine nominated gabapentin, which restored neurotransmitter-receptor levels in mice and prevented virus-induced hyperexcitability in explants — implicating synaptic remodeling, not just inflammation, in bunyavirus neuropathology and suggesting a neuromodulatory rescue.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cerebral organoids, post-mortem brain-explant cultures and mice; proteomics-based network analysis plus pharmacology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77284-8

### T-B interactions yield CD3+ B cells and CD20+ T cells with pathogenic features in multiple sclerosis
**Selia Baier & Edgar Meinl | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** Single-cell proteogenomics shows B and T cells exchange lineage-defining surface markers by trogocytosis, producing CD3+ B cells and CD20+ T cells ("masquerading" lymphocytes); ~40% of plasmablasts carried CD3 and ~80% of tonsillar follicular-helper T cells carried CD20. In MS, CD20+ T cells were enriched in CSF, carried cytotoxic signatures, accumulated in perivascular inflammatory cuffs and showed increased myelin reactivity; anti-CD20 therapy (ofatumumab) both depleted them and prevented their reinduction — revising the assumption that these markers are lineage-exclusive and implicating masquerading lymphocytes in MS immunopathology.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human blood, tonsil and CSF; single-cell proteogenomics, FACS, ImageStream and in vitro coculture.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2605806123

### T1R2/T1R3 identified as the Aβ receptor: a new mechanism linking Aβ42/40 ratio to neuronal hyperexcitability in Alzheimer's disease
**Saito, M. L. & Saito, M. R. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The authors propose the sweet-taste receptor heterodimer T1R2/T1R3 as a sensor of the soluble Aβ42/40 ratio. ### Extracellular cGAMP transmission exacerbates neuroinflammation in aging and multiple sclerosis
**R. Carvalho, D. & Li, L. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In mice, both advanced age and experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE) raised cytosolic mtDNA in microglia, driving cGAMP production and export, while the brain's dominant cGAMP hydrolase ENPP1 was downregulated in EAE mice and in MS patient tissue — releasing the brake on extracellular cGAMP. Hydrolysis-dead Enpp1 mice showed worse EAE paralysis and premature motor/behavioral aging in a STING-dependent manner, and single-cell RNA-seq identified microglia, astrocytes and inhibitory neurons as the principal responders importing cGAMP through distinct transporters (LRRC8A:C; system xc−). The work nominates CNS ENPP1 enhancement or cGAS inhibition as therapeutic strategies; causal claims are animal-based.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mice (aging, EAE, Enpp1H362A), MS patient tissue and primary glia/neurons; scRNA-seq, behavior and transporter dissection.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.30.754737

### Nuclear acetyl-CoA accumulation drives reversible RBP hyperacetylation and transcriptome-wide splicing shifts in age-equivalent Alzheimer's disease neurons
**Traxler, L. & Mertens, J. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In directly converted induced neurons from sporadic AD patients and age-matched controls (which retain donor-specific aging signatures), AD neurons diverted citrate through ATP-citrate lyase (ACLY) to raise nuclear acetyl-CoA, causing widespread hyperacetylation of RNA-binding proteins and splice regulators and transcriptome-wide splicing shifts that mirror post-mortem AD cortex. Inhibiting ACLY selectively lowered nuclear acetyl-CoA, normalized RBP acetylation and re-established a control-like splicing landscape, nominating a reversible nuclear acetyl-CoA–RNA-processing axis in aged human AD neurons.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human fibroblast-derived induced neurons (age-preserving); metabolomics, acetyl-proteomics, single-cell long-read RNA-seq and ACLY inhibition.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.26.754222

### Wild-type bone marrow-derived microglia-like cells mitigate strain-dependent neuropathology in Csf1r mutant mice
**Taylor, I. & Hume, D. A. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Mice lacking microglia (Csf1r FIRE-enhancer knockout) show strain-dependent pathology — perinatal lethality and hydrocephalus on a C57BL/6J background, rescued by crossing to CBA or BALB/c — and, with age, astrocytosis and severe thalamic calcification, without effects on myelination or behavior. Intraperitoneal wild-type bone-marrow transfer repopulated the brain with microglia-like cells expressing damage-associated markers and prevented the thalamic calcification, leading the authors to argue the damage-associated microglial phenotype is a protective response to injury rather than a cause of neuropathology. Note: v2 of a preprint first posted January 2025.

**Model/system & primary method:** Csf1rΔFIRE mice across genetic backgrounds; multi-region RNA-seq, bone-marrow transfer and histology.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.01.16.633478
