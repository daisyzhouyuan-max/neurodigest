### Cold activates a BH2 metabolic network promoting longevity and proteostasis
**Hyun Ju Lee & David Vilchez | Nature**

**Why it matters:** A cold-induced, muscle-derived metabolite relay is shown to enforce proteostasis in distant tissues, including neurons. Cold upregulates GCH1 (CAT-4), raising dihydrobiopterin (BH2), which is exported from muscle and converted to the cofactor BH4 in intestine and neurons, driving tyrosine synthesis via PAH-1 and extending lifespan. Crucially, boosting BH2 synthesis in muscle alone suppresses protein aggregation in distal tissues even at normal temperature, and in human cells BH2 or tyrosine supplementation reduces aggregation of polyQ-expanded huntingtin and ALS-linked TDP-43 and FUS variants — a tractable, cell-non-autonomous handle on aggregation-driven neurodegeneration.

**Model/system & primary method:** *C. elegans* genetics with tissue-specific manipulation and intertissue metabolite tracing; validation in human cell models of polyQ/TDP-43/FUS aggregation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11152-9

### Cas9 nickase-mediated contraction of CAG/CTG repeats is associated with phenotypic improvements in a Huntington's disease mouse model
**Alvaro Murillo & Vincent Dion | Sci Transl Med**

**Why it matters:** Rather than silencing mutant HTT, this work shrinks the pathogenic repeat itself. A CRISPR-Cas9 nickase targeted to CAG/CTG repeats drove allele-selective contractions in HD patient-derived neurons and astrocytes and in myotonic dystrophy type 1 neurons, with no detectable off-target mutations. AAV delivery to the R6/1 striatum contracted repeats in more than half of striatal neurons and reduced inclusion bodies, normalized gene expression, and improved open-field locomotion. Benefits exceeded what the measured contractions predicted, hinting at non-cell-autonomous contributions — a proof of concept for repeat contraction as a disease-modifying strategy across >15 repeat-expansion disorders.

**Model/system & primary method:** Patient-derived neurons/astrocytes and the R6/1 HD mouse; AAV-delivered Cas9 nickase with repeat-length and off-target assessment plus behavioral readouts.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aef9466

### Persistent neuronal stress signalling underlies Angelman syndrome phenotypes
**Kwang-Soo Kim & Jason J Yi | Nature**

**Why it matters:** The work links loss of the UBE3A ubiquitin ligase to a specific overabundant substrate with disease consequences. Unbiased proteomics identified asparagine synthetase (ASNS) as a UBE3A target; ASNS protein is elevated in *Ube3a*-null mouse brain and human neurons. Genetically correcting ASNS overabundance rescued microcephaly and many behavioural deficits, and mechanistically ASNS excess activates stress signalling and globally suppresses neuronal protein synthesis — framing Angelman syndrome as neurons trapped in persistent stress with stunted developmental growth, and nominating ASNS and the stress response as therapeutic targets.

**Model/system & primary method:** *Ube3a*-null mice and human neurons; UBE3A gain/loss-of-function with unbiased proteomics and genetic rescue of ASNS.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11165-4

### BCG vaccination mitigates tau pathology and restores cognitive function in PS19 mice
**Somnath Shee & William Bishai | Sci Adv**

**Why it matters:** Epidemiology has linked intravesical BCG to lower Alzheimer's incidence; this study tests the mechanism in a tauopathy model. BCG reduced hippocampal phospho-tau and microgliosis while preserving neuronal markers, attenuated brain atrophy on volumetric MRI, and improved novel-object recognition, body weight and survival in PS19 mice. Hippocampal transcriptomes nearly normalized toward wild type, and BCG shifted brain myeloid cells toward a trained-immunity state (CD80+) with enhanced tau-fibril phagocytosis. The causal evidence is mouse-only, but it offers a concrete immunomodulatory route to tau clearance consistent with the clinical signal.

**Model/system & primary method:** PS19 tau transgenic mice; systemic BCG with volumetric/CEST MRI, hippocampal transcriptomics, flow cytometry of brain myeloid cells and tau-fibril phagocytosis assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aej4603

### CD51 delineates functionally distinct astrocyte subtypes in neural circuits
**Junsung Woo & Benjamin Deneen | Nature**

**Why it matters:** Moving beyond molecular catalogues of astrocyte heterogeneity, a CD51-FLP mouse line allowed direct functional comparison of CD51+ and CD51− astrocytes in vivo. The subpopulations differ morphologically, physiologically and developmentally in cortex and hippocampus: CD51+ astrocytes selectively promote cortical synapse formation (via glypican 6) and hippocampal circuit plasticity (via CD51 itself), and after stroke they drive synaptic remodelling and functional recovery. This establishes that astrocyte molecular diversity maps onto specialized, circuit-level functions with direct relevance to injury repair.

**Model/system & primary method:** CD51-FLP intersectional mouse genetics with morphological/physiological profiling and a stroke recovery paradigm.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11186-z

### Functional architecture of the human insula
**Julian Quabs & Josef Parvizi | Nature**

**Key findings:** Combining intracranial stimulation, task EEG and connectivity in 87 implanted participants, the study resolves the insula into four regions: a dorsal-posterior somatotopic nociceptive/thermoceptive zone, a ventral-posterior multi-body somatic zone, a mid-anterior visceral/anxiety zone, and an anterior-polar region largely silent to stimulation but active during salience detection. The anterior-polar region, strongly coupled to prefrontal/anterior cingulate cortex, exerts directed influence over the other insular subregions while receiving weaker indirect input — an electrophysiological pathway for top-down cognitive modulation of pain and bodily perception.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human intracranial electrical stimulation and task-based EEG with cytoarchitectonic connectivity mapping.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11175-2

### SIK3 controls synaptic localization of NMDA receptors by phosphorylating GluN1
**Rui Lu & Thomas C Südhof | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** The kinase SIK3 localizes to synapses and phosphorylates the obligatory NMDAR subunit GluN1 at a single site to control receptor recruitment. SIK3 deletion selectively reduced synaptic GluN1 by ~60% without affecting AMPARs, pharmacological SIK3 inhibition impaired NMDAR responses within 45 minutes, and mutating the phospho-site blocked synaptic recruitment. The sleep-promoting gain-of-function 'Sleepy' SIK3 mutation increased synaptic NMDARs via greater GluN1 phosphorylation, tying a single phosphorylation event to NMDAR abundance and, intriguingly, to sleep.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse neurons with genetic deletion, phospho-site mutants and pharmacological SIK3 inhibition; electrophysiology and biochemistry.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02469-9

### A corticohypothalamic circuit that mediates maternal awakening to pup cries
**Peiwen Tang & Feixue Liang | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** In mice, layer 6 neurons of the temporal association/ectorhinal cortex (TeA/Ect) projecting to the preoptic area become more responsive to pup calls after the transition to motherhood, an enhancement mediated by increased dopamine receptor D1 expression in these projection neurons. An auditory-thalamus → L6 TeA/Ect pathway relays pup cues during sleep. The circuit grants infant cries privileged access to arousal systems, defining a dedicated substrate for sleep-interrupting maternal vigilance.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse circuit dissection with projection-specific recording/manipulation across the maternal transition.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02458-y

### A GABAergic preoptic-raphe circuit mediates paradoxical coupling of anxiety and social preference after comparative social deficit
**Yan-Yi Zhang & Hong Lian | Neuron**

**Key findings:** A comparative social deficit model in adult male mice produced a loneliness-like phenotype: elevated anxiety together with heightened social preference. Medial preoptic GABAergic (mPOAGad2) neurons became hyperexcitable via downregulation of the Kv4.3 channel (Kcnd3) and bidirectionally controlled the behaviour; their strengthened monosynaptic inhibition of dorsal raphe serotonergic neurons mediated the paradoxical coupling — a circuit-and-channel account of how social disconnection reshapes affective state.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse behavioural model with transcriptomics, electrophysiology and circuit manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.032

### The kinase inhibitor dabrafenib halts progression of cerebral cavernous malformations in a BRAF-independent manner
**Maximiliano Arce & Peetra U Magnusson | Sci Transl Med**

**Key findings:** A high-throughput screen in CCM3 (PDCD10)-deficient endothelial cells identified the approved BRAF inhibitor dabrafenib as restoring cell-cell junctions and barrier function. In acute and chronic CCM mouse models dabrafenib reduced cerebral lesion burden and permeability and rescued retinal vascular defects. Mechanistically it acted through ERK5–KLF2/4 signalling and suppressed F-actin stress fibers independent of BRAF, offering a pharmacological alternative to surgery for a condition in which patients often carry multiple lesions.

**Model/system & primary method:** CCM3-deficient endothelial cells and acute/chronic CCM mouse models; compound screen with mechanistic signalling analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.adr0448

### Single-nucleotide polymorphism-directed design of allele-specific antisense oligonucleotides for spinocerebellar ataxia type 3
**Jacob Helm & Stefan Hauser | Sci Transl Med**

**Key findings:** Because wild-type ATXN3 has neuroprotective deubiquitinase functions, allele specificity matters in SCA3. Multiplexed nanopore sequencing of 79 patients from Germany, UK and Portugal identified ~200 polymorphisms in three linkage-disequilibrium haplotype blocks tightly associated with the CAG expansion; modelling indicated two SNPs could cover most European patients. Of 17 candidate ASOs, seven achieved >65% allele-preferential knockdown, and a single application reached up to 90% mutant ATXN3 reduction in patient iPSC-derived cortical neurons — a template for SNP-directed, allele-selective ASO therapy.

**Model/system & primary method:** Patient cohorts with allele-specific nanopore sequencing; ASO screening in SCA3 iPSC-derived cortical neurons.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.adz2140

### Serum β-synuclein predicts dementia conversion and mortality beyond amyloid-β and phospho-tau
**Timo Jan Oberstein & Markus Otto | Brain**

**Key findings:** In 259 non-demented memory-clinic patients, serum β-synuclein — a blood-accessible marker of synaptic damage — was selectively elevated in the A+T1+ Alzheimer's group (Cohen's d = 0.69 vs controls) but not in the A−T1+ "PASSED" group, and tracked CSF pTau181 more closely than the Aβ42/40 ratio. Each standard-deviation increase raised the hazard of dementia conversion (HR 1.40) over a median 4.1 years, with effects partly independent of established CSF amyloid and tau biomarkers. As a single-centre cohort it needs external replication, but it points to a scalable synaptic prognostic marker.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human single-centre memory-clinic cohort with CSF biomarker stratification and longitudinal dementia/mortality follow-up.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag361

### Translational control of microglial metabolism, inflammatory responses, and survival by the eIF4E-binding proteins
**Sara Bermudez & Nahum Sonenberg | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** This study places the mTORC1 effectors 4E-BP1/2 at the center of microglial function in Alzheimer's disease. CSF 4E-BP1 is elevated in AD and strengthens the association between soluble TREM2 and neurodegeneration in amyloid-positive individuals. In microglia, amyloid-β inactivates 4E-BP1 in a SYK-dependent manner, and deleting 4E-BP1/2 increased mitochondrial mass and oxidative phosphorylation, reduced pro-inflammatory signalling, and protected microglia from amyloid-induced apoptosis — arguing that dysregulated cap-dependent translation drives microglial metabolic exhaustion and death.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human CSF biomarker analysis with murine microglia in vitro and 4E-BP1/2 genetic deletion.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2610766123

### Accelerated epigenetic aging in frontotemporal dementia highlights smoking, inflammation, and metabolic signals
**Daphne Nijland & Lianne M Reus | Ann Neurol**

**Key findings:** Across two independent cohorts (Amsterdam Dementia Cohort and the GENFI study; 415 patients, 223 controls), FTD patients showed accelerated GrimAge2 epigenetic age (β = 1.17 years, p = 0.001) versus controls. Component analyses attributed the acceleration to increased smoking-related DNA methylation, systemic inflammation (TIMP-1, CRP) and altered glucose metabolism, implicating modifiable lifestyle and metabolic contributors to biological aging in a neurodegenerative disease often considered primarily genetic.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human peripheral-blood DNA methylation (GrimAge2 clock) with meta-analysis across two cohorts.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78395

### RIPK3 acts as a molecular switch between neuroprotection and neuropathology during herpes simplex encephalitis
**Shuqi Wang & Hongyan Guo | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Using single-cell transcriptomics and cell-type-specific knockouts in HSV-1 encephalitis, the authors show RIPK3 plays opposing roles by cell type: its scaffolding function supports interferon responses and viral control across CNS cells, and RIPK3-dependent apoptosis limits myeloid accumulation, whereas RIPK3 kinase activity in astrocytes triggers necroptosis, neuronal loss and lethal neuroinflammation. Genetic or pharmacological inhibition of RIPK3 kinase suppressed astrocyte necroptosis, preserved brain integrity and improved survival — defining a selectively druggable node balancing antiviral defense against immunopathology.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse HSV-1 encephalitis with single-cell transcriptomics, genetic knockouts and pharmacological RIPK3-kinase inhibition.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77881-7

### Oropouche virus disrupts neurodevelopment and exhibits congenital infection potential
**Carl Bannerman & Kellie A Jurado | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Testing an emerging arbovirus as a congenital pathogen, the study shows Oropouche virus infects neurons across differentiation states in human forebrain organoids and, relative to Zika, exhibits broader neuronal tropism, greater insensitivity to neuronal type I interferon responses, and more severe neonatal neuropathology (cerebral hemorrhage) in mice. In an interferon-blockade model OROV productively infected the placenta and caused fetal growth restriction, supporting a framework in which placental/fetal barriers normally limit fetal brain infection but OROV is markedly neuropathogenic once it reaches developing neural tissue.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human forebrain organoids and neonatal/pregnant mouse models with historical and circulating OROV strains.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77859-5

**F. L. Pereira & L. T. Grinberg | medRxiv**

### Aβ42-driven α-synuclein fibril polymorphism and distinct intracellular aggregation
**P. Saha & M. Lee | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Addressing the frequent co-occurrence of amyloid-β and α-synuclein pathology, this biophysical study (v2 revision of a preprint first posted September 2026) shows that the aggregation state of Aβ42 shapes α-synuclein fibril structure: Aβ42 monomers delayed α-syn fibrillization while preformed fibrils trended toward acceleration, and both generated distinct fibril conformations by ThT kinetics, proteinase-K digestion and solid-state NMR. α-Syn fibrils templated in the presence of Aβ42 fibrils seeded more intraneuronal α-syn aggregation in dopaminergic cells than standard fibrils, offering a structural mechanism for cross-seeding in mixed proteinopathies. Findings are in vitro and in cell models.

**Model/system & primary method:** In vitro fibril assembly (ThT, TEM, ssNMR) with seeding assays in dopaminergic neuronal cells.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.09.750398

### VO659, a CAG repeat-targeting antisense oligonucleotide, lowers multiple neurotoxic mutant huntingtin proteins in Huntington's disease
**B. Röttgering & N. Datson | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** VO659 is a CAG-repeat-targeting ASO that lowers mutant HTT by blocking translation. The authors report allele-preferential lowering of multiple neurotoxic species — full-length mHTT, the HTT1a fragment, and repeat-associated non-AUG (RAN) translation products — in a CAG-length-dependent manner that spares wild-type HTT and becomes more effective as somatic expansion lengthens the repeat. A long brain tissue half-life in mice supports infrequent dosing, and a first-in-human phase 1/2a trial (NCT05822908) is ongoing. The distinctive feature is simultaneous targeting of several core neurotoxic proteins via the repeat itself rather than a single transcript.

**Model/system & primary method:** HD patient fibroblasts, exon-1 overexpression constructs and mouse brain pharmacokinetics; translation-blocking ASO.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.16.758452

### Neuronal expression of E2F4DN restores adult neurogenesis in homozygous 5xFAD mice via TrkB signaling
**A. Lozano-Ureña & J. M. Frade | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In this revised preprint (v2 of a version first posted December 2024), AAV-driven neuronal expression of a phosphomutant E2F4 (Thr249Ala/Thr251Ala, E2F4DN) restored the proliferative and differentiation capacity of neural stem cells in 5xFAD mice both in vivo and in vitro. Conditioned medium from E2F4DN-expressing cortical neurons rescued NSC neurogenic potential, and this effect required TrkB signalling — pharmacological TrkB inhibition abolished it while direct TrkB activation reproduced it. The data position impaired neuron-to-NSC TrkB signalling as a mechanism of early neurogenic failure in AD. Evidence is from a single amyloid mouse model.

**Model/system & primary method:** 5xFAD mice with AAV.PHP.eB neuronal E2F4DN; SVZ neural stem cell assays with TrkB pharmacology.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.12.17.628978

### Glymphatic dysfunction across sleep disorders and its cognitive implications
**R. M. Whitehouse & J. Boltze | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** This systematic review and meta-analysis of clinical and preclinical studies asks whether glymphatic transport is impaired across obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), sleep deprivation and narcolepsy type 1. OSA showed significant glymphatic impairment, with a moderate negative correlation between apnea-hypopnea index and the DTI-ALPS index (r = −0.53), consistent with a route from OSA to cognitive decline and neurodegenerative risk. Evidence did not reach significance for sleep deprivation or narcolepsy, and substantial between-study heterogeneity limits firm conclusions and argues for standardized glymphatic metrics.

**Model/system & primary method:** Meta-analysis of human and preclinical glymphatic imaging (DTI-ALPS and related metrics) across sleep-disorder cohorts.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.20.26366043
