1Intracellular complement factor H protects neurons during CNS inflammation

Nature  Christina Mayer & Manuel A Friese
Why it matters
Using single-nucleus RNA-seq of retinal ganglion cells from people with multiple sclerosis, the authors found that complement factor H (CFH) expression tracks with neuronal resilience to inflammatory degeneration — but the protective activity is intracellular and independent of CFH's canonical secreted, complement-regulatory role. CFH localizes to the ER, limits ROS accumulation and lipid peroxidation, and its effect requires the C-terminal SCR20 domain but not complement component C3. This is a non-canonical, cell-intrinsic neuroprotective mechanism validated in both mouse models and human tissue, with direct relevance to inflammatory neurodegeneration beyond MS.
Model & method
Human MS retinal ganglion cell snRNA-seq plus mouse CNS inflammation models; domain-deletion and complement-independence experiments.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10981-y

2Somatic mutations reveal the ontogeny of microglia in human ageing

Nature  Julia A Belk & Siddhartha Jaiswal
Why it matters
Using naturally accumulated somatic mutations as clonal lineage tags, the authors show that bone-marrow-derived myeloid cells infiltrate the aging human brain in all 20 individuals examined and can constitute a large fraction of the microglial pool — challenging the assumption that human microglia are exclusively embryonically seeded and self-renewing, as in mice. Cohort analysis further links most forms of clonal hematopoiesis to reduced Alzheimer's disease risk, tying microglial replenishment dynamics to disease susceptibility.
Model & method
Human postmortem brain (20 older individuals), somatic mutation-based and mitochondrial DNA single-cell lineage tracing; epidemiological clonal hematopoiesis cohort analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10939-0

3Metabolic reallocation in spinal cord oligodendrocytes drives chronic pain in mice through neuronal Aβ42 production

Sci Transl Med  Yannick Fotio & Daniele Piomelli
Why it matters
After peripheral injury, spinal oligodendrocytes down-regulate myelin synthesis and up-regulate lipid biosynthesis, and this metabolic reallocation compromises axonal integrity, elevates BACE1, and drives neuronal accumulation of insoluble amyloid-β42 during the window when pain becomes chronic. BACE1 inhibition or antibody-mediated Aβ42 clearance prevented persistent pain, and oligodendrocyte-specific deletion of NAAA (the metabolic node) blocked Aβ42 release altogether. An unexpected mechanistic bridge between a peripheral-injury model and amyloidogenic processing, nominating the NAAA→Aβ42 axis as a disease-modifying target — evidence is mouse-only.
Model & method
Mouse hindpaw formalin and sciatic nerve ligation models; snRNA-seq, lipidomics, pharmacological BACE1 inhibition and 4G8 antibody clearance, oligodendrocyte-specific NAAA knockout.
https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aea8738

4Proteomic signatures of APOE ε4 across human tissues and cell types in Alzheimer's disease

bioRxiv  Li, X. C. & Shvetcov, A.
Why it matters
Pooling plasma, CSF, brain multi-omic, and iPSC-derived astrocyte/neuron proteomic data across three large cohorts (ROSMAP, AMP-AD, ADNI; combined n>3,600), the authors identify a consistent APOE ε4-associated plasma proteomic signature enriched for immune processes, independent of AD diagnosis, that a classifier trained on plasma data could also detect in independent CSF proteomics. APOE ε4 carriage tracked with higher Braak and CERAD scores, but bulk brain transcriptomic/proteomic changes were limited and poorly concordant across molecular layers — the ε4 signal is much clearer in biofluids and cell-type-resolved iPSC models than in bulk brain tissue, arguing for cell-type-resolved multi-omic designs going forward.
Model & method
Human plasma, CSF, brain tissue (multi-cohort, n>3,600) and iPSC-derived astrocytes/neurons; multi-omic proteomic/transcriptomic profiling and machine-learning classification.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.27.746928

3D epigenome of glial cell types in developing human cortex

Nature  Ian R Jones & Yin Shen
Key findings
Comprehensive 3D epigenomic profiling (expression, chromatin accessibility, methylation, chromatin interactions) of four glial populations from mid-gestational human cortex identifies cell-type-specific candidate regulatory elements. Machine learning prioritized 112 schizophrenia risk variants within glial regulatory elements, and the authors confirmed in vivo that the rs4449074 risk allele disrupts an outer radial glia enhancer; outer radial glia elements were also enriched for human-accelerated genomic regions relative to chimpanzee, some with activity differences linked to neurodevelopmental genes.
Model & method
Human mid-gestational neocortex; multi-omic 3D epigenomics with transgenic mouse validation of regulatory elements.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10987-6

Connectome analysis of a cerebellum-like circuit for sensory prediction

Nature  Krista E Perks & Nathaniel B Sawtell
Key findings
Connectomic mapping of a cerebellum-like circuit in electric fish reveals inhibitory and disinhibitory input pathways and structured recurrent connectivity that together solve a distributed credit-assignment problem for canceling predictable sensory input. A computational model constrained by electrophysiology shows how multiple, spatially distributed plasticity sites cooperate to produce fast, accurate, noise-robust sensory cancellation — a structural blueprint for how distributed synaptic plasticity supports learning more generally.
Model & method
Electric fish cerebellum-like circuit; serial-section connectomics combined with cell-type-specific electrophysiology and computational modeling.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10690-6

Glutamate concentration tunes AMPA receptor function through conductance-state occupancy

Nat Neurosci  Gokulakrishna Banumurthy & Jacques I Wadiche
Key findings
At mouse interneuron synapses, lowering synaptic glutamate concentration reduces hallmark AMPAR properties (rectification, polyamine block, Ca2+ permeability) independent of subunit composition, by shifting receptor occupancy across conductance states with differential spermine affinity. This means glutamate concentration itself — not just subunit stoichiometry — is a key, previously unrecognized variable shaping how excitatory synapses encode information, complicating standard biophysical inference of AMPAR composition from these same properties.
Model & method
Mouse interneuron synapses; patch-clamp electrophysiology of native and isolated AMPARs with numerical simulation.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02414-w

Mitochondrial calcium influx-driven bioenergetics selectively enable drug addiction

Nat Neurosci  Jun Gao & Xin Pan
Key findings
Opioids and methamphetamine, but not natural rewards, trigger mitochondrial calcium influx via the mitochondrial calcium uniporter (MCU) specifically in dopaminergic terminals of the nucleus accumbens during high-intensity activation, fueling rapid ATP production that sustains drug-evoked dopamine release. Genetic or pharmacological MCU inhibition selectively blunted drug-induced dopamine release and addictive behaviors while sparing natural reward, nominating MCU as an addiction-selective therapeutic target that spares normal reward signaling.
Model & method
Mouse nucleus accumbens dopaminergic terminals; optogenetics, fiber photometry, genetic/pharmacological MCU manipulation.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02421-x

Elevated translocator protein in skull bone marrow is linked to human chronic pain

Sci Transl Med  Mehrbod Mohammadian & Marco L Loggia
Key findings
Integrated PET/MRI in 125 people with chronic low back pain or knee osteoarthritis versus 22 controls shows elevated TSPO (a myeloid-cell density marker) in skull bone marrow, more pronounced in osteoarthritis, correlating with pain and pain-comorbid symptoms in partially distinct spatial patterns; limited immunohistochemistry in a chronic-pain donor supported the imaging finding. This nominates skull marrow as a previously overlooked neuroimmune hub relevant to chronic pain — the immunohistochemical support is preliminary (single donor comparison).
Model & method
Human PET/MRI cohort (n=125 chronic pain, n=22 controls) with limited postmortem immunohistochemistry.
https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aed8729

Activation of transposable elements is linked to a region- and cell type-specific interferon response in Parkinson's disease

Sci Adv  Raquel Garza & Johan Jakobsson
Key findings
Bulk and single-nucleus RNA-seq of postmortem PD brain across four regions shows region- and cell-type-specific transposable element (LINE-1, ERV) activation in microglia and neurons of substantia nigra and putamen but not amygdala or prefrontal cortex, correlating with an innate immune/interferon signature in the same regions. The interferon–TE link was mechanistically confirmed in hPSC-derived microglia and neurons, implicating TE derepression as a contributor to region-selective neuroinflammation in PD.
Model & method
Human postmortem PD brain (4 regions), bulk/single-nucleus RNA-seq; hPSC-derived microglia and neurons for mechanistic validation.
https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aed2952

When the Brain Grows Old Too Soon: Aging and Senescence as a Driver of MS Progression

J Neurosci  Sienna S Drake & Alyson E Fournier
Key findings
This review synthesizes evidence for an accelerated aging and cellular senescence-like phenotype in MS brain cells, both in patients and preclinical models, and its interaction with age and hormonal status in driving worse prognosis and declining treatment response in older MS patients. The authors frame progressive MS partly as an age-accelerated neurodegenerative phenotype and outline anti-aging/senolytic strategies as an emerging therapeutic direction.
Model & method
Narrative review of human MS and preclinical animal model literature on cellular senescence and aging.
https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0308-26.2026

Neuroinflammation induces aberrant activation of Wnt signaling in astrocytes and impairment of cholesterol homeostasis and myelin integrity

Cell Rep  Jiannan Zhang & Kang Zheng
Key findings
Inflammation-driven Wnt activation in astrocytes raises intracellular cholesterol (via SREBF2) while suppressing APOE expression and cholesterol efflux, starving oligodendrocytes of the extracellular cholesterol needed to drive MYRF and myelin gene expression. In transgenic mice, defective oligodendrocyte differentiation and myelination caused by astrocytic Wnt activation was partially rescued by pharmacologically boosting ApoE expression, defining an inflammation→Wnt→cholesterol→myelin pathway with a candidate therapeutic entry point for demyelinating disease.
Model & method
Transgenic mouse models of neuroinflammation; astrocyte/oligodendrocyte co-culture and pharmacological ApoE rescue.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117871

T Cell-Mediated Targeting of Interneurons in Mice Shapes Hippocampal Remodeling and Epilepsy

Ann Neurol  Daniel S Galvis-Montes & Julika Pitsch
Key findings
A new mouse model expressing ovalbumin selectively in CA1 interneurons alongside OVA-specific CD8+ T cells reproduces key features of GAD65-antibody autoimmune encephalitis: CD8+ T cell infiltration and interneuron targeting cause acute seizures, followed by a chronic phase of spontaneous recurrent seizures, hippocampal sclerosis, sustained gliosis, reduced Reelin expression, and granule cell dispersion arising from displacement (not aberrant neurogenesis). This causally links cell-type-specific autoimmune targeting of inhibitory neurons to temporal lobe epileptogenesis.
Model & method
Transgenic mouse model of interneuron-specific CD8+ T cell-mediated autoimmunity; histology, seizure monitoring, proliferation assays.
https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78349

Repairing the Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis Trial Credibility Gap

Neurology  Michael Benatar & Thomas I Cochrane
Key findings
This perspective argues that a "clinical trial industrial complex" has incentivized recurring methodological fallacies in early/mid-phase ALS trials — misused biomarkers, mismatched design and objectives, selective reporting, over-reliance on post hoc and open-label extension data — that generate premature efficacy claims failing to replicate in phase 3, with direct harm to patients. The authors call for stricter adherence to reporting standards, clearer separation of hypothesis-generating from hypothesis-testing analyses, and more disciplined phase 2 triage.
Model & method
Perspective/commentary on ALS clinical trial design and reporting practices; no primary data.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218471

A Two-Stage Plasma Biomarker Algorithm Reduces Amyloid PET Use and the p-Tau217 Gray Zone

medRxiv  Parankusham, H. & Frank, B.
Key findings
GRAD, a two-stage classifier that layers plasma biomarkers, genetics, and demographics onto an indeterminate p-Tau217 "gray zone" (developed in 145 ADNI participants, externally validated in 1,644 A4/LEARN participants), improved gray-zone AUC from 0.719 (p-Tau217 alone) to 0.793, raised correct amyloid-PET-free classification from 41% to 56%, and cut projected PET referrals by roughly a third — with modeled testing costs 44–52% lower than p-Tau217-plus-confirmatory-PET. Cautious interpretation: performance is model-based and cost estimates are modeled, not from a prospective clinical-workflow trial.
Model & method
Human plasma biomarkers in ADNI (development, n=145) and A4/LEARN (external validation, n=1,644) cohorts; two-stage logistic regression classifier against amyloid-PET reference standard.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.02.03.26345302

Single nucleus RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics reveal unique functionalities of gray matter versus white matter oligodendrocytes in aging and Alzheimer's Disease

bioRxiv  Voth, J. P. & Keene, C. D.
Key findings
Profiling prefrontal white matter from 48 human brain donors (young adulthood through late life, low vs. high AD neuropathologic change) with snRNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics, the authors find white-matter oligodendrocytes mount stronger immune programs with aging and pathology (cytokine signaling, antigen presentation) while gray-matter oligodendrocytes' synapse/neurotransmitter signature declines with aging and high pathology. High-pathology white matter also showed amplified stress/proteostasis signatures (chaperones, ferritin) and an expanded population inferred to progress along a differentiation→lipid-remodeling→stress/ISR-reactive trajectory, consistent with a senescence-like oligodendrocyte state in diseased white matter.
Model & method
Human postmortem prefrontal white matter, 48 donors; single-nucleus RNA-seq, CosMx spatial transcriptomics, pseudotime trajectory inference.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.29.747923

Linoleic Acid-Lyso PG Axis promoting lipid droplet-mitochondria tethering by stabilizing Noncanonically Mitochondrial PPARβ/δ to Ameliorate Microglial Dysfunction in subarachnoid hemorrhage

bioRxiv  Zou, L.-X. & Li, W.
Key findings
CSF linoleic acid (LA) was elevated and strongly discriminated aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) patients (n=30) from controls (n=10; AUC 0.997). In hemoglobin-exposed mouse microglia, LA restored phagocytosis, mitochondrial membrane potential and ATP production via a lysophosphatidylglycerol(16:0)–PPARδ axis: LPG(16:0) stabilizes a mitochondrial-membrane pool of PPARδ that tethers lipid droplets to mitochondria and supports fatty acid oxidation. LA treatment reduced neuroinflammatory injury and partially rescued anxiety-like behavior and spatial memory in a mouse SAH model — causal evidence for the pathway is mouse-only; the human data are correlative.
Model & method
Human CSF metabolomics (SAH patients vs. controls) plus mouse blood-injection SAH model and primary microglia; lipidomics, mitochondrial/phagocytosis assays, structural modeling, behavioral testing.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.26.747434

Tau isoforms modulate the axon initial segment controlling axonal trafficking and neuronal excitability

bioRxiv  Arnaiz Yepez, C. & Falzone, T. L.
Key findings
In mouse primary neurons and hiPSC-derived neurons, tau isoform balance (independent of total tau levels) regulates axon initial segment (AIS) maturation: tau deficiency or 3R-tau predominance delays Ankyrin G accumulation and AIS stabilization, and shifting hiPSC-derived neurons toward 3R-tau reduces sodium currents, impairs action potential firing, and alters lysosomal transport within the AIS. Since aberrant exon-10 splicing (the 3R/4R ratio) is a defining feature of primary tauopathies, this links isoform imbalance mechanistically to altered neuronal excitability and trafficking, independent of tau aggregation.
Model & method
Mouse primary neurons and hiPSC-derived neurons; super-resolution DNA-PAINT imaging, electrophysiology, live axonal trafficking assays.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.01.745409

Markerless Motion Capture Reveals Movement Abnormalities in Isolated REM Sleep Behavior Disorder

medRxiv  Wegner, P. & Faber, J.
Key findings
Single-camera markerless motion capture of 93 walking videos from three clinical sites, analyzed with pose estimation and machine-learning classifiers (best: tsfresh+XGBoost, AUROC 0.739), distinguished people with isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (a prodromal PD stage) from healthy controls, outperforming the MDS-UPDRS III clinical score when trained across sites. SHAP analysis identified hip-related kinematic features as key discriminators, more closely tracking regional dopaminergic deficits than the clinical scale — a low-cost, scalable candidate digital biomarker for prodromal PD, based on a modest retrospective sample.
Model & method
Human iRBD patients vs. controls (n=93 videos, 3 sites); single-RGB-camera pose estimation and machine-learning classification benchmarked against MDS-UPDRS III.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.28.26361609
