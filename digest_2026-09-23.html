1Blood proteomics of menopause map to brain aging and dementia risk

Nat Med  Madeline Wood Alexander & Kaitlin B Casaletto
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Menopause is implicated in later neurodegenerative risk, but the mediating biology has been unclear. Using serum NULISAseq proteomics in 80 rigorously STRAW+10-staged pre-, peri- and postmenopausal women (43–58 years), spontaneous menopause was characterized by dysregulated inflammatory, synaptic, metabolic and Alzheimer's-associated proteins that tracked more tightly with reproductive hormones than with chronological age; an age-matched 2,814-woman plasma Olink cohort replicated the shifts and revealed broader inflammatory/catabolic upregulation. The result nominates hormone-driven inflammatory and synaptic remodeling — rather than age per se — as a route from the menopause transition to dementia vulnerability. The design is cross-sectional and female-only, so the ordering is inferred across women rather than followed longitudinally.
Model & method
Human serum proteomics (NULISAseq discovery, n=80; plasma Olink validation, n=2,814); STRAW+10 menopause staging mapped onto brain-aging and AD biomarker processes.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04648-4

2Compartmentalized B cell–microglia crosstalk drives ischemic white matter demyelination

Nat Commun  Lulu Xu & Chuan Qin
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
White matter injury underlies much vascular cognitive impairment, but the role of B cells has been unclear. Under chronic cerebral hypoperfusion in adult male mice, B cells expanded and were activated in the dura mater and adopted an IgG-secreting phenotype; microglia recruited these meningeal B cells via MIF–CD74/CXCR4 signaling, and the secreted IgG drove microglial ferroptosis through Fcγ receptors, worsening myelin thinning, neuroinflammation and cognition. B cell depletion alleviated demyelination and cognitive deficits, placing a meningeal-B-cell/microglia ferroptosis axis causally upstream of ischemic white matter damage. Evidence is mouse-only and male-only, but the loss-of-function and the defined signaling arms are clean.
Model & method
Mouse chronic cerebral hypoperfusion model; B cell depletion, MIF–CD74/CXCR4 and Fcγ-receptor/ferroptosis dissection with meningeal immunophenotyping.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76817-5

3Sensory neuronal mTORC1 signaling establishes a neuroimmune memory that initiates allergic immunity

Immunity  Xueping Zhu & Caroline L Sokol
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Allergic sensitization accrues from repeated subthreshold allergen exposures, but how neurons store that exposure history was unknown. In mouse skin, protease-allergen exposure induced sustained mTORC1 signaling and PGC-1α-associated mitochondrial remodeling in cutaneous sensory neurons, establishing a metabolically primed state with heightened responsiveness; upon re-exposure these neurons drove enhanced itch and CD301b+ dendritic-cell recruitment to skew Th2 immunity. The work defines a sensory-neuron-intrinsic form of "neuroimmune memory," giving a concrete neuronal substrate to allergic priming. The evidence is mechanistic and preclinical, in a defined cutaneous allergen model.
Model & method
Mouse cutaneous protease-allergen model; sensory-neuron mTORC1/PGC-1α manipulation with behavioral itch and dendritic-cell recruitment readouts.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2026.08.012

4Genetic resilience and resistance in Alzheimer's disease diagnosis and pathology

Ann Neurol  Ganna Leonenko & Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Some individuals avoid AD pathology as they age, or retain cognition despite substantial pathology, and educational attainment is protective for reasons that remain unclear. Partitioning educational-attainment polygenic scores into cognitive (intelligence-linked) and non-cognitive (behavioral/social) components across four cohorts — two with living-participant biomarkers (BioHermes-1, n=770; ADNI, n=1,361) and two postmortem — the authors found the components differentially associated with AD diagnosis and neuropathology, dissociating resistance to pathology from resilience of cognition. This gives a genetically grounded handle on why education tracks protection. The findings are associational and cohort-based, without direct mechanism.
Model & method
Human multi-cohort genetics (two biomarker cohorts, two postmortem); component-partitioned polygenic scores tested against AD diagnosis and neuropathology.
https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78367

An anterior zona incerta to ventral tegmental area circuit controls compulsive eating in a binge-eating model

Neuron  Zongming Chen & Wei L Shen
Key findings
In a mouse binge-eating-disorder model, GABAergic anterior zona incerta (aZI) neurons became hyperexcitable after intermittent high-fat-diet training, and their activity was required for the development of compulsive, aversion-resistant intake. These neurons project to the ventral tegmental area, where training weakened their inhibitory control over dopamine neurons through both direct and indirect pathways, enhancing dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens. The circuit was dissected causally with optogenetics and genetic manipulation alongside single-cell RNA sequencing, defining an aZI→VTA node that sustains compulsive binge eating.
Model & method
Mouse binge-eating-disorder model; fiber photometry, optogenetics, circuit tracing and single-cell RNA sequencing.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.014
Neurodegeneration & aging

Cross-ethnic replication of intersectin-1 (ITSN1) as a Parkinson's disease susceptibility gene

Mov Disord  Chunyu Li & Huifang Shang
Key findings
Rare loss-of-function ITSN1 variants, recently linked to Parkinson's disease in individuals of European ancestry, were tested in 2,063 unrelated Chinese PD patients against 3,298 controls plus East Asian reference datasets. Three previously unreported frameshift loss-of-function variants — all absent from controls and classified likely pathogenic — drove significant gene-based enrichment of rare damaging variants, replicating ITSN1 (an endocytic scaffolding protein) as a PD risk gene across ancestries. As a rare-variant burden analysis, the association rests on a small number of carriers.
Model & method
Human whole-exome sequencing case-control (Chinese cohort); SKAT-O rare-variant burden testing.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70554

MRI subtypes reveal distinct biological substrates and disability profiles in multiple sclerosis

Brain  Paolo Preziosa & Maria A Rocca
Key findings
Applying the unsupervised Subtype and Stage Inference (SuStaIn) framework to multimodal 3T brain MRI — lesion volume, white-matter mean diffusivity and atrophy — from 1,017 MS patients and 548 controls, the authors resolved data-driven imaging subtypes that mapped onto distinct disability, cognition, age-at-onset, genetic-severity and relapse-independent-progression profiles, capturing heterogeneity that conventional clinical phenotypes miss. The subtyping offers a biologically anchored stratification for prognosis and trial enrichment. The staging inference is cross-sectional and needs longitudinal confirmation of subtype trajectories.
Model & method
Human multimodal brain MRI (n=1,017 patients, 548 controls); SuStaIn machine-learning subtype-and-stage inference.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag320

Prosocial helping behaviors attenuate APOE ε4-driven accelerated cognitive decline

Alzheimers Dement  Sae Hwang Han & Jessica Danielle Faul
Key findings
In a multiethnic US sample (N=17,972) followed for over two decades, within-person adoption and sustained maintenance of formal volunteering attenuated the accelerated cognitive decline typically observed in APOE ε4 carriers, with protective associations evident at modest engagement. The multilevel models isolate within-person change and address key sources of endogeneity, positioning prosocial engagement as a modifiable buffer against a genetic risk that engages overlapping neuroinflammatory, vascular and stress-regulatory pathways. The design is observational, so residual confounding and reverse causation cannot be fully excluded.
Model & method
Human longitudinal cohort; multilevel within-person models of volunteering and informal helping moderating APOE ε4-related decline.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71877

Integrated multi-omics nominates the APP–CD74 axis as an immunoregulatory target in pediatric high-grade glioma

Acta Neuropathol  Zhuang Wang & Rachid Drissi
Key findings
Combining bulk RNA sequencing of 26 diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG) autopsy specimens with single-cell data from eight patients and integrated pediatric high-grade glioma datasets plus transwell and flow-cytometry assays, the authors showed that mesenchymal-like tumor cells chemotactically recruit monocyte-derived tumor-associated macrophages and identified an APP–CD74 signaling axis as a node in the immunosuppressive microenvironment. This nominates APP–CD74 as a candidate immunotherapeutic target in a tumor with almost no options. The mechanistic links are largely correlative and in vitro, without in vivo target validation.
Model & method
Human DIPG/pediatric high-grade glioma bulk and single-cell transcriptomics with transwell and flow-cytometry validation.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03089-0

Kidney function tracks cognitive and structural brain health across cohorts

Alzheimers Dement  Min Li & Hao Wang
Key findings
Across two population-based cohorts (META-KLS and UK Biobank; 6,847 adults) plus 426 CKD patients, higher cumulative estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) associated with better MoCA scores, whereas greater eGFR variability associated with worse cognition and with white-matter and cortical structural signatures on MRI. The data implicate both the level and the instability of renal function in brain aging, arguing eGFR variability is an under-recognized vascular contributor to cognitive risk. The associations are cross-sectional, so the causal direction between renal and brain aging remains open.
Model & method
Human multi-cohort epidemiology with brain MRI; eGFR level and variability versus MoCA and brain structure.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71840

The United States CADASIL Consortium: baseline findings from a natural-history study

medRxiv  J S Paulsen & The United States CADASIL Consortium
Key findings
This study establishes baseline clinical, functional and multimodal-biomarker characteristics of the first US cohort of CADASIL — a monogenic (NOTCH3) small-vessel disease used here as a single-cause model for sporadic vascular contributions to cognitive impairment and dementia (VCID), the second leading cause of dementia. The stated aim is to quantify the marked phenotypic and mechanistic heterogeneity that hampers clinical management and trial readiness. As a cross-sectional baseline it characterizes the cohort rather than testing progression or intervention; its value is in standardized, trial-ready phenotyping of a monogenic SVD (version 2 of the preprint).
Model & method
Human cross-sectional natural-history cohort (monogenic CADASIL); multimodal clinical, functional and biomarker phenotyping.

The value of a family history to distinguish monogenic from polygenic amyotrophic lateral sclerosis

medRxiv  P Beele & W van Rheenen
Key findings
Because both rare Mendelian variants and polygenic burden can cause familial clustering of ALS, the authors used current estimates of ALS genetic architecture to model how accurately a positive family history predicts the underlying genetic basis. The analysis indicates that familial recurrence is an unreliable proxy for monogenic inheritance — reduced-penetrance pathogenic variants can present as sporadic in small modern pedigrees, while polygenic risk can mimic Mendelian clustering — with consequences for genetic testing, counseling and gene-discovery strategy. This is a genetic-epidemiology modeling study rather than new sequencing.
Model & method
Human genetic-epidemiology modeling of ALS/FTD family history against monogenic versus polygenic architecture.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.16.26362994

Peripheral immunoinflammatory markers in LRRK2 G2385R and R1628P Parkinson's disease

medRxiv  H X Ding & L C Lit
Key findings
In 240 participants, plasma immunoinflammatory markers were profiled across PD carriers of the Asian-prevalent LRRK2 G2385R and R1628P variants, idiopathic PD and controls. Several markers were paradoxically lower in variant carriers than in idiopathic PD or controls (TNF, CCL2, CX3CL1 and CCL5 in G2385R; IL-6, TNF, CCL2 and CCL5 in R1628P), and higher monocyte LRRK2 kinase activity (pRab10-Thr73) correlated with lower TNF — suggesting variant-specific peripheral immune signatures rather than uniform inflammation. The study is cross-sectional and correlational in a moderate sample.
Model & method
Human plasma multiplex immunoassay with monocyte LRRK2 kinase (pRab10) readouts across PD genotypes.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.20.26363505

Cell-type-specific transcriptional risk scores and longitudinal multiple sclerosis outcomes

medRxiv  M Upcott & K L Kreft
Key findings
Integrating the largest MS GWAS (14,802 patients) with blood and brain eQTL data via summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR) and the HEIDI test, and with single-cell expression from ~1.27 million PBMCs, the authors constructed cell-type-specific transcriptional risk scores and tested them against longitudinal MS outcomes, arguing these expression-anchored scores may distinguish clinical phenotypes better than standard polygenic risk scores. The work is methodological and associational; superiority over PRS needs prospective validation.
Model & method
Human integrative genomics (GWAS × eQTL × single-cell RNA-seq) linked to longitudinal MS phenotypes.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.21.26363545

Paired CSF and plasma metabolomics define a CSF-centered signature in idiopathic normal-pressure hydrocephalus

medRxiv  D Visentin & Z Maglica
Key findings
In a small exploratory case-control study (10 iNPH patients, 10 controls) using paired CSF and plasma HILIC-based metabolomics, iNPH — a potentially treatable dementia — showed a robust CSF-centered metabolic phenotype that was only partly reflected in peripheral blood, indicating compartment-specific alterations. The findings are hypothesis-generating given the very small sample and exploratory design.
Model & method
Human paired CSF/plasma metabolomics (HILIC-LC-MS) case-control.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.21.26363535
