### An astrocyte-microglia MFG-E8 axis promotes synapse engulfment in early amyloid pathology
**Dimitra Sokolova & Soyon Hong | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** The work identifies an upstream signal that renders synapses vulnerable before widespread plaque deposition and overt gliosis. In mouse amyloidosis, an early region-specific astrocyte state — bulbous perisynaptic processes enriched in p62 and glycogen, with impaired autophagic flux and calcium signaling — upregulates MFG-E8, which drives local microglial synapse engulfment through microglial β3 integrin. Astrocyte-specific Mfge8 deletion reduces microglial engulfment and synapse loss and is associated with lower amyloid pathology, gliosis and cognitive impairment, nominating astrocytic MFG-E8 as a tractable node linking reactive astrocytes to synapse elimination. Causal evidence is mouse-only.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse amyloidosis models; astrocyte-specific Mfge8 gain- and loss-of-function, microglial β3 integrin manipulation, synapse engulfment assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.011

### A randomized, placebo-controlled Phase 1 trial of the Aβ vaccine AV-1959R in non-cognitively impaired participants
**Armine Hovakimyan & Michael G. Agadjanyan | Molecular Neurodegeneration**

**Why it matters:** This is a first-in-human Phase 1 of a next-generation MultiTEP-based active Aβ vaccine engineered to overcome the weak immunogenicity that limited earlier Aβ vaccines while avoiding Aβ-specific T-cell activation. In 16 healthy adults aged 40–60, AV-1959R was safe and well tolerated, with no serious adverse events or ARIA and only mild, transient injection-site reactions, and elicited anti-Aβ antibody responses directed at pathological Aβ species. The result supports further development toward secondary prevention in preclinical AD, but n is small and the trial establishes safety and immunogenicity rather than efficacy.

**Model/system & primary method:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 1; three intramuscular immunizations (100–300 µg) with Advax-CpG55.2 adjuvant; validated ELISA and conformational Aβ microarray.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00980-y

### CREST functions as a molecular bridge between neuron-specific enhancers and epigenetic regulators in mature motor neurons
**Kaitlin N. Montanera & Ho Sung Rhee | Nature Communications**

**Why it matters:** CREST, a neuron-specific BAF chromatin-remodeling subunit mutated in some ALS cases, is shown to couple lineage transcription factors to histone acetylation during motor neuron maturation. CREST interacts with ONECUT1, NGN2 and the BRM ATPase to target pre-existing accessible enhancers of neuronal genes; its deletion selectively prevents CBP recruitment and reduces H3K27 acetylation at these enhancers, downregulating mature neuronal genes without disrupting BRM binding, enhancer accessibility or long-range architecture. The study clarifies the genomic mechanism of an ALS-linked chromatin factor, though the system is developmental maturation rather than a direct degeneration model.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse embryonic stem cell–directed motor neuron differentiation; CREST deletion; chromatin accessibility and acetylation profiling with protein–interaction assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78396-x

### Reconstructing developmental and disease progression with sample-level embeddings
**Longda Jiang & Rahul Satija | Cell**

**Why it matters:** scSLIDE reframes single-cell analysis around samples rather than individual cells, converting each sample's cells into a compact density profile in high-dimensional space and embedding samples to capture within-group variation that binary case/control comparisons discard. Applied to Alzheimer's disease it reconstructs a progression trajectory and coordinated cellular programs across patients, and it separately resolves COVID-19 infection and severity axes and zebrafish embryonic pseudostages. Shipped in Seurat v6, it offers a route to patient-level stratification from existing single-cell atlases.

**Model/system & primary method:** Computational framework (per-sample density estimation and sample embeddings); human AD, COVID-19 and zebrafish single-cell datasets; implemented in Seurat v6.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.10.018

### Age-Dependent Nonlinear Association Between Gait Impairment and Incident Dementia
**Youjung Choi & Ki Woong Kim | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In a Korean prospective cohort (4,822 included; median 6.2-year follow-up; 251 incident dementia, 458 deaths), the relationship between Performance-Oriented Mobility Assessment gait performance and incident dementia was significantly nonlinear (p = 0.002), with hazard rising steeply as scores departed from the ceiling, and the association was modified by age. The findings argue for gait decline as a graded, age-dependent marker of dementia risk rather than a linear dose-response. Observational, with imprecise estimates at the lowest gait scores.

**Model/system & primary method:** Korean Longitudinal Study on Cognitive Aging and Dementia; cause-specific Cox regression with restricted cubic splines and death as a competing event.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218711

### Phenotypic and genetically predicted sleep traits in relation to dementia incidence in Swedish twins
**Manasa Shanta Näsholm & Ida K. Karlsson | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** In the Swedish Twin Registry, longer self-reported sleep duration was associated with modestly lower dementia risk (HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.86–1.00) over ~13 years, but the association attenuated in within-pair analyses, consistent with familial confounding. A one-SD higher polygenic score for daytime napping was associated with higher dementia risk (HR 1.12, 95% CI 1.01–1.23). Separating phenotypic from genetically predicted sleep traits is the contribution; effects are modest and observational, with cases within 10 years of baseline excluded to limit reverse causation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Screening Across the Lifespan Twin study; phenotypic sleep traits plus polygenic scores; Cox models with within-pair analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71977

### Plasma metabolites in early adulthood are associated with midlife cognitive performance and brain tissue integrity in the CARDIA study
**Zsuzsanna Ament & W. Taylor Kimberly | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** In CARDIA, 11 of 575 plasma metabolites measured in early adulthood (mean age 32) were independently associated with cognition assessed 23 years later; five were also associated with total brain volume, but none with white matter hyperintensity burden. Total brain volume mediated 8%–33% of the metabolite–cognition associations. The results point to early-adult metabolic signatures as markers of midlife brain structural health, with the caveats of a long exposure–outcome gap and mediation inference.

**Model/system & primary method:** CARDIA cohort (metabolites N = 1,736 at Year 7; MRI and cognition N = 646 at Year 30); 575 plasma metabolites with mediation analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71982

### Sex Differences in Blood Pressure Dynamics and Outcomes After Endovascular Thrombectomy
**Renjun Gu & the ENCHANTED2/MT Collaboration | Neurology**

**Key findings:** This exploratory secondary analysis of the ENCHANTED2/MT trial (816 patients with persistent elevated systolic BP after successful reperfusion) found intensive BP lowering associated with worse 90-day ordinal mRS in male participants (adjusted common OR 1.91, 95% CI 1.29–2.83) but near-null in female participants (acOR 0.94, 95% CI 0.56–1.57; ratio of acORs 0.49, 95% CI 0.26–0.94), indicating sex modifies the harm of intensive post-thrombectomy BP reduction. This is a hypothesis-generating subgroup analysis of an open-label trial.

**Model/system & primary method:** Secondary analysis of a multicenter open-label blinded-endpoint RCT (China); location-scale mixed-effects modeling of BP trajectories and variability; 90-day mRS.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218703

### Developing the World-Wide FINGERS biorepository for dementia risk reduction trials
**Anna Matton & the World-Wide FINGERS network | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** A network-wide survey of World-Wide FINGERS multidomain prevention studies found most (30/39) had or were developing biorepositories, with broadly aligned but still variable biospecimen collection, processing and biomarker practices; blood-based amyloid/tau/neurodegeneration markers and multi-omics platforms were increasingly used, including in lower-resourced settings. The work establishes a harmonized biorepository framework intended to enable comparable cross-population biomarker discovery in prevention trials. It is a descriptive infrastructure survey rather than a clinical result.

**Model/system & primary method:** Cross-sectional, network-wide survey of WW-FINGERS teams assessing biospecimen and biomarker readiness for harmonization.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71949

### Stathmin-2 regulates microtubule dynamics and axonal resilience in human motor neurons
**K. J. E. Matson & E. L. F. Holzbaur | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Loss of STMN2 — a near-universal downstream consequence of TDP-43 pathology in ALS — is linked here to axonal cytoskeletal resilience rather than steady-state growth. In human iPSC-derived motor neurons, STMN2 depletion increased baseline microtubule polymerization but impaired recovery of axonal microtubules after transient nocodazole-induced depolymerization and reduced axonal recoil after local optical microtubule severing, and it disrupted formation of neuromuscular-junction-like contacts in motor neuron–myocyte co-cultures. The data position STMN2 as required for efficient cytoskeletal remodeling under stress. Evidence is from an in vitro human model.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived motor neurons; TIRF single-microtubule imaging, optically induced severing, and motor neuron–myocyte co-culture.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.07.764673

### Ogg1 regulates context-dependent glial states in Alzheimer's disease
**A. Abentung & K. Scheffler | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The oxidative DNA-repair glycosylase Ogg1 is reported to have opposing effects by context: Ogg1 deficiency impaired memory and perturbed glial transcription in otherwise healthy mice, yet in APP/PS1 mice it improved memory, reduced amyloid burden and attenuated AD-associated glial remodeling. These effects occurred without changes in global 8-oxo-dG levels but with altered chromatin accessibility and gene expression, implying a non-canonical transcriptional role; single-nucleus transcriptomics found Ogg1-dependent glial programs strongly conserved in human AD. The disease-protective knockout effect is counterintuitive and the human link is correlational.

**Model/system & primary method:** APP/PS1 mice with and without Ogg1 deficiency; single-nucleus RNA-seq and chromatin accessibility, behavior, and comparison to human AD.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.06.764857

### Subcellular spatial profiling unveils links between neuritic plaques and selective tau vulnerability in Alzheimer's disease
**H. Li & S. Das | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Subcellular spatial transcriptomics co-registered with Aβ and pTau immunohistochemistry addresses why cortical layers II/III and V accumulate somatic tau while layer IV is relatively resistant. Plaque-associated neuritic stress was similar across layers, but layer IV neurons showed attenuated pTau accumulation and reduced expression of a shared neurite–soma stress program (DCTN1, CALM3, NORAD, PIN1); astrocytic APOE–neuronal LRP1 co-expression increased along the pTau-vulnerable trajectory, and APOE ε4 was associated with stronger coupling of local plaque pathology to pTau programs. The findings nominate neuron–glia interactions in selective tau vulnerability. Human postmortem and cross-sectional.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human AD cortex; subcellular spatial transcriptomics with co-registered Aβ/pTau IHC and within-layer trajectory analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.05.764692

### Plasma phosphorylated tau species across neurodegenerative and cerebrovascular diseases: An ONDRI study
**E. Sanchez & M. Masellis | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Across 551 participants (506 from the ONDRI multi-site cohort plus 45 Aβ-PET-negative controls) spanning ten diagnostic groups — MCI, AD, Parkinson's disease (cognitively unimpaired, MCI, dementia), frontotemporal dementia, cerebrovascular disease, and ALS — the study profiles plasma p-tau217 and p-tau181 alongside Aβ42/40, NfL and GFAP and evaluates their discriminatory value in ratio combinations. The analysis characterizes p-tau specificity across neurodegenerative and cerebrovascular disease, relevant given reports of elevated p-tau in ALS. Cross-sectional, with ROC-based discrimination.

**Model/system & primary method:** ONDRI multi-site human cohort; plasma p-tau217/181, Aβ42/40, NfL, GFAP; ROC-AUC analyses of single and combined markers.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.06.26367445

### Accelerometry-derived REM sleep behavior disorder score is associated with incident Parkinson's disease in the UK Biobank
**G. R. Mejia & E. During | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** A validated machine-learning RBD classifier applied to 7-day wrist accelerometry in 87,975 UK Biobank participants identified a highest-risk stratum (>99th percentile) with an approximately fivefold increased hazard of incident Parkinson's disease over 10 years, with a dose-dependent gradient across the score distribution and synergy with PD polygenic risk. High specificity (99.2%) but low sensitivity (7.4%) suits prodromal risk enrichment rather than population screening. This is v2 of a preprint first posted July 2026; the analysis is observational.

**Model/system & primary method:** UK Biobank wrist accelerometry (N = 87,975); ML RBD classifier; Cox analysis of incident PD with polygenic-risk interaction.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.02.26356952
