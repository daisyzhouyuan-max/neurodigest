1Two parallel neural ectoderm progenitors contribute to the developing brain

Nat Neurosci  Rayyan T Jokhai & Kyle M Loh
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Lineage tracing in mouse embryos argues against a single common progenitor for the brain: two spatially distinct neural ectoderm progenitors arise together at gastrulation, an anterior one committed to forebrain/midbrain and a posterior one committed to hindbrain. Directed differentiation of human pluripotent stem cells into anterior- or posterior-like neural ectoderm reproduced the same fate restriction, with diverging chromatin landscapes that prefigure regional identity — and yielded hindbrain rhombomere 5/6 motor neurons that had been hard to make in vitro. The dual-progenitor scheme reframes the brain as a composite organ and may be conserved across ~550 million years.
Model & method
Mouse embryo lineage tracing plus human pluripotent stem cell directed differentiation, with chromatin (accessibility) profiling.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02433-7

2A miR-10a-5p–γCaMKII axis links periphery-to-brain signaling to cognitive vulnerability during female midlife

Neuron  Jing Qu & Huan Ma
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
The study proposes a peripheral origin for female midlife cognitive susceptibility. Senescence-associated features rose preferentially in middle-aged female brain and spleen (human and mouse); in mice, epigenetic upregulation of the X-linked RNA-binding protein RBMX raised splenic miR-10a-5p, and complementary in vivo approaches supported a peripheral contribution to cerebral miR-10a-5p. The miRNA repressed γCaMKII, and manipulating this axis altered mitochondrial function, cellular senescence and memory. In neurons derived from Alzheimer's patients and in model mice, miR-10a-5p inhibition attenuated disease-associated phenotypes, nominating a druggable node — though the periphery-to-brain transfer rests on correlative and indirect in vivo evidence rather than a definitive tracer experiment.
Model & method
Human brain/spleen tissue and mouse models, patient-derived neurons; miRNA gain/loss-of-function with mitochondrial, senescence and memory readouts.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.022

3Targeting glial PD-1/PD-L1 restores microglial homeostasis and reduces neuronal hyperactivity in an Alzheimer's disease model

Sci Adv  Taeyoung Park & Minah Suh
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Immune-checkpoint signaling is repurposed as a brain-intrinsic regulator of glial state. Glial PD-1 and PD-L1 were elevated in 5xFAD mice and human AD tissue; intracortical anti-PD-L1 in 8–10-month-old 5xFAD mice restored microglial process convergence and the homeostatic marker P2RY12 while attenuating aberrant neuronal hyperactivity, with astrocyte-specific PD-L1 knockdown reproducing the effect. This points to astrocytic PD-L1 as an upstream controller of microglia–neuron interactions and a candidate target, with the causal evidence confined to mouse and the human data correlative.
Model & method
5xFAD mice with intracortical anti-PD-L1 and astrocyte-specific PD-L1 knockdown; in vivo two-photon imaging and functional analysis, plus human tissue.
https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adx0731

4Drug screen and machine learning predict neuroprotective agents in a preclinical human model of childhood dementia

Nat Commun  Zarina Greenberg & Cedric Bardy
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Patient iPSC-derived cortical cultures from children with Mucopolysaccharidosis Type IIIA (Sanfilippo syndrome) recapitulated lysosomal dysfunction, heparan sulfate accumulation, progressive neurodegeneration and astrocytic reactivity. A multimodal platform combining machine learning, high-content imaging, single-nuclei transcriptomics and electrophysiology identified at least nine repurposed compounds that mitigated these phenotypes within two weeks in vitro. The work offers a tractable human model and a screening framework with plausible translational reach across childhood and adult neurodegeneration, with the caveat that efficacy so far is entirely in vitro.
Model & method
Patient-derived iPSC cortical cultures (MPS IIIA); machine-learning-guided multimodal drug screen.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76837-1

5Reciprocal connections dynamically build consensus between neocortical areas

Nat Neurosci  Mitra Javadzadeh & Guillaume Hennequin
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
How separate cortical areas reconcile conflicting signals into a coherent percept has been unclear. Simultaneous recordings from mouse V1 and higher-order LM visual cortex, combined with focal optogenetic perturbations and data-driven latent circuit models, showed that reciprocal excitatory V1↔LM connections implement an approximate line attractor in their joint dynamics: congruent activity patterns decay slowly while inconsistent ones decay fast, yielding inter-area consensus. The model's prediction of distinct timescales for consistent versus inconsistent patterns was borne out in the data, identifying dynamic consensus-building as a candidate principle of distributed cortical computation.
Model & method
Mice; simultaneous multi-area electrophysiology with focal optogenetic perturbation and nonlinear latent circuit modeling.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02437-3

Serotonin neurons in the dorsal raphe control food-craving-like behavior during pregnancy in mice

Nat Neurosci  Qianru Zhao & Yanlin He
Key findings
Pregnant mice showed food-craving-like behavior paralleling human patterns. Dorsal raphe 5-HT neuron firing fell during pregnancy through increased SK3 (small-conductance calcium-activated potassium) channel activity; SK3 knockout in these neurons abolished the firing suppression and reduced craving, while SK3 overexpression recreated craving in virgin females. Activating 5-HT dorsal raphe→ventral tegmental area projections suppressed the behavior, identifying a specific circuit and channel controlling pregnancy-associated appetite.
Model & method
Mouse genetics (SK3 knockout/overexpression), electrophysiology and projection-specific circuit manipulation.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02445-3

Oligodendrocytes in central nervous system health and disease

Cell  Lucy J Ryan & Veronique E Miron
Key findings
A review recasting oligodendrocytes as active participants in CNS function — providing metabolic support to axons, tuning neural signaling through adaptive myelination, and repairing myelin after injury — that become dysfunctional in aging and disease and can themselves drive pathology. It synthesizes how oligodendrocyte heterogeneity shapes roles across development, homeostasis, aging and disease, and frames these cells as therapeutic targets while flagging open questions.
Model & method
Narrative review.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.003

Temporal regulation of progenitor lineage progression and output by NFIs underlying human neocortical malformation

Neuron  Qiangqiang Zhang & Song-Hai Shi
Key findings
Nuclear factor I (NFI) transcription factors act as global temporal regulators of radial glial progenitor lineage progression and lifespan, bidirectionally controlling neocortical development. Human cerebral organoids carrying patient NFI mutations showed expression-level-dependent, bidirectional impairments matching patient phenotypes; in mice, NFI removal protracted progenitor lifespan with excess progeny, cortical overgrowth and abnormal folding, while overexpression accelerated lineage progression and caused precocious neurogenesis. NFIs showed positive autoregulation and progressively rising expression, engaging distinct temporal-specific targets.
Model & method
Human cerebral organoids with patient-mimicking mutations and mouse gain/loss-of-function models.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.021

The recipe for intelligence in natural and artificial systems

Neuron  Christopher Summerfield & Kimberly Stachenfeld
Key findings
A perspective revisiting, ten years on, open problems posed for AI from a neuroscience/cognitive-science standpoint, arguing that many were solved largely without direct brain inspiration. It contends both fields are now moving from cataloguing the "ingredients" of intelligence to grasping the "recipe" — how components fit together — and that this recipe shows surprising parallels across biological and artificial systems, while outlining computational problems that remain unsolved.
Model & method
Perspective/review.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.013

Aβ1-40 induces venular endothelial cell ferroptosis in cerebral amyloid angiopathy via the ATF3/xCT axis

Alzheimers Dement  Chunmei Wu & Shanshan Huang
Key findings
Single-cell RNA-seq of cortical microvessels from 11-month-old APP23 mice localized the strongest ferroptosis signature to venular endothelial cells, which showed progressive ferroptosis-related changes during CAA. Mechanistically, Aβ1-40 raised ATF3 and its occupancy at the Slc7a11 promoter, repressing Slc7a11/xCT and glutathione-dependent antioxidant defense; endothelial ATF3 knockdown attenuated venular endothelial ferroptosis, lowered cerebral Aβ burden and improved cognition in APP23 mice, nominating ATF3/xCT as an early-stage CAA target. Causal evidence is mouse-based.
Model & method
APP23 mouse model; single-cell RNA-seq with biochemical, histopathological and knockdown validation.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71858

Genetic evidence suggests a protective role of immunoglobulin M in Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimers Dement  Shihui Peng & Tianyuan Lu
Key findings
Integrating two-sample Mendelian randomization with plasma biomarker analyses across All of Us, UK Biobank and two Wisconsin cohorts, higher genetically proxied IgM was consistently associated with lower AD risk, higher Aβ42/40 and lower p-tau217, but not with NfL or GFAP; no consistent associations emerged for IgG or IgA. The pattern implicates IgM-related humoral immunity as protective in AD and a candidate for early intervention, with the usual MR caveats about instrument validity and pleiotropy.
Model & method
Human genetics — Mendelian randomization and polygenic risk scores with plasma biomarker association.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71865

Allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation in late-onset metachromatic leukodystrophy

Brain  Daphne H Schoenmakers & Nicole I Wolf
Key findings
In the largest European registry cohort of late-juvenile and adult metachromatic leukodystrophy, 15 pre-symptomatic and 37 symptomatic HSCT-treated patients were compared with 79 untreated patients over follow-ups up to 25 years. Advanced disease stage occurred in 27% of pre-symptomatically transplanted patients versus 72–78% of symptomatic-treated or untreated patients; HSCT appeared to delay advanced disease (HR 1.49, 95% CI 0.96–2.30) and improved quality-of-life and daily functioning across stages, with the greatest benefit from pre-symptomatic treatment. As an observational registry comparison, it cannot fully exclude selection effects.
Model & method
Human multicenter observational registry study with regression analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag323

GBA1 in Parkinson's disease: past, present and future

Brain  Ziv Gan-Or
Key findings
A comprehensive review of GBA1 as one of the most important genetic risk factors for Parkinson's disease, tracing from Gaucher disease to current mechanistic models linking reduced glucocerebrosidase activity to α-synuclein accumulation and neurodegeneration, and weighing loss-of-function versus gain-of-function hypotheses. It surveys GCase-targeted therapeutics (small molecules, chaperones, substrate reduction, gene approaches) and recent trial outcomes, arguing for variant-specific, precision-medicine stratification and better biomarkers to define true GBA1-associated PD.
Model & method
Narrative review.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag322

Serum metabolomic profiles unveil biomarkers and core metabolic pathways in multiple system atrophy

Mov Disord  Linlin Wan & Hong Jiang
Key findings
Untargeted serum metabolomics in 85 MSA patients versus 85 controls, with targeted validation in internal and external cohorts (including Parkinson's disease comparators), implicated pathways spanning arginine/proline, pentose phosphate, branched-chain amino acid biosynthesis, purine and riboflavin metabolism. A riboflavin–xanthine panel separated MSA from controls (AUC 0.876 internal, 0.943 external), and reduced xanthine modestly improved neurofilament light-based MSA-versus-PD discrimination (AUC 0.821→0.890). In hSNCA-overexpressing cells, riboflavin and xanthine improved viability, with riboflavin showing therapeutic effects in SH-SY5Y cells.
Model & method
Human serum metabolomics (UHPLC-QE-MS) with cell-based (MO3.13, SH-SY5Y) validation.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70541

Population-scale burden analysis of rare damaging coding variants identifies novel risk genes for Alzheimer's disease and related dementias and Parkinson's disease and related disorders

medRxiv  Le Guen, Y. & Greicius, M. D.
Key findings
Gene-burden testing of loss-of-function and deleterious missense variants across biobanks and disease-focused sequencing cohorts, using calibrated proxy-phenotypes to boost power for late-onset disease, confirmed rare-variant burden in established ADRD genes (TREM2, ABCA7, SORL1, PSEN1, GRN, ATP8B4, ABCA1, ADAM10) and PDRD genes (GBA1, LRRK2). It reports a novel ADRD association at IMPA2 and novel PDRD associations at ANKRD27 and CCL7, with the strongest new signal at ANKRD27, where damaging variants clustered in domains mediating Rab GTPase and retromer interactions. Preprint; novel loci await replication.

Myo-inositol as a spectroscopic marker of neuroinflammation in frontotemporal lobar degeneration

medRxiv  Tan, S. Y. & Rowe, J. B.
Key findings
In 54 people with FTLD-associated disorders (behavioural-variant FTD and progressive supranuclear palsy) and 73 matched controls, 7-Tesla single-voxel proton MRS found myo-inositol elevated in the disease-affected right inferior frontal gyrus (β=0.20, p≈9×10⁻⁷) but not in a control occipital voxel. Within patients, right IFG myo-inositol tracked a peripheral inflammatory profile (IL-6, IL-1, GFAP, CSF2, CRP, CCL22; β=0.39, p≈4×10⁻⁶) and correlated inversely with cognition (ACE-R; β=-44.6, p≈0.0002), supporting MRS myo-inositol as a non-invasive, repeatable neuroinflammation biomarker. Cross-sectional and correlational; preprint.
Model & method
Human 7T ¹H-MRS with serum inflammatory profiling (NULISA) and cognitive testing.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.18.26363386

Associations of menopause hormone therapy with late-life cognition and brain health vary by formulation and APOEε4 presence

medRxiv  Perovic, M. & Galea, L. A. M.
Key findings
In 8,741 women over 70, associations between menopause hormone therapy and cognition were formulation- and domain-specific, with estradiol-based therapy linked to more favourable cognition than conjugated equine estrogens, particularly in APOEε4 carriers. In a neuroimaging subset (n=930), APOEε4 carriers on estradiol-based therapy showed a consistent pattern of higher volume and cortical thickness and less white matter damage. The authors frame prior mixed findings as partly an exposure-definition problem and argue for formulation- and genetic-risk-aware menopause care. Observational and cross-sectional; preprint.
Model & method
Human observational cohort with cognition and structural neuroimaging, stratified by APOEε4.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.16.26363120
