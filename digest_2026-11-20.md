### Genome writing reveals SVA retrotransposon consequences in X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism
**Weimin Zhang & Jef D Boeke | Cell**

**Why it matters:** XDP is caused by an intronic SINE-VNTR-Alu (SVA) insertion in TAF1, but whether the element itself or the resulting hybrid gene structure drives disease was unresolved. By genomically rewriting ~78 kb of the mouse Taf1 locus with the patient-derived human TAF1 allele, the authors show the intronic SVA alone — actively transcribed and acting as a barrier to transcription elongation — lowers hybrid TAF1 mRNA, downregulates TATA-box–enriched genes, and triggers apoptosis. Neuronal-lineage humanization was lethal in male mice within two months with striatal atrophy matching patients, and CRISPRa reactivation of hyTAF1 restored stem-cell viability, nominating transcriptional restoration as a therapeutic route.

**Model/system & primary method:** Genome-written (~78-kb) humanized Taf1/TAF1 mouse embryonic stem cells and mice; chromatin and transcriptome profiling; CRISPRa.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.07.057

### Myelin pathology is a key feature of X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism
**Priya Prakash & Shane A Liddelow | Cell**

**Why it matters:** In a companion study using a conditional humanized XDP mouse (hyTAF1 with the pathogenic SVA_F activated in Nestin+ cells), the authors find that beyond striatal atrophy and motor impairment, XDP features a reduction in oligodendrocyte-lineage cells and overt myelin disruption. Postmortem XDP patient brain showed matching myelin loss in medial prefrontal cortex. Together these results reframe XDP as involving white-matter and glial pathology, implicating oligodendrocyte dysfunction — not neuronal loss alone — as a major disease contributor.

**Model/system & primary method:** Conditional humanized XDP mouse (Nestin-driven hyTAF1); transcriptomics, histology, electron microscopy, human postmortem validation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.10.046

### CD19 CAR T cell therapy alters disease-associated immune signatures in multiple sclerosis
**Felix Fischbach & Manuel A Friese | Cell**

**Why it matters:** In five patients with progressive or relapsing-remitting MS treated with autologous CD19 CAR T cells, the therapy remodeled the CSF B-cell pool and produced a sustained reduction in CSF humoral biomarkers — evidence that CAR T cells access and modulate compartmentalized CNS inflammation rather than only depleting peripheral B cells. CSF-infiltrating CAR T cells selectively upregulated phosphodiesterase 7B, and reconstituted peripheral B cells showed altered cytokine and homing profiles. The data are early and safety-oriented (n = 5) but motivate controlled trials in progressive MS, where current immunotherapies do little.

**Model/system & primary method:** First-in-human case series (n = 5); CSF and blood immunophenotyping and biomarker profiling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.10.044

### A neural code for generative speech production in human Wernicke's area
**Alexander B Silva & Edward F Chang | Science**

**Why it matters:** Resolving the long-standing paradox that Wernicke's-area damage impairs speech production as well as comprehension, direct human recordings, cortical stimulation, and lesion analysis identify a discrete subzone in the left posterior superior temporal gyrus that activates before speech, is causally necessary for production, and encodes features of whole upcoming words rather than low-level acoustics or articulation. Its activity is distinct from nearby auditory-feedback responses and could decode intended speech in people with paralysis — directly relevant to speech neuroprostheses.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human intracranial recordings, cortical stimulation, lesion analysis, and neural decoding.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aef8963

### GFAP upregulation by astrocytes attenuates tau neurofibrillary tangle pathology in a mouse model of tauopathy
**Clara Muñoz-Castro & Alberto Serrano-Pozo | Molecular Neurodegeneration**

**Why it matters:** Reactive-astrocyte GFAP upregulation is usually treated as structural, but here it is protective in proteinopathy: viral GFAP overexpression in astrocytes of THY-Tau22 mice reduced phospho-tau neurofibrillary tangle burden. The GFAP interactome in human control and AD brain (co-immunoprecipitation/mass spectrometry) links it to chaperone-mediated autophagy and endolysosomal trafficking, suggesting astrocytic GFAP participates in a proteostatic program that limits tangle accumulation — a candidate glial mechanism relevant across tauopathies.

**Model/system & primary method:** THY-Tau22 tauopathy mice with AAV-mediated astrocytic GFAP overexpression; human-brain GFAP co-IP/mass-spectrometry interactome.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00988-4

### The neural mechanisms of fast versus slow decision-making
**Mostafa Nashaat & Matthew E Larkum | Neuron**

**Key findings:** Using a paradigm that directly compares decision-making in mice and humans, the authors show that history-dependent refinement of neural sequences in the medial frontal cortex (MFC), versus a thresholding mechanism in the lateral frontal cortex (LFC), provides a unified logic for trial-by-trial decision strategy and timing. Optogenetic inhibition of MFC (more than LFC) profoundly disrupted performance and strategy, revealing a dominant role for MFC in decision selection and timing. The dynamics reflected reinforcement-learning updates that explained timing variability better than accumulated sensory evidence.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse frontal-cortex widefield and 2-photon imaging with optogenetic inhibition; parallel human task; reinforcement-learning modeling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.024

### A thalamo-amygdalar circuit drives female vulnerability to chronic pain after early-life stress
**Anna Maria Borruto & Sebastian Wieland | Science**

**Key findings:** In mice, social isolation and nerve injury interacted to amplify activity-dependent synaptic gain and anticipatory recruitment of the paraventricular thalamus→central amygdala pathway in females but not males, enhancing mechanical hypersensitivity and avoidance in adulthood. Chemogenetic inhibition rescued these behaviors selectively when stress and injury co-occurred, whereas sustained and phasic pathway activation reproduced hypersensitivity and avoidance, respectively. The work defines a latent, sex-specific synaptic gain state unmasked by a later injury (mouse-only causal evidence).

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse; chemogenetic and activity-dependent circuit manipulation, electrophysiology, behavior.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adx5757

### The fate of poised pre-mRNAs shapes mitochondrial activity and cellular differentiation
**Xavier Rambout & Lynne E Maquat | Science**

**Key findings:** On-chromatin quality control of poised intron-containing polyadenylated RNAs (PIA-RNAs) is decided by competing engagement with nuclear cap-binding protein 3 (NCBP3, favoring maturation) versus anillin (favoring nuclear-exosome degradation). Many PIA-RNAs reciprocally controlled this way encode mitochondrial factors; NCBP3 depletion in mouse and pathogenic RNA-exosome mutations in humans reduced mitochondrial activity, with muscle weakness, cerebellar hypomyelination, and impaired motor coordination — linking nuclear RNA surveillance to mitochondrial function and neurological disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse and human cells; RNA-metabolism biochemistry and genetics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aee8018

### Neurobiological correlates of longitudinal grey matter volume changes in preclinical Alzheimer's disease
**Wiesje Pelkmans & Juan Domingo Gispert | Molecular Neurodegeneration**

**Key findings:** In 627 cognitively unimpaired adults across three cohorts with repeated MRI (~3.5 y) and a broad CSF panel decomposed by non-negative matrix factorization into six latent components, the amyloid-β component was the strongest, most widespread predictor of longitudinal grey-matter atrophy (temporal and frontal) and of cognitive decline, independent of tau, neuroaxonal-injury, and neuroinflammatory components. Tau-related/synaptic-injury signal lost significance after adjustment, while microglial, astrocytic, and cytokine components were associated with transient grey-matter increases that varied by amyloid/tau stage. Observational, multi-cohort.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cohorts (ALFA+, Wisconsin ADRC, WRAP); longitudinal MRI, CSF NeuroToolKit panel, NMF decomposition.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00987-5

### Reinforced hippocampus–accumbens pathway marks a shift toward cognitive decline in aging
**Aurore Ribera & Paula A Pousinha | Cell Reports**

**Key findings:** In aging mice, a sex-dependent shift of the dorsal CA1 excitation–inhibition balance toward pyramidal-neuron hyperactivity strengthened dorsal hippocampus→nucleus accumbens (dHPC→NAc) outputs, selectively enhancing synaptic drive onto D1-receptor medium spiny neurons through a previously unrecognized long-range parvalbumin projection. Chemogenetically reducing dHPC→NAc activity improved memory in aged mice, and human fMRI showed heightened posterior-hippocampal activity and stronger posterior HPC–NAc coupling during navigation — nominating a targetable pathway within an intervention-sensitive aging window.

**Model/system & primary method:** Aging mice (optogenetics, electrophysiology, chemogenetics, behavior) with human navigation fMRI.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118362

### Relative pericentral tau distribution in cerebral amyloid angiopathy compared with Alzheimer disease
**Ya Su & Xin Cheng | Neurology**

**Key findings:** Using amyloid (18F-florbetapir) and tau (18F-florzolotau) PET, the authors tested whether tau topography separates cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) from AD. Although CAA showed lower global and pericentral tau than AD, the pericentral-to-global tau ratio was significantly higher in CAA (0.93 ± 0.07 vs 0.82 ± 0.08); a 0.90 cutoff gave AUC 0.836 (derivation) and 0.844 in an external 18F-flortaucipir cohort. In CAA, pericentral tau correlated with regional amyloid and white-matter hyperintensity volume. Cross-sectional PET cohorts.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human dual-tracer tau/amyloid PET (80 CAA, 64 AD, 22 controls) with external validation; regional SUVR and conditional-probability analyses.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218786

### Biomarker-informed differences in treatment-associated cognitive decline in dementia with Lewy bodies
**Cen Chen & Maria Eriksdotter | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** Linking the Swedish SveDem registry with CSF biomarkers in 360 incident DLB patients (54.4% AD biomarker-positive), AD biomarker positivity marked faster decline — an additional 0.80 MMSE points/year (95% CI −1.17 to −0.43). Cholinesterase-inhibitor use was associated with slower decline overall and specifically among biomarker-positive patients, with treatment-associated MMSE trajectories differing by biomarker status in interaction analyses. The treatment findings are observational and require prospective, biomarker-stratified confirmation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human registry cohort (SveDem + CSF Aβ42/40, p-tau); linear mixed-effects models with inverse-probability-of-treatment and censoring weighting.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.72021

### Iron dysregulation: implications for pathology in multiple sclerosis
**Dorsa Moezzi & V Wee Yong | Molecular Neurodegeneration**

**Key findings:** This review synthesizes iron biology and its dysregulation in multiple sclerosis, where iron accumulation drives oxidative stress and localized injury but also acts through oxidative-stress–independent routes including protein aggregation, abnormal sphingolipid metabolism, and ferroptosis. It frames open mechanistic questions — why brain iron homeostasis fails and how the resulting damage might be reversed — and reviews therapeutic strategies targeting iron toxicity, drawing parallels to iron-lowering approaches in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** Narrative review of iron biology and iron-targeted therapeutics in demyelinating and neurodegenerative disease.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00989-3

### Multi-omic–guided iPSC dopaminergic differentiation enriches PD-vulnerable SOX6+ midbrain neurons with motor recovery after transplantation
**R. Garcia Swinburn & E. Arenas | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Standard human pluripotent-stem-cell protocols fail to generate the SOX6+ midbrain dopaminergic subtypes most vulnerable in PD. A multiomic-guided strategy — combining enhanced Sonic Hedgehog agonism with more aggressive, prolonged Wnt activation at a differentiation bottleneck localized by longitudinal single-nuclei multiomics — enriched SOX6+ mDA neurons that secreted dopamine and transcriptionally resembled postmortem midbrain. Intrastriatal transplantation into 6-OHDA hemiparkinsonian mice produced motor recovery. Preclinical mouse grafts; v4 revision of a preprint first posted June 2025.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived midbrain dopaminergic neurons; single-nuclei multiome, intrastriatal transplantation into 6-OHDA mice.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.06.18.660098

### Evolutionarily conserved coupling of lysosomal response sensing and disease-associated microglial reprogramming scales with pathological timescales
**Y. Liang & Y. Zhang | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Integrating six transcriptomic datasets — three murine injury/amyloidosis models and three human neurodegenerative cohorts (>320,000 microglial nuclei) — with a unit-corrected statistical framework and AD GWAS, the authors find the MiT/TFE-regulated lysosomal-biogenesis program temporally precedes disease-associated microglia (DAM) reprogramming and maintains a conserved baseline coupling even in homeostatic tissue. Computational re-analysis of public data.

**Model/system & primary method:** Cross-species single-cell microglial transcriptomic meta-analysis with AD GWAS enrichment.

### Distinct relaxation kurtosis imaging–derived white matter microstructural phenotypes tracking Alzheimer's disease pathology and cognitive decline
**D. Yablonskiy & T. L. S. Benzinger | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 305 adults spanning amyloid-negative to early symptomatic AD, a new MRI Relaxation Kurtosis Imaging (RKI) method resolved two white-matter phenotypes with divergent trajectories: one reflecting axonal alteration without myelin loss, anatomically coupled to cortical grey-matter pathology and strengthening with disease stage (a Wallerian-degeneration signature), the other reflecting demyelination. A model adding RKI metrics to age, sex, amyloid status, APOE, and brain volume classified CDR-based impairment at AUC 0.933, with RKI contributing 27% of the signal (second only to amyloid). Human, cross-sectional.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human MRI (relaxation kurtosis imaging) across the AD continuum (n = 305); multivariable classification.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.17.26368344

### Lipidome of compact myelin from cortical regions of the aging murine brain
**V. P. Liu & K. L. Furber | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Compact myelin was density-purified from cortex of young, middle-aged, and aged mice and profiled by LC-MS/MS. Of more than 2000 lipid species, 414 changed across the age span; young-mouse myelin was biochemically distinct, and aging brought broad decreases in abundant glycerophospholipids and sphingolipids (phosphatidylcholine, phosphatidylethanolamine, sphingomyelin, cerebrosides), implicating altered lipid metabolism and membrane biophysics before overt myelin loss. Descriptive mouse lipidomics.

**Model/system & primary method:** Aging mouse cortical compact-myelin LC-MS/MS lipidomics.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.01.763492

### Body-wide microbial ecosystem reorganization across the cognitive impairment spectrum in Parkinson's disease
**M. Arıkan & S. Yildirim | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Across five body sites (stool, saliva, tongue, nasal cavity, axilla) in 150 participants — healthy controls, PD with mild cognitive impairment (PD-MCI), and PD dementia (PDD), 616 samples by 16S rRNA sequencing — a multisite microbial signature with prominent oral and gut contributions varied with cognitive status, including a module distinguishing PD-MCI from PDD (in-sample AUROC 0.810) and a net loss of 45 genus-site core memberships in cognitively impaired participants (including the butyrate-producing genus Roseburia). Microbial features added ~8 percentage points to cognitive-outcome variance beyond demographics. Cross-sectional and associative.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multisite 16S microbiome (n = 150); MOFA2 and MintTea multi-omics factor analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.17.767360
