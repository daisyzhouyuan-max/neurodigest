### Longitudinal plasma phosphorylated tau217 classification and the association with Alzheimer's disease progression
**Guoyu Lan & Tengfei Guo | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** In a multi-cohort study of 2,117 individuals, baseline plasma p-tau217 cutoffs for amyloid-PET positivity remained 86–95% accurate over up to 5 years of follow-up, and most p-tau217-positive individuals stayed stable while an "intermediate zone" group progressed to positivity at high rates (44–78%). Stable- and progress-to-positive profiles tracked with faster tau accumulation, hippocampal atrophy, and incident dementia, supporting p-tau217 as a scalable longitudinal risk-stratification tool rather than a single-timepoint screen.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multi-cohort longitudinal study; plasma p-tau217 immunoassay benchmarked against amyloid-β PET.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71783

### AGG repeat expansion and aggregation of BIN1 in multiple system atrophy
**Kodai Kume & Hideshi Kawakami | Brain**

**Why it matters:** Long-read sequencing of a familial multiple system atrophy (MSA) proband identified an AGG repeat expansion in BIN1, the first candidate genetic driver reported for this fatal, sporadic α-synucleinopathy. Expansions >80 repeats were significantly enriched in pathologically confirmed MSA brains versus controls (13.4% vs 0%), and correlated with BIN1-positive glial cytoplasmic inclusions and increased insoluble BIN1; the association was weaker and not statistically significant in the larger clinically diagnosed cohort, so replication in independent pathology-confirmed series is needed.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human genetics (long-read whole-genome sequencing, clinical and pathology-confirmed patient cohorts) with postmortem neuropathology and biochemical fractionation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag194

### Neuron-derived SPP1 instructs microglia to limit degeneration
**Song Li & Tatjana C Jakobs | Sci Adv**

**Why it matters:** SPP1 (osteopontin) secreted by stressed neurons reprograms microglia toward a neuroprotective, autophagy-competent state after injury in mouse models of glaucoma and optic nerve damage, preserving neuronal survival and visual function. SPP1 is elevated in human/primate glaucomatous retina and correlates with neuronal resilience, and in postmortem AD brain neuronal SPP1 tracks with neuronal survival despite defective microglial autophagy around plaques; in human iPSC co-cultures SPP1 enhanced microglial Aβ clearance. The causal injury-response work is mouse/retina-based, with human data limited to correlation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse glaucoma/optic-nerve-injury models, human and primate glaucomatous retina, postmortem AD brain tissue, human iPSC-derived microglia–neuron co-culture.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee4940

### Leucine-rich repeat kinase 2 impairs the release sites of Parkinson's disease vulnerable dopamine axons
**Chuyu Chen & Loukia Parisiadou | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** Using intersectional genetic mouse models, this study shows pathogenic LRRK2 is enriched in the specific SNc dopamine-neuron subtypes most vulnerable in Parkinson's disease and disrupts presynaptic active-zone organization by increasing RAB3 phosphorylation and impairing its interaction with RIM1/RIM2, reducing spontaneous and evoked striatal dopamine release in vivo. This defines a cell-autonomous, subtype-specific synaptic mechanism that precedes overt neuronal loss and nominates early synaptic deficits as a therapeutic target — evidence to date is confined to mouse models.

**Model/system & primary method:** Intersectional genetic mouse models, proximity-labeling proteomics, in vivo striatal dopamine release measurements.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75194-3

### Characterizing shared and distinctive molecular phenotypes across motor regions in ALS with and without TDP-43 pathology in a veteran cohort
**[PREPRINT — NOT PEER REVIEWED]**

**Doyle, P. H. & Orr, M. | bioRxiv**

**Why it matters:** Spatially resolved profiling of postmortem primary motor cortex and cervical/lumbar spinal cord from ALS patients (pTDP-43-positive and -negative) and controls found convergent senescence-associated transcriptomic and proteomic signatures in vulnerable motor neurons across both pathology groups, alongside region- and pathology-specific differences in RNA processing, mitochondrial function, proteostasis, and inflammation pathways. The findings support a model in which motor neurons adopt a persistent stress-adaptive senescence state that permits short-term survival at the cost of progressive dysfunction, nominating senescence pathways as a therapeutic target — this is cross-sectional postmortem human tissue and a preprint, so causal and temporal claims are not established.

**Model/system & primary method:** Postmortem human ALS motor cortex and spinal cord (veteran cohort); targeted bulk transcriptomics and GeoMx digital spatial profiling of individual motor neurons.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.28.747944

### A bifunctional optical reporter for tracking estrogen response dynamics in neurons
**Alexandra L Cara & J Edward van Veen | Neuron**

**Key findings:** The authors developed neuro-seeER, a fluorescent bifunctional reporter that tracks estrogen receptor-dependent estrogen response dynamics in vitro and longitudinally in behaving mice. Using repeated "snapshot" photometry, they detected coordinated exogenous-estrogen responses across multiple hypothalamic sites and captured endogenous estrogen response dynamics across the estrous cycle, revealing individual differences in response timing — a tools/methods advance rather than a disease-mechanism finding.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse in vivo fiber photometry (with in vitro validation) using an estrogen-receptor-dependent fluorescent reporter.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.07.031

### Extracellular matrix remodeling upregulates hippocampal neurogenic niche stiffness and impairs neurogenesis in Alzheimer's disease
**Weiqi Sun & Jie Zheng | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** The hippocampal dentate gyrus subgranular zone stiffens as early as 3 months in 5×FAD mice, driven by ECM remodeling; artificially stiffening this niche in wild-type mice suppressed adult neurogenesis, while softening it in 5×FAD mice with glycosaminoglycanases (HAase1) preserved neurogenesis. Single-nucleus sequencing and conditional knockdowns implicate an integrin-β1/YAP1 mechanotransduction axis, and matched ECM/neurogenesis changes were seen in postmortem AD tissue. Causal manipulation is mouse-only; the human data are correlative.

**Model/system & primary method:** 5×FAD mice, atomic force microscopy stiffness mapping, single-nucleus RNA sequencing, viral conditional knockdown/overexpression; postmortem human AD hippocampus.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71784

### Core activation program and selective regional responsiveness of microglia during aging and parabiosis
**Huma Naz & Guoyan Zhao | Cell Rep**

**Key findings:** Single-cell profiling across four mouse brain regions identifies a reproducible core gene program driving microglial activation and marked region-specific heterogeneity in how microglia respond to aging and to parabiosis-mediated exposure to young or old blood. The cerebellum was consistently the most responsive region to parabiosis while the striatum was largely unresponsive, pointing to region-specific vulnerability that should inform where microglia-targeted rejuvenation strategies are likely to work.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse parabiosis (young/old blood exchange) with single-cell RNA sequencing across cerebellum, cortex, hippocampus, and striatum.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117919

### CELF2 promotes 4R Tau splicing via nuclear clustering and drives cognitive dysfunction in tauopathy models
**Xin Li & Lizhen Chen | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** The RNA-binding protein CELF2 promotes pathogenic 4R-tau exon-10 splicing through an intrinsically disordered hinge domain that drives multivalent nuclear clustering with NOVA2 and SFPQ; a single conserved residue (D388) is required for this assembly and for splicing activity. In vivo, CELF2's assembly capacity correlated with 4R-tau expression and with locomotor and cognitive performance, linking condensate formation by an RNA-binding protein to the tau-isoform imbalance central to tauopathy.

**Model/system & primary method:** In vitro splicing and condensate-formation assays combined with in vivo behavioral (locomotor/cognitive) testing in a tauopathy model.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76241-9

### Temporal progression of auditory brainstem dysfunction and behavioral phenotypes in mouse models of tauopathy
**Ann M Nguyen & Xiangmin Xu | J Neurosci**

**Key findings:** In PS19 (P301S tau) mice, auditory brainstem response (ABR) abnormalities — reduced, delayed neural responses to sound — appeared by 3 months, before any detectable anxiety-like or motor changes. Adding the ApoE4 allele accelerated and sustained ABR deficits alongside emerging behavioral impairment by 7 months, suggesting ABR could serve as an early, noninvasive biomarker of tau-driven neurodegeneration and that ApoE4 modulates the pace of circuit dysfunction. Findings are limited to a single transgenic tau model.

**Model/system & primary method:** PS19 and ApoE4/PS19 mice; auditory brainstem response recordings paired with elevated-plus-maze and rotarod behavioral assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0511-26.2026

### Centrally Acting Medications, Chronic Pain, and Orthopedic Surgical History Among Former American Football Players
**Alexa Puleio & as the DIAGNOSE CTE Research Project | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In the DIAGNOSE CTE cohort of 236 former professional and collegiate American football players and unexposed controls, centrally acting medication (CAM) use and average chronic pain scores — but not orthopedic surgery count — were independently associated with higher odds of neurobehavioral dysregulation and, for CAMs, lower MoCA scores, after adjusting for age, education, race, and years played. Neither CAMs nor pain were associated with consensus-diagnosed cognitive impairment. This is an important confound for interpreting cognitive/behavioral symptoms in CTE research, since polypharmacy and chronic pain — not just repetitive head impact exposure — track with neurobehavioral symptoms.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cross-sectional cohort study (n=236) with logistic and linear regression against traumatic encephalopathy syndrome (TES) research criteria.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218475

### Mitochondrial DNA copy number in neurodegenerative diseases: a global meta-analysis of 156 comparisons across 76 studies
**[PREPRINT — NOT PEER REVIEWED]**

**Mathews, R. & Havird, J. C. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** A systematic review and meta-analysis of 76 studies (156 comparisons) found no overall difference in mitochondrial DNA copy number (mtDNA-CN) between people with and without neurodegenerative disease, but heterogeneity across studies was extreme (I²=99.5%), explained mostly by the specific disease diagnosed: Alzheimer's disease showed a 21% mtDNA-CN decrease while Parkinson's disease showed none. Decreases were larger at older ages and in earlier-published studies, suggesting the field's prevailing assumption of universal mtDNA-CN decline in neurodegeneration is an oversimplification carried forward from early, possibly biased, studies — a caution for using mtDNA-CN as a general biomarker without disease-specific validation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of published human observational studies; no new experimental data generated.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.25.747144

### Hyperactivation of the AXL-ICD/SIRT2 axis by Amyloid-β impairs astrocytic autophagic flux and exacerbates neuroinflammation
**[PREPRINT — NOT PEER REVIEWED]**

**Kim, T. Y. & Lee, C. J. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Amyloid-β drives γ-secretase-dependent cleavage of astrocytic AXL, generating a nuclear intracellular domain (AXL-ICD) that phase-separates and, via SIRT2-mediated chromatin remodeling, initially promotes autophagy gene transcription; excessive AXL-ICD, however, paradoxically hyperstabilizes microtubules, impairs autophagosome–lysosome fusion, and drives H2O2 accumulation and astrogliosis. This axis is active in astrocytes of postmortem AD brain, and a peptide that disrupts the AXL-ICD/SIRT2 interaction restored autophagic flux and reduced plaque burden and astrogliosis in APP/PS1 mice — a mechanistically detailed and potentially druggable pathway, though the therapeutic peptide has only been tested in mice. *Note: this is a v2 revision; the preprint was first posted in late August 2026 (~1 week before this digest window), with the current version posted 2026-08-31.*

**Model/system & primary method:** Postmortem human AD astrocytes and APP/PS1 mice; biochemical/imaging assays of AXL-ICD phase separation and autophagic flux, peptide-based pathway disruption.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.23.746508

### RGC-specific reversal of lipid peroxidation drives neuroprotection and vision restoration in optic nerve ischemia by targeting GPX4
**[PREPRINT — NOT PEER REVIEWED]**

**Yang, M. & Liao, Y. J. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In human NAION (nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy) retina and a validated mouse model, ischemic injury triggers marked phospholipid peroxidation in retinal ganglion cells (RGCs). RGC-specific, and especially mitochondria-targeted, overexpression of GPX4 restored axonal and mitochondrial bioenergetics, suppressed oxidative stress, and preserved RGC survival, axonal transport, and visual function; the clinically tested GPX mimetic Ebselen reproduced these protective effects. This nominates lipid-peroxide detoxification as a translatable therapeutic strategy for a currently untreatable ischemic optic neuropathy, though clinical efficacy remains to be tested.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human NAION retinal tissue and a validated mouse NAION model; AAV-mediated GPX4 overexpression, real-time multiparametric in vivo imaging, pharmacologic rescue with Ebselen.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.25.747113

### Mitochondrial dysfunction as a hallmark of brain senescence in telomerase-deficient mice
**[PREPRINT — NOT PEER REVIEWED]**

**Palomares, D. & Suelves, N. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In telomerase-deficient mice (a model of telomere-attrition-driven cellular senescence), proteomic profiling — more than transcriptomic — identified impaired electron transport chain activity and reduced energetic status as the dominant dysregulated pathway in the aging brain, despite preserved ETC complex integrity and mitochondrial content, indicating a functional rather than structural mitochondrial deficit. Senescent primary neurons showed increased ROS and reduced ATP, and affected mice showed region-specific (subiculum, cortical layer V) rather than widespread reductions in neuronal density, supporting mitochondrial dysfunction as a candidate driver of selective regional vulnerability in brain aging.

**Model/system & primary method:** Telomerase-deficient (telomere-attrition) mouse model; brain proteomics/transcriptomics, isolated-mitochondria functional assays, primary neuron culture.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.25.746691
