### Bepranemab, a tau mid-region antibody, fails to slow prodromal-to-mild Alzheimer's disease (phase 2)
**Matthew E Barton & Martin Citron | Lancet Neurol**

**Why it matters:** N-terminal tau antibodies have repeatedly failed in Alzheimer's disease; bepranemab was designed to bind the tau mid-region to block neuron-to-neuron spread more effectively. In this 466-participant, 76-week trial the primary endpoint — change in CDR-SB at week 80 — was not met: differences versus placebo were small and numerically unfavourable (90 mg/kg +0.34, p=0.12; 45 mg/kg +0.04, p=0.87). Safety was acceptable with no clinical or MRI neuroinflammatory signal. Hypothesis-generating differences in tau PET and ADAS-Cog14 were noted but need prospective, tau-PET-powered testing. Another negative tau immunotherapy, tempering the mid-region targeting rationale.

**Model/system & primary method:** Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial; prodromal-to-mild AD; IV bepranemab 45 or 90 mg/kg vs placebo every 4 weeks for 76 weeks; primary endpoint CDR-SB at week 80.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00357-1

### Two functionally distinct amyloid-β intermediates link aggregation routes to pathology
**Agnieszka Urbanek & Suman De | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** Transient Aβ aggregation intermediates are thought to drive AD toxicity but have been hard to resolve in human tissue. Combining super-resolution and single-molecule functional imaging with kinetic modelling, the authors classify Aβ species by membrane-disrupting activity and identify two functionally distinct intermediate populations — present in recombinant Aβ42, an AD knock-in mouse and human AD brain — that form via separate aggregation routes and differ in neurotoxicity and abundance; both damage neurons and glia, but only one converts into plaques. Pharmacologically suppressing these intermediates in mice lowered plaque burden, attenuated astrogliosis and improved cognition, nominating intermediate-formation pathways as actionable targets. Causal/therapeutic evidence is mouse-only; the human data are observational.

**Model/system & primary method:** Recombinant Aβ42, AD knock-in mouse and human AD postmortem tissue; super-resolution plus single-molecule functional imaging with kinetic modelling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-79028-0

### APOE Christchurch limits Aβ-seeded tau and α-synuclein aggregation in a knock-in mouse
**Carlos M Soto-Faguás & Vivek K Unni | Cell Rep**

**Why it matters:** The APOE Christchurch (CC) variant has drawn attention for delaying autosomal-dominant AD, but its effect on synucleinopathies was unknown. A new APOE-CC knock-in mouse showed reduced Aβ and Aβ-seeded tau pathology after AD-tau injection into 5xFAD mice and — more novel — reduced formation and brain and peripheral-to-spinal-cord spread of α-synuclein aggregates, alongside increased direct APOE–α-syn binding affinity. Notably, CC did not change baseline Aβ or tau in single transgenic models without seeding, so the protection emerges under a seeding challenge. Suggests APOE-targeted mechanisms may generalise beyond amyloid to α-synuclein. Causal evidence is mouse-only.

**Model/system & primary method:** APOE Christchurch knock-in mouse; intracortical AD-tau seeding into 5xFAD, α-synuclein spreading assays, APOE–α-syn binding measurements.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118301

### Calpain cleavage of TBP disrupts neuronal AMOT–YAP1 signalling in SCA17
**Rana Dilara Incebacak Eltemur & Jonasz Jeremiasz Weber | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** In spinocerebellar ataxia type 17, polyglutamine-expanded TATA-box-binding protein (TBP) drives neurodegeneration by unclear mechanisms. The authors map a major calpain cleavage site at TBP residue T106; the resulting polyQ-containing N-terminal fragments mislocalise to the cytoplasm and aggregate, and both the N- and C-terminal fragments further activate calpain, implying a feed-forward proteolytic loop. Multi-omics across SCA17 cell lines, patient fibroblasts, induced neurons, and rat and zebrafish models converge on angiomotin (AMOT)–YAP1 signalling as a downstream effector. Extends TBP's role beyond transcription and nominates AMOT–YAP1 as a candidate pathway in polyglutamine and neurodevelopmental disease. Mechanistic, largely in vitro and model-organism evidence.

**Model/system & primary method:** SCA17 cell lines, patient fibroblasts and induced neurons, rat and zebrafish; proteomic cleavage-site mapping and multi-omics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-79055-x

### Ser65-phosphorylated ubiquitin marks a seeding-competent tau population in Alzheimer's disease
**Tingxiang Yan & Wolfdieter Springer | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** Markers that specifically tag seeding-competent tau are lacking. In human postmortem AD and control brain, a subset of tau is covalently modified by Ser65-phosphorylated ubiquitin (pS65-Ub), a PINK1-derived mitochondrial stress signal; pS65-Ub-associated tau is enriched in soluble, high-molecular-weight, oligomeric and hyperphosphorylated species and is elevated in AD. Soluble pS65-Ub-tau levels correlate with tau seeding activity across cases, pS65-Ub-isolated tau seeds in cell assays, and depleting it markedly reduces seeding from AD brain extracts — linking cellular stress signalling to tau propagation and offering a handle on the seed-competent pool. Human tissue is observational; the seeding link is associational plus depletion.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem AD and control brain; biochemical fractionation, pS65-Ub immunoisolation and cell-based tau seeding assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78418-8

### Single-cell machine learning anchors cell-type-specific Alzheimer's programs to genetic risk
**Adithya V Madduri & Chirag J Patel | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Univariate differential expression dominates AD single-cell studies but misses multivariate, disease-associated structure. TriSCOPE integrates multivariate predictive modelling, donor-aware differential expression and pseudotime across six AD snRNA-seq datasets (~4.2 million nuclei, 627 donors). It identifies cell-type-specific predictively expressed genes largely non-overlapping with differentially expressed genes; conditional GWAS–eQTL colocalization anchors 27 gene–cell-type pairs (e.g., ARL17B, JAZF1) to inherited AD risk without differential expression. Convergent programs span astrocytic stress (GFAP, MT2A), neuronal signalling (LINGO1, NRG1) and microglial/oligodendrocyte regulators (SPP1, APOD); clinical and neuropathological models diverge by cell type. A computational framework; signals replicate across cohorts but remain associational.

**Model/system & primary method:** Six human AD snRNA-seq datasets (~4.2M nuclei, 627 donors); multivariate ML, donor-aware DE, pseudotime, GWAS–eQTL colocalization.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77651-5

### A gait-based "age gap" predicts future cognitive impairment
**Victor Sungmin You & Farwa Ali | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In the Mayo Clinic Study of Aging (19,637 walk trials, 5,794 participants), a machine-learning gait model generated a "gait age gap" — predicted minus chronological age — driven mainly by stability measures. A larger gap was associated with worse cognitive and functional status and, longitudinally, higher risk of converting from normal cognition to impairment (HR 1.07 per gait-age-gap year), outperforming baseline gait velocity (HR 0.99). Positions quantitative gait as a scalable functional marker of brain aging and incipient decline. The cohort is single-site and predominantly Caucasian, and associations are observational and need replication in diverse populations.

**Model/system & primary method:** Population-based aging cohort; ML gait-age prediction model, longitudinal cognitive outcomes.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218766

### Modifiers of multiple sclerosis risk after infectious mononucleosis in a population cohort
**Dalia L Rotstein & Colleen J Maxwell | Neurology**

**Key findings:** Using Ontario health-claims data, the authors followed 671,410 people with infectious mononucleosis — a symptomatic EBV infection — and identified 2,470 (0.4%) who developed MS over a median 16.7 years, with a median interval from mononucleosis to MS of 11.4 years. Female sex (HR 1.9), mononucleosis at age 10–25 versus under 10 (HR 6.0) and mental-health-care use (HR 1.2) were associated with higher MS rates, while immigrant status was protective. Within an EBV-exposed population, this maps demographic and age-at-infection modifiers of MS risk, consistent with EBV as a necessary-but-insufficient exposure. Administrative-data cohort with ICD-coded diagnoses; residual confounding is possible.

**Model/system & primary method:** Retrospective population-based cohort (administrative data); cause-specific hazards modelling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218756

### Blood lipid-peroxidation and ferroptosis markers in Friedreich's ataxia
**Lidia Sarro & Caterina Mariotti | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** In 62 Friedreich's ataxia patients (48 adults, 14 children) versus 63 controls, plasma markers of oxidative stress and ferroptosis were dysregulated: elevated 4-HNE and oxidised glutathione (GSSG) with reduced GSH. Children with FRDA had lower GPX4 mRNA than adults; over 1-year follow-up plasma 4-HNE stayed stable while GPX4 mRNA rose only in adults, and GPX4 correlated with age, disease duration and ataxia severity. Provides preliminary longitudinal blood evidence of impaired GPX4-dependent ferroptosis defence early in FRDA. The pediatric subgroup is small and the evidence is biomarker-level, not causal.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human case-control with 1-year longitudinal follow-up; plasma 4-HNE/15-HETE, GSH/GSSG and NRF2-target mRNA.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70603

### Diagnostic criteria for autoimmune encephalitis perform well in suspected rapidly progressive dementia
**Yoav D Piura & Gregory S Day | Neurology**

**Key findings:** Across two tertiary centres, 261 patients with suspected rapidly progressive dementia were evaluated and 74 (28%) had autoimmune encephalitis. The Graus criteria for possible autoimmune encephalitis showed 89% sensitivity, 96% specificity and 94% accuracy against consensus diagnosis, supporting their use to triage autoantibody testing and empiric immunotherapy in this population — a setting where identifying treatable autoimmune dementia is high-stakes. Observational, tertiary-referral cohort (selection bias; predominantly non-Hispanic White).

**Model/system & primary method:** Observational two-centre RPD cohort; diagnostic-accuracy analysis of the Graus possible-AE criteria.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218783

### A HOX transcription-factor axis in Huntington's disease neurons, targetable independent of mutant huntingtin
**M Klukinov & J Mamet | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Applying a transcription-factor network-decoding metric to more than 100 postmortem HD and control neuronal transcriptomes, the authors report that the HOX TF family is uniquely quiescent in controls but functionally dominant in vulnerable HD neurons, already at Vonsattel grade 0; HOX dominance scales with disease grade, inherited CAG length and age at onset and targets >4,000 patterning, stress and senescence genes. In zQ175 mice, lowering mutant huntingtin only transiently reduced HOX dominance, whereas a HOX inhibitor (COR-H800) restored TF-network hierarchy toward wild type. Proposes a largely mutant-huntingtin-independent transcriptional axis and a candidate drug. Cautious interpretation: bioinformatic plus in vitro and mouse evidence, without in vivo efficacy or safety data here.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human HD postmortem neuronal transcriptomes, zQ175 mice and the HOX inhibitor COR-H800; transcription-factor network decoding.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.09.765359

### Presymptomatic lysosomal failure and dipeptide-repeat secretion in Drosophila C9orf72 ALS/FTD
**P H Smeele & T Vaccari | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In Drosophila expressing toxic C9orf72 repeats, lysosomal function was impaired presymptomatically while autophagosome formation was largely preserved. Valproic acid partially rescued neurodegenerative and locomotor phenotypes in a manner dependent on Mitf (which drives lysosomal biogenesis); genetically blocking residual lysosomal degradation worsened toxicity, whereas reducing secretory-autophagy genes suppressed it. Disabling lysosomes increased neuronal release of autophagy-associated proteins and dipeptide-repeat proteins, which were taken up by neighbouring neurons and glia — implicating secretion in spread. Cautious interpretation: fly and in vitro models, with relevance to human ALS/FTD inferred.

**Model/system & primary method:** Drosophila C9orf72 repeat models plus in vitro assays; pharmaco-genetic manipulation of the autophagy–lysosome pathway.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.09.765393

### How "organ age" is defined shapes its link to brain structure and neurodegenerative disease
**R Zendehdel & J Vogel | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Across 52,667 UK Biobank participants (5,078 with brain MRI), the authors compared three plasma-proteomic organ-aging metrics — supervised age-gap, unsupervised PC1 and phenotype-guided PLS — over 15 organ systems. The metric classes were related but not interchangeable, and supervised organ age-gaps showed the most consistent associations with brain morphometry and with incident ICD-coded neurodegenerative and vascular disease. The practical point: how an "organ age" is constructed determines what biology it captures, which matters for using proteomic aging clocks as brain-health readouts. Cautious interpretation: observational associations, not causal, in preprint.

**Model/system & primary method:** UK Biobank plasma proteomics (n=52,667) with brain MRI (n=5,078); three organ-level proteomic metrics and incident-disease analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.09.765339

### Plasma GFAP is higher in women, but sex does not clearly modify its link to cognitive decline
**C Reichert Plaska & Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In two community cohorts (MERI, N≈584; ADNI as replication), plasma GFAP — a marker of astrocytic reactivity — was consistently higher in females than males. Higher baseline GFAP tracked with a more AD-like biomarker profile (lower Aβ42/40, higher p-Tau and NfL) and with steeper longitudinal decline in global cognition and episodic memory. However, GFAP×sex×time interactions were not significant, so although women show higher astrocytic-reactivity markers, sex did not consistently modify the GFAP–cognition relationship — a result that tempers a simple "astrocyte reactivity drives female AD risk" narrative. Cautious interpretation: observational biomarker study, in preprint.

**Model/system & primary method:** Two human cohorts (discovery plus ADNI replication); plasma GFAP and AD biomarkers with longitudinal cognition.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.01.30.702902

### Serum brain-derived tau distinguishes traumatic brain injury from spinal cord injury
**R Shi & T K Karikari | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 342 neuro-intensive-care patients (285 traumatic brain injury, 57 spinal cord injury) plus 22 controls, baseline serum brain-derived tau (BD-tau) was markedly higher in TBI than SCI and discriminated the two with an AUC of 0.91. Serum BD-tau outperformed paired CSF BD-tau (AUC 0.95 vs 0.80) and established neurotrauma markers (NfL, GFAP, UCH-L1) for injury-type separation, and stayed elevated in TBI — but not SCI — for up to 144 hours. Positions BD-tau as a brain-specific blood marker to separate TBI from SCI in acute and mixed-trauma settings. Cautious interpretation: observational multi-cohort study, in preprint.

**Model/system & primary method:** Multi-cohort human neuro-ICU study; ultrasensitive serum and CSF BD-tau versus standard blood biomarkers.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.08.26365143
