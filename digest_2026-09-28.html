1Deletion or immunotherapeutic blockade of FcγRIIb (CD32b) impairs α-Syn propagation in vivo

Acta Neuropathologica  James M Hennegan & Jessica L Teeling
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
The inhibitory Fc receptor FcγRIIb has been implicated in α-synuclein uptake in vitro, but its role in spreading pathology in the intact brain was unproven. Here, genetic ablation of murine FcγRII reduced phosphorylated α-synuclein burden and its anatomical distribution 90 days after intracranial preformed-fibril (PFF) injection, with less microglial reactivity, preserved nigrostriatal dopaminergic integrity, and protection from motor and exploratory deficits. Crucially, monoclonal-antibody blockade of human FcγRIIb in transgenic mice cut acute-phase pα-Syn accumulation, nominating a receptor-targeted, mechanistically distinct disease-modifying route for Parkinson's disease and related synucleinopathies. Evidence is mouse-only and causal at the circuit level; human relevance rests on the transgenic-receptor model.
Model & method
Mouse α-Syn PFF intracranial seeding model; FcγRII knockout and anti-hFcγRIIb monoclonal antibody in humanised transgenic mice; immunohistochemistry and behaviour.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03082-7

2Dynamic [18F]PI-2620 recording facilitates prediction of β-amyloid positivity and simultaneous staging of tau and neurodegeneration

Molecular Neurodegeneration  Johannes Gnörich & Matthias Brendel
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
A/T/N classification normally needs separate amyloid-PET, tau-PET and FDG-PET. Using 60-minute dynamic tau-PET in 129 patients with tauopathies plus controls, kinetic modelling extracted three parameters from one acquisition: tracer efflux K2a (amyloid status), DVR (regional tau) and relative perfusion R1 (neurodegeneration). K2a predicted β-amyloid positivity with an area under the curve of 0.99 (positive predictive value 91.5%, negative predictive value 95.1%), holding up in an independent cohort of 97 (AUC 0.98), and remained sensitive even where visual tau read-outs were negative. This "one-stop-shop" acquisition could streamline diagnostic workflows and reduce radiation exposure, though it requires dynamic scanning and specialist kinetic analysis.
Model & method
Human tauopathy patients (3/4-repeat and 4-repeat) and controls; dynamic [18F]PI-2620 tau-PET with kinetic modelling, validated against amyloid-PET, FDG-PET, MRI and CSF.
https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00997-3

3Activation of TREM2 via single-domain antibodies improves synaptic and cognitive function in 5×FAD mice

Cell Reports  Xiaoxiao Yu & Wei Xie
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
TREM2 loss-of-function raises Alzheimer's risk, and TREM2-agonist antibodies are in development, but their downstream mechanisms are unclear. Two TREM2-activating single-domain antibodies (nanobodies), 10C11 and 10E12, delivered by ventral hippocampal injection into early-stage 5×FAD mice activated microglia and rescued cognitive deficits by dissociable routes: 10E12 reduced Aβ plaques and preserved synaptic function, whereas 10C11 protected synapses without changing plaque burden. Both lowered complement C1q, indicating TREM2 activation acts on synaptic and plaque pathology through partly independent mechanisms. Findings are from a single AD mouse model with direct intracerebral dosing, so translational route and generality remain open.
Model & method
5×FAD transgenic mice; two TREM2-activating single-domain antibodies via ventral hippocampal injection; behaviour, synaptic and plaque histology, C1q quantification.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118063

4Integrative synaptosome multi-omics reveals disrupted synapse organization and localized cryptic transcripts in C9ORF72-frontotemporal dementia

Acta Neuropathologica  Ashton M Spillman & Rita Sattler
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Synaptic dysfunction and TDP-43 pathology are central to C9ORF72-FTD/ALS, but synapse-specific molecular changes were undefined. Multi-omic profiling of synaptosomes from postmortem frontal cortex and iPSC-derived cortical neurons found 1,324 differentially abundant proteins enriched for synaptic vesicle transport and synapse organization, and 2,835 differentially expressed genes, with iPSC neurons recapitulating patient pathways and showing progressive network-activity decline. Notably, TDP-43-associated cryptic-exon transcripts (including KALRN and STMN2) localised to synaptic compartments — evidence that aberrantly spliced RNAs reach the synapse, a previously unrecognised feature of TDP-43 proteinopathy. Protein–RNA divergence suggests disrupted post-transcriptional regulation at diseased synapses.
Model & method
Human postmortem frontal cortex synaptosomes and C9-FTD iPSC-cortical neurons; synaptosomal proteomics and RNA-seq, multi-electrode network recording.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03084-5

5Lewy Body Disease Across Aging: Clinical and Neuropathological Correlates in a Population Brain Bank

Movement Disorders  Vitor Ribeiro Paes & Lea Tenenholz Grinberg
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
In 2,480 autopsied participants from a population-based São Paulo cohort enriched for younger individuals, Lewy body disease frequency rose steeply with age (0.7% under 50 to 20% at 90+), almost always in a mixed-pathology context — advanced Braak-PD stage co-occurred with greater Alzheimer's pathology, TDP-43 and cerebral amyloid angiopathy. In adjusted models, Braak-PD V–VI was associated with worse cognition and with hallucinations (OR 3.87) and delusions (OR 2.79), whereas total parkinsonism score was not independently associated; nocturnal disturbances appeared already at Braak-PD I–II. The data argue that LBD is expressed more through cognition and psychosis than caregiver-reported motor burden, refining real-world biomarker interpretation. Clinical data are retrospective informant interviews, a cross-sectional design constraint.
Model & method
Human population-based brain bank (n=2,480); Braak-PD staging with copathology assessment and structured postmortem informant interviews (CDR-SB, Tanner, NPI).
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70515

Hippocampal representations of alternative possibilities are flexibly generated to meet cognitive demands

Neuron  Alison E Comrie & Loren M Frank
Key findings
Recording hippocampal activity in rats foraging a complex patch environment with changing reward probabilities, the authors found neural representations of multiple alternative locations along paths ahead and behind the animal, including distant alternative patches. These were modulated by cognitive demand: alternative paths were represented in proportion to their evolving relative value and predicted upcoming decisions, whereas distant alternatives were most prevalent during value updating. The brain thus selectively generates the alternatives relevant to current needs rather than representing all possibilities uniformly.
Model & method
Rat hippocampal electrophysiology during naturalistic patch-foraging navigation.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.023

A basolateral amygdala to dorsolateral striatum projection modulates stimulus-evoked motor behavior

Neuron  Zachary B Hobel & Joshua L Plotkin
Key findings
A direct BLA→dorsolateral striatum (DLS) projection, arising from neurons distinct from those targeting the dorsomedial striatum, preferentially innervated distal dendrites of striatal projection neurons — positioning it to modulate heterosynaptic integration. Repeated optogenetic activation of these inputs potentiated stimulus-evoked grooming and induced heterosynaptic plasticity, and the projection was pathologically strengthened in compulsively grooming Sapap3-null mice. The work identifies a limbic-to-sensorimotor route through which emotionally salient input shapes motor output, with relevance to compulsive behaviour.
Model & method
Mice; optogenetics, ex vivo electrophysiology, circuit tracing, Sapap3-null compulsive-grooming model.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.025

A hypothalamic neuronal population coordinates metabolic adaptations to cold

Neuron  Hailan Liu & Yong Xu
Key findings
Cold-responsive neurons in the dorsal posterior periventricular hypothalamus coordinate feeding, body temperature and brown-fat thermogenesis. Activating GABAergic or cold-activated neurons increased food intake, temperature and thermogenesis, while inhibition blunted cold-induced hyperphagia and thermogenesis. snRNA-seq identified Lef1 marking cold-activated neurons; Lef1 positively regulated Kcnk2 (TREK-1), and cold lowered Kcnk2 expression and potassium currents. Pharmacological or genetic Kcnk2 inhibition increased feeding and temperature, whereas gain-of-function blunted cold responses — defining a Lef1–Kcnk2 axis linking ambient temperature to neuronal activity and systemic metabolism.
Model & method
Mice; chemogenetic/optogenetic manipulation, single-nucleus RNA-seq, Kcnk2 gain- and loss-of-function.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.024

Identity-by-descent to resolve reduced penetrance and advance gene discovery in neurodegenerative disease

Brain  Lyndal Henden & SALSA-SGC consortium
Key findings
Genome-wide identity-by-descent (IBD) analysis of 3,524 individuals across the ALS–FTD spectrum uncovered cryptic relatedness — 793 related pairs, 31.5% previously unrecognised — and showed that 5.5% of "sporadic" ALS and 15.9% of "sporadic" FTD were genetically related to another affected person, quantifying how reduced penetrance masks heritable disease. IBD reconstructed cross-continent founder networks for SOD1 and C9orf72 and built 73 new pedigrees from apparently unrelated probands; in one extended five-person pedigree it narrowed a candidate locus to 13q21.33 with three shared rare variants supported by linkage. A framework for gene discovery in otherwise underpowered families and clearer inherited-risk counselling.
Model & method
Human ALS/FTD cohort (n=3,524, European ancestry); genome-wide IBD inference, pedigree reconstruction, linkage and variant filtering.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag326

DDX39A unwinds viral RNA G-quadruplexes to limit α-Synuclein amyloidogenesis

Cell Reports  Aanchal Jain & Swasti Raychaudhuri
Key findings
RNA G-quadruplexes (rG4s) promote α-synuclein aggregation. The DEAD-box helicase DDX39A bound α-synuclein in an rG4-dependent manner and decelerated its amyloidogenesis; its phase separation with α-synuclein in turn boosted the helicase's rG4-unwinding activity. Viral infection transiently raised cytoplasmic rG4s, and introducing genomic rG4 sequences from H1N1 influenza or SARS-CoV-2 accelerated intracellular aggregation, while DDX39A overexpression alleviated aggregation in SARS-CoV-2-infected primary neurons. The results propose a viral contribution to Parkinson's-relevant aggregation via disruption of an RNA–protein equilibrium; evidence is cell-based and mechanistic.
Model & method
In vitro biochemistry, cultured cells and virus-infected primary neurons; α-synuclein aggregation assays with viral rG4 sequences.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118060

Deep learning aging marker from retinal images unveils sex-specific clinical and genetic signatures

Nature Communications  Olga Trofimova & Sven Bergmann
Key findings
Fine-tuning a retinal foundation model on 71,343 UK Biobank participants predicted chronological age from fundus images (mean absolute error 2.85 years). The resulting "retinal age gap" was associated with cognitive performance, all-cause mortality, dementia, cancer and incident cardiovascular disease, and GWAS implicated longevity, metabolism and neurodegeneration genes. Sex-stratified models showed divergent biology — stronger metabolic links in males, greater retinal-vascular involvement in females — and postmenopausal females showed higher age-gap values, positioning the retinal age gap as a non-invasive, sex-specific marker of systemic and brain aging. Associations are cross-sectional and correlational.
Model & method
Human UK Biobank retinal fundus images (n=71,343); fine-tuned RETFound deep-learning model, phenome- and genome-wide association.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77102-1

Multimodal Magnetic Resonance Imaging and Machine Learning Uncovers Distinct Progression Patterns in Friedreich Ataxia

Movement Disorders  Susmita Saha & Ian H Harding
Key findings
Longitudinal structural and diffusion MRI from 54 Friedreich ataxia patients and 57 controls, clustered by Gaussian mixture models, revealed three reproducible progression patterns: predominantly microstructural degeneration, predominantly macrostructural atrophy, and minimal measurable progression (the last enriched for controls). GAA1 repeat length was the only variable consistently associated with cluster membership, with larger expansions in the microstructure-dominant group, whereas disease duration and clinical progression rates did not distinguish patterns. Imaging captures heterogeneity invisible to conventional clinical measures, offering a data-driven basis for participant stratification in trials.
Model & method
Human FRDA cohort; longitudinal multimodal MRI, Gaussian mixture clustering and random-forest modelling.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70551

Navigation strategies predict spatial memory and hippocampal recruitment

Cell Reports  E Zita Patai & Nikolai Axmacher
Key findings
Across epilepsy patients with intracranial recordings and two fMRI groups — young adults at genetic risk for Alzheimer's (APOE-ε4 carriers) and controls — virtual-navigation trajectories were quantified into strategy metrics (straightness, boundary deviation, path overlap). Strategy use varied within individuals and shifted across the task, and its adaptivity was group-dependent. Hippocampal BOLD activity and theta power were generally inversely related, but hippocampal recruitment was both group- and strategy-specific, revealing disease- and risk-linked differences in navigation and compensatory neural dynamics. Cross-sectional, modest samples across heterogeneous recording modalities.
Model & method
Humans; virtual navigation with intracranial electrophysiology and fMRI, including APOE-ε4 carriers.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117971

Neurodegeneration-inducing macromolecules exit the brain via nanovascular conduits formed by reticular fibroblasts

bioRxiv  Nortley, R. & Attwell, D.
Key findings
In mice and humans, the authors report ~2 µm conduits — formed by reticular fibroblasts expressing lymphatic markers (podoplanin, VEGFR3, Prox1) and collagen I/VI — that remove fluorescently labelled tau and Aβ from the brain. The conduits form an organised mesh within penetrating-arteriole and pial-artery walls and around ascending venules and deep veins, spanning the CSF space and wrapping cranial nerves. Parenchymal tau drained at similar rates via arteriolar and venular conduits, which the authors argue is inconsistent with preferential glymphatic clearance; in AD-model mice Aβ was seen traversing these lymph-node-like structures. If confirmed, this reframes brain waste clearance anatomy and links slowed transfer to cerebral amyloid angiopathy — a bold claim resting on tracer imaging that will need independent replication.
Model & method
Mouse and human tissue; fluorescent tau/Aβ tracer imaging and marker characterisation of vascular-associated conduits.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.21.752863

Reduced Microtubule Polyglutamylation Perturbs Neuronal Function and Accelerates Tau-Induced Neurodegeneration

bioRxiv  Chakraborty, S. & Magiera, M. M.
Key findings
Because excess microtubule polyglutamylation causes early-onset neurodegeneration, the authors tested the opposite — mouse models with reduced polyglutamylation. These mice developed late-onset atrophy of the olfactory bulb and frontal cortex, progressively reduced dendritic spine density, and deficits in olfaction, memory and cognitive control. Crossing them onto a human P301S tau tauopathy model accelerated premature death, indicating that this microtubule post-translational modification must be kept within a physiological window and that its reduction worsens tau-linked degeneration. Mouse genetic evidence; mechanism of the tau interaction is not fully dissected (v2 preprint).
Model & method
Mouse genetic models with reduced polyglutamylation, crossed to P301S tau transgenics; behaviour and neuropathology.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.31.742027

Restoring Aquaporin-4 alleviates molecular and motor phenotypes in zQ175 mouse model of Huntington disease

bioRxiv  Liu, H. & Duan, W.
Key findings
Premanifest zQ175 Huntington's mice received a single retro-orbital AAV9-PHP.eB delivering the AQP4-M1 isoform at 2 months. This restored perivascular AQP4 polarisation in striatum and cortex, increased glymphatic tracer influx and drainage to deep cervical lymph nodes, and by 12 months reduced CSF neurofilament light and total tau, mutant huntingtin aggregate number/size, and striatal microgliosis/astrogliosis, while preserving DARPP32 and attenuating striatal atrophy on MRI. Motor benefit reached significance in females but not males. A proof-of-concept that impaired glymphatic clearance is a modifiable component of HD pathology; single model, sex-limited behavioural effect.
Model & method
Heterozygous zQ175 knock-in mice; AAV9-PHP.eB AQP4 gene delivery, glymphatic tracer imaging, 11.7T MRI, CSF biomarkers and behaviour.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.20.753038

An evolutionarily conserved interferon-responsive oligodendroglial state emerges during white matter aging

bioRxiv  Serneels, P.-J. & Moons, L.
Key findings
Establishing the killifish optic nerve as a translational white-matter aging model, integrated transcriptomic, histological and ultrastructural analyses recapitulated mammalian hallmarks — oligodendrocyte progenitor depletion, metabolic dysfunction and reduced myelin thickness. Aging was accompanied by an interferon-responsive oligodendroglial state, marked by interferon-response and antigen-presentation programs resembling pathology-associated oligodendroglia seen in murine CNS-disease models. Because this state emerged before overt neurodegeneration, the authors propose it as a candidate cellular link between white-matter aging and neurodegenerative disease. Cross-species inference from a non-mammalian model; associative.
Model & method
Killifish optic nerve aging model; transcriptomics, histology and electron microscopy with cross-species comparison.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.23.753731

Diagnostic accuracy of remote self-administered digital cognitive assessment by clinical diagnosis and Alzheimer's disease PET imaging biomarkers

medRxiv  Stricker, N. H. & Petersen, R. C.
Key findings
In 2,131 cognitively unimpaired and 118 cognitively impaired participants, the remote self-administered Mayo Test Drive (MTD) battery discriminated unimpaired from MCI/dementia with AUC 0.89, rising to 0.95 when contrasting amyloid-negative unimpaired versus amyloid-positive MCI/dementia. For biomarker-defined groups (A−T− vs A+T+) MTD reached AUC 0.76, outperforming an estimated MMSE and matching an in-person composite; combining raw and normative cutoffs and a two-stage interpretation improved sensitivity. Unsupervised, at-home cognitive testing can approximate in-person composites for detecting impairment and biomarker-defined disease, supporting scalable screening — though the cohort is largely population-based and self-selected.
Model & method
Human observational cohorts (n≈2,249; amyloid/tau-PET in 1,224); remote self-administered digital cognitive battery vs consensus diagnosis and PET biomarkers.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.20.26362296
