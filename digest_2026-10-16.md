### An aging-sensitive secreted phospholipase confers neuroprotection and cognitive resilience
**Cinzia Vicidomini & Amar Sahay | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** A screen for factors induced by dentate granule cell synapse loss identified Pla2g2f, a secreted phospholipase that rises in aging dentate granule cells and whose expression tracks cognitive resilience in humans. Dentate-specific deletion worsened age-associated synapse loss, inflammatory microglia, reactive astrogliosis, impaired neurogenesis and lipid-droplet accumulation with memory decline, whereas AAV-mediated boosting prevented these changes and protected memory — a gain-of-function rescue rather than a correlate. Catalytic-site mutagenesis abolished the benefit, and neuronal PLA2G2F lowered lipid-droplet and cholesterol-ester load in human iPSC-derived microglia, positioning it as a neuron-to-glia lipostatic signal. Causal evidence is mouse; the human link is associative.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse dentate gyrus genetic screen with AAV gain- and loss-of-function, human cognitive-resilience association, and human iPSC-derived microglia.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02465-z

### Genome-wide CRISPR screen reveals that Wnt signaling regulates lipid accumulation in APOE4 oligodendrocytes
**Leyla Anne Akay & Li-Huei Tsai | Cell Stem Cell**

**Why it matters:** APOE4 drives lipid-droplet accumulation in oligodendrocytes and impairs myelination; a genome-wide CRISPR screen with ATAC-seq in human iPSC-derived oligodendrocytes pinpointed reduced Wnt signaling and overactive GSK3B as regulators. Genetic or pharmacological GSK3B inhibition cleared lipid droplets and restored myelination in 3D iPSC organoids, and pharmacological GSK3B inhibition reduced droplets and improved myelination in APOE4;PS19 tau mice. The work nominates GSK3B as a tractable target for APOE4-associated Alzheimer's acting through oligodendrocyte lipid metabolism rather than neurons, with convergent human-iPSC and mouse causal evidence.

**Model/system & primary method:** Genome-wide CRISPR screen and ATAC-seq in iPSC-derived oligodendrocytes, iPSC brain organoids, and APOE4;PS19 mice.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2026.09.012

### Parvalbumin cell state dynamics regulate gamma oscillations and are disrupted in Alzheimer's disease
**Keran Ma & Jorge J Palop | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** Simultaneous calcium imaging and electrocorticography in awake mice showed that parvalbumin interneurons generate gamma not as a uniformly synchronized pool but through sequential recruitment, transient inactivation and frequent state transitions. In J20 Alzheimer's mice these transitions became dysregulated, uncoupling parvalbumin activity from gamma and producing network hyperexcitability; raising interneuron excitability via Nav1.1 expression restored parvalbumin dynamics, gamma and network stability. The study reframes gamma generation around dynamic cell states and identifies their breakdown as a circuit mechanism of Alzheimer's network dysfunction. Mechanistic, mouse only.

**Model/system & primary method:** Awake-mouse dual calcium imaging with electrocorticography, J20 model, Nav1.1 rescue.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.025

### An integrative transcriptomic atlas of ALS and aging skeletal muscle defines shared and distinct molecular programs of degeneration
**Mercedes Grima-Terrén & Pura Muñoz-Cánoves | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Why it matters:** A single-nucleus atlas of SOD1-G93A and lifespan wild-type muscle mapped shared versus condition-specific myonuclear states. Early ALS muscle already carried molecular hallmarks of aged muscle at the myofiber level — evidence of premature aging that reinforces aging as an ALS risk factor — while synaptic myonuclei at the neuromuscular junction showed disease-responsive programs. At advanced stages both conditions converged on degenerative pathways in fast glycolytic fibers, yet an ALS-enriched myonuclear subpopulation remained uniquely regulated. The data are transcriptomic and descriptive, complemented by structural and functional analyses in the mouse model.

**Model/system & primary method:** Single-nucleus RNA-seq atlas of SOD1-G93A ALS and wild-type mouse muscle across the lifespan, with structural and functional validation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2613546123

### Interrogating DNA methylation associated with Lewy body pathology in a cross brain-region and multi-cohort study
**Joshua Harvey & Katie Lunnon | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** A methylation meta-analysis across cortical regions (1,205 samples, 832 donors) identified 24 FDR-significant loci tracking Lewy body burden, with UBASH3B, PTAFR and an intergenic locus passing genome-wide significance and ontology enrichment for synaptic alterations. Cross-pathology comparison revealed a shared epigenetic profile across Lewy body, Alzheimer's and Huntington's disease while top loci retained disease specificity, and integration with GWAS summary statistics showed enrichment in the SNCA region, linking genetic risk, methylation QTLs and alternative SNCA transcript isoforms. Observational human post-mortem epigenetics.

**Model/system & primary method:** Cross-region cortical DNA-methylation meta-analysis of human post-mortem tissue with GWAS and mQTL integration.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77009-x

### 5-HT2A receptors in layer 5 pyramidal neurons mediate the neuroplastic but not the hallucinogenic effects of psilocybin
**Jacob J Baker & Yi Zuo | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Psilocybin promoted synapse formation and maturation while accelerating elimination of pre-existing synapses. Cell-type-specific manipulation showed that 5-HT2AR signaling in cortical layer 5 pyramidal neurons is necessary and sufficient for this synaptic remodeling but dispensable for the head-twitch response, a rodent proxy of hallucination — dissociating the plasticity-promoting from the hallucinogenic action at a defined cellular locus.

**Model/system & primary method:** In vivo two-photon dendritic imaging with cell-type-specific 5-HT2AR manipulation in mice.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02490-y

### A half-center oscillator regulates sleep pressure
**Peter S Hasenhuetl & Gero Miesenböck | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Drosophila dorsal fan-shaped body neurons produce slow-wave activity causally linked to sleep, arising from bistable cells that oscillate between hyperpolarized DOWN and depolarized UP states through interhemispheric competition of two half-centers coupled by inhibitory glutamatergic synapses. Interfering with their glutamate release disrupted slow-wave activity and both baseline and rebound sleep, while optogenetic replay of slow-wave activity induced sleep; the oscillation's power scaled with sleep need via history-dependent excitability and homeostatic synaptic depression.

**Model/system & primary method:** Drosophila electrophysiology and optogenetics of the dorsal fan-shaped body circuit.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02431-9

**Adam M R Groh & Jo Anne Stratton | Nat Neurosci**

### Recovery sleep induces analgesia via sleep ensembles of the zona incerta acting through distinct neural circuits
**Haiyan Sun & Shuming An | Neuron**

**Key findings:** Sleep-activated zona incerta ensembles drove both NREM sleep and analgesia, and chronic pain suppressed their activity. Activation increased NREM sleep and reduced pain sensitivity while inhibition reversed both effects. Neurotensin was enriched and required, and the outputs dissociated anatomically: zona incerta projections to the lateral hypothalamus regulated NREM sleep without affecting nociception, whereas projections to the posterior thalamic nucleus mediated analgesia without affecting sleep.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse optrode recording with chemo- and optogenetics, neurotensin knockdown, and projection-specific manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.026

### A brainstem module for backward locomotion reveals direction-selective premotor circuits
**Zhixiang Liu & Minmin Luo | Neuron**

**Key findings:** The pontine backward locomotion center was identified as a brainstem node whose glutamatergic neurons are preferentially recruited during backward walking; their activation elicited naturalistic backward gait and their inhibition suppressed it. The behavior was relayed through descending projections to the paramedian reticular nucleus and onward to the spinal cord, and the center sits within an anteroposteriorly organized set of brainstem regions associated with forward locomotion, turning and arrest.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse optogenetics, ablation and circuit tracing of pontine and reticular premotor neurons.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.027

### An opioid efficacy switch for reversible optical control of analgesia
**Luca Posa & Joshua Levitz | Cell**

**Key findings:** AM-3, a reversibly photoswitchable morphinan agonist, interconverts between low- (trans) and high-efficacy (cis) states at the mu-opioid receptor under light, with cryo-structures revealing distinct binding modes and G-protein dynamics per state. In mice, AM-3 gave reversible, repeatable optical control of antinociception across pain models with a reduced side-effect profile, attributed to limited brain accumulation and local activation — a tool for isolating therapeutic from adverse opioid effects.

**Model/system & primary method:** Photopharmacology with structural analysis of the mu-opioid receptor and mouse pain models.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.09.042

### Cellular scaling laws in the mammalian brain
**Fei Chen & Evan Z Macosko | Cell**

**Key findings:** This synthesis of whole-brain cell atlases proposes that the greatest neuronal type diversity resides in deep subcortical structures rather than the neuron-rich cortex and cerebellum, reflecting a division between a "learning subsystem" (cortex, striatum, cerebellum) that scales by replicating repetitive modules and a "steering subsystem" (hypothalamus, pallidum, brainstem) that scales by diversifying bespoke cell types encoding innate drives. The framework offers a unified lens on brain architecture and disease etiology. Conceptual review.

**Model/system & primary method:** Synthesis of whole-brain single-cell atlases (perspective).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.044

### Dendritic computation for abstract rule-based flexible decision-making
**Yuan Zhang & Ning-Long Xu | Science**

**Key findings:** During a rule-based flexible decision task, apical dendrites of auditory cortex layer 5 extratelencephalic neurons preferentially encoded the inferred abstract rule while somata preferentially represented sensory stimuli, with choice signals in both compartments. Optogenetic dendritic inhibition impaired rule-dependent somatic choice coding, indicating that within-neuron dendrite-to-soma integration supports flexible cognitive behavior.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse in vivo two-photon imaging and optogenetics in auditory cortex.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adz5323

### Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder polygenic risk score and incidence of cognitive impairment in older adults
**Douglas Teixeira Leffa & Tharick A Pascoal | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In 7,761 cognitively unimpaired older adults with whole-genome sequencing across eight ADSP cohorts, a higher ADHD polygenic risk score predicted incident MCI or Alzheimer's dementia (10-year cumulative incidence 33.8% in the top versus 23.4% in the bottom tertile), surviving adjustment for education, APOE ε4 status and vascular risk. Polygenic scores for other major psychiatric disorders were not associated, and in a biomarker subsample the association was not modified by baseline amyloid status. Associative human-genetics finding.

**Model/system & primary method:** Prospective multicohort polygenic-risk analysis with Cox models.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71770

### GCH1 p.Ser80Asn confers risk for Parkinson's disease in East Asian populations
**Yi Wen Tay & Ai Huey Tan | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** A GCH1 p.Ser80Asn variant segregating in a Malaysian Chinese Parkinson's family was enriched in East Asian cases (GP2 plus East Asian meta-analysis; odds ratio 5.1, 95% CI 2.3–10.7) but undetected in other ancestries, with carriers sharing a ~330-kb haplotype. Affected carriers had a mean onset of 56 years with additional dystonia and rare dementia, defining a rare, ancestry-specific Parkinson's risk variant.

**Model/system & primary method:** Whole-genome sequencing of a family with multi-ancestry GP2 and East Asian case-control meta-analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70530

### Predictors of antiamyloid medication utilization and adherence among people with Alzheimer disease and mild cognitive impairment
**Evan Lee Reynolds & Brian C Callaghan | Neurology**

**Key findings:** Across 1,198,362 patients with MCI or dementia in the Vizient database, only 2,427 (0.2%) were prescribed an amyloid monoclonal antibody (aducanumab, lecanemab or donanemab). ### The right temperature at the right time: a framework for understanding thermoregulatory pathophysiology in Alzheimer's disease
**Esther Blessing | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** This review argues that neuromodulatory subcortical thermoregulatory neurons, which hold core and brain temperature within a narrow diurnal range, are selectively vulnerable in Alzheimer's because their temperature-sensitive cellular pathways overlap with tau-aggregation risk pathways. It marshals human evidence of tau pathology in thermoregulatory neurons in early disease and abnormal body-temperature rhythms in advanced disease, alongside rodent evidence that diurnal temperature change modulates tau pathways. Conceptual framework, not primary data.

**Model/system & primary method:** Narrative review integrating human and rodent findings.

**Boyle, E. A. & Mollenhauer, B. | medRxiv**

**Zhao, B. & Li, J. | bioRxiv**
