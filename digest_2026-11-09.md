### Rhythm-guided neuromodulation instructs human brain organoid grafts to reconstruct layer-4 circuitry and restore vision
**Zhe-Han Chang & Xiao-Hong Li | Cell Stem Cell**

**Why it matters:** Human brain organoid (hBO) grafts normally integrate into host cortex in a disorganised way, limiting repair. Here, closed-loop stimulation steered by endogenous narrowband gamma (NBG) rhythms instructed grafts in injured adult mouse visual cortex to acquire layer-4–appropriate molecular and functional identity and to wire bidirectionally into the host visual circuit. Graft neurons developed direction selectivity and phase-locked their spiking to host NBG oscillations, and host visual acuity and contrast sensitivity were significantly restored. The result reframes grafting from passive cell replacement toward activity-instructed circuit assembly, with causal evidence confined so far to the injured adult mouse visual cortex.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human brain organoid grafts in lesioned adult mouse visual cortex; neural-interface NBG-rhythmic stimulation, in vivo electrophysiology with phase-locking analysis, and visually guided behaviour.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2026.10.011

### CD8⁺ T cells drive RIPK1-dependent brain endothelial death in cerebral malaria
**Alexandros Hadjilaou & Manuel A. Friese | Sci Adv**

**Why it matters:** Blood–brain-barrier breakdown in cerebral malaria is immune-mediated, but the link from cytotoxic immunity to endothelial injury was unclear. The authors define a CD8⁺ T-cell–dependent, RIPK1-centred death programme in brain endothelial cells: RIPK1 phosphorylation was elevated in brain endothelium in human cerebral malaria, and in mice, brain-endothelial-specific genetic inactivation or systemic pharmacological RIPK1 kinase inhibition prolonged survival without altering parasitemia. Combined RIPK1 and caspase inhibition protected antigen cross-presenting endothelial cells in vitro. The work nominates RIPK1 — already pursued as a neurodegeneration target — for neurovascular protection, with causal mouse evidence corroborated by human pathology.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse experimental cerebral malaria with cross-species human pathology; endothelial-specific genetics, pharmacological RIPK1 inhibition, MRI, tissue-cleared 3D imaging, and endothelial translatomics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aej4381

### Optimised amyloid-PET and MMSE cutoffs stratify 3-year progression from MCI to AD dementia
**Rosita Shishegar & ADNI/AIBL consortia | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** In 454 amyloid-positive (>25 Centiloid) MCI participants pooled from AIBL and ADNI, Youden-optimised thresholds of Aβ-PET >71 Centiloid (HR 2.67; 36.7% 3-year progression) and MMSE <27 (HR 2.27; 36.8%) each predicted progression to CDR-Global 1.0 AD dementia. Combining high amyloid with low MMSE conferred a 6.39-fold risk and 47.8% 3-year progression, with the steepest decline on CDR-SoB and the PACC composite. The study supplies concrete, cross-cohort prognostic cutpoints usable for trial enrichment and clinical counselling, though it is observational and restricted to amyloid-positive MCI.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human (AIBL + ADNI, follow-up to 7 years); Aβ-PET Centiloid and MMSE thresholds, adjusted Cox models and linear mixed models.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71892

### Probable LATE declines across cognitive domains more slowly than AD but faster than normal aging
**Charlotte Moss et al. (ADNI) | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** Among 108 ADNI participants classified by cognitive status, amyloid/tau biomarkers and hippocampal volume, probable limbic-predominant age-related TDP-43 encephalopathy (LATE) declined faster than cognitively normal controls but generally more slowly than AD across domains — for example, ADAS-Cog annual change of 0.81 (probable LATE) versus 2.08 (AD), and Trail Making Test B change of 5.83 versus 10.98. The separable trajectory supports clinical identification of probable LATE and distinct modelling in trial design. Classification is biomarker/imaging-based rather than autopsy-confirmed in life, and the sample is modest.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human (ADNI); biomarker- and imaging-based group assignment with prospective multidomain cognitive trajectories.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71954

## NEURODEGENERATION & AGING — TOP 5

### Coronary and cardiac pathology are pervasive in cerebral small vessel disease and post-stroke dementia
**Yoshiki Hase & Raj N. Kalaria | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In a clinicopathological study of 75 stroke survivors (CogFAST cohort), every subject had occult or obstructive coronary artery disease with >50% stenosis in at least one main coronary artery, and cardiac pathology was highly prevalent (~80% with fibrosis, disarray or myocyte damage; 62% with myocardial infarcts). Coronary stenosis tracked with internal carotid stenosis but was not associated with pathological measures of cerebral small vessel disease or neurodegenerative burden per se, pointing to shared large-vessel atherosclerosis rather than a direct coronary–parenchymal link. The cohort is small, elderly and autopsy-based.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem clinicopathology of coronary arteries, heart and brain in post-stroke dementia versus non-demented stroke survivors.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71979

### Age-linked biological context shapes whether adaptive immunity protects or harms in Alzheimer's disease
**Sophie Shirenova & Eitan Okun | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** This review synthesises human, genetic, mouse and clinical-trial evidence that adaptive immunity in AD is context-dependent rather than uniformly protective or pathogenic. Human studies show T-cell accumulation in disease-relevant brain compartments, peripheral B-cell remodelling, endogenous anti-amyloid-β antibodies and cytokine dysregulation, while genetic signals implicate antigen presentation, lymphocyte regulation and immune checkpoints. The authors argue that age biases responses from earlier regulatory states toward later maladaptive inflammation, offering a framework for stage-aware immunomodulation. As a review, it integrates rather than tests mechanism.

**Model/system & primary method:** Narrative synthesis across human studies, genetics, mouse models and trials.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71978

### A "push–pull" framework links amyloid-driven endolysosomal failure to tau spread in Alzheimer's disease
**Matthew Townsend | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** This hypothesis review proposes that amyloid-β impairs neuronal and microglial endolysosomal activity, collapsing proteostasis and reducing tau degradation so that neurons begin actively "pushing" aggregated tau into interstitial space and onto synaptically connected neurons (a rapidly progressing state), while cortical amyloid distribution "pulls" spreading tau outward from inferior temporal cortex. In microglia, amyloid-induced endolysosomal impairment is proposed to mimic viral infection and drive chronic interferon-type inflammation. The framework is conceptual and makes testable predictions about therapeutic sequencing rather than presenting new data.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mechanistic synthesis/hypothesis integrating four decades of AD findings.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71721

### Health-utility values fall steeply across multiple system atrophy stages
**Igor Kuchin et al. | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** Using EQ-5D data from the European MSA Study Group cohort and Bayesian modelling, stage-specific EQ-5D-5L health-utility values declined from 0.672 (95% CrI 0.628–0.719) at UMSARS IV Stage 1 to 0.262 (0.158–0.364) at Stage 4, quantifying the severe and progressive quality-of-life burden of this synucleinopathy. The estimates fill a gap for health-economic evaluation and support health-related quality of life as a trial endpoint. Values derive from mapping EQ-5D-3L to 5L with a single (German) value set, which constrains generalisability.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human (European MSA cohort); EQ-5D mapping with Bayesian stage-specific estimation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70593

### Undiagnosed dementia in Brazil fell over five years but remains substantial
**Marcelo Madrid de Bittencourt & Wyllians Vendramini Borelli | Neurology**

**Key findings:** Across two waves of the nationally representative ELSI-Brazil study of adults ≥60, the prevalence of undiagnosed dementia (algorithm-positive dementia without a prior physician diagnosis) decreased from 5.53% (95% CI 4.46–6.59) in 2015–2016 to 3.86% (3.11–4.61) in 2019–2021 (−1.66 pp; p = 0.013), while total dementia prevalence did not change significantly. The findings document persisting under-diagnosis in a middle-income setting and its shifting sociodemographic profile. Dementia is defined algorithmically rather than clinically, and the design is cross-sectional across waves.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; survey-weighted analysis of two ELSI-Brazil waves with design-based prevalence comparisons.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218735

### GPR34 maintains microglial homeostasis and restrains the disease-associated microglial response in an Alzheimer's model
**Geller, A. & Kunwar, P. S. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The microglia-enriched GPCR GPR34, which is downregulated in AD and MS, appears to hold microglia in a homeostatic state. Gpr34-knockout mouse microglia shifted transcriptionally toward disease-associated (DAM) and inflammatory profiles resembling 5xFAD microglia, and crossing Gpr34-KO with 5xFAD exacerbated the DAM signature and glial pathology without changing amyloid plaque burden. Human iPSC-derived microglia lacking GPR34 showed reduced Ca²⁺ and pERK signalling to GPR34 agonists (lyso-phosphatidylserine, myelin) and selectively impaired phagocytosis. The findings are associative-to-causal in mouse and cellular models; v3 revision of a preprint first posted November 2025.

**Model/system & primary method:** Gpr34-KO and 5xFAD mice plus human iPSC-derived microglia; RNA-seq, calcium/pERK signalling and phagocytosis assays.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.26.690816

### A CNS-selective plasma p-tau217 measure sharpens detection of Alzheimer pathology and progression
**Zeng, X. & Karikari, T. K. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Because circulating p-tau217 arises from both CNS and peripheral tissues, the authors developed "brain-derived p-tau217%" — the fraction of circulating tau that is CNS-derived and phosphorylated at Thr217. Across neuropathologically defined, amyloid/tau-imaging–characterised and memory-clinic cohorts, the CNS-selective measure identified AD pathology and clinical AD with larger effect sizes and higher discriminative accuracy than conventional plasma p-tau217, p-tau217/Aβ42 and p-tau217% alternatives. If it holds, the refinement could improve blood-test specificity in older adults with age-related comorbidities; v2 revision of a preprint first posted May 2026.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; next-generation assay quantifying brain-derived p-tau217% across multiple biomarker-defined cohorts.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.18.26353208

### α-Synuclein seed amplification outperforms immunohistochemistry in gut biopsies of Parkinson's disease
**Keshavarzian, A. & Goetz, C. G. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In this cross-sectional, proof-of-concept diagnostic study, an α-synuclein seed amplification assay (SAA) detected seeding-competent aggregates in endoscopic mucosal biopsies with high specificity (97.5–100%) and region-dependent sensitivity (48.7% ileum, 64.3% sigmoid) in Parkinson's disease, registering pathology more often than phospho-Ser129 immunohistochemistry. Sigmoid SAA positivity rose with disease duration (40% at <5 years to 93.75% at >10 years) independent of motor severity, suggesting accumulating peripheral synucleinopathy. Sample sizes are modest and MSA arms exploratory.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; α-synuclein SAA versus proteinase-K-resistant pSer129 IHC on duodenal, ileal and sigmoid biopsies.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.05.764662

### Aging, more than amyloid, reorganises the brain proteome in mice and humans
**Rayaprolu, S. & Rangaraju, S. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Tandem-mass-tag proteomics across the lifespan in wild-type and 5xFAD mice (8,535 proteins, 51 co-expression modules) found aging to be the predominant driver of proteome remodelling — broad changes in RNA metabolism, translation and neuronal pathways independent of amyloid — while Aβ selectively modified a subset of age-dependent processes, suppressing mitochondrial ATP-biosynthetic machinery and amplifying astrocytic and microglial activation. The key aging and disease signatures were conserved in human AD brain proteomes, positioning amyloid as a modifier of an aging-set molecular landscape rather than its origin.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse (wild-type and 5xFAD) TMT proteomics integrated with human brain proteomic networks; co-expression module analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.05.764669

### Combined MLK3/LRRK2 kinase inhibition shows early neuroimmune rescue in an α-synuclein Parkinson's model
**Chiang, W. & Gelbard, H. A. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Arguing that MLK3 and LRRK2 remain valid Parkinson's targets only in combination, this pilot study treated rats given unilateral intranigral low-dose AAV-haSyn with the multi-kinase inhibitor URMC-099. Daily URMC-099 attenuated early lateralised motor impairment (cylinder test) and nigral neuropathology relative to vehicle, offered as early evidence for restoring neuroimmune homeostasis before symptom onset. This is a small, pre-symptomatic pilot with short follow-up, so the therapeutic signal is preliminary and will need dose-ranging and powered replication.

**Model/system & primary method:** Rat AAV-haSyn intranigral Parkinson's model; URMC-099 versus vehicle, motor testing and substantia nigra immunohistochemistry.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.06.764938
