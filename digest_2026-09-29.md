### Peripheral CXCR3 blockade mitigates T cell infiltration and neurodegeneration in a mouse model of tauopathy
**Joshua T Emmerson & David M Holtzman | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** Adaptive immunity is increasingly implicated in tau-driven neurodegeneration, but whether the T-cell arm is therapeutically tractable from the periphery was unclear. Hippocampal IFNγ acutely drove CXCL10 induction and parenchymal T-cell entry, and both genetic deletion and antibody blockade of the chemokine receptor CXCR3 prevented that influx. In a tauopathy model, chronic systemic anti-CXCR3 reduced CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell accumulation, attenuated neurodegeneration, and improved aspects of cognition, while lowering microglial MHC-II without broadly suppressing the disease-associated microglial phenotype. Because the antibody acts peripherally, it nominates a mechanistically distinct disease-modifying route for tauopathies. Evidence is mouse-only and the cognitive readouts are partial.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse tauopathy model with IFNγ challenge; CXCR3 genetic deletion and systemic blocking antibody; single-cell RNA-seq and flow cytometry of brain T cells and microglia.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.030

### BAG3 coordinates astrocytic proteostasis of Alzheimer's disease-linked proteins via proteasome, autophagy, and retromer interactions
**Zachary M Augur & Tracy L Young-Pearse | PNAS**

**Why it matters:** BAG3 is a co-chaperone genetically and pathologically tied to proteostasis, but its astrocyte-specific role — despite being most highly expressed there in brain — was undefined. CRISPR knockout in human iPSC-derived astrocytes caused broader disruption than in neurons: reduced autophagy, lysosomal abundance and proteasome function, plus a newly identified interaction with the retromer subunit VPS35 that altered APP endosomal localization. Functionally, BAG3-null astrocytes failed to support amyloid-β proteostasis when co-cultured with APP/PSEN1 mutant neurons, and in postmortem brain BAG3 marked a stress-responsive astrocyte subtype in aged individuals. Together this positions BAG3 as a central node of astrocytic protein quality control and a candidate target. Causal disease evidence rests on iPSC and co-culture systems.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived astrocytes and neurons with CRISPR/Cas9 BAG3 editing; proteomics, single-nucleus RNA-seq, co-immunoprecipitation, and neuron–astrocyte co-culture Aβ assays; postmortem human brain.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2528514123

### LRSAM1, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, modulates TDP-43 proteostasis in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis
**Takayuki Shirakawa & Makoto Tamura | Brain**

**Why it matters:** Cytoplasmic mislocalization and aggregation of TDP-43 define most ALS, yet the ubiquitin machinery governing its clearance is incompletely mapped. In patient spinal cord, the E3 ligase LRSAM1 colocalized with diffusely mislocalized cytoplasmic TDP-43 but not dense aggregates, suggesting early-stage action. In ALS patient iPSC-derived motor neurons, LRSAM1 knockdown increased cytoplasmic TDP-43 accumulation and phosphorylation and disrupted STMN2 splicing, whereas overexpression rescued stress-induced mislocalization selectively in patient — not control — cells. Mechanistically LRSAM1 bound aggregation-prone C-terminal fragments via the TDP-43 RRM2 domain and routed them for proteasomal degradation, nominating a disease-specific therapeutic target. Findings are cellular and correlative in human tissue; in vivo validation is not included.

**Model/system & primary method:** Postmortem sporadic ALS spinal cord; ALS patient and control iPSC-derived motor neurons with LRSAM1 knockdown/overexpression; immunofluorescence, splicing assays, and binding/degradation biochemistry.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag334

### Non-invasive control of gene editing in vivo by photoswitchable Cas12f and focused ultrasound
**Daesun Song & Michael Z Lin | Cell**

**Why it matters:** Restricting CRISPR editing to a defined time and place in vivo has been limited by delivery size and the inability to trigger editing noninvasively deep in tissue. This work engineers compact photoswitchable Cas12f (psCas12f) small enough to be co-delivered with its sgRNA in a single AAV, then activates it in living mice using focused ultrasound paired with mechanoluminescent particles that emit light locally. The combination achieved spatiotemporally controlled editing in targeted skeletal muscle and brain regions without surgery, addressing both the single-particle delivery and the noninvasive deep activation problems at once. It is a genuinely new toolset; therapeutic editing efficiency and off-target behavior in brain remain to be benchmarked.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse in vivo gene editing; engineered single-AAV photoswitchable Cas12f activated by focused ultrasound and mechanoluminescent particles, with editing quantified in brain and muscle.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.09.005

### α-Synuclein Seeding Discriminates Between LRRK2 G2019S Variant Carriers with and Without Parkinson's Disease
**Theresa Lüth & Global Parkinson's Genetics Program (GP2) | Movement Disorders**

**Why it matters:** LRRK2 G2019S is a common genetic cause of Parkinson's, but its variable, sometimes α-synuclein-negative pathology complicates biomarker use. In a CSF seed amplification assay, misfolded α-synuclein seeding was detected in 80% of LRRK2-PD patients versus a single unaffected carrier (AUC 0.97), showing the assay separates affected from unaffected carriers. An exploratory meta-analysis further linked higher mitochondrial polygenic burden to increased seeding and faster assay kinetics (shorter time-to-threshold, higher maximum slope), tying a genetic modifier to aggregation propensity. The seeding cohort is modest (10 PD, 30 unaffected carriers), and the mitochondrial association is exploratory and correlational.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human CSF from LRRK2 G2019S carriers, idiopathic PD and controls (Norway, PPMI); α-synuclein seed amplification assay with kinetic analysis; mitochondrial polygenic score from genotyping.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70553

### A molecular and spinal circuit basis for the functional segregation of itch and pain
**Myung-Chul Noh & Rebecca P Seal | Neuron**

**Key findings:** Dorsal-horn Grpr+ neurons, long treated as dedicated itch neurons, in fact comprise functionally distinct, transcriptomically conserved subpopulations split by tachykinin-1 (Tac1). Loss- and gain-of-function showed a Tac1- subset is required for chemical itch, whereas a bombesin-insensitive, Tac1-enriched population is necessary for mechanical hypersensitivity across diverse injury states — identifying a convergent spinal node for mechanical hypersensitivity. Computationally identified enhancers provided subtype-selective genetic access, confirming the roles in itch, mechanical hypersensitivity and sustained pain.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse spinal dorsal horn; intersectional genetics, enhancer-based viral tools, behaviour across injury models, cross-species transcriptomics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.029

### A microglial SPP1-ITGB1 feedback loop sustains thalamic hyperexcitability and post-stroke pain
**Ruicheng Dai & Minmin Luo | Neuron**

**Key findings:** In a mouse hemorrhagic-stroke model of central post-stroke pain, ATP release activated microglial P2X7 receptors, inducing secreted phosphoprotein 1 (SPP1), which engaged a self-amplifying autocrine integrin-β1 (ITGB1) loop that maintained a pro-nociceptive state. Optogenetically driving this microglial state was sufficient to produce mechanical allodynia and thalamic hyperexcitability without injury. A temporal dissociation was key: SPP1 is induced acutely, but its ITGB1 signaling is specifically required to maintain — not initiate — chronic pain, defining the axis as a chronification switch and candidate target.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse intracerebral hemorrhage model; microglial P2X7–SPP1–ITGB1 genetic and pharmacological dissection, optogenetics, thalamic electrophysiology, pain behaviour.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.028

### Exponentiated gradient learning yields brain-like synaptic distributions
**Jonathan Cornford & Blake A Richards | Neuron**

**Key findings:** Standard gradient-descent training lets synapses flip sign and produces weight distributions unlike those measured in brains. The authors show an alternative rule, exponentiated gradient (EG), whose multiplicative updates mathematically forbid sign changes and preferentially scale already-large weights, naturally generating log-normal distributions and concentrating computation on a minority of strong connections. EG-trained recurrent networks were more robust to synaptic pruning and better at learning from sparsely relevant inputs than GD-trained networks, while matching performance on standard cognitive tasks — tying a normative optimization principle to observed synaptic organization.

**Model/system & primary method:** Computational; recurrent neural networks trained with exponentiated versus standard gradient descent, evaluated on weight statistics, pruning robustness and cognitive tasks.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.026

### GLP-1R agonist semaglutide protects human forebrain organoids from cholesterol-induced neurotoxicity
**Feifei Yu & Shaohua Ma | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** In human iPSC-derived forebrain organoids, cholesterol overload induced cellular stress and transcriptomic changes partially overlapping Alzheimer's signatures. The GLP-1R agonist semaglutide protected neural cells from this lipotoxic injury, engaging cAMP-PKA and PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling, reducing lipid-droplet accumulation and oxidative stress, and stabilizing calcium activity; single-cell analysis indicated partial preservation of disrupted intercellular communication, including midkine signaling. The work provides a direct-on-human-neural-tissue rationale for GLP-1R neuroprotection under metabolic stress, though it is an organoid model of an artificial lipid insult rather than in vivo disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived forebrain organoids under cholesterol overload; bulk and single-cell transcriptomics, calcium imaging, biochemical assays, and mapping to postmortem neurodegenerative datasets.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71888

### Sub-second and multi-second dopamine dynamics underlie variability in human time perception
**Renata Sadibolova & Devin B Terhune | Brain**

**Key findings:** Recording caudate dopamine and serotonin by fast-scan cyclic voltammetry during awake deep-brain-stimulation surgery in Parkinson's patients performing a temporal bisection task, the authors dissociated phasic from tonic dopaminergic contributions to timing. Elevated phasic dopamine (~625–670 ms post-stimulus) biased toward "short" judgements and reduced overestimation bias, independent of objective clock time, whereas lower tonic dopamine tracked poorer temporal precision both within patients and versus controls. No comparable serotonin effects appeared, indicating dopamine specificity across timescales and reconciling discrepant human and animal accounts. Evidence is from a small intraoperative cohort (N=5 patients).

**Model/system & primary method:** Human caudate fast-scan cyclic voltammetry during awake DBS surgery in Parkinson's disease; temporal bisection task with machine-learning-decoded neurochemical time series and hierarchical mixed-effects modelling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag336

### Long term clinical performance and analytical robustness of the Lumipulse plasma p-tau217 assay in routine clinical practice
**Susan Ashrafzadeh-Kian & Alicia Algeciras-Schimnich | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** In 744 Mayo Clinic patients evaluated for cognitive impairment (July 2024–December 2025), the Fujirebio Lumipulse plasma p-tau217 assay detected Alzheimer's pathology with 93% accuracy against CSF or amyloid-PET reference, with 23% intermediate results. Reduced eGFR increased intermediate results among amyloid-negative individuals — a practical caveat for renal impairment. Analytical robustness held up over 18 months: stable result frequencies, ~8% average imprecision, and ≤11% bias across ten lot-to-lot and two reagent-version comparisons, supporting real-world reliability of the assay as it enters routine use.

**Model/system & primary method:** Retrospective clinical cohort (n=744); plasma p-tau217 immunoassay benchmarked against CSF p-tau181/Aβ42 or amyloid-PET, with longitudinal quality-control and lot-comparison analyses.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71887

### Masking phosphatidylserine prevents neuronal loss in two distinct Drosophila models of neurodegeneration
**Naden Khateb & Estee Kurant | PNAS**

**Key findings:** Phosphatidylserine (PS) exposed as an "eat me" signal on stressed-but-viable neurons can trigger their premature glial phagocytosis (phagoptosis). Using a truncated MFG-E8 that binds PS without promoting engulfment, the authors masked PS in two mechanistically distinct fly models — adult skpA knockdown and an embryonic Huntington's model — and preserved specific neuronal populations in both, with motor function and lifespan improved to degrees depending on damage timing and severity. The result implicates aberrant glial phagocytosis as a shared contributor to neuronal vulnerability and PS masking as a candidate cross-disease strategy. Evidence is invertebrate and the rescue is population-selective.

**Model/system & primary method:** Drosophila neurodegeneration models (skpA knockdown; Huntington's disease); PS masking with truncated MFG-E8; neuronal counts, motor assays, lifespan.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2622558123

### Integrative prediction of Alzheimer's disease and related dementias using multi-omics aging clocks and genetic data
**Shayan Mostafaei & Sara Hägg | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** Combining ADRD polygenic risk scores with biological-aging markers (clinical, telomere, proteomic, metabolomic) in 16,215 UK Biobank participants (397 incident cases over ~10 years) yielded a fully integrated model reaching AUC 0.90 in held-out data. Polygenic risk was the strongest single predictor, followed by proteomic age (ProtAge), and the top predicted-risk quartile carried markedly higher incidence (subdistribution HR 16.7). External replication in TwinGene showed moderate transportability (AUC 0.76) with preserved risk stratification, indicating that layering aging biology onto genetics improves ADRD risk stratification. As a prediction study, associations are not causal and low precision (AUPRC ~0.24) reflects the rare outcome.

**Model/system & primary method:** UK Biobank cohort with TwinGene replication; integration of ADRD polygenic risk scores and multi-omics biological-aging clocks in survival/prediction models.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71869

### CAR T cell targeting of inflammatory myeloid progenitors in the bone marrow remodels border-associated macrophages and reverses cognitive aging
**Harris, A. S. & Amor, C. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The authors identify inflammatory border-associated macrophages (BAMs) as drivers of aging neuroinflammation and report that eliminating them via intrathecal CAR T cells restored cognitive performance in aged and Alzheimer's-model mice. Strikingly, targeting aged inflammatory bone-marrow myeloid progenitors — with intravenous CAR T cells that do not enter the brain, or by transplanting CAR T-treated progenitors — was itself sufficient to reduce neuroinflammation and cognitive impairment, implying a heritable proinflammatory transcriptional state transmitted from progenitors to BAM progeny. Surface markers on these progenitors reportedly enable precise peripheral targeting. These are bold, therapeutically framed claims in mice awaiting peer review; human relevance rests on a conserved transcriptional signature.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse models of aging and Alzheimer's disease; intrathecal and intravenous CAR T cells, progenitor transplantation, single-cell profiling of BAMs and bone-marrow myeloid cells.

### α-Synuclein Seeding in Dopaminergic Neurons Transplanted into the Putamen of Parkinson's Disease Patients
**Duvernay, J. B. & Kordower, J. H. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Grafted embryonic neurons in the putamen of Parkinson's patients develop Lewy pathology years after transplantation; whether this reflects host-to-graft seeding or a hostile environment has been contested. Using a multiplex in situ seed immunodetection assay across human tissue, the authors detected α-synuclein seeding within and proximal to Lewy pathology in 16- and 27-year grafts, and in grafted neurons of an 18-month graft before overt pathology, whereas a 4-year graft showed seeding only in amorphous deposits, not in tyrosine-hydroxylase-positive neurons. Because seeding accompanied but was not fully explained by neuromelanin, the data support host-derived seeds as a contributor to graft pathology. Evidence is observational human postmortem with few, long-interval grafts.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem brain, including long-term intraputamenal neuronal grafts and PD/DLB/MSA/PSP tissue; multiplex in situ seed immunodetection (isSID) assay.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.22.751740

### Endosomal pH Triggers Amyloid β Oligomerization and Maladaptive Phenotypic Plasticity in Alzheimer's Disease
**Meena, R. K. & Prasad, H. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Building a quantitative model of endosomal acidification (vacuolar-ATPase pumping, NHE6-mediated proton leak), the authors argue that AD-associated NHE6 downregulation drives endosomal hyperacidification with threshold-like behaviour below ~50% expression. The model predicts that this pH shift accelerates amyloid-β oligomerization and enhances β-secretase activity, correlating with disruptions in calcium and synaptic signaling, and frames early adaptive compensation transitioning to maladaptive neurodegeneration. Human brain datasets showed reciprocal neuronal NHE6 / glial NHE9 enrichment, and NHE6 loss-of-function variants tracked with late regression. This is primarily a computational/conceptual framework compared against existing NHE6-null and cell-culture data, not new experimental disease evidence.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mathematical model of neuronal endosomal pH regulation; analysis of human brain expression datasets and curated NHE6 patient variants, compared with published NHE6-null mouse and AD cell-culture results.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.23.752284

### Lysosomal microautophagic pathway links proteostasis to protein aggregation disease
**Fujiwara, Y. & Kabuta, T. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** This v3 revision of a preprint first posted August 2020 defines a mammalian microautophagy pathway in which the lysosomal membrane protein SIDT2 delivers cytosolic proteins — including aggregation-prone disease proteins — directly into the lysosomal lumen, independent of macroautophagy and chaperone-mediated autophagy. A conserved catalytic serine was required for SIDT2 ATPase activity and for forming intraluminal vesicles that sequester cytosol; SIDT2 loss impaired substrate uptake without altering luminal acidification or proteolysis. A dominant-negative SIDT2 mutation was identified in familial neuromyopathy with rimmed vacuoles, and both knockout and knock-in mice recapitulated aggregate accumulation and rimmed-vacuole-like lesions, linking pathway failure to human protein-aggregation disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** Reconstitution and imaging of SIDT2-mediated lysosomal uptake; Sidt2-knockout and patient-mutation knock-in mice; human familial neuromyopathy genetics.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2020.08.11.245688

### Early life social isolation primes flies for more rapid TDP-43 dependent neurodegeneration later in life
**Murthygowda, S. & Dubnau, J. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Testing whether a psychosocial stressor can modify TDP-43 proteinopathy, the authors housed young adult Drosophila alone or in groups and then induced pathological TDP-43. Early-life social isolation acted as a primer, accelerating subsequent neurodegeneration, driving faster activation of the endogenous retrovirus mdg4 (which mediates TDP-43 effects) and more aggressive glia-to-neuron propagation of TDP-43 pathology, alongside shortened lifespan. Providing isolated flies with visual, olfactory and tactile cues from conspecifics behind a divider did not rescue the effect. The work offers a tractable model for the epidemiological link between loneliness and neurodegenerative risk, but causal mechanisms and mammalian relevance remain open.

**Model/system & primary method:** Drosophila social isolation versus enrichment paradigm with inducible pathological TDP-43; neurodegeneration, mdg4 retroelement activation, pathology propagation and lifespan readouts.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.23.753732
