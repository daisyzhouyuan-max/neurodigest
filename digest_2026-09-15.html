1Loss of interruption in the HTT CAG repeat is associated with somatic expansion and loss of medium spiny neurons in Huntington's disease.

Neuron  Chris Kay & Michael R Hayden
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
This work ties a known clinical modifier of Huntington's disease directly to the cell-autonomous pathology it predicts. Single-cell CAG sizing in human post-mortem striatum shows that medium spiny neurons (MSNs) carrying large (111–150) and very large (>150) somatic expansions are rare in early manifest disease but accumulate with disease duration, and that in carriers of the deleterious CAG-CCG loss-of-interruption modifier the fraction of MSNs with such expansions is roughly 5-fold higher — accompanied by reduced caudate MSN counts. It provides a concrete mechanistic bridge between somatic instability and selective neuronal loss.
Model & method
Human post-mortem striatal tissue from manifest HD; single-neuron somatic CAG-length quantification with genotype stratification (correlative human data, not causal manipulation).
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.010

2A microprotein atlas of the human frontal cortex in Alzheimer's disease.

Nat Aging  Brendan Miller & Alan Saghatelian
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Small open reading frames are pervasively translated in brain but their protein products are largely unannotated. Integrating transcriptomics, mass spectrometry and deep-learning-predicted spectra across more than 600 post-mortem frontal cortex samples, the authors catalogue 1,067 microproteins absent from reviewed UniProtKB, a subset differentially expressed in AD independently of the annotated main ORF at the same locus. Beyond the resource, they show a 63-amino-acid microprotein from the MKKS locus is the locus's dominant translation product, is downregulated in AD, and — on knockdown — impairs microglial mitochondrial respiration, nominating a specific microprotein in microglial bioenergetics.
Model & method
Human post-mortem frontal cortex (AD and control); multi-omic microprotein discovery with functional follow-up in a microglial model.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-026-01207-x

3TogoPhosTAC as a delivery-ready platform for targeted protein dephosphorylation.

Nat Commun  Yu-Yu Chen & Po-Han Chen
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Phosphorylation-targeting chimeras (PhosTACs) can strip phosphate marks by induced proximity, but recruiting phosphatases with small molecules has been the bottleneck. This platform preassembles a small-molecule PhosTAC with an engineered FKBP12(F36V)-phosphatase and delivers it — as protein complex or mRNA — via lipid nanoparticles, achieving selective dephosphorylation of EGFR, α-synuclein and tau. Intrahippocampal or intranasal delivery in PS19 tau transgenic mice reduced phospho-tau across multiple sites (Ser202, Thr205, Thr231, Ser396, Ser404), lowered tau burden and improved AD-related behavioural deficits. The evidence is mouse-only, but it establishes a genuinely new, deliverable therapeutic modality for tauopathy.
Model & method
Cell-based dephosphorylation assays and PS19 tau transgenic mice (male); LNP-delivered PhosTAC–phosphatase complexes/mRNA with biochemical, pathological and behavioural readouts.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76725-8

4Simultaneous speech and gesture decoding for multimodal communication in paralysis.

Nat Neurosci  Samantha C Brosler & Edward F Chang
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Prior brain-computer interfaces have decoded speech or movement in isolation. Here a single high-density electrocorticography implant supported simultaneous decoding of both across three people with paralysis, driving a personalized virtual avatar through attempted speech and gestures — together or separately. Training on both isolated and simultaneous data improved performance across behavioural contexts, showing one cortical implant can support multi-effector control and pointing toward more natural, multimodal communication restoration.
Model & method
Three human participants with paralysis; single high-density ECoG implant with parallel speech and gesture decoders and avatar control.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02446-2

5Myeloid cell replacement induces intercellular mitochondrial transfer and restores metabolism in a mouse model of mitochondrial disease.

Nat Commun  Hyunmin Cho & Natalia Gomez-Ospina
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Friedreich's ataxia is a frataxin-deficiency mitochondrial disease causing progressive neurodegeneration and cardiomyopathy with few disease-modifying options. The authors show myeloid cell replacement drives neurological and cardiac recovery in FA mice, and that donor-derived mitochondria are transferred from microglia and macrophages to CNS cells and cardiomyocytes, raising oxidative phosphorylation and ATP-synthesis gene expression and mitochondrial protein abundance. In culture, transfer required direct cell-cell contact and partially restored respiration in frataxin-deficient recipients. Findings are mouse and in vitro, but they nominate intercellular mitochondrial transfer as a mechanism supporting hematopoietic transplantation for FA and other mitochondrial disorders.
Model & method
Frataxin-deficient FA mice and cultured cells; myeloid cell replacement with tracking of donor mitochondria, metabolic profiling and behavioural/cardiac outcomes.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76775-y

Overlapping representations in the ventral hippocampus support rapid fear memory retrieval.

Nat Neurosci  Robert R Rozeske & Mark P Brandon
Key findings
Contrary to the canonical view that the hippocampus builds dissimilar context representations to aid discrimination, ventral CA1 (vCA1) increased representational overlap after fear conditioning: as mice were instantaneously switched between threatening and neutral contexts, vCA1 (but not dorsal CA1) made the two contexts more similar, and the change scaled with freezing. During retrieval vCA1 reinstated the threat representation faster and more strongly, and the relative dominance of the two representations predicted freezing — consistent with a shallower attractor landscape that permits rapid switching between neutral and threatening memories.
Model & method
Mice; simultaneous dCA1 and vCA1 population imaging during context transitions and fear conditioning/retrieval.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02435-5

Amygdalostriatal transition zone neurons encode sustained cue responses to guide defensive behaviors.

Neuron  Fergil Mills & Kay M Tye
Key findings
The amygdalostriatal transition zone (ASt) is identified as a missing circuit element for sustained defensive behaviour. snRNA-seq shows the ASt is genetically distinct from adjacent striatal and amygdalar structures; in vivo electrophysiology and calcium imaging reveal robust, sustained ASt responses to shock-predicting cues (longer than the transient responses typical of amygdala neurons). Photostimulation drove freezing and avoidance, and optogenetic inhibition of Drd2+ ASt neurons showed they are necessary for cue-conditioned fear — establishing the ASt as a critical structure encoding learned threat associations.
Model & method
Mice; snRNA-seq, in vivo electrophysiology/calcium imaging, and optogenetic gain- and loss-of-function.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.012

Circulating Exosomes Drive Persistent Neuronal Dysfunction in Post-Herpetic Neuralgia Patients.

Ann Neurol  Christy S Niemeyer & Andrew N Bubak
Key findings
Testing what sustains neuropathic pain after herpes zoster, exosomes isolated from post-herpetic neuralgia (PHN) patient sera reproduced and amplified a pathological phenotype in uninfected human nociceptive neurons — independent of direct varicella-zoster infection. PHN exosomes suppressed neurite-extension gene networks via predicted inhibition of the RNA-binding protein ELAVL4, impaired neurite outgrowth, and induced matrix-remodelling (MMP-9) programs, whereas direct viral infection produced a distinct pro-inflammatory, metabolically active state. The work implicates circulating exosomal cargo as a driver of persistent sensory-neuron dysfunction, though causal evidence is in cultured human neurons rather than patients.
Model & method
Human nociceptive neurons exposed to patient-derived serum exosomes (control, acute HZ, PHN); RNA-seq, cytokine/MMP assays, live-cell neurite imaging and exosome proteomics.
https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78352

CD36-mediated lipid-accumulation in reactive microglia contributes to retinal degeneration via the NLRP3-IL-1β pathway in mice.

Nat Commun  Tian Zhou & Chang He
Key findings
Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics of NaIO3-induced retinal degeneration identified a lipid-accumulated reactive microglial subset (aLARM) in the outer retina, conserved across mouse models and patients and marked by high CD36. Microglia-specific CD36 deletion abolished aLARM-driven inflammation and degeneration, while subretinal transplantation of CD36+ aLARM worsened structural and functional damage. Mechanistically, CD36+ aLARM activated the NLRP3 inflammasome and secreted IL-1β, engaging IL-1R1 on microglia/macrophages and pericytes/smooth-muscle cells in a feed-forward circuit; CD36-blocking antibody reduced aLARM and protected against degeneration — nominating a targetable microglial state linking neuroinflammation to CNS (retinal) degeneration.
Model & method
Mouse retinal degeneration models plus patient samples; scRNA-seq/spatial transcriptomics, microglia-specific CD36 knockout, cell transplantation and antibody blockade.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76685-z

A De Novo 16p13.3 Triplication Underlying Early-Onset Complex Neurodegeneration.

Mov Disord  Ze-Hong Zheng & Wan-Jin Chen
Key findings
In a molecularly undiagnosed early-onset neurodegenerative case presenting with ataxia and cognitive impairment, combined short- and long-read whole-genome sequencing identified a de novo copy-number gain at 16p13.3, modelled as a triplicated segment in tandem with a proximal duplication joined to a distal duplication via an inverted junction. Blood transcriptomics showed significant upregulation of ATP6V0C, AMDHD2 and PDPK1. The single-case report highlights complex structural variation as a cause of early-onset neurodegeneration and the value of pairing copy-number profiling with long-read sequencing to resolve such rearrangements.
Model & method
Single human patient; short- and long-read whole-genome sequencing with blood-leukocyte RNA-seq and RT-qPCR validation.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70540

Modelling the effect of ventricular expansion on white matter in normal pressure hydrocephalus.

Brain  Martina Del Giovane & David J Sharp
Key findings
Testing whether ventriculomegaly damages white matter mechanically in idiopathic normal pressure hydrocephalus (iNPH), the authors combined tract-based diffusion imaging in 22 iNPH patients (versus AD, moderate-severe TBI and healthy controls) with a finite-element biomechanical model of ventricular expansion. iNPH showed a distinctive pattern of both increased and decreased fractional anisotropy, distinct from the ventricular enlargement also seen in AD and TBI, and the model predicted regional white-matter stretch and compression that mapped onto the observed diffusion changes — supporting a mechanical mechanism for the reversible deficits treatable by shunting.
Model & method
Human diffusion MRI (tract-based spatial statistics) across four groups plus a finite-element biomechanical model of ventricular expansion.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag312

Brain fluorodeoxyglucose PET anatomical segmentation via AI: extensive validation in the neurodegenerative spectrum.

Brain  Luísa C Silva & Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative
Key findings
To enable objective [18F]FDG-PET quantification without a paired structural MRI, the authors trained a deep-learning model to segment 52 anatomical regions directly from FDG-PET, using 1,628 scans spanning cognitively normal subjects, mild cognitive impairment, subjective memory concerns, Alzheimer's disease, frontotemporal lobar degeneration and Lewy body dementia, with an additional external validation set. Against MRI-derived ground truth (and atlas-based segmentation as benchmark), the model achieved favourable Dice overlap and agreement in pons-normalized SUVR across these varied metabolic profiles — a validated, MRI-free segmentation tool for differential neurodegenerative diagnosis.
Model & method
Human [18F]FDG-PET across the neurodegenerative spectrum; deep-learning segmentation validated against MRI-based ground truth with internal and external test sets.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag314
