1Alzheimer's disease genetics converges on microglial and neuronal dysregulation

Nature  Emil Uffelmann & PGC-ALZ consortium
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
The largest Alzheimer's GWAS to date — 109,479 cases, 74,141 proxy cases and >2.6 million controls across diverse ancestries — identifies 118 multi-ancestry loci (plus 9 ancestry-specific), 48 of them new, and roughly triples the estimated SNP-based heritability to 16%. Risk genes were enriched among transcripts upregulated in microglia and downregulated in neurons in cases, sharpening the two-cell-type model of AD and nominating fresh drug targets. The headline methodological point is that the common-variant contribution has been substantially underestimated, countering the view that little further common-variant risk remains to be found.
Model & method
Multi-ancestry case/proxy-case GWAS with fine-mapping, gene prioritization and polygenic scoring in human cohorts.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11206-y

2Somatic DNA-repeat expansions initiate distinct yet analogous changes in Huntington's disease cortical and striatal neurons

Cell  Seva Kashin & Steven A. McCarroll
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Striatal vulnerability in HD tracks somatic expansion of the HTT CAG tract beyond ~150 repeats; whether cortical degeneration follows an independent logic has been unclear. Here cortical pyramidal neurons are shown to pass through the same sequence of pathological phases as striatal neurons, with toxicity again commencing near ~150 CAGs but then diverging in downstream gene expression. The rate of somatic expansion was set by a neuron's precise molecular identity rather than its areal or laminar position, varying up to 20-fold across pyramidal subtypes — reframing selective vulnerability as a cell-intrinsic, expansion-rate property.
Model & method
Single-cell transcriptomics with per-cell CAG-repeat measurement in human postmortem HD cortex and striatum.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.10.001

3Perinatal elimination of genetically aberrant human cortical neurons

Nature  Diane D. Shao & Christopher A. Walsh
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Sequencing 2,357 single neurons from human brain spanning fetal to postnatal life, the study finds that ~26% of prenatal cortical neurons carry highly aberrant genomes (widespread multi-chromosome CNVs), a fraction that falls ~4.5-fold after birth. Neurons with widespread CNVs are eliminated perinatally, roughly in parallel with programmed cell death, while surviving neurons are depleted for CNVs hitting dosage-sensitive and neurodevelopmental-disorder genes. snATAC/snRNA-seq links high CNV burden to abnormal synaptic gene expression, implying a developmental genome quality-control step — a purifying selection on the neuronal genome whose failure could matter for neurodevelopmental disease.
Model & method
Single-neuron whole-genome sequencing (two amplification methods) plus single-nucleus ATAC/RNA-seq on human postmortem cortex.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11190-3

4Serotonin signaling enhances phagocytosis by brain macrophages through Htr2b and alleviates brain injury after stroke

Nature Neuroscience  Yueman Zhang & Peiying Li
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Using an MDM-specific fate-mapping mouse line and single-cell RNA-seq, the authors define an Htr2b⁺ monocyte-derived macrophage subset that accumulates in the ischemic penumbra and resolves neuroinflammation. Mechanistically, Htr2b signaling drives nuclear translocation of the lysosomal master regulator Tfe3, boosting lysosomal biogenesis and phagocytic debris clearance. MDM-specific Htr2b deletion worsened injury, whereas pharmacological Htr2b agonism or Tfe3 activation improved post-stroke outcomes — a tractable serotonergic lever on reparative myeloid function. Causal evidence is mouse-only.
Model & method
Mouse ischemic stroke with Ms4a3creERT2 fate mapping, scRNA-seq, conditional Htr2b ablation and pharmacology.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02482-y

5Brain-sensing synNotch-CAR T cells enable precise targeting and clearance of diffuse midline glioma

Science Translational Medicine  Senthilnath Lakshmanachetty & Hideho Okada
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
A brain-restricted T-cell circuit uses a synNotch receptor for brevican (BCAN) to gate CAR expression against EphA2, IL-13Rα2 or B7-H3, confining cytotoxicity to the CNS. In immunodeficient DMG models a single IV or ICV infusion drove tumor infiltration, persistence and extended survival; in an immunocompetent syngeneic model a murinized BCAN-gated B7-H3 CAR achieved durable clearance and rechallenge resistance without neurotoxicity. The cautionary result is that ICV delivery of a B7-H3 CAR caused fatal neurotoxicity from low-level B7-H3 in normal CNS, underscoring how antigen gating and route interact in a disease with narrow therapeutic margins.
Model & method
Engineered synNotch-CAR T cells in immunodeficient and immunocompetent mouse diffuse midline glioma models.
https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aed9994

Multimodal spatial transcriptomics redefines cortical pathology in Huntington's disease

Cell  Sonia Vazquez-Sanchez & Don W. Cleveland
Key findings
A spatial method that reads transcriptional programs, somatic CAG-repeat length and eight pathology markers (including HTT inclusions) in each of 428,173 cells identifies layer 5–6 near-projecting and L6b deep-layer excitatory neurons as selectively vulnerable, with >50% loss tied to large somatic expansions (>450 ± 55 CAGs). Intranuclear inclusions peaked at intermediate expansions (250–350 CAGs) and correlated with broader transcriptional change than repeat length alone, dissociating inclusion formation from the longest expansions.
Model & method
Subcellular-resolution multimodal spatial transcriptomics on human control and HD cortex.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.09.064

Presynaptic homeostasis mediated by exocytosis of a SNARE protein

Nature  Hongfei Xu & Robert H. Edwards
Key findings
High-frequency stimulation reconfigures release through the v-SNARE VAMP7, which suppresses neurotransmission by inhibiting Ca²⁺ entry specifically through P/Q-type presynaptic channels, independent of postsynaptic receptors. VAMP7 resides on an AP-3–derived synaptic-vesicle pool that responds selectively to high-frequency activity, delivering VAMP7 to the plasma membrane where it acts with syntaxin — a mechanism coupling activity history to a presynaptic, postsynaptic-receptor-independent form of homeostatic metaplasticity.
Model & method
Electrophysiology and vesicle-trafficking assays in neurons.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11201-3

Clostridium perfringens Delta toxin targets CD9 to drive necrotizing enterocolitis and brain injury

Nature  Shimeng Zhang & Xiaozhe Xiong
Key findings
Delta-toxin–bearing C. perfringens pathobionts were enriched in the gut microbiome of infants with severe/fatal necrotizing enterocolitis and associated with worse neurological outcomes. In mice, the toxin — or infection — recapitulated intestinal necrosis and acute cerebral injury by preferentially targeting macrophages, activating inflammasome signaling and pyroptosis, compromising the blood–brain barrier and driving neuroinflammation. A genome-wide CRISPR screen identified the tetraspanin CD9 as the host receptor; myeloid Cd9 deletion protected against BBB dysfunction and lethality, implicating macrophage targeting as the primary driver of gut-to-brain injury.
Model & method
Human NEC microbiome analysis plus mouse infection/toxin models with cell-type-specific Cd9 knockouts and a genome-wide CRISPR screen.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11211-1

Emotion regulation capacity lacks a shared neural signature

Nature  Maurizio Sicorello & Carmen Morawetz
Key findings
A preregistered consortium analysis of 40 task-fMRI datasets (2,175 participants) tested whether three standard indices of cognitive-reappraisal ability — trait questionnaires, task-based affective ratings and amygdala down-regulation — converge, and whether they map onto canonical prefrontal regulatory circuits. They did not: the three indicators shared no common core, trait measures showed no detectable neural associations, and affective ratings tracked amygdala and sensorimotor cortex instead. Canonical control circuits were engaged during both emotion generation and reappraisal, challenging their presumed specificity and cautioning against inferring regulatory capacity from regional activation.
Model & method
Preregistered multi-dataset task-based fMRI mega-analysis in humans.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11203-1

Connectome simulations identify a central pattern generator for fly walking

Nature  Sarah M. Pugliese & Bingni W. Brunton
Key findings
Dynamic simulations of the Drosophila ventral-nerve-cord connectome, combined with an activation screen of descending neurons, isolated a minimal three-neuron rhythm generator (one inhibitory, two excitatory interneurons) that was necessary and sufficient for leg motor rhythms across all six legs and reproducible across four connectome datasets. The simulations further predicted a separate descending pathway (DNb08) driving rhythmic leg movement, confirmed optogenetically in behaving flies — a rare case of a walking CPG resolved to identified cells and synapses.
Model & method
Biophysical connectome simulation plus optogenetic validation in Drosophila.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11195-y

Computational modeling and preclinical evaluation support targeting somatic instability for Huntington's disease treatment

Nature Communications  Bryan P. Simpson & Beverly L. Davidson
Key findings
Pairing a patient-data-derived model of how reducing somatic CAG instability would alter HD onset/progression with an AAV-delivered artificial miRNA against MSH3 (miMSH3), the authors achieved 48–94% MSH3 mRNA knockdown in medium spiny neurons of nonhuman primates using an MSN-targeting capsid. Feeding that knockdown into the predictive model projected a meaningful delay in disease measures, lending preclinical and quantitative support to MSH3-directed, instability-lowering strategies. Clinical benefit remains model-projected rather than demonstrated.
Model & method
Computational modeling of HD progression plus AAV-miMSH3 knockdown in nonhuman primates.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78769-2

An oxidized ribonucleotide 8-oxoGTP drives neuroinflammation and accelerates cognitive decline

Science Advances  Jin Li & Jian-Ping Cai
Key findings
Beyond its role as a damage marker, oxidized GTP (8-oxoGTP) is identified as an endogenous signaling molecule that activates microglia and drives neuroinflammation. 8-oxoGTP accumulated in the aging mouse brain, and raising its levels was sufficient to induce synaptic dysfunction, tau hyperphosphorylation, neuronal loss and cognitive decline — positioning NTP-pool oxidation, not just macromolecular damage, as an upstream driver of age-related neurodegeneration. Causal evidence is mouse-based.
Model & method
Aging mouse brain with manipulation of 8-oxoGTP levels and microglial/behavioral readouts.
https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee0110

Plasma β-synuclein associates with hippocampal synaptic density ([11C]UCB-J PET) and plasma tau in Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer's & Dementia  Darby C. Peter & Patrick Oeckl
Key findings
In 83 middle-aged and older adults (69% cognitively unimpaired), higher plasma β-synuclein tracked amyloid-PET positivity in both unimpaired and impaired participants, while lower synaptic density (UCB-J PET) was seen only in the cognitively impaired. Across the full sample, higher plasma β-synuclein correlated with lower hippocampal synaptic density and with higher plasma p-tau217, brain-derived tau and GFAP, supporting a blood-based readout of synaptic degeneration in AD. The sample is modest and cross-sectional.
Model & method
Human cohort combining plasma biomarkers, [11C]UCB-J and amyloid PET, and neuropsychological testing.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71900

Neuroprotectant development from small-molecule inhibitors of CHOP/ATF4 expression

Cell Reports  Mu Li & Yang Hu
Key findings
Targeting the ATF4–CHOP branch of ER stress implicated in glaucomatous neurodegeneration, high-throughput screening yielded chemical series that inhibit ER-stress–induced CHOP expression; structure–activity optimization produced analogs that potently suppress CHOP/ATF4. In two optic-neuropathy models the compounds increased survival of retinal ganglion cell somata and axons and preserved visual function — proof-of-concept neuroprotectants for glaucoma and potentially other CNS axonopathies. Efficacy is at the preclinical, rodent proof-of-concept stage.
Model & method
HTS/medicinal chemistry plus two rodent optic-neuropathy models.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118225

Neuromelanin-sensitive MRI as a marker of clinical severity and disease burden in Parkinson's disease

Movement Disorders  Rayo Akande & Gennaro Pagano
Key findings
This PRISMA meta-analysis (39 studies, 1,983 patients) finds reduced NM-MRI signal consistently associated with greater motor impairment (pooled r = −0.41), longer disease duration (r = −0.43), higher Hoehn–Yahr stage and higher levodopa equivalent dose. Substantia nigra measures tracked motor dysfunction and stage, whereas locus coeruleus measures preferentially linked to cognitive, affective, autonomic and sleep symptoms; four longitudinal studies reported ~5–19% annual decline — supporting NM-MRI as a biologically interpretable, progression-sensitive biomarker, though between-study heterogeneity tempers the pooled estimates.
Model & method
Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of human NM-MRI studies in PD.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70588

Parkin deficiency rewires the genetic dependencies that govern neuronal survival

bioRxiv  Wang, S. & Finkbeiner, S.
Key findings
Beyond the canonical mitochondrial-quality-control model of PRKN-linked Parkinson's disease, isogenic Prkn⁻/⁻ rat cortical neurons died at 2.6× the wild-type rate, and a genome-scale CRISPR screen (14,646 genes) in post-mitotic neurons found 160 survival modifiers converging on endosomal trafficking, energy metabolism and p53–Mdm2 signaling. Strikingly, dependencies inverted with Parkin status — loss of Rab7a, Cs or Crym killed wild-type but protected Prkn⁻/⁻ neurons, while Ldha loss killed only Prkn⁻/⁻ neurons — implying genotype can determine whether an intervention helps or harms. Cell-model evidence; cautious interpretation warranted pending in vivo confirmation.
Model & method
Isogenic Prkn⁻/⁻ rat cortical neurons with genome-wide CRISPR screening and single-cell survival tracking.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.27.762340

Single-nucleus profiling reveals neuronal metabolic collapse during the transition to Alzheimer's disease dementia

bioRxiv  Lu, S. & Zhao, Z.
Key findings
To address why neuropathological staging predicts cognition imperfectly, the authors applied single-nucleus RNA-seq and multiome data with a custom normalization framework and identified neuron-specific suppression of nuclear-encoded mitochondrial genes and upstream mitochondrial biogenesis signaling as a primary correlate of clinical dementia — distinct from plaque/tangle burden. Integration with multi-omic QTLs, GWAS and epigenomic profiles tied the signature to genetic and epigenetic regulation. Associations are cross-sectional and computational; causality is not established.
Model & method
Single-nucleus RNA-seq/multiome of human AD brain with integrative multi-omic analysis.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.26.761376

Charting vulnerability of brainstem functional pathways in aging and prodromal synucleinopathy

bioRxiv  Hua, L. & Bianciardi, M.
Key findings
Using 7-Tesla fMRI to map connectivity between 58 brainstem nuclei and cortex in young adults, older adults and individuals with isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD, a prodromal synucleinopathy), long-range brainstem–cortical connectivity weakened with aging and further in iRBD — but through distinct systems: aging affected arousal/cognition nuclei and association cortex, whereas iRBD affected sleep/autonomic nuclei extending to sensorimotor cortex, with distinct neurochemical and transcriptomic signatures. Broader patterns reproduced at 3T. Cross-sectional; iRBD sample as a prodromal proxy.
Model & method
High-resolution 7T (and confirmatory 3T) resting-state fMRI in humans across aging and iRBD.

The Aging Brain Matrix: cross-species evidence linking FREM3 to brain and cognitive aging

bioRxiv  Clifford, K. P. & Nikolova, Y.
Key findings
The age-dependent extracellular-matrix gene FREM3, enriched in age-sensitive long-range excitatory neurons, was tested causally via an excitatory-neuron-specific Frem3 conditional-knockout mouse and genetically regulated FREM3 expression (FREM3-GReX) in humans. Frem3-cKO mice showed age-dependent global and regional brain-volume loss, strongest in cognitively relevant cortex and paralleled by lower cognition; in humans, lower FREM3-GReX predicted smaller cortical surface area, which mediated an indirect link to poorer cognition across age-sensitive domains. Human evidence is genetic/mediational rather than interventional.
Model & method
Excitatory-neuron-specific Frem3 conditional-knockout mouse plus human imaging-genetics (GReX) mediation analysis.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.27.762265

Cognition in healthy aging, ages 36 to 108: associations with dementia risk factors and neurodegenerative disease biomarkers

medRxiv  Arnold, S. E. & Salat, D. H.
Key findings
In the Aging Adult Brain Connectome cohort (N = 1,396; ages 36–108; mean 5-year follow-up), five modifiable factors — education, diabetes, low motor function, hearing loss and low vision — associated with cognitive performance level, but none with rate of decline. Plasma ADRD markers, most consistently pTau217 positivity, associated with cognition only from age 65 and with faster decline (PACC, 0.058 SD/year). Cognition in later life thus separated into a level component tied to modifiable risks and a decline component tied to emerging Alzheimer's pathology. Observational and partly cross-sectional in its risk-factor associations.
Model & method
Longitudinal human cohort with repeated cognition, modifiable risk factors and plasma AD biomarkers.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.25.26366428
