### Stimulated protein degradation to promote mitochondrial function and neuronal survival
**Srividya Chandrasekhar & Michael Rapé | Science**

**Why it matters:** Targeted protein degradation has so far removed one disease driver at a time. The authors introduce stimulated protein degradation (SPD): small-molecule agonists of the reductive-stress E3 ligase CUL2^FEM1B that boost turnover of multiple endogenous substrates at once, improving mitochondrial protein import and electron transport chain assembly. This preserved mitochondrial function and neuronal survival in cellular models of neurodegenerative COX-deficiency syndrome and Friedreich's ataxia. By co-opting a native quality-control ligase rather than engineering a bespoke degrader, SPD offers a route into disorders of pervasive proteostatic dysregulation, though the evidence here is in cellular/disease models rather than in vivo therapy.

**Model/system & primary method:** Cellular models of COX-deficiency syndrome and Friedreich's ataxia; chemical agonism of the CUL2^FEM1B E3 ligase with proteomics and mitochondrial functional assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aed2525

### Intrinsic gestational timing governs human cerebellar development after preterm birth
**Georgios Sanidas & Panagiotis Kratimenos | Science**

**Why it matters:** Pairing in vivo neuroimaging and neurodevelopmental outcomes with postmortem spatial transcriptomics and histopathology, the study shows prematurity does not simply slow cerebellar growth but diverts its trajectory. Cerebellar growth and functional outcomes scaled with gestational age independent of modifiable perinatal exposures, and the molecular signature featured incomplete granule-cell maturation and impaired Purkinje-cell structural refinement — lineage-specific developmental asynchrony. Framing prematurity as biologically displaced maturation, in which infants of equivalent postmenstrual age occupy divergent developmental states, reframes how preterm cerebellar injury should be modeled. The human evidence is observational.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human preterm cohorts — in vivo MRI plus neurodevelopmental outcomes, with postmortem spatial transcriptomics and histopathology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aeb9095

### ARRDC4 drives Tau pathology via USP14-mediated deubiquitination and acts as a novel therapeutic target in Alzheimer's disease
**Jiaqi Liu & Steven X Hou | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Why it matters:** The work nominates ARRDC4 as an upstream regulator of tau proteostasis: it is upregulated in human and animal AD datasets, correlates with tau burden, and brings tau into proximity with the deubiquitinase USP14 to suppress tau ubiquitination — thereby promoting tau phosphorylation, self-association, seeded aggregation, secretion and inflammatory activation. Genetic ARRDC4 deletion or pharmacological disruption of the ARRDC4–USP14 interface restored tau ubiquitination and ameliorated pathology, neuroinflammation, neuronal injury and behavioral deficits across cellular, Drosophila and mouse models, identifying a druggable protein–protein interface. Causal evidence is from model systems; human data are correlative.

**Model/system & primary method:** Drosophila RNAi screen, human AD datasets, and mouse tauopathy models; proteomics and proximity labeling with small-molecule interface disruption.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71926

### APOE ε4 gene dose drives a canonical and predictable topography of amyloid deposition
**Augusto J Mendes & Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Why it matters:** In voxel-wise amyloid PET from 781 amyloid-positive individuals across two cohorts (ADNI and Geneva), APOE ε4 not only raised amyloid burden but constrained where amyloid deposited. Carriers' distributions aligned more closely with canonical AD topography than non-carriers, with a clear gene-dose effect on spatial consistency: homozygotes showed the highest inter-subject homogeneity, then heterozygotes, then non-carriers, robustly across tracers and clinical stages. This argues for genotype-stratified spatial readouts in anti-amyloid trials. The design is cross-sectional and associative.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human amyloid PET (n=781, two cohorts); voxel-wise topographic-similarity and gene-dose regression adjusted for global amyloid burden.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71935

### The TBK1–OTUB2–IRF3 signalling complex promotes neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration
**Jianzhao Zhang & Xu Wang | Brain**

**Why it matters:** cGAS–STING-driven chronic inflammation contributes to neurodegeneration in aging and ALS. The authors identify the deubiquitinase OTUB2 as a node that amplifies STING signaling: OTUB2 suppresses K63 ubiquitination of TBK1 (favoring RIPK1-mediated cell death) and scaffolds the TBK1–IRF3 complex to enable IRF3 phosphorylation and dimerization. Genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition of OTUB2 reduced neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration in hTARDBP transgenic, AAV-TDP43 and aged mouse models, nominating OTUB2 as a therapeutic target. Causal evidence is mouse-based.

**Model/system & primary method:** hTARDBP transgenic and AAV-TDP43 mouse models plus aged mice; genetic and pharmacological OTUB2 loss-of-function with biochemical ubiquitination assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag378

### Rapid longitudinal flights cause time shifts and Sun compass error
**Patrick Lewin & Tim Guilford | Science**

**Key findings:** GPS tracking of homing Manx shearwaters shows that east–west flights create mismatches between the birds' endogenous clock and local solar time, producing predictable Sun-compass orientation errors. Sun-compass use persisted even when compass-independent cues (coast following) were available. The work establishes that conserved chronobiological mechanisms impose measurable navigational constraints and opens wild chronobiology to GPS-scale study.

**Model/system & primary method:** Free-ranging Manx shearwaters (*Puffinus puffinus*); GPS tracking of homing flights with Sun-compass orientation modeling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adz2542

### Pervasive patterns in bird songs resemble those in human speech and song and are independent of vocal learning
**Logan S James & Jon T Sakata | Science**

**Key findings:** Across 367 bird species (>74,500 vocal elements) spanning vocal learners (oscines, hummingbirds) and non-learners (suboscines, nightjars), plus 52 human languages, bird songs showed acoustic patterning resembling prevalent patterns in human speech and song. Vocal learning did not systematically affect this patterning or its similarity to human vocalization, pointing to convergent motor mechanisms rather than learning itself as the shared substrate.

**Model/system & primary method:** Comparative acoustic analysis of global corpora (367 bird species, 52 human languages).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adr9983

### Whole-body single-cell atlas of an adult vertebrate, Danionella cerebrum
**Kutay Deniz Atabay & Peter W Reddien | Science**

**Key findings:** Region-stratified single-cell RNA-seq with spatial transcriptomics across the transparent teleost *Danionella cerebrum* yields a whole-vertebrate cell-type atlas, delineating spatially distinct neural cell types, cells carrying adult molecular positional memory, and paedomorphic features; regeneration datasets captured expression dynamics during telencephalon regeneration. The resource enables comparative and regenerative neuroscience in a tractable adult vertebrate.

**Model/system & primary method:** Adult *Danionella cerebrum*; region-stratified scRNA-seq with spatial transcriptomics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aeg1125

### ALS-linked FUS mutation impairs GEMIN5-dependent mRNA recruitment to neuronal late endosomes
**Adriano Setti & Irene Bozzoni | Cell Reports**

**Key findings:** In human motor neurons, RAB7A-positive late endosomes act as selective carriers for mRNAs enriched in endosomal, axonal and synaptic functions; conserved 5′UTR elements serve as localization signals read by the RNA-binding protein GEMIN5 to load transcripts. ALS-associated conditions showed loss of endosome-associated transcripts and mislocalization of GEMIN5 off endosomes, identifying endosomal mRNA loading as a vulnerable axis in neuronal homeostasis.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived motor neurons; APEX2 proximity labeling of RAB7A endosomes with eCLIP and localization assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118206

### Alzheimer's pathology mediates arteriolosclerosis and cerebral amyloid angiopathy associations with cognition
**Cellas A Hayes & Kyan Younes | Brain**

**Key findings:** In 1,269 autopsy-confirmed decedents, arteriolosclerosis was independently associated with worse memory, executive function and language, whereas cerebral amyloid angiopathy's cognitive associations were largely (64–91%) statistically mediated by AD neuropathologic change. Arteriolosclerosis retained significant direct effects while CAA acted mostly through AD pathology, clarifying distinct pathways by which vascular and neurodegenerative processes shape cognition. The cohort is cross-sectional at autopsy and the mediation is statistical, not causal.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human autopsy cohort (n=1,269); multivariable regression with bootstrap mediation of domain-specific cognition.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag380

### A sulfotyrosine motif in the human TrkB extracellular domain is required for activation by the agonist antibody ZEB85
**David C Briggs & Neil Q McDonald | PNAS**

**Key findings:** Structural and functional mapping localized the TrkB agonist antibody ZEB85's epitope to the receptor's juxtamembrane domain and identified a sulfotyrosine modification at residue 400 as essential for ZEB85 binding and TrkB activation. Co-structures of ZEB85 and BDNF revealed independent binding sites, with the BDNF site extending into the juxtamembrane domain but not requiring sulfotyrosine-400, explaining how BDNF can displace ZEB85. The work details a route to selective TrkB activation that avoids the problematic BDNF–p75 death axis.

**Model/system & primary method:** Structural biology (co-structures, AlphaFold modeling) with ligand-sequestration assays in cultured human neurons.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2618780123

### Cerebellar subregions support social cognition in early behavioural variant frontotemporal dementia
**Yu Chen & Katherine P Rankin | Brain**

**Key findings:** In 107 early-stage bvFTD patients and 100 controls, structural integrity of posterior cerebellar subregions — particularly right Crus I/II — contributed selectively to high-level theory-of-mind performance, independent of cortical atrophy and not mediated by executive function, while dynamic emotion-reading tasks engaged a more distributed cerebello-cerebral pattern. The findings extend cortex-centric models of social dysfunction in bvFTD to include cerebellar networks. Evidence is cross-sectional and structural.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human bvFTD cohort (n=107 plus 100 controls); functional cerebellar parcellation of structural MRI with partial-least-squares/dominance analysis and structural equation modeling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag381

### IFN-γ and proinflammatory cytokines induce astrocyte programs that support antigen-dependent CD4+ T cell restimulation
**Theodore M Fisher & Shane A Liddelow | Cell Reports**

**Key findings:** IFN-γ with proinflammatory cytokines drives astrocytes into an MHC-II+ state expressing costimulatory molecules and lymphocyte-recruiting chemokines; such MHC-II+GFAP+ astrocytes localize to brain surfaces across mouse neuroinflammation models and in human neurological disease tissue. In co-culture these astrocytes supported rapid antigen-dependent expansion of pre-activated CD4+ T cells, and human iPSC-derived astrocytes showed equivalent induction, defining a cytokine-induced astrocyte antigen-presenting state at neuroimmune interfaces.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse neuroinflammation models, primary and human iPSC-derived astrocytes, and astrocyte–T cell co-culture; single-cell RNA-seq reanalysis with in vitro functional assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118168

### Tau is an m6A-associated RNA binding protein that causes RNA dysregulation in disease
**G. Dixon & M. Kampmann | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In postmortem MAPT-variant brain and MAPT V337M iPSC-derived neurons, tau pathology was accompanied by widespread changes in RNA-binding protein abundance and elevated total RNA. A CRISPRi screen implicated m6A-associated proteins including hnRNPA2B1; eCLIP showed tau binds neuron-specific transcripts (3′UTR and broadly intronic regions) overlapping m6A peaks, with binding reduced by hnRNPA2B1 knockdown or m6A-writer inhibition. The data argue for a direct RNA-regulatory role for tau in tauopathy. Cellular and postmortem evidence; interpret cautiously.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem MAPT-variant brain and MAPT V337M iNeurons; CRISPRi screen with eCLIP.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.26.762095

### Disrupting SORL1–retromer endosomal recycling in mice drives the age-dependent neurodegenerative signature of Alzheimer's disease
**Y. H. Qureshi & S. A. Small | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Across multi-year studies in over 140 wild-type and SORL1-deficient mice, SORL1 deficiency induced an age-dependent, AD-like atrophy pattern centered on the trans-entorhinal cortex; atrophy was prevented by restoring SORL1 or overexpressing the retromer protein VPS26b, and postmortem analysis confirmed the atrophy reflects neuronal loss. Proteomics linked atrophy to reductions in cell-death suppressors, one of which was also deficient in the trans-entorhinal cortex of AD patients, suggesting SORL1–retromer disruption as a tractable common neurodegeneration pathway. Mouse causal evidence; not peer reviewed.

**Model/system & primary method:** Wild-type and SORL1-deficient mice (n>140); longitudinal MRI with postmortem validation and proteomics.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.26.761980

### Genetic ablation of Sarm1 attenuates axonal degeneration in a mouse model of CMT2J
**A. Claessens & M. D'Antonio | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Crossing MpzT124M (CMT2J) mice onto a Sarm1-null background did not rescue electrophysiological deficits, but in 12-month homozygotes Sarm1 ablation partially reduced peripheral-nerve axonal degeneration and lowered plasma neurofilament light chain; slower-progressing heterozygotes showed improved motor performance and reduced pathology by 18 months. **Model/system & primary method:** MpzT124M × Sarm1-null mice; electrophysiology, nerve morphology and plasma neurofilament light chain.

### Repetitive levodopa treatment drives cell type-specific striatal adaptations associated with progressive dyskinesia in parkinsonian mice
**R. M. Paz & A. B. Nelson | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 6-OHDA parkinsonian mice, worsening of levodopa-induced dyskinesia with repeated treatment tracked with potentiation of levodopa-evoked responses in both D1- and D2-expressing medium spiny neurons, independent of dopamine release or intrinsic excitability. Strengthening of glutamatergic synapses onto D1-MSNs emerged as a key signature, while reduced D2-receptor sensitivity paralleled dyskinesia duration, implicating circuit-level striatal adaptations in progressive dyskinesia. Mouse model; not peer reviewed.

**Model/system & primary method:** 6-OHDA mouse model of PD/levodopa-induced dyskinesia; in vivo and ex vivo recordings of D1-/D2-MSNs.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.05.16.654598

### Differential endothelial cell remodeling in Down syndrome and APP duplication drives distinct blood–brain barrier dysfunction
**L. Valay & M.-C. Potier | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** iPSC-derived endothelial cells from Down syndrome and APP-duplication backgrounds both secreted more Aβ40/Aβ42 than isogenic controls but with distinct peptide profiles and opposite barrier phenotypes: Down-syndrome cells increased junctional protein expression and reduced permeability, whereas APP-duplication cells showed increased permeability. Transcriptomics revealed extensive dysregulation in Down-syndrome cells (junctional, angiogenic, immune and apoptosis pathways) versus milder APP-duplication changes, suggesting that non-APP chromosome-21 genes modulate cerebral amyloid angiopathy vulnerability. In vitro iPSC model; not peer reviewed.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived endothelial cells (Down syndrome vs APP duplication, with isogenic controls); permeability, junctional-protein and transcriptomic profiling.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.29.762815
