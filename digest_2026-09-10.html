1Closed-loop auditory stimulation in phase with slow waves during sleep enhances cerebrospinal fluid flow in humans

Sci Transl Med  Joshua Levitt & Laura D Lewis
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
This provides the first causal, in-human demonstration that cortical slow waves drive cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) flow, rather than the two merely co-occurring in NREM sleep. Using real-time EEG denoising and a neural-network targeting strategy, the authors delivered auditory clicks during simultaneous EEG-MRI and increased both slow-wave amplitude and downstream CSF inflow waves in healthy adults. The effect was strictly phase-dependent — present only when stimuli aligned to slow-wave peaks, not troughs — which is strong evidence for a genuine slow-wave-to-flow coupling and a plausible lever on glymphatic clearance relevant to brain aging.
Model & method
Healthy adults; closed-loop auditory stimulation with concurrent EEG and fast fMRI measurement of CSF inflow.
https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aea5469

2Mapping cell-type- and age-dependent neuronal vulnerability through genome-wide in vivo CRISPRi screens in the mouse brain

Neuron  Risheng Lin & Ruilin Tian
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Brain atlases are largely correlative; this work makes them functional. A scalable, cell-type-resolved in vivo CRISPRi platform run across four neuronal populations at three ages defines a consensus set of 269 neuronal core essential genes, many missed by in vitro screens, and resolves divergent excitatory-versus-inhibitory dependencies (validated for Exosc9 and Ostm1). Critically for aging biology, it identifies age-specific dependencies enriched in mitochondrial and translational pathways that align with transcriptional signatures of the aging human brain, offering a candidate map of where neurons become vulnerable with age.
Model & method
Mouse brain in vivo; genome-wide CRISPR interference with cell-type-resolved readout across youth-to-aging timepoints.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.006

3Plasma phosphorylated tau 217 concentrations, APOE genotype, and timing of cognitive impairment across diverse racial and ethnic groups

Lancet Neurol  Yuexuan Xu & Richard Mayeux
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
In a pooled participant-level analysis of seven multi-ethnic cohorts (8,582 adults; 16% Black, 37% Hispanic or other), APOE-ε4 status meaningfully modified the prognostic value of plasma p-tau217. Higher baseline p-tau217 predicted incident cognitive impairment more strongly in ε4 carriers (HR 1.76) than non-carriers (HR 1.26), and each 1-SD rise shortened time to impairment by 24% in carriers versus 13% in non-carriers, with survival curves diverging 3–4 years before symptom onset. The diversity of the sample and the pre-symptomatic timing signal make this directly relevant to biomarker-guided risk stratification and prevention-trial enrollment.
Model & method
Human; pooled longitudinal cohort analysis with logistic/Cox models, survival analysis, and random survival forests.
https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00313-3

4Safety, tolerability, and efficacy of the RIPK1 inhibitor SAR443820 in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (HIMALAYA): a phase 2 trial

Lancet Neurol  Merit E Cudkowicz & HIMALAYA Phase 2 Study Group
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
RIPK1-driven necroptosis and inflammation have been a leading neuroinflammatory target in ALS, and SAR443820 is a selective, oral, CNS-penetrant inhibitor. In this multicentre, double-blind phase 2 trial (n=305, 2:1), the drug produced no benefit on ALSFRS-R decline over 24 weeks (least-squares mean difference −0.41; 95% CI −1.71 to 0.88) and was associated with more hepatic enzyme elevation and discontinuations; the sponsor concluded further development is not warranted. This is an important negative result that constrains the RIPK1 hypothesis in ALS, though the short 24-week window and early termination limit inference about longer-term or subgroup effects.
Model & method
Human; randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 RCT with ALSFRS-R primary endpoint.
https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00277-2

5Neocortical long-range inhibition promotes cortical synchrony and sleep

Nature  Jacob M Ratliff & Renata Batista-Brito
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Sleep regulation has been framed as largely subcortical; this study shows the neocortex itself carries a dedicated sleep-promoting circuit. A sparse, genetically distinct class of Sst/Chodl-co-expressing GABAergic neurons is selectively active in low-arousal states and, despite its rarity, exerts neocortex-wide influence through long-range axons targeting multiple regions at once. Optogenetic activation is sufficient to induce the multi-region cortical synchronization characteristic of slow-wave sleep and to drive sleep behaviour, establishing long-range cortical inhibition as an active regulator of state rather than a passive readout.
Model & method
Mouse; in vivo recordings with cell-type-specific optogenetic activation of Sst-Chodl interneurons.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10876-y

Nat Neurosci  Wouter H van der Valk & Heiko Locher
Key findings
HIEDRA maps sensory and nonsensory epithelia, neurons, and mesenchyme across the first and second trimesters of human inner ear development, including undercharacterized secretory cells required for ion homeostasis. SORDINO: silent, sensitive, specific and artifact-resistant fMRI in awake, behaving mice

Nat Neurosci  Martin J MacKinnon & Yen-Yu Ian Shih
Key findings
SORDINO is a zero-echo-time fMRI technique that acquires data during gradient direction changes at ultra-low slew rate. At 9.4 T it outperforms conventional gradient-echo EPI and standard ZTE while being effectively silent and resistant to motion and susceptibility artifacts, and it reveals a T1-based contrast mechanism distinct from BOLD. The authors demonstrate brain-wide activity and functional connectivity mapping in awake, behaving mice, addressing stress and motion confounds that limit rodent fMRI.
Model & method
Awake mouse; zero-echo-time fMRI at 9.4 T.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02424-8

Imaging cellular activity across all organs reveals body-wide circuits

Nature  Virginie M S Ruetten & Misha B Ahrens
Key findings
WHOLISTIC combines volumetric fluorescence microscopy, machine learning, and pancellular calcium-sensor expression to record second-timescale activity across the entire vertebrate body at cellular resolution in larval zebrafish (with proof of concept in adult Danionella). Neurally relevant findings include meningeal responses to ketamine and brainstem-controlled redistribution of body-wide blood flow, and pairing with optogenetics enabled all-optical causal dissection of brain–body interactions.
Model & method
Larval zebrafish; whole-organism live calcium imaging with expansion microscopy and optogenetics.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10979-6

Neural geometry in the human hippocampus enables generalization across spatial position and gaze

Neuron  Assia Chericoni & Benjamin Y Hayden
Key findings
Recording from human hippocampal populations during a virtual prey-pursuit task, the authors found mixed-selective neurons encoding the positions of self, prey, and predator plus gaze direction in mostly orthogonal subspaces. The geometries of these subspaces could be aligned by simple linear transformations and supported partial generalization — a rule learned for one agent transferred to another — suggesting hippocampal spatial knowledge is organized as a family of geometrically related manifolds.
Model & method
Human single-neuron recordings; population geometry / subspace analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.004

Caloric restriction modulates genome-wide somatic mutation in mice

Cell  Marta Grońska-Pęski & Gilad D Evrony
Key findings
Using high-fidelity duplex sequencing of liver, kidney, hepatocytes, and cerebellar neurons, the authors show caloric restriction reduces genome-wide somatic mutation burden — both substitutions and indels — across tissues, including postmitotic cerebellar neurons. CR decreased activity of the ubiquitous SBS5 mutational process, and the reduction was greatest in transcriptionally inactive regions, establishing genomic integrity as a diet-modifiable axis of aging relevant to neuronal genome maintenance.
Model & method
Mouse; duplex DNA sequencing of bulk tissues and sorted cerebellar neurons.

Intravenous gene therapy improves life span and clinical outcomes in a feline model of Sandhoff disease

Sci Transl Med  Anne S Maguire & Douglas R Martin
Key findings
In a feline model of Sandhoff disease treated presymptomatically at 1 month, a single intravenously delivered bicistronic AAV vector extended survival dose-dependently — from 4.3 months untreated to 8.3 (low dose) and 12.4 months (high dose) — while preventing full-body tremors and reducing CSF/serum markers of CNS damage. MRI/spectroscopy, GM2 ganglioside storage, Hex activity, neuroinflammation, and myelin deficits were partially normalized, supporting intravenous delivery as a route toward clinical translation.
Model & method
Feline (cat) SD model; intravenous AAV gene therapy with imaging and biomarker readouts.

Monoclonal antibodies inhibiting MMP-9 activity alleviate peripheral neuropathy in diabetic mice

Sci Transl Med  Ki Baek Lee & Xin Ge
Key findings
The authors raised selective, proteolytically stable monoclonal antibodies against MMP-9 — a driver of neuroinflammation in diabetic peripheral neuropathy where small-molecule inhibitors lack specificity. Systemic mAb treatment alleviated mechanical and cold pain and restored hind-paw wound healing in diabetic mice, reduced epidermal nerve-fiber degeneration, improved nerve mitochondrial function, and promoted angiogenesis. Human dorsal root ganglia from patients with diabetes showed elevated MMP-9, and both rare coding and common MMP9 variants associated with neuropathic pain, adding human genetic support.
Model & method
Diabetic mouse models plus human DRG and genetic analysis; function-blocking monoclonal antibodies.
https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.adw3558

TDP-43 controls RNA structure through high affinity lattice interactions

Nat Commun  Rahul Vivek & Samuel E Butcher
Key findings
TDP-43, the aggregation-prone protein central to ALS and FTD, is shown to bind GU-repeat RNA with one of the highest affinities and specificities measured for an RNA-binding protein. Binding and formation of the intramolecular pUG quadruplex ("pUG fold") are mutually exclusive: slow folding plus a rapid on-rate lets TDP-43 capture single-stranded RNA, which it recognizes as a 1D lattice of overlapping sites. RNA-facilitated protein–protein interactions and an auto-inhibitory interaction tune cooperative occupancy, revealing an RNA-folding/protein-recognition interplay potentially relevant to disease.
Model & method
In vitro biophysics/biochemistry of TDP-43–RNA interactions.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76721-y

Tau-associated neuronal loss in the intermediate nucleus of the human hypothalamus, a putative VLPO analog, in PSP and Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimers Dement  Shima Rastegar-Pouyani & Lea T Grinberg
Key findings
Stereology of post-mortem hypothalami (n=30) shows the intermediate nucleus — a putative ventrolateral preoptic (NREM-promoting) analog — is a disease-sensitive node. Advanced PSP showed ~84% neuronal reduction versus baseline, while AD neuropathologic change caused progressive, Braak-stage-dependent loss preferentially affecting galanin-positive neurons (~77% reduction in late AD), which also carried higher phospho-tau burden. The findings offer a neuropathological substrate for the divergent sleep phenotypes of PSP and AD, though sleep/wake data were not available for this autopsy sample.
Model & method
Human post-mortem hypothalamus; marker-guided immunohistochemistry and stereology.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71808

ARID5A RNA-binding coordinates microglial defense and ferroptosis in iPSC-derived models

Nat Commun  Danielle M Schafer & Gene W Yeo
Key findings
ARID5A is identified as an RNA-binding protein highly expressed in microglia that regulates splicing and translation of targets in lysosomal, immune, and iron-metabolism pathways. In iPSC-derived microglia it modulates lysosomal activity, cytokine secretion, iron accumulation, and ferroptosis; ARID5A knockdown reduced neuronal ferroptosis in co-culture, and in TREM2-T66M mutant microglia ARID5A depletion restored dysregulated lysosomal and metabolic function — linking an RBP regulatory network to microglial states and iron-dependent neurodegeneration.

Astrocytes mediate the dopaminergic modulation of tonic GABAergic signaling in substantia nigra

Sci Adv  DeNard V Simmons & D James Surmeier
Key findings
In ex vivo mouse midbrain slices, D2-receptor signaling not only presynaptically inhibited phasic GABA release but also unexpectedly suppressed a tonic, GABA_A-mediated inhibition of substantia nigra pars reticulata (SNr) neuron firing. This tonic modulation depended on GAT-1-mediated GABA release from ALDH1A1-expressing dopaminergic neurons, while the disinhibitory action of D2 agonists required D2-expressing astrocytes and GAT-3 uptake — implicating astrocytes as active participants in the nigral circuit dysfunction underlying Parkinsonian motor deficits.
Model & method
Ex vivo mouse brain slice electrophysiology of SNr circuits.
https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adq8840

Shared genetic architecture between DTI-ALPS traits and neurodegenerative diseases

Alzheimers Dement  Hong Zhou & Feng Liu
Key findings
Integrating GWAS for three DTI-ALPS (perivascular diffusivity) traits and seven neurodegenerative diseases, the authors quantify widespread but heterogeneous polygenic overlap and identify 22 shared loci — including novel signals at GAK and SIAH3 and strongest convergence at 17q21.31 (the MAPT region). Shared loci mapped to 183 genes enriched in the endolysosomal system and microtubule cytoskeleton, providing genetic support that glymphatic/perivascular clearance and neurodegeneration share mechanistic underpinnings rather than being merely correlated.
Model & method
Human genetics; cross-trait polygenic overlap (MiXeR), conjunctional FDR, and gene mapping.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71820

mRNA editing of the Alzheimer's risk gene APOE

bioRxiv  V Blanc & R Lathe
Key findings
Challenging the assumption that APOE allelotype is fixed in the genome, the authors report that human brain expresses APOE transcripts with C-to-U (and U-to-C) changes at exactly the two positions (AE4, AE2) that define the ε2/ε3/ε4 alleles, with no corresponding genomic change — up to ~10% editing, and up to ~8% at AE2 in single-cell microglial data. APOBEC1 transfection efficiently edited APOE4 mRNA at AE4, and up to 4% of plasma APOE peptides were non-genomically encoded, suggesting functional edited protein in vivo. If robust, RNA editing that interconverts APOE allelic forms would reframe how APOE risk is assessed; the in vivo editing extent and its disease relevance remain to be established.
Model & method
Human brain/plasma datasets, APOE knock-in mice, and cell-line APOBEC1 transfection; transcriptomic and proteomic analysis.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.749263

Dystrophic changes of nigrostriatal axons harboring a Synj1 Parkinson mutation suggest catastrophic failure of endocytic mechanisms

bioRxiv  Y Wu & P De Camilli
Key findings
In mice carrying the synaptojanin-1 R258Q mutation (which selectively impairs the Sac1 domain and causes early-onset familial Parkinsonism), correlative light–FIB-SEM reveals that a subset of dopaminergic axons in the dorsolateral striatum develop focal dilations packed with massive onion-like, DAT-enriched plasma-membrane infoldings, sometimes engulfing neighboring cells. The dysmorphia coincided with a regional dopamine-release deficit, consistent with an exocytosis/endocytosis imbalance that only a vulnerable subset of axons cannot compensate for. This v2 revision was first posted June 2026.
Model & method
Synj1 R258Q knock-in mouse striatum; 3D correlative light microscopy–FIB-SEM with dopamine-release measurement.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.24.733515

Impaired cerebrovascular–cerebrospinal fluid coupling is associated with non-motor features of Lewy body disease

medRxiv  A Ignatavicius & E Matar
Key findings
In 39 participants (17 Parkinson's disease, 10 dementia with Lewy bodies, 12 controls), resting-state fMRI-derived BOLD–CSF coupling — a proposed non-invasive proxy for glymphatic function — was weaker in both patient groups than controls, most prominently in unimodal and attentional networks. Weaker coupling tracked hallucination severity, cognitive fluctuations, poorer sleep, and impaired attentional working memory, but not motor burden, and related partly age-dependently to basal forebrain and brainstem volumes. This is a small, cross-sectional study; associations are correlational and await replication. This v5 revision was first posted May 2026.
Model & method
Human; resting-state fMRI BOLD–CSF cross-correlation with clinical and volumetric measures.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.13.26353090

Pre-symptomatic proteomic and metabolomic profiling identifies compensated ER-redox-metabolic adaptation and early nuclear vulnerability in neuronal ERO1L toxicity

bioRxiv  L Lo Piccolo & S Jantrapirom
Key findings
In Drosophila, endogenous ERO1L (an ER oxidative-folding enzyme elevated in aging and neurodegeneration) rose with age, and neuronal — but not glial, muscle, or fat-body — ERO1L elevation shortened lifespan and caused progressive locomotor decline. Multi-omics at a defined pre-symptomatic stage (before ROS accumulation) showed adaptive remodeling of ER proteostasis, redox defense, and mitochondrial-energy pathways alongside selective loss of chromatin/RNA-homeostasis proteins (HP1, dFmr1, Piwi) and de-repression of transposable elements — pointing to early nuclear/RNA vulnerability preceding overt decline. Evidence is from an invertebrate overexpression model.
Model & method
Drosophila melanogaster; tissue-specific ERO1L elevation with proteomic and metabolomic profiling.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.06.749746
