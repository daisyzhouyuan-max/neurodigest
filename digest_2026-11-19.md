### Hypothalamic volume and cell-type changes in ageing and Alzheimer's disease
**Doudou Yu & Ashley E Webb | Nature**

**Why it matters:** HypoAD pairs high-resolution MRI from 458 people with single-nucleus RNA-seq of 614,403 hypothalamic nuclei (young, AD, age-matched non-dementia controls), filling a long-standing gap in how this neuroendocrine hub changes with age and disease. Subregions governing metabolism, stress and circadian rhythm are selectively vulnerable, losing volume and shifting transcriptionally in both ageing and AD. Machine-learning models nominate the inflammatory response and circadian regulators as the leading cellular predictors of AD, with microglia adopting a pro-inflammatory state and inhibitory neurons in sleep- and circadian-regulating subregions showing the most profound changes, including disrupted ligand–receptor and GPCR signalling.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem hypothalamus; structural MRI (n = 458) and snRNA-seq (614,403 nuclei), with machine-learning classification. Cross-sectional, so vulnerability is associative rather than causal.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11305-w

### Comparing plasma p-tau217 and plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 with CSF biomarkers for assessing Alzheimer's disease pathology
**Noëlle Warmenhoven & Sebastian Palmqvist | Nat Aging**

**Why it matters:** Across a memory clinic (n = 129), primary care (n = 220) and research cohort (n = 165), fully automated Lumipulse plasma p-tau217 and the p-tau217/Aβ42 ratio identified amyloid pathology (Aβ-PET reference) with AUCs of 0.90–0.96. Using regulatory-authorized cutoffs, plasma p-tau217 (83–91% accuracy, single cutoff; 90–97% with two cutoffs) was statistically comparable to regulatory-authorized CSF biomarkers in symptomatic individuals. The result strengthens the case for replacing lumbar puncture with a blood test for amyloid confirmation in clinical practice.

**Model/system & primary method:** Three human cohorts (n = 514 total); automated plasma immunoassay versus CSF Aβ42/Aβ40 and p-tau181/Aβ42, benchmarked against Aβ-PET visual read. Performance shown in symptomatic individuals only.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-026-01259-z

### Safety and efficacy of daily oral edaravone (FNP122) in patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ADORE): a multicentre, double-blind, phase 3, randomised controlled trial
**Ruben P A van Eijk & ADORE Study Group | Lancet Neurol**

**Why it matters:** This 48-week, 2:1 randomised phase 3 trial (302 treated; 205 edaravone, 97 placebo across 37 European hospitals) tested a once-daily oral formulation of the free-radical scavenger edaravone in a broad ALS population. The primary endpoint was negative: least-squares mean change in ALSFRS-R was −12.6 (edaravone) versus −13.4 (placebo), a difference of 0.78 points (95% CI −1.44 to 3.00; p = 0.49). The drug was well tolerated with no excess adverse events, but the data do not support oral edaravone at this dose for slowing ALS progression — a clarifying result for an agent whose disease-modifying effect has long been debated.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human phase 3 double-blind RCT; ALSFRS-R total score change at 48 weeks, intention-to-treat on treated patients.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00336-4

### Temporal interference stimulation targeting the ventromedial prefrontal cortex enhances reward learning and improves depression scores
**Feixue Wang & Yiheng Tu | Sci Transl Med**

**Why it matters:** Transcranial temporal interference stimulation (tTIS) non-invasively reaches deep cortex by delivering two offset high-frequency fields that interfere at a target. Here, neuroimaging and modelling first localised a bidirectional vmPFC–ventral striatum loop encoding reward prediction errors and scaling with individual learning rate. Alpha-frequency (10 Hz) tTIS over vmPFC — but not theta (5 Hz) — enhanced reward learning and reshaped frontostriatal connectivity. In a randomised trial of 69 individuals with subclinical depression, three daily alpha-tTIS sessions reduced depressive symptoms, with benefit persisting at 7-day follow-up. A proof-of-concept for circuit-targeted, non-invasive modulation of deep reward circuitry.

**Model/system & primary method:** Healthy adults (fMRI + computational modelling) and a randomised sham-controlled trial in subclinical depression (n = 69). Early-stage and subclinical; durability beyond one week untested.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.adz9823

### Rescue of glutaric aciduria type I by GalNAc-conjugated siRNA against aminoadipate semialdehyde synthase
**Yunhan Ma & Karl-Dimiter Bissig | Sci Transl Med**

**Why it matters:** Glutaric aciduria type I (GCDH deficiency) drives accumulation of neurotoxic organic acids, striatal injury and movement disorders; one-third of patients still deteriorate despite dietary management. A liver-targeted, GalNAc-conjugated siRNA against *Aass* (an upstream enzyme in lysine degradation) implements substrate reduction upstream of the block. In neonatal *Gcdh*−/− mice it rescued lethality, improved brain pathology and lowered toxic metabolites in brain and other organs, while serum glutaric acid, 3-hydroxyglutaric acid and glutarylcarnitine tracked neurological outcome and could serve as therapeutic-monitoring biomarkers.

**Model/system & primary method:** Neonatal *Gcdh*−/− mouse model; GalNAc-siRNA knockdown of hepatic *Aass*, with metabolite and survival readouts. Mouse-only causal evidence.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aej2067

### A glucose-powered artificial neuroimmune axis for metabolic control
**Weijun Xiu & Yuxin Liu | Nature**

**Key findings:** NeuroAxis is a battery-free, closed-loop electroceutical that emulates the vagal anti-inflammatory reflex and is powered solely by endogenous glucose, with metabolic interoceptive feedback. In a rat type 1 diabetes model it promoted recovery of insulin-producing β-cells and dampened inflammation; in a type 2 model it drove macrophage remodelling and reduced systemic inflammation. Both models showed restored glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity, with effects persisting after treatment stopped.

**Model/system & primary method:** Rat models of type 1 and type 2 diabetes; implantable self-powered autonomic neuromodulation device.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11251-7

### TransA-MERFISH reveals divergent post-retinal direction-selective circuits
**Fei Wang & Xin Duan | Nature**

**Key findings:** Combining an anterograde trans-neuronal tracer (mWGA-mCherry) with MERFISH spatial transcriptomics, the authors mapped the synaptic targets of four retinal ganglion cell subclasses in the superior colliculus at cell-type resolution. On–off direction-selective ganglion cells innervate several direction-selective collicular cell types but, unexpectedly, not a type previously reported as strongly direction-selective — revealing commingled ooDSGC-dependent and -independent direction-selective circuits, confirmed by targeted ablation of retinal direction-selective input.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse visual system; trans-neuronal tracing + MERFISH (TransA-MERFISH), with functional validation after circuit ablation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11235-7

### Neotenic expansion of adult-born dentate granule cells reconfigures GABAergic inhibition to enhance social memory consolidation
**Ain Chung & Amar Sahay | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Synchronous expansion of 4-week-old immature (but not 8-week-old mature) adult-born dentate granule cells recruited fast-spiking parvalbumin interneurons in CA3/CA2, increased feed-forward inhibition, reduced social memory interference and enhanced social memory consolidation. In vivo recordings showed larger, longer sharp-wave ripples with greater modulation by social experience; chemogenetic manipulation implicated CA3/CA2 interneurons as necessary and sufficient. The work mechanistically links protracted neurogenesis (neoteny) to inhibitory-circuit remodelling and memory.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse hippocampus; targeted expansion of adult-born granule cells, LFP recording, and chemogenetic interneuron manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02503-w

### Fast-scan cyclic voltammetry for real-time serotonin dynamics in human iPSC-derived neural spheroids
**Bettina Bohl & Parastoo Hashemi | Cell Stem Cell**

**Key findings:** The authors adapted fast-scan cyclic voltammetry — a workhorse for neurotransmitter measurement in animals — to human iPSC-derived 3D serotonergic spheroids, achieving reproducible detection of spontaneous and evoked serotonin release. Serotonin dynamics differed between reuptake inhibitors, responded to a molecular stress mediator relevant to antidepressant action, and could be tracked in co-cultures with microglia-like cells under inflammatory conditions, giving a label-free human platform for dissecting serotonergic signalling.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived 3D serotonergic spheroids and microglia co-cultures; fast-scan cyclic voltammetry.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2026.10.019

### Noradrenergic input from sympathetic nerves to fibroblasts regulates lung fibrosis
**Genta Ishikawa & Erica L Herzog | Sci Transl Med**

**Key findings:** Sympathetic nerve fibres were enriched in fibrotic alveolar regions near α-SMA⁺ effector fibroblasts in the bleomycin mouse model and in human IPF and SSc-ILD lung. Co-culture with sympathetic nerves boosted fibrotic markers, and sympathetic-specific deletion of the NGF receptor NTRK1 mitigated fibrosis. α1-adrenoreceptor blockade reduced collagen accumulation in mice and human precision-cut lung slices, and fibroblast-specific deletion of *Adra1d* suppressed fibrosis — defining a noradrenaline–ADRA1D fibrogenic unit and a candidate neural target for fibrotic lung disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** Bleomycin mouse model, human IPF/SSc-ILD explants and precision-cut slices; genetic and pharmacologic targeting of sympathetic innervation and α1-adrenoreceptors.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.adw3631

### Development and validation of interpretable multidomain models for multi-pathology prediction in Alzheimer's disease and related dementias
**Chenyin Chu & Yijun Pan | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Mixed neuropathology is the rule rather than the exception in dementia, yet is rarely predicted jointly. Using 1,463 autopsied ROSMAP participants and external validation in 2,613 NACC participants, an explainable boosting-machine classifier chain predicted seven neuropathologic outcomes with macro-averaged AUROC 0.80 (ROSMAP) and 0.77 (NACC). Predictor contributions showed both shared and pathology-specific patterns, and a lower-burden 15-predictor model traded modest accuracy for practicality — a step toward antemortem characterisation of co-pathology.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human autopsy cohorts (ROSMAP, NACC); interpretable multi-label machine learning. Retrospective; prospective validation still needed.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71996

### Galectin-3 links microglial neuroinflammation to Aβ aggregation and lysosomal processing
**Lluís Camprubí-Ferrer & Tomas Deierborg | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** In human AD tissue, galectin-3⁺ (Gal3) microglia preferentially surround cored plaques and correlate with dystrophic neurites. In APP mice, Gal3 deficiency reduced plaque burden and neuritic dystrophy, increased plaque compaction and enhanced TREM2 coverage. Mechanistically, Gal3 shapes microglial Aβ uptake and lysosomal processing; cell-free, Gal3 binds fibrillar Aβ, inhibits secondary nucleation and alters fibril morphology, while Gal3 deletion suppresses type I interferon and neurodegenerative microglial signatures — placing Gal3 at the interface of amyloid remodelling, lysosomal homeostasis and microglial state.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem AD tissue, APP mice, in vitro microglia and cell-free aggregation assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2625362123

### Plasma eMTBR-tau243 and p-tau205 as T2 biomarkers for biological staging and prognosis in Alzheimer's disease
**Sohyun Yim & Sang Won Seo | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Beyond early (T1) markers, the endogenously cleaved microtubule-binding-region fragment eMTBR-tau243 and p-tau205 rose later in disease, consistent with fibrillar tau aggregation. Measured by dedicated LC-MS/MS against amyloid- and tau-PET, eMTBR-tau243 showed the strongest stage-dependent associations with regional tau-PET and cognition, outperforming T1 markers for predicting cross-sectional and longitudinal cognition. A plasma staging system combining p-tau217 and eMTBR-tau243 closely tracked PET-based tau staging — a route to blood-based biological staging to complement diagnostic markers.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cohort with amyloid-PET (subset tau-PET); multi-platform p-tau immunoassays plus LC-MS/MS for eMTBR-tau243.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78582-x

### Neuronal IL-34 programs microglial activity via the TGF-β–MEF2C axis to constrain neuroinflammation following brain ischemia
**Minshu Li & Qiang Liu | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** After middle cerebral artery occlusion, ischemic neurons upregulate IL-34–CSF1R signalling to restrain damaging microglial inflammation. Neuronal *Il34* overexpression reduced infarct volume and deficits, while knockdown worsened injury; microglial depletion abolished the protection. Single-cell profiling showed IL-34 preserved homeostatic microglial signatures and suppressed inflammatory genes via the transcription factor MEF2C, downstream of TGF-β signalling — microglia-specific *Mef2c* overexpression rescued protection even with TGF-β blockade, nominating a neuron-driven endogenous brake on post-stroke neuroinflammation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse MCAO stroke model; neuron- and microglia-specific genetic manipulation with single-cell RNA-seq. Mouse causal evidence.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2613902123

### Early middle meningeal artery embolisation plus standard treatment versus standard treatment alone for chronic subdural haematoma in France (OTEMACS): a randomised, open-label, controlled trial
**Sam Ng & OTEMACS study group | Lancet Neurol**

**Key findings:** In 214 patients (modified ITT) with symptomatic chronic subdural haematoma, adding early middle meningeal artery embolisation to standard care reduced the composite of reoperation, residual haematoma >10 mm or death at 90 days (32.7% vs 49.0%; absolute difference −16.3 points, 95% CI −29.3 to −3.3; nominal p = 0.015), with reoperation falling to 7.3% from 18.3% and no excess mortality or major complications. The authors caution that enrolment stopped early outside the prespecified efficacy boundary and the primary analysis excluded patients without an assessable outcome, so the benefit may be overestimated and needs confirmation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multicentre open-label RCT; MMA embolisation with Onyx liquid embolic system versus standard care.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00360-1

### Alpha-synuclein and neuromelanin converge to promote Parkinson's disease pathology
**A. Nicolau-Vera & M. Vila | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The authors propose a pathogenic feedback loop between α-synuclein (aSYN) and neuromelanin (NM) in the substantia nigra. In human midbrain, high-NM ventral nigral neurons showed elevated aSYN mRNA and preferential loss in PD. In a rat model with human-like, age-dependent NM accumulation (via targeted tyrosinase), co-expressing aSYN accelerated NM buildup, enhanced aSYN oligomerisation and worsened nigrostriatal degeneration. Mechanistically, aSYN oligomers reduced vesicular dopamine storage and impaired vesicle fusion, raising cytosolic dopamine available for oxidation into NM. If confirmed, this aSYN–NM axis could help explain the selective vulnerability of pigmented nigral neurons. Preprint; rodent and post-mortem associative evidence.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem midbrain and a tyrosinase-based rat NM model with aSYN co-expression.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.19.760151

### Altered age-dependent somatic mutational trajectories in ALS motor cortex neurons
**L. Liu & K. P. Kenna | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Reanalysing three single-nucleus RNA/ATAC datasets from ALS and control motor cortex, the authors recovered expected features of neuronal somatic mutagenesis (neuronal enrichment, age-associated accumulation, the clock-like SBS5 signature). ALS showed a disease-specific age-dependent trajectory: higher somatic mutation burden in younger ALS donors with a narrowing difference at older ages, with SBS5 the dominant fitted component and a similar pattern in an independent single-cell whole-genome dataset. The finding tentatively links somatic mutation to the age-dependence of neuronal vulnerability in ALS. Preprint; indirect variant calling restricted to accessible genomic regions.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human ALS and control motor cortex; somatic variant calling from single-nucleus RNA/ATAC-seq, with single-cell WGS replication.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.17.767052

### Spatiotemporal profiling reveals emergence of cell type-specific molecular aging and senescence programs in the spinal cord
**O. Kuksenko & H. Phatnani | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** A cross-species multi-omic atlas (LC-MS/MS proteomics, Visium spatial transcriptomics, snRNA-seq, multiplexed immunofluorescence) of mouse and human spinal cord aging. Shared age-associated increases in fibrinogen, laminin and immune proteins emerged across species. Downregulated programs (synaptic signalling, mitochondrial organisation, autophagy) were concentrated in grey-matter neurons, while upregulated vascular/immune programs localised to white-matter endothelial cells and microglia and overlapped senescence-associated genes; in human cord, endothelial cells and microglia were most enriched for CDKN1A and CDKN2A, respectively. **Model/system & primary method:** Mouse (lifespan) and human spinal cord; integrated spatial transcriptomics, snRNA-seq, proteomics and imaging.

### A plasma proteomic atlas of rare genetic neurodevelopmental disorders reveals convergent molecular signatures, neurodegenerative overlap, and a cross-disorder severity index
**R. N. Ramirez & A. C. Pfalzer | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** A harmonised plasma proteomic resource (>10,000 proteins across 27 rare genetic neurodevelopmental disorders plus unaffected relatives, with standardised adaptive-behaviour phenotypes). Genetically distinct disorders converged onto two proteomic clusters along a dominant axis separating cytoskeletal/vesicle-trafficking from metabolic/hepatic proteins, independent of gene function. Epilepsy left a reproducible genotype-independent plasma signature, and most disorders were enriched — more often inversely than concordantly — against AD, PD, FTD or ALS signatures, placing early-life monogenic disorders on a shared axis with late-onset degeneration. A multivariate plasma severity index tracked adaptive communication in held-out disorders. Preprint; some disorders recruited at single sites and reported descriptively.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human plasma proteomics across 27 neurodevelopmental disorders; differential abundance, clustering and cross-disorder modelling.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.16.767059

### α-synuclein seed amplification assay positivity is associated with motor progression in the prodromal stage independent of phenoconversion
**X. Deng & B. Xiao | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 2,532 PPMI prodromal participants (1,714 Syn-SAA-positive, 818 negative) followed up to 12 years, baseline α-synuclein seed amplification assay positivity was associated with faster MDS-UPDRS Part III motor progression (0.611 vs 0.139 points/year; p < 0.001), a difference that persisted after excluding phenoconverters (0.430 vs 0.202 points/year; p = 0.014). SAA positivity also carried a markedly higher hazard of phenoconversion to PD or dementia with Lewy bodies (adjusted HR 5.72, 95% CI 3.78–8.68), whereas assay kinetic parameters did not predict conversion. Supports Syn-SAA positivity as a prognostic, not just diagnostic, prodromal biomarker. Preprint; observational cohort.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human PPMI prodromal cohort; linear mixed-effects and time-to-event analysis of Syn-SAA status.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.16.26368235
