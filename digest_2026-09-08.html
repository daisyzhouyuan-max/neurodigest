1Inhibitory circuits restrain adult glioma progression by suppressing calcium-driven oncogenic programs

Neuron  Naofumi Uesaka & Daisuke Kawauchi
Why it matters
First demonstration that adult gliomas receive functional GABAergic synaptic input from local inhibitory interneurons that acts as a tumor-suppressive circuit signal, complicating the field's dominant model in which neuronal activity uniformly drives glioma growth. Enhancing inhibitory tone (genetically or pharmacologically) restrains proliferation and prolongs survival, while disrupting inhibitory output accelerates growth, and the effect traces mechanistically to inhibition-dependent suppression of tumor calcium dynamics that gate YAP1/mTOR oncogenic programs. Reframes glioma-neuron circuit interactions as a balance of opposing excitatory and inhibitory influences and nominates strengthening inhibitory synaptic input as a therapeutic strategy, though translation to human tumors and the safety of chronically boosting inhibitory tone in the brain remain untested.
Model & method
Mouse adult glioma models; genetic and pharmacological manipulation of inhibitory interneuron input; calcium imaging with gain- and loss-of-function calcium manipulation in tumor cells.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.008

2A Lactate-H4K12la-HDAC1 epigenetic axis governs microglial state transitions during experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis in female mice

Nat Commun  Jiahong Li & Yinan Zhao
Why it matters
Identifies a metabolic-epigenetic axis — lactate-driven histone H4K12 lactylation, opposed by HDAC1 — that governs microglial state transitions during CNS autoimmunity, with cerebrospinal fluid lactate levels reported to correlate with multiple sclerosis severity. Causal manipulation in EAE mice (microglia-specific LDHA deletion, exogenous lactate, HDAC1 inhibition) shows the axis is not merely correlative: lactylation at neurotrophic gene promoters drives a reparative microglial program, and its erasure by HDAC1 exacerbates pathology. Nominates HDAC1 inhibition as a tractable, metabolically-targeted therapeutic strategy for neuroinflammatory disease, distinct from broadly immunosuppressive MS therapies, though the causal evidence is confined to a female-only mouse model and the human CSF correlation is observational.
Model & method
Human CSF lactate correlated with MS severity; female mouse EAE model with microglia-specific LDHA deletion, exogenous lactate administration, and HDAC1 inhibition; epigenomic histone-lactylation profiling.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76302-z

3Tracking Socio-emotional Brain Changes in Premanifest C9orf72 Repeat Expansion Carriers: A Structural and Functional 2-Year Longitudinal Magnetic Resonance Imaging Study

Ann Neurol  Jiaze Sun & Jan Van den Stock
Why it matters
One of few longitudinal, multimodal (structural plus task-based fMRI) studies tracking socioemotional circuit dysfunction in presymptomatic carriers of the most common genetic cause of FTD/ALS, showing that thalamic atrophy, altered cingulate/cerebellar activation, and reduced resting-state connectivity are present at baseline and progress over two years despite stable behavioral performance. Circuit-level functional imaging changes precede detectable clinical decline and outpace behavioral measures in sensitivity, positioning task-based fMRI combined with multivariate pattern analysis as a candidate biomarker for staging premanifest C9orf72 disease ahead of upcoming trials. Small sample (21 carriers, 24 controls) limits precision of progression-rate estimates and generalizability to non-C9orf72 FTD/ALS genotypes.
Model & method
Human 2-year longitudinal multimodal MRI (structural, resting-state, and task-based fMRI during facial emotion processing) in premanifest C9orf72 carriers vs. controls; multivariate pattern analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78355

4Salovum® reduces mortality in isolated severe traumatic brain injury: a randomized phase II trial

Brain  David Cederberg & Peter Siesjö
Why it matters
A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase II trial in isolated severe TBI patients undergoing ICP-guided neurointensive care found that antisecretory factor (Salovum) reduced 30-day mortality from 39% to 20% (relative risk 0.52, p = 0.040) — a rare positive mortality signal in a field with a long history of failed pharmacological TBI trials. The effect occurred without corresponding differences in ICP or therapy intensity level, suggesting a mechanism independent of the ICP-lowering pathways most TBI drugs target, though the trial does not identify that mechanism. Modest sample size (n=100, single-center, LMIC setting) means the result needs replication in a multicenter trial before it can change practice, but the effect size and clean safety profile justify that next step.
Model & method
Human randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase II trial (n=100) in isolated severe TBI with ICP monitoring; enteral antisecretory factor (Salovum) vs. placebo; 30-day mortality as primary endpoint.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag241

5Tyrosyl-tRNA sequestration triggers tyrosyl-tRNA synthetase-associated peripheral neuropathy

Brain  Moushami Mallik & Erik Storkebaum
Why it matters
Resolves the molecular mechanism of CMT-YARS1 peripheral neuropathy, showing that CMT-mutant tyrosyl-tRNA synthetase sequesters tRNATyr itself — rather than simply losing catalytic function — depleting the free tRNATyr pool and causing ribosome stalling at tyrosine codons. Restoring tRNATyr levels, via transgenic overexpression in three Drosophila CMT-YARS1 models or AAV9 gene therapy in a Yars1E196K mouse model, fully rescued motor performance, nerve conduction velocity, and motor axon caliber, and resolved the integrated stress response in spinal motor neurons, while restoring wild-type TyrRS protein did not. Establishes tRNA sequestration as a distinct, targetable disease mechanism among the aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase-linked CMT neuropathies and provides direct preclinical proof-of-concept for a gene-therapy strategy, with efficacy so far shown only in animal models.
Model & method
Drosophila CMT-YARS1 transgenic models (three CMT-TyrRS variants); Yars1E196K mouse model with AAV9-mediated tRNATyr gene therapy; ribosome profiling and biochemical tRNA-sequestration assays.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag204

Targeting m6A writer METTL3 with engineered nanovesicles reduces neuroinflammation in vitro and in vivo

Nat Commun  Liangfu Xu & Lang Rao
Key findings
Engineers a hybrid nanovesicle (siMETTL3-hNVs) combining natural microglia-derived nanovesicles with siRNA-loaded liposomes targeting the m6A writer METTL3, achieving inflamed-brain delivery via CCR2-CCL2 chemotaxis and caveolae-mediated transcytosis across the blood-brain barrier. In female mouse models of acute neuroinflammation and radiation-induced brain injury, the nanovesicles simultaneously act as pro-inflammatory cytokine decoys and silence METTL3 intracellularly, driving microglial M2 repolarization, reducing cytokine levels, attenuating hippocampal damage, and improving cognitive deficits. Combines a delivery vehicle with a dual mechanism of action for CNS-targeted immunomodulation; evidence is preclinical (mouse), and the durability and safety of long-term METTL3 suppression are not addressed.
Model & method
Female mouse models of acute neuroinflammation and radiation-induced brain injury; engineered microglia-derived/liposomal hybrid nanovesicles carrying siMETTL3; in vitro blood-brain-barrier transcytosis assays.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75862-4

A meta-refined human Alzheimer's disease-associated gene subset shows partial mouse-model pathway correspondence and limited cross-cohort machine-learning transportability

Alzheimers Dement  Linsong Chai & Bingbing Lin
Key findings
Integrating 15 postmortem human brain datasets against nine AD mouse-model molecular-profiling datasets, the authors derive "Refined107," a 107-gene meta-refined human AD-associated gene subset (37 Tier 1, 70 Tier 2), and find only partial, model-dependent correspondence with mouse models — just 226 of 636 recurrent human pathways showed same-direction support in any evaluated mouse-model family. Classifiers built on Refined107 performed well within a single cohort (AUC 0.83–0.98), but cross-cohort transportability dropped sharply (AUC 0.29–0.72), a cautionary finding for AD biomarker/classifier work that trains and validates within one dataset. Useful as a benchmark for how much confidence to place in mouse-model AD mechanisms and machine-learning AD classifiers, though it is a bioinformatic re-analysis rather than new experimental data.
Model & method
Meta-analysis/re-analysis of 15 human postmortem brain transcriptomic datasets and 9 AD mouse-model datasets; machine-learning classification with within- and cross-cohort validation.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71804

Distinct ocular microvascular alterations in Alzheimer's disease and cerebral small vessel disease

Alzheimers Dement  Ziyi Zhang & Shuting Zhang
Key findings
In 650 participants (149 AD, 276 cerebral small vessel disease [CSVD], 225 cognitively unimpaired) imaged with optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA), CSVD patients showed a higher burden of retinal ischemic perivascular lesions than AD patients, while AD patients showed reduced choriocapillaris density that correlated with elevated plasma phosphorylated tau-181. A machine-learning model combining OCTA metrics distinguished AD from CSVD with AUC 0.86, suggesting the retina carries disease-specific vascular signatures rather than a generic "vascular dysfunction" signal shared across both conditions. Positions OCTA as a non-invasive candidate biomarker for differentiating AD from CSVD-driven cognitive impairment, though the cross-sectional design cannot establish whether these ocular changes track disease progression over time.
Model & method
Human cross-sectional cohort (n=650); optical coherence tomography angiography; machine-learning classification integrating OCTA metrics with plasma p-tau181.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71809

The Cog-Aging: a 15-year cohort study of cognition in Brazilian older adults

Alzheimers Dement  Gabriela Tomé Oliveira Engelmann & Maria Aparecida Camargos Bicalho
Key findings
A 15-year longitudinal cohort of 887 older Brazilian adults (274 cognitively unimpaired, 378 MCI, 235 AD-type dementia at baseline) reports an annualized MCI-to-dementia conversion rate of 10.72 per 100 person-years, with 40.4% of at-risk participants progressing to dementia over a mean 3.75-year follow-up and a competing-risk-adjusted cumulative incidence of 29.85% at 3 years. Fills a notable gap in longitudinal dementia epidemiology from a low-/middle-income-country setting, where such data has been scarce despite disproportionately high dementia prevalence. Conversion-rate estimates should be read with attrition and competing mortality risk in mind, as the authors note, and generalizability beyond this Brazilian cohort is untested.
Model & method
Human longitudinal cohort (n=887, enrolled 2011–2025, followed through 2026); competing-risks cumulative incidence and Fine-Gray regression modeling of dementia conversion.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71780

mTOR drives cerebrovascular dysfunction and blood-brain barrier breakdown in a model of Alzheimers disease with cerebral amyloid angiopathy

bioRxiv  Van Skike, C. E. & Galvan, V.
Key findings
In the Tg2576 mouse model of Alzheimer's disease with cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA), mTOR signaling drives accumulation of fibrillar vascular amyloid-β, impairing endothelium-dependent cerebrovascular reactivity, breaking down the blood-brain barrier via tight-junction remodeling, and increasing cerebral microhemorrhages; mTOR also disrupts neurovascular coupling through nNOS dysfunction. The mTOR inhibitor rapamycin reversed contextual memory impairment alongside reductions in vascular amyloid load, restored BBB integrity and endothelium-dependent vasodilation, and reduced microhemorrhage, tying a single druggable pathway to multiple CAA-associated vascular pathologies simultaneously. Preclinical mouse evidence only, in a single transgenic model, with rapamycin's systemic (non-CNS-selective) mTOR inhibition not yet weighed against off-target effects.
Model & method
Tg2576 transgenic mouse model of AD with CAA (equal male/female); rapamycin treatment; cerebrovascular reactivity, blood-brain barrier integrity, and neurovascular coupling assays; contextual memory testing.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.23.733858

Chemical Genetic Targeting of the LRRK2 GTPase Domain

bioRxiv  Prorok, R. E. & Shokat, K.
Key findings
Rather than targeting the LRRK2 kinase domain directly — the strategy behind clinical LRRK2 inhibitors whose progression has been limited by on-target peripheral toxicity from strong kinase suppression — this study uses a chemical genetic approach to sensitize the Roc-COR GTPase domain, which intramolecularly regulates LRRK2 kinase output, to existing GTPase inhibitors. Pharmacologically engaging the sensitized GTPase domain in cells partially, but not fully, reduced LRRK2-mediated Rab10 T73 phosphorylation, demonstrating that GTPase-domain engagement is a viable, potentially more tunable alternative route to dial down pathogenic LRRK2 kinase activity. Early-stage proof-of-concept using an engineered/sensitized system rather than wild-type LRRK2, so translation to endogenous LRRK2 and ultimately to Parkinson's patients remains to be shown.
Model & method
Cell-based chemical genetic system sensitizing the LRRK2 Roc-COR GTPase domain to small-molecule GTPase inhibitors; Rab10 T73 phosphorylation as pharmacodynamic readout.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.749217

Deep Representation Learning of Wrist-Worn Sensor Signals for Latent Motor Abnormality Scoring in Parkinson's Disease

medRxiv  Mohtavipour, S. M.
Key findings
A multi-stream deep representation network trained on wrist-worn inertial sensor signals — combining a convolutional local feature extractor, a Transformer-based global feature extractor, and supervised contrastive/center-loss training — derives a continuous latent "motor abnormality score" from wearable data, rather than the discrete symptom classifications most prior wearable-PD approaches produce. Cross-validated PD-vs-healthy-control classification reached 83.1% accuracy and ROC-AUC 0.917, and the latent score correlated with non-motor symptom burden and disease duration, suggesting it captures graded disease-related information beyond a binary diagnostic label. A methods/algorithm-development study without an independent validation cohort or clinical-outcome linkage, so its utility as a trial or monitoring endpoint remains to be established prospectively.
Model & method
Human wrist-worn inertial sensor data; deep representation learning (CNN + Transformer) with supervised contrastive learning and center loss; five-fold subject-level cross-validation.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.26362173

Remote ambulation monitoring enhances composite disability measurement in MS to deliver reduced trial sample size

medRxiv  Festanti, A. & Rinderknecht, M. D.
Key findings
Using data from the Phase 3b CONSONANCE study (N=667), the authors convert high-frequency remote smartphone-based gait assessments into digital progression events and blend them with clinical composite confirmed disability progression (cCDP) into a new composite endpoint. The digitally enhanced endpoint reduced the trial sample size required to detect a treatment effect by 24.9% at Week 48 relative to standard cCDP, and reached equivalent statistical power 24 weeks earlier (Week 72 vs. Week 96) — a substantial potential reduction in MS trial cost and duration if it replicates prospectively. Built on real Phase 3b trial data rather than simulation, which strengthens credibility, though as a secondary/derived-endpoint analysis it still needs prospective validation as a primary registrational endpoint.
Model & method
Human Phase 3b MS clinical trial data (CONSONANCE, N=667); remote smartphone-based gait monitoring converted into digital progression events; composite endpoint and sample-size/power re-analysis.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.26361524

Computer vision-aided locomotor behavioral analysis identifies therapeutic motor signatures in a mouse model of Huntington disease

bioRxiv  Du, L. & Duan, W.
Key findings
Applying markerless pose estimation and Keypoint-MoSeq behavioral segmentation to standard open-field video recordings in the zQ175DN Huntington disease mouse model, the authors decompose locomotion into recurrent behavioral "syllables" and transition networks, finding disease-stage-dependent changes in syllable duration, behavioral-state composition, and network organization that traditional open-field metrics miss. These features are combined into an HD motor dysfunction (HDMD) score that discriminates HD from wild-type mice across multiple symptomatic stages, correlates with HD pathology and severity, and improves statistical power for detecting treatment effects over conventional locomotor readouts. A practical, retrospectively-applicable preclinical tool for HD drug development, though so far validated only in one HD mouse model tracking natural disease progression rather than an actual therapeutic intervention.
Model & method
zQ175DN Huntington disease mouse model; markerless pose estimation and Keypoint-MoSeq unsupervised behavioral segmentation of open-field video; effect-size/power analysis for preclinical trial design.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.749147
