### Persistent and compartmentalized T cell immunity in ALS
**Zhenzhen Chen & John Andersson | Nat Med**

**Why it matters:** Single-cell RNA/TCR sequencing and longitudinal immune profiling of ALS patients reveal that intrathecal and systemic T cell responses are not just present but opposed in direction: higher CSF CD4+ effector, CD4+ regulatory and CD8+ T cell frequencies tracked slower progression, whereas higher circulating CD4+ effector and regulatory T cells tracked faster progression. Long-lived intrathecal clonotypes formed antigen-presenting-cell–T cell complexes with immunological-synapse features, arguing for local antigen presentation within the CNS rather than passive infiltration. CMV-specific CSF T cells and anti-CMV serum antibodies correlated with faster decline and shorter survival. The compartment-dependent sign of the association reframes T cell immunity in ALS as protective centrally but harmful peripherally — a caution for any blanket immunomodulation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Sporadic ALS patient blood and CSF; single-cell RNA + TCR sequencing, longitudinal immune profiling, imaging flow cytometry (observational human correlations, not causal).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04728-5

### Trait-linked brainstem circuit resilience balances tau pathology-induced cellular vulnerability in Alzheimer's disease
**Jie-Qun Zheng & Ling-Qiang Zhu | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** The study dissociates cellular vulnerability from circuit-level resilience in AD. Tau accumulation in the Edinger-Westphal nucleus (EW) impaired attention by rendering CART+ neurons hypoexcitable through Kcnma1 upregulation, and this vulnerability predicted future decline in both 3×Tg-AD mice and AD patients. A subset of mice with heightened exploratory behavior engaged a compensatory medial parabrachial→EW circuit via substance P–Tacr1 signaling that sustained CART+ neuron function and delayed deterioration. Framing resilience as an active, trait-linked circuit state — rather than merely less pathology — nominates concrete molecular nodes (Kcnma1, Tacr1) for early intervention.

**Model/system & primary method:** 3×Tg-AD mice with behavioral phenotyping, circuit manipulation and electrophysiology, cross-referenced to human AD (mouse causal evidence; human data correlative).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.002

### Glioma regulates GABAergic signaling to drive hyperactivity and tumor progression
**Malcolm F McDonald & Benjamin Deneen | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** Moving beyond the excitatory-neuron focus of neuron–glioma coupling, in vivo screening in autochthonous glioma models showed that loss of the GABA transporter Slc6a1 accelerated tumor progression and network hyperactivity, and that stimulating inhibitory neurons suppressed tumor growth. A secreted negative allosteric modulator of GABA-A, diazepam binding inhibitor (DBI), emerged as the link between glioma-driven hyperexcitability and proliferation. The work shows gliomas actively manipulate inhibitory signaling to generate the hyperactivity that fuels their growth, implicating GABAergic modulation and DBI as therapeutic handles.

**Model/system & primary method:** Autochthonous mouse glioma models; in vivo functional screening, electrophysiology, neuronal manipulation (mouse causal evidence).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.001

### Stimulus-specific recruitment of human amygdala neurons predicts episodic memory encoding success
**Jiayang Xiao & Ueli Rutishauser | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Single-neuron recordings in people forming and later retrieving memories show that category-selective amygdala neurons fired more strongly during encoding of items that were subsequently remembered, specifically for each cell's preferred category. The effect emerged earlier and was stronger in amygdala than hippocampus, was independent of stimulus valence and arousal, and coincided with enhanced spike–field coherence between amygdala category cells and hippocampal field potentials. This positions right-amygdala category cells as a stimulus-specific gate on episodic encoding and a candidate target for memory-disorder interventions.

**Model/system & primary method:** Intracranial single-neuron recordings in human neurosurgical patients during a recognition-memory task with confidence ratings.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02493-9

### Circuit mechanisms for disentangled representations and generalization in the hippocampus
**Wenbo Tang & Antonio Fernandez-Ruiz | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Analysis of hippocampal population dynamics in behaving mice reveals that apparently drifting, multiplexed single-neuron activity resolves into disentangled variables carried by the co-activity dynamics of cell assemblies. Temporal components of these representations were governed by local inhibitory interactions, while contextual components were input-driven, and the geometry supported flexible memory and rapid generalization to new situations. The result offers a circuit-level account of how heterogeneous single-cell tuning yields structured, generalizable population codes.

**Model/system & primary method:** Behaving mice; large-scale hippocampal population recordings with population-geometry analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02491-x

### Longitudinal real-time quaking-induced conversion analysis of misfolded α-synuclein in idiopathic REM sleep behavior disorder patients with baseline CSF negativity
**Gerard Mayà & Alex Iranzo | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** Among 43 polysomnography-confirmed iRBD patients who were CSF α-synuclein seed-amplification–negative at baseline, 9 (20.9%) converted to positivity over a mean 2.8-year follow-up on a second lumbar puncture. Baseline converters already carried a heavier prodromal-PD burden (hyposmia, abnormal DAT-SPECT, increased REM-sleep EMG activity) than those who stayed negative, and longer inter-puncture intervals associated with higher conversion. Serial CSF seed-amplification assays can therefore capture a very early synucleinopathy stage and refine selection for disease-modifying trials, albeit in a modest single-cohort sample.

**Model/system & primary method:** Longitudinal human iRBD cohort; CSF α-synuclein seed amplification (RT-QuIC) across two lumbar punctures.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70592

### Pathway-specific polygenic risk scores modify the association between amyloid and tau PET accumulation in Alzheimer's disease
**Carolina Valentim & Michael Ewers | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Mapping GWAS signal into gene-set–derived pathway-specific polygenic risk scores (adaptive immune response, leukocyte activation, transport, intracellular signaling) across amyloid-positive ADNI (n=441) and A4 (n=250) participants, only the transport pPRS strengthened amyloid–tau coupling cross-sectionally across Braak stages in ADNI and at Braak I in A4, with stronger effects in females and longitudinal persistence at higher Braak stages. The finding localizes a specific biological pathway — beyond APOE — that governs how amyloid drives tau accumulation, though effect sizes are modest and the two cohorts only partially concordant.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human amyloid-positive cohorts (ADNI, A4) with longitudinal tau PET; GWAS meta-analysis, pathway-specific PRS, pPRS-by-amyloid interaction models.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71939

### CSF and plasma lipid profiling predicts risk of cognitive impairment in cognitively unimpaired older adults
**Takahisa Kanekiyo & Xianlin Han | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Shotgun lipidomics of CSF and plasma in cognitively unimpaired older adults, followed longitudinally for CDR conversion, showed CSF lipidomes tracked age and established AD biomarkers while plasma lipids were dominated by sex. Specific CSF species — TAG(C49:6), PG(18:0-18:1), CE(20:3) and PE(D16:0-18:1) — independently predicted CDR conversion and improved risk prediction, and CSF phosphatidylethanolamine subspecies correlated with total tau, p-tau181, neurogranin and YKL-40. The data mark brain lipid metabolism as a source of preclinical predictive biomarkers and candidate targets.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cognitively unimpaired cohort with longitudinal CDR follow-up; CSF and plasma shotgun lipidomics (observational).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71924

### Rate and shape of functional decline identify clinically distinct ALS phenotypes
**Andrea Calvo & Adriano Chiò | Ann Neurol**

**Key findings:** In 1,686 ALS patients from the Piemonte/Valle d'Aosta register, a rate-based mixture model and a shape-based latent-class spline model each yielded four classes with only modest concordance (κ=0.273). Adding trajectory shape barely changed overall discrimination (C-index 0.761 vs 0.755) but separated subgroups within rate classes — within the "catastrophic" rate group, median survival split 30.1 vs 12.0 months — and a late-decline shape class combined moderate early decline with occult respiratory compromise (median FVC 83.5%, 22.7-month survival). Notably, the common three-visit sampling criterion hid 367 ultrarapid progressors, replicated in an external Emilia-Romagna register. Rate predicts overall burden while shape predicts timing of deterioration — a practical caution for trial stratification.

**Model/system & primary method:** Two population-based ALS registers (derivation + external validation); Gaussian mixture and latent-class mixed spline modeling of ALSFRS-R trajectories.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78394

### Intraventricular clindamycin enhances interstitial brain fluid drainage and the blood-brain barrier after traumatic brain injury
**Landon S Ashby & Benjamin J Umlauf | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** Intracerebroventricular clindamycin after TBI in focal and diffuse mouse models increased ependymal ciliary motility by 30–60%, which correlated tightly with interstitial fluid drainage through the cortex (r>0.9). Treatment restored the blood-brain barrier, reduced lesion size, lowered serum GFAP 2.8-fold, and improved recognition memory. The counterintuitive combination — enhancing toxin clearance while simultaneously tightening the BBB — suggests a drainage-based route to limiting TBI secondary pathogenesis, pending translation beyond rodents.

**Model/system & primary method:** Focal and diffuse mouse TBI models; intracerebroventricular drug delivery, ciliary motility and fluid-drainage imaging, behavioral testing (mouse causal evidence).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2536755123

### Structural basis for repeat-associated non-AUG translation initiation on C9orf72 mRNA
**Kaufhold, R. & Brito Querido, J. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Cryo-EM structures of a human C9orf72 translation initiation complex (3.2 Å in vitro; 3.5 Å near-native from cell lysate) indicate that RAN translation of the G4C2 repeat initiates predominantly at a near-cognate CUG codon 24 nt upstream of the repeat, flanked by a strong Kozak sequence. The structures reveal a direct kissing-loop interaction between the G4C2 repeat and 18S rRNA expansion segment ES9S on the 40S subunit. If it holds, this defines a specific, structurally resolved initiation mechanism for the toxic dipeptide-repeat proteins central to C9orf72 ALS/FTD, and a potential interface to target.

**Model/system & primary method:** In vitro reconstituted and cell-lysate-purified human translation initiation complexes; cryo-electron microscopy.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.25.761709

### The human-specific lncRNA HD-LINC drives lipid droplet-associated neurodegeneration in Huntington's disease
**Kwon, J.-S. & Yoo, A. S. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** HD-LINC (LINC01139), a human-specific long intergenic noncoding RNA, was selectively induced in symptomatic HD patient-derived medium spiny neurons and human HD caudate, but not in non-degenerative MSNs reprogrammed from pre-manifest individuals. Reducing HD-LINC attenuated mutant HTT aggregation, dystrophic neurites, DNA damage and neuronal death, while overexpression worsened them; transcriptomic and lipidomic profiling linked it to neuronal lipid regulation and to aberrant accumulation of enlarged, low-mobility lipid droplets within neurites. As a human-specific, bidirectionally manipulable driver tied to a previously unrecognized lipid-droplet phenotype, it is a provocative candidate mechanism — in a patient-derived in vitro model.

**Model/system & primary method:** HD patient-derived reprogrammed medium spiny neurons and human HD caudate; single-cell transcriptomics, lncRNA knockdown/overexpression, lipidomics.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.23.761349

### Ms4a4a loss preferentially remodels microglia with limited, compartment-specific peripheral effects in 5xFAD mice
**Danhash, E. & Karch, C. M. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Constitutive loss of the AD risk/resilience gene Ms4a4a in 5xFAD mice produced its strongest immune effects in brain microglia — increased microglial abundance and activation — with comparatively limited, compartment-specific changes across dural meninges, skull and tibial bone marrow, and spleen. Multicompartment flow cytometry argues that MS4A4A acts predominantly on brain-resident microglia in the context of amyloid, supporting the interpretation that its protective effect on plaque and tau spreading is CNS-microglia–driven rather than peripheral-myeloid-mediated.

**Model/system & primary method:** 5xFAD mice with and without constitutive Ms4a4a loss; multicompartment flow-cytometric immune profiling (mouse).

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.26.762005

### Tetrahydrobiopterin restores oligodendrocyte maturation after hypoxic white matter injury by reprogramming translational output
**Saric, N. & Ishibashi, N. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In a transgenic ribosomal-reporter mouse model of chronic hypoxia (modeling congenital-heart-defect cerebral hypoxemia), TRAP-seq of oligodendrocyte-lineage cells showed daily BH4 supplementation restored translational programs disrupted by hypoxia, with differential translation and co-expression analyses linking BH4 to recovered oligodendrocyte maturation. The work proposes translational control as the mechanistic layer through which BH4 rescues hypoxia-induced white matter dysmaturation, extending BH4's role beyond its canonical nitric-oxide-synthase cofactor function.

### Cortical pyramidal tract neurons constrain pathological dopaminergic motor responses
**Atudorei, M. & Rickhag, M. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Chemogenetic activation of motor-cortical pyramidal tract (PT) neurons suppressed spontaneous locomotion in healthy mice and, in 6-OHDA-lesioned mice, did not rescue baseline motor deficits but selectively attenuated D1-agonist-provoked contraversive rotations and axial levodopa-induced dyskinesia (LID). Mechanistically, PT activation reduced acute D1-mediated striatal c-Fos without affecting chronic ΔFosB accumulation. The results identify a cortical PT-to-striatal-D1 interaction that limits maladaptive motor responses, suggesting a circuit node relevant to LID rather than to primary parkinsonian bradykinesia.

**Model/system & primary method:** Unilateral 6-OHDA mouse model of Parkinson's disease; chemogenetic PT-neuron activation, rotational and dyskinesia assays, c-Fos/ΔFosB mapping (mouse causal evidence).

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.27.758468
