### A multi-institutional investigation of psilocybin's effects on mouse behavior
**Odilia D Lu & Robert C Malenka | Nature Neuroscience**

**Why it matters:** Reports of persistent, antidepressant-like effects of a single psilocybin dose in rodents have driven much of the field, but their reproducibility was untested. Five laboratories ran an identical protocol (2 mg/kg i.p., ~200 mice per experiment) and found that the acute effects — increased anxiety- and avoidance-related behavior and reduced fear expression — were robust and replicable across sites. The widely cited persistent effects 24 h later were not: there was no reliable reduction in anxiety- or depression-like behavior, no facilitation of fear extinction, and no change in social preference or social reward learning. This directly tempers the preclinical case for durable single-dose benefit and sets a replication benchmark for psychedelic behavioral neuroscience.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mice (both sexes, ~200 per experiment) across five independent laboratories; standardized acute and 24-h behavioral battery.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02500-z

### Plasma P-Tau217 and NfL longitudinal changes across neurodegenerative diseases: impact of α-synuclein in Alzheimer disease
**Diana Esteller-Gauxax & Mircea Balasa | Neurology**

**Why it matters:** In a 223-participant memory-clinic cohort followed for 36 months with CSF biomarkers and RT-QuIC-defined α-synuclein status, co-existing α-synuclein pathology sharpened plasma Alzheimer biomarker trajectories. AD patients who were α-synuclein-positive showed the steepest rise in plasma p-tau217 and carried NfL levels as high as frontotemporal dementia, indicating greater neuroaxonal injury, whereas DLB, FTD and non-neurodegenerative groups remained comparatively stable. The data argue that α-synuclein co-pathology identifies a biologically distinct, faster-progressing AD subgroup, with practical implications for prognostic stratification and clinical-trial enrichment. Findings come from a single center and modest α-synuclein-positive subgroups (n = 15 AD-aSyn+).

**Model/system & primary method:** Prospective human cohort (n = 223); longitudinal plasma p-tau217 and NfL (Lumipulse) with CSF and RT-QuIC, linear mixed-effects models.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218685

### Understanding the pattern of cognitive decline in GBA1-related Parkinson's disease
**Merle Bode & Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative | Annals of Neurology**

**Why it matters:** Pooling three longitudinal cohorts (211 GBA1 carriers, 903 non-carriers, 546 controls), this study maps how pathogenic GBA1 variants shape the trajectory, not just the risk, of PD cognitive decline. Carriers performed worse at baseline and declined more rapidly and more broadly across executive function, attention, visuocognitive skills and memory; processing speed and visuoperceptual deficits emerged roughly 1.2 and 2.5 disease-years earlier than in non-carriers. A latent-trajectory analysis isolated a rapidly declining subgroup enriched for male sex, depressive symptoms and severe GBA1 mutations. The work refines who is at risk of early dementia in PD and when, informing patient selection for GBA1-targeted trials. As an exploratory multi-cohort analysis, the trajectory subgroups await prospective confirmation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multi-cohort longitudinal study (PPMI, ABC-PD, NCER-PD); standardized neuropsychology with generalized linear mixed models, latent-trajectory and time-to-event analyses.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78362

## NEURODEGENERATION & AGING — TOP 5

### Astrocyte lipid metabolism in brain health and neurodegeneration: from regulatory hub to maladaptive stressor
**Weihua Wang & Fei Yin | Molecular Neurodegeneration**

**Key findings:** This review frames astrocytic lipid handling as a central axis linking brain metabolic health to neurodegeneration. Under physiology, astrocytes act as the principal lipid hub — de novo cholesterol synthesis, APOE-mediated transport, fatty-acid uptake and degradation. The authors detail how aging, chronic stress, proteinopathy and genetic risk (notably APOE4) corrupt these programs into cholesterol dyshomeostasis, lipid-droplet accumulation, impaired lipid turnover and pro-inflammatory lipid signaling, converting astrocytes to maladaptive reactive states that both starve vulnerable neurons of metabolic support and amplify neuroinflammation. It closes on strategies to restore lipid flux and dampen lipid-driven pathogenic signaling.

**Model/system & primary method:** Narrative review synthesizing astrocyte lipid biology across aging and neurodegenerative disease.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00981-x

### Deciphering the metabolome in Alzheimer's disease
**Xiaoli Yang & Yong Shen | Molecular Neurodegeneration**

**Key findings:** A focused review of metabolomic alterations in Alzheimer's disease, cataloguing dysregulated metabolites in brain and body fluids and their correlations with amyloid/tau pathology and cognitive phenotypes across disease stages. The authors organize these signatures by biochemical pathway to argue how specific metabolic disturbances feed into AD pathogenesis, and frame the metabolome as a substrate for both biomarker development and metabolically targeted therapeutics. Useful as a current map of where fluid and tissue metabolomics stand in AD, while noting that a unifying metabolic model of the disease remains incomplete.

**Model/system & primary method:** Review of human AD metabolomics across brain tissue and biofluids, organized by pathway.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00982-w

### Optimizing genetic analyses for movement disorders: a unified panel approach and targeted reanalysis of 4,046 clinical exomes
**Teodora Barbarii & Erik-Jan Kamsteeg | Movement Disorders**

**Key findings:** To address heterogeneous virtual gene-panel design across laboratories, the authors built a unified movement-disorder panel from a curated gene–phenotype dataset, tiering 640 selected genes (537 Green, 36 Amber, 67 Red) and expanding the prior panel by 283 genes. The curation highlights pervasive phenotypic pleiotropy — only 17% of Green genes mapped to a single movement-disorder feature — and broad age-at-onset ranges (both pediatric and adult presentations in 43%). Targeted reanalysis of 4,046 previously undiagnosed exomes with a 220-gene list found 37 clinically relevant variants across 28 genes, resolving 16 cases (a 0.4% yield increase), demonstrating concrete diagnostic benefit from systematic reanalysis.

**Model/system & primary method:** Evidence-based panel curation plus targeted reanalysis of 4,046 clinical exomes.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70563

### Substrate coupling and inhibition in the human Na⁺-dependent Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger NCBE (SLC4A10)
**Peixin Sun & Hanting Yang | Nature Communications**

**Key findings:** NCBE (SLC4A10), abundant in the choroid plexus, helps set CSF pH and supports neuronal excitability, but its transport mechanism was unresolved. Cryo-EM structures of human NCBE in multiple states reveal conserved Na⁺, Cl⁻ and HCO₃⁻ sites plus an unexpected K⁺ site indicating relaxed cation selectivity; the inhibitor DIDS binds above the substrate cavity and locks an outward-facing state, defining a subfamily-selective inhibitory mechanism. Comparison with an AlphaFold3 inward-facing model supports an elevator-like transport cycle. The structures provide a framework for pharmacologically modulating a transporter central to CSF ion homeostasis.

**Model/system & primary method:** Cryo-EM of human NCBE in multiple functional states with functional assays and molecular dynamics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77504-1

### Sexually dimorphic control of excitatory synapse maintenance in ventral hippocampal circuits by the Neurexin2α ligand Vstm2b
**Nicolas Chofflet & Hideto Takahashi | Nature Communications**

**Key findings:** Unbiased proteomics identified Vstm2 family members as α-neurexin-specific interactors dependent on the alternative splice-site-2 insert; Vstm2b acts as a selective ligand of axonal Nrxn2αSS2+ to promote glutamatergic presynaptic assembly. In mice, Vstm2b loss impaired glutamatergic synaptic function and maintenance in ventral CA1 while sparing initial synaptogenesis, and these deficits were sex- and input-specific, producing sexually dimorphic anxiety-related and social behavioral phenotypes. The work defines a synaptic-adhesion complex governing activity-relevant synapse stability and reveals the α-neurexin interactome as a driver of sex-dependent circuit vulnerability — a mechanism relevant to selective synapse loss.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse ventral hippocampus; interactome proteomics, conditional Vstm2b loss, electrophysiology and behavior.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78199-0

### In-vivo MRI of noradrenergic and dopaminergic nuclei predicts future cognition and Alzheimer's disease progression
**W. Zhao & M. Mather | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Using 739 ADNI participants, the authors derived in-vivo MRI integrity measures of the locus coeruleus (LC) and substantia nigra–ventral tegmental area (SN-VTA) and tested their prognostic value. Both measures predicted cognitive and behavioral function ~10 years later despite only weak links to concurrent function, and their associated brain-wide atrophy patterns aligned with PET norepinephrine-transporter and dopamine-receptor maps, respectively. A one-SD lower baseline LC contrast was associated with 84% higher five-year odds of MCI-to-AD conversion, with effects mediated by the corresponding atrophy patterns; the SN-VTA pattern tracked less amyloid-related memory decline. The results position catecholaminergic-nucleus MRI contrast as an early marker forecasting decline before clinical onset, pending prospective and cross-cohort validation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human (ADNI, n = 739) LC and SN-VTA MRI contrast with MRI–histology correspondence and longitudinal outcome modeling.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.29.762942

### Clinically meaningful cognitive improvement from intermittent neurostimulation of the nucleus basalis of Meynert in Parkinson's disease
**K. B. Wilkins & H. M. Bronte-Stewart | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** The nucleus basalis of Meynert (NBM), the cortical cholinergic hub, degenerates early in PD and is linked to cognitive impairment. In an ongoing trial, five PD patients approved for STN DBS also received NBM leads — three via a vertical trajectory, two via a novel lateral trajectory along the NBM efferent bundle — delivering continuous STN stimulation plus 1 h/day of intermittent patterned NBM stimulation. Leads were placed and programmed without adverse effects, and after six months all five showed clinically meaningful cognitive improvement on a neuropsychological battery alongside expected STN motor benefit. This is an early feasibility result in five patients with no sham control, so efficacy claims are preliminary, but it supports combined STN+NBM stimulation as a route to the largely untreated cognitive burden of PD.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human first-in-concept feasibility study (n = 5); combined continuous STN and intermittent NBM DBS with 6-month neuropsychological assessment.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.02.26366824

### Conformation governs cellular behaviors of LRRK2 Parkinson's disease variants
**K. S. Hatch & S. L. Reck-Peterson | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** LRRK2 is autoinhibited when its leucine-rich repeats block the kinase active site; displacing them ("unlatching") primes activity. The authors define a broad class of PD-associated "unlatching" mutations by their effect on LRRK2 conformation, showing these variants localize more to membranes — where LRRK2's Rab substrates reside — and exhibit decreased S935 phosphorylation, proposed as a conformational biomarker. This framework explains variants such as I2020T that structural studies alone could not predict and unifies most PD-linked LRRK2 mutations under a single mechanism, offering measurable readouts to identify LRRK2-driven disease. Evidence is cell-based and biochemical.

**Model/system & primary method:** Cell-based assays of LRRK2 PD variants; conformational, membrane-localization and phosphorylation readouts.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.30.763152

### Autophagy activator AA-20 engages CDK5 to promote lipophagy and improve neurodegeneration-associated phenotypes
**E. P. Tan & J. W. Kelly | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Pharmacological lipophagy inducers are scarce. AA-20, an mTORC1-independent autophagy activator, enhanced lipid-droplet recruitment to autophagosomes and lysosomal acid-lipase–dependent degradation in mouse hippocampal neuronal cells. In Swedish-APP cerebral organoids, two weeks of AA-20 normalized aberrant lipidomes by selectively lowering disease-associated lipids and corrected AD-associated electrical hyperexcitability. The authors identify CDK5 — a kinase tied to AD hyperexcitability and PD pathology — as an AA-20 target required for its autophagy and lipid-clearance effects, and show AA-20 reduced pS129-α-synuclein in a neuronal seeding model. Together this nominates lipophagy induction, via a defined CDK5 axis, as a strategy against both lipidopathy and proteinopathy. Evidence is in cellular and organoid models only.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse neuronal cell lines, human Swedish-APP cerebral organoids and an α-synuclein seeding model; autophagy, lipidomic and electrophysiology readouts.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.30.760220

### Tau PET Braak staging across the Alzheimer's disease continuum using Flortaucipir and MK6240
**A. Rocha & HEAD Study | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In a prospective head-to-head study of 682 individuals spanning aging to AD, Braak staging from [¹⁸F]Flortaucipir and [¹⁸F]MK6240 was internally coherent (89–92% hierarchical adherence) but not interchangeable: cross-tracer agreement was only 43% (weighted κ = 0.54), falling to 14–23% at early-to-intermediate stages — the window most relevant to therapy. Discordance was directional, with MK6240 assigning a higher stage in >90% of discordant cases (≈1 stage higher on average); MK6240 upstaging tracked greater amyloid burden, worse plasma biomarkers and poorer cognition. Against a neuropathological cohort (n = 4,422), PET staging lagged pathology by ~2 (MK6240) to ~3 (Flortaucipir) Braak stages early on. The results warn that tracer choice materially changes staging for trial selection and demand tracer-aware harmonization.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human head-to-head tau-PET study (n = 682) with neuropathological benchmarking (n = 4,422).

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.01.26367034
