### Human microglia clear intraneuronal α-synuclein aggregates by GPNMB-mediated trogocytosis
**Hung-Ju Chueh & George K Tofaris | Sci Transl Med**

**Why it matters:** In iPSC cocultures where α-synuclein aggregates either form de novo inside dopaminergic neurons (SNCA triplication) or are seeded by fibrils, human microglia cleared Ser129-phosphorylated α-synuclein by contact-dependent trogocytosis — nibbling aggregate-laden neuronal subcompartments — independently of TREM2 or phosphatidylserine. The Parkinson's GWAS gene GPNMB was identified as the lysosomal effector of this clearance and was upregulated in substantia nigra microglia of people with incidental Lewy bodies or Parkinson's disease, reframing a risk locus as a candidate handle on a protective microglial state. The response was tuned by P2RY12 (sensing) and CD22 (inhibitory) signalling.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived microglia–dopaminergic neuron cocultures; single-cell RNA-seq; validation in post-mortem substantia nigra.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.adz9258

### Astrocytes regulate axonal mitochondrial transport deficits in C9ORF72 amyotrophic lateral sclerosis motor neurons
**Maria Stavrou & Bhuvaneish T Selvaraj | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Human iPSC-derived astrocytes carrying the C9ORF72 mutation disrupted mitochondrial axonal transport and mitochondrial function in cocultured motor neurons, and isogenic gene correction of the astrocytes — or boosting astrocytic mitochondrial bioenergetics — rescued the transport deficit. This assigns a defined axonal phenotype to a non-cell-autonomous, astrocyte-driven mechanism and nominates astrocytic bioenergetics as a target upstream of motor-neuron mitochondrial failure. The causal evidence is from an in vitro human model.

**Model/system & primary method:** C9ORF72 and isogenic-corrected iPSC astrocyte–motor neuron cocultures; live axonal mitochondrial transport imaging.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02464-0

### Network-dependent remote effects and local tau burden jointly shape neuronal dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease
**Lukas Frontzkowski & Nicolai Franzmeier | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** In 131 amyloid-positive individuals studied with dynamic [18F]PI-2620 tau-PET, regional hypoperfusion tracked connectivity-based distance from tau epicentres more closely than local tau deposition — most strongly in early disease, consistent with diaschisis-like remote disconnection — while local tau and hypoperfusion converged as severity advanced. Critically, hypoperfusion mediated the relationship between tau accumulation and cognitive decline, arguing that tau degrades the network beyond its immediate deposition site. The mediation is cross-sectional, so causal ordering is inferred rather than demonstrated.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; dynamic tau-PET with kinetic modelling (distribution volume ratio, perfusion R1), normative functional-connectivity mapping, mediation analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71914

### Genetic evidence supports a cortical neuroprotective role of GLP-1 receptor activation in Alzheimer's disease
**Li Jiang & Beisha Tang | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** Drug-target Mendelian randomization using cis-eQTLs for GLP-1R found higher GLP-1R expression associated with lower Alzheimer's risk and lower CSF phosphorylated tau, with tissue-specific analysis attributing the effect almost entirely to cortical (posterior inclusion probability 0.998) rather than blood GLP-1R expression; of 11 candidate mediators only systolic blood pressure emerged, and GLP-1R retained a direct effect. This supports direct CNS neuroprotection — not purely peripheral metabolic benefit — as a mechanism for GLP-1R agonists in Alzheimer's, strengthening the rationale for ongoing trials. Mendelian randomization gives genetic, not interventional, causal evidence.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human genetics; cis- and multivariable Mendelian randomization with brain-cortex and blood eQTL instruments.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71845

### A split attractor design for rapidly writing a navigational goal
**Aaron J Lanz & Katherine I Nagel | Nature**

**Why it matters:** Whole-cell recordings in the Drosophila navigation centre show that recurrently connected hΔK and PFG ring neurons share persistent "bump" activity that switches on with odour and terminates at the end of a goal-directed run. Persistence in hΔK depends on recurrence — slow recurrent excitation with fast inhibition — and disinhibition of hΔK acts as a gate: when hΔK is inhibited, PFG follows compass input; when disinhibited, recurrence locks that input into a stable heading memory. This supplies a concrete synaptic mechanism for how an attractor network can be written and switched off on demand, a long-standing puzzle for working-memory circuits.

**Model/system & primary method:** Drosophila; in vivo whole-cell patch-clamp recording with computational attractor modelling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11144-9

### Modality-specific reactive and predictive responses in the human superior colliculus
**Danlei Chen & Jordan E Theriault-Brown | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Using 7-Tesla fMRI in 80 participants, visual and somatosensory stimulation produced distinct depth-dependent responses across superior colliculus layers; the same modality- and depth-specific pattern also appeared during task periods preceding stimulation, when sensorimotor input was identical between conditions — a predictive signal, with corresponding effects in thalamic sensory nuclei and controls ruling out cross-trial carryover. The work extends predictive-processing accounts to a human subcortical structure.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; 7-Tesla layer-resolved fMRI.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02480-0

### Immunoregulatory artificial lymph nodes restore antigen-specific tolerance in advanced multiple sclerosis
**Jingsen Ji & Weisheng Guo | Sci Transl Med**

**Key findings:** A 3D-printed "tolerogenic artificial lymph node" — a gelatin-methacrylate scaffold releasing CCL21 to recruit naïve CD4+ T cells and shield tolerogenic dendritic cells from the inflamed native node — induced antigen-specific Foxp3+ regulatory T cells and attenuated experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis even at advanced stages; conditional Treg depletion abolished protection, and a multivalent version additionally suppressed inflammatory B cells and autoantibodies in MOG1-125 EAE. It achieved durable remission without impairing systemic immunity, though the evidence is preclinical (mouse EAE).

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse EAE; implantable 3D-printed CCL21-releasing tolerogenic scaffold.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aea7960

### miR-214-3p and NCKAP1: A microglial functional dimension of Alzheimer's progression
**Min-Young Noh & Seung Hyun Kim | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Across 114 participants spanning the Alzheimer's continuum, patient-derived induced microglia-like cells (iMG) showed progressively declining phagocytic capacity accompanied by reduced NCK-associated protein 1 (NCKAP1) and the strongest association with plasma GFAP; this cellular readout captured a dimension of progression beyond conventional plasma biomarkers, with a longitudinal subset (n=38). The findings suggest a blood-derived assay of microglial function tracks clinical trajectory. Evidence is observational and human.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; patient-derived iMG phagocytosis assays with plasma biomarkers and longitudinal clinical follow-up.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71903

### Longitudinal clinical outcomes after amyloid clearance in aducanumab phase 3 studies
**Menglan Pang & David Knopman | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Pooling ENGAGE and EMERGE participants, those reaching treatment-related amyloid clearance (TRAC) by week 78 showed less clinical decline at weeks 78 and 134 and at EMBARK baseline than non-TRAC participants, and post-clearance roughly two-thirds declined less than 1 point per year on CDR-Sum of Boxes. This links depth of amyloid removal to outcome, but as an exploratory, post hoc stratification it is not a randomized contrast.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; post hoc analysis of phase 3 RCT extension and redosing cohorts stratified by amyloid-PET clearance.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71758

### Physical Activity and Motor and Cognitive Progression in Parkinson Disease: A Longitudinal Cohort Study
**Dong-Woo Ryu & Alberto J Espay | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In mild-to-moderate Parkinson's disease (Cincinnati Cohort Biomarker Program), higher total and vigorous physical activity were associated with slower annual worsening of MDS-UPDRS-III and better MoCA trajectories in linear mixed-effects models. The associations are observational, so reverse causation — less-affected patients being able to exercise more — cannot be excluded.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human longitudinal cohort; standardized activity interviews, linear mixed-effects modelling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218682

### MATRIN3 deficiency in human cells triggers an autoinflammatory response via cGAS-STING activation
**Zohirul Islam & Stefan A Muljo | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** MATR3 — mutated in ALS and distal myopathy — was deleted in human iPSCs and HAP1 cells, and its loss upregulated interferon-stimulated genes through cGAS-STING. PAR-CLIP identified direct MATR3 targets including TDRD3, which acts with TOP3B in R-loop metabolism, linking MATR3 loss of function to innate-immune activation. This offers a neuroinflammatory mechanism for MATR3-related disease, though it is shown in cell models rather than neurons in vivo.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC and HAP1 gene-editing; RNA-seq and PAR-CLIP.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2608835123

### Herpes simplex virus pUL56 abolishes neuronal activity by removing voltage-gated ion channels from the plasma membrane
**Daniel A Nash & Stephen C Graham | Sci Adv**

**Key findings:** Across the HSV-1 lytic cycle in human iPSC-derived cortical glutamatergic neurons, the virus remodelled the plasma-membrane surface proteome, stripping voltage-gated sodium, potassium and calcium channels and abolishing synchronous calcium signalling; the viral ubiquitin-ligase adaptor pUL56 was the principal effector, as mutating its E3-ligase-binding motifs preserved channels and synchrony. This gives a molecular route from neurotropic infection to loss of neuronal excitability, relevant to encephalitis and to proposed infection–neurodegeneration links.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived cortical neurons; surface proteomics, calcium imaging, viral mutants.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aei1796

### TDP-43 toxic gain of function links ALS, FTD and Alzheimer's Disease through splicing dysregulation
**van Zuiden, W. & Hornstein, E. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** TDP-43 pathology in Alzheimer's disease was associated with a conserved TDP-43-dependent mis-splicing signature, including elongated amyloid precursor protein (APP) isoforms; APP isoform imbalance correlated with and partially mediated the link between TDP-43 pathology and Aβ burden, and co-localized spatially with phospho-TDP-43 and mis-spliced STMN2 in LATE hippocampus. The authors attribute the mis-splicing to cytoplasmic TDP-43 gain-of-function that co-sequesters splicing-regulatory RNA-binding proteins — knockdown of those proteins reproduced disease-relevant changes — rather than a pure nuclear loss-of-function, proposing a shared splicing-factor-sequestration mechanism that unites Alzheimer's with ALS/FTLD-TDP. This is a revision of a preprint first posted April 2025; the human post-mortem and cell-model data are not peer reviewed.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem brain transcriptomics with cell models; splicing analysis and RNA-binding-protein knockdown.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.20.648873

### Brain region- and solubility-dependent proteomic signatures of neurofibrillary tangle pathology in Alzheimer's disease
**Zeng, X. & Karikari, T. K. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Data-independent-acquisition mass-spectrometry proteomics of inferior temporal versus middle frontal cortex across Braak stages (27 autopsy cases), with sequential solubility fractionation, identified 452 differentially expressed, 183 bidirectionally expressed and 693 differentially soluble proteins, enriched for cytoskeletal organization, metabolism, vesicular trafficking and proteostasis. ### Hypothalamic tauP301L pathology disrupts pair bonding in monogamous prairie voles
**Kelberman, M. A. & Donaldson, Z. R. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Expressing dementia-causing tauP301L in the paraventricular hypothalamus of prairie voles produced sex- and age-dependent social-bonding deficits — 18-month males failed to form partner preferences and showed blunted sociability — whereas hippocampocortical tau expression did not; healthy males paired with tau-expressing females also showed reduced partner preference (a vicarious effect), alongside elevated oxytocin signal. This positions hypothalamic tau and oxytocin circuitry in the social dysfunction of dementia, in a species that forms selective long-term bonds. The effects are model-specific and rest on targeted overexpression in modest numbers of animals.

**Model/system & primary method:** Prairie vole; region-targeted AAV tauP301L expression, bonding assays, oxytocin measures.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.28.755178

### Bone regulates locomotion by sustaining motoneuronal mitochondrial function
**Romeo Guitart, D. & Oury, F. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The bone-derived hormone osteocalcin, acting through GPR158 selectively enriched in spinal cholinergic motor neurons, fine-tuned autophagy, mitophagy and mitochondrial activity; loss of GPR158 or osteocalcin impaired locomotion and lowered choline acetyltransferase, while pharmacologically inducing mitophagy or chemogenetically activating mitochondria rescued the deficits. Restoring osteocalcin in aged mice reversed age-related locomotor decline through a motor-neuron autophagy/mitophagy-dependent mechanism. This proposes a skeleton-to-motor-neuron endocrine axis and partial reversibility of motor aging, on mouse genetic and pharmacological evidence.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse; conditional Gpr158 and osteocalcin loss, mitophagy induction, chemogenetic mitochondrial activation.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.07.750453

### Aging reshapes odor-evoked dynamics in central olfactory circuits
**Chapman, I. F. & Bolding, K. A. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Simultaneous Neuropixels recordings from olfactory bulb and piriform cortex in awake young versus old mice revealed a shift toward greater excitation with reduced piriform inhibitory activity, restructured beta oscillations and coherence, and less discriminable odour representations in the bulb — but not piriform — despite largely preserved odour responsivity. The authors identify reduced olfactory-bulb population discriminability as the clearest neural correlate of age-related odour-discrimination deficits, notable because olfactory decline precedes later cognitive decline and neurodegenerative disease. Evidence is from mice.

**Model/system & primary method:** Aged versus young mice; dual-region Neuropixels recordings with single-unit, LFP and population analyses.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.30.755620
