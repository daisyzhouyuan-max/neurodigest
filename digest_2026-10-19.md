### TGF-β inhibition attenuates tau accumulation and neurodegeneration in PSP patient-derived organoids
**Mariagrazia Talarico & Aldo Quattrone | Mov Disord**

**Why it matters:** Progressive supranuclear palsy is a 4-repeat tauopathy with no disease-modifying therapy, and TGF-β is elevated in PSP patient-derived material. Pharmacological TGF-β receptor I inhibition (SB431542) lowered phospho-tau (AT8, Ser396, Thr181), rescued dopaminergic neuron loss (restored tyrosine hydroxylase, reduced NfL) and curbed astrocytic reactivity (GFAP), while recombinant TGF-β1/2/3 worsened each feature — a bidirectional, causal result in a human model nominating TGF-β signaling as a tractable target. Interpretation is limited by organoids derived from only two PSP (Richardson's syndrome) donors versus two controls.

**Model/system & primary method:** iPSC-derived ventral midbrain organoids (96 organoids; 2 PSP, 2 control donors), day 60–90 treatment; immunoblotting, immunofluorescence, TUNEL.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70574

### Ataxin-2 downregulation rewires neuronal and astrocytic metabolism to protect against TDP-43 toxicity
**Denise Gastaldo & Ana Jovičić | Cell Rep**

**Why it matters:** ATXN2 lowering is a leading ALS strategy, and this work defines its mechanism: reduction activates orthogonal stress-adaptive metabolic programs rather than simply reversing TDP-43 damage. In neurons, ataxin-2 knockdown upregulated glycolysis and IDH1-driven reductive glutamine carboxylation, restoring ATP independently of impaired mitochondria; in astrocytes it raised HMGCS1-dependent cholesterol biosynthesis and trophic support. Full neuroprotection required both arms — neuronal survival was only completely rescued in neuron–astrocyte co-culture — and protection held against both TDP-43 gain- and loss-of-function toxicity.

**Model/system & primary method:** Yeast and mouse TDP-43 models with global proteomics; neuron–astrocyte co-cultures.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118053

### Noninvasive ultrashort-wave neuromodulation enhances glymphatic clearance in traumatic brain edema and Alzheimer's pathology
**Jinwei Liu & Nan Peng | Sci Adv**

**Why it matters:** Non-pharmacological ways to augment glymphatic clearance are scarce. In healthy mice, 40.68 MHz ultrashort wave raised glymphatic influx and efflux within 10 minutes, coupled to increased delta-wave synchronization. Chemogenetic silencing abolished the effect (neural-activity dependent) and aquaporin-4 blockade abolished it (astrocytic water-channel dependent), with USW promoting perivascular AQP4 polarization. USW reduced traumatic brain injury-induced cerebral edema and improved cognitive deficits in Alzheimer's model mice by restoring glymphatic function. Causal via chemogenetics/pharmacology, but mouse-only.

**Model/system & primary method:** Healthy, TBI, and Alzheimer's transgenic mice; local field potentials/EEG, chemogenetics, AQP4 pharmacology, glymphatic tracer imaging.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeh8870

### The shifting landscape of brain-wide myelination across the human lifespan
**Sahar Ahmad & Pew-Thian Yap | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** This provides a normative, brain-wide reference for myelination — T1w/T2w ratios across 214 regions in individuals aged 0–100. Changes followed a triphasic trajectory: rapid infancy growth (primary myelination), an adolescence/adulthood reacceleration (secondary myelination), and late-life decline (demyelination), with marked regional heterogeneity in timing, related to regional volume and cortical thickness. It offers a population-level baseline for interpreting myelin alterations in aging and demyelinating disease. The T1w/T2w ratio is an MRI proxy, and the design is cross-sectional.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cross-sectional MRI, 214 brain regions, ages 0–100; T1w/T2w ratio mapping.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77073-3

### Habitual diet, gut microbiome dysbiosis, and α-synucleinopathy conversion in isolated REM sleep behavior disorder
**Suyi Xie & Bei Huang | Mov Disord**

**Why it matters:** Isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD) is the strongest prodrome of α-synucleinopathy, and this cohort identifies modifiable diet–microbiome factors. Among 287 iRBD patients and 208 controls with shotgun metagenomics, iRBD was marked by a lower healthful plant-based diet index and higher intake of unhealthful plant foods. Over a median 4.2 years, 15.7% converted; higher coffee consumption was associated with lower conversion risk (adjusted HR 0.30), and the iRBD-enriched species *Ruthenibacterium lactatiformans* mediated the link between unhealthful diet and dementia-first conversion. The design is observational, so diet–outcome links are associative.

**Model/system & primary method:** Prospective human cohort (n=495; 456 metagenomes); food-frequency plant-based diet indices, Cox models, mediation analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70562

### EGFR-targeted antibody-toxin conjugate in recurrent glioma: a phase 1 trial
**Vidyalakshmi Chandramohan & Annick Desjardins | Nat Med**

**Key findings:** A single convection-enhanced infusion of D2C7-ATC (targeting wild-type EGFR and EGFRvIII) was tested in 81 patients with biopsy-confirmed recurrent malignant glioma. Median overall survival was 7.9 months among 72 IDH-wild-type recurrent glioblastoma patients; treatment was generally tolerated (mostly grade 1–2 events), with the maximum tolerated dose exceeded at the highest dose level. Exploratory biomarker analyses flagged longer survival in subgroups (low pre-treatment tumor D2C7 reactivity, mOS 17.4 months; low post-treatment plasma IL-8, mOS 16.6 months), with signatures of intratumoral and systemic immune activation. Single-arm, single-site design.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human single-arm phase 1 trial (NCT02303678); convection-enhanced delivery with survival and immune biomarker correlates.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04715-w

### Raphe nuclei in cognition: a network-level framework for behavioral-state control
**Nima Khalighinejad & Matthew F S Rushworth | Neuron**

**Key findings:** This review argues against treating raphe-nuclei functions (arousal, inhibition, patience, affect, flexibility) in isolation, proposing instead a network-level account in which the raphe are embedded in distributed cortico-subcortical and neuromodulatory circuits. Across species, raphe activity tracks environmental statistics — value, uncertainty, threat, reward context — and governs transitions between motivational and cognitive states, framed as regulating when a behavioral state should persist, update, or switch. It synthesizes circuit tracing, cell-type-specific manipulation, high-resolution imaging/neurostimulation, and computational modeling rather than presenting new data.

**Model/system & primary method:** Narrative/conceptual review across species (not primary data).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.013

### Midlife and late-life Alzheimer disease blood biomarkers and 11-year cognitive decline
**Jason R Smith & Priya Palta | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In the ARIC-NCS cohort (n=1,515), each SD-higher midlife p-tau181 (mean age 59) was associated with faster late-life memory decline (β=−0.030) emerging about 20 years later, whereas midlife Aβ42/40, NfL, and GFAP were not associated with decline in any domain. In late-life (mean age 77), p-tau181, NfL, and GFAP all tracked faster domain-specific decline, with the strongest associations for p-tau181 and memory. The results underscore a potentially long prodromal window but are observational.

**Model/system & primary method:** Prospective human cohort; Simoa plasma biomarkers, linear mixed-effects models.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218692

### Role of APOE ε4 and ε2 with longitudinal cognitive decline among those with exceptional longevity
**Hilary L Colbeth & Rachel A Whitmer | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In LifeAfter90 (n=445, mean age 93, multiethnic), after accounting for the competing risk of death, ε4 carriers declined faster in executive function (β=−0.07) but not in verbal episodic or semantic memory, while ε2 carriers declined more slowly in verbal episodic memory (β=0.07). Neither genotype substantially altered mortality hazard. The findings extend ε4's executive-function cost into the tenth decade of life, though effect sizes are modest and the design observational.

**Model/system & primary method:** Longitudinal human cohort aged 90+; joint linear mixed-effects and Cox models.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218596

### Salivary dim-light melatonin onset is associated with functional decline, respiratory symptoms, and survival in early ALS
**Alessandro Bombaci & Alessandro Cicolin | Ann Neurol**

**Key findings:** In 50 people with ALS within 18 months of onset, central circadian phase (salivary dim-light melatonin onset, DLMO) was earlier than in controls (20:24 vs 20:58) despite similar sleep timing and chronotype. Within the ALS cohort, a later baseline DLMO correlated with worse functional/motor status, faster progression, incident dyspnea/orthopnea by 6 months (adjusted OR 3.11), and poorer survival/tracheostomy-free outcome; DLMO was stable over ~6 months. The prognostic associations are exploratory and the sample small.

**Model/system & primary method:** Prospective observational human cohort; home-based dim-light salivary melatonin profiling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78398

### Integration of neuroimaging signatures of aging and Alzheimer's with fluid biomarkers
**Mitzi M Gonzales & Mohamad Habes | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In 1,297 participants without dementia, an MRI brain-aging atrophy signature (SPARE-BA) correlated most strongly with plasma neurofilament light, while an AD-pattern signature (SPARE-AD) correlated most strongly with plasma p-tau217 — dissociating nonspecific aging from AD-specific atrophy. Combining the imaging signatures with plasma biomarkers achieved the highest diagnostic discriminability and the strongest associations with clinical progression. The biomarker associations are cross-sectional.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multi-cohort T1 MRI plus plasma biomarkers; partial correlations, logistic and Cox models.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71889

### Associations of antibodies against several infections with Alzheimer disease neuropathology
**Caterina Felici & Dylan M Williams | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In the 1946 British birth cohort, serologic exposure to 14 pathogens measured at age 60–64 showed no notable association with plasma p-tau217 or Aβ-PET positivity at 69–71 — a largely null test of the hypothesis that cerebral amyloid aggregates as an antimicrobial defense. Exceptions were exploratory: a nonsignificant trend toward higher Aβ-PET odds with Epstein–Barr virus (OR 2.84) and *Toxoplasma gondii* (OR 1.69), plus possible APOE ε4 and education interactions for *H. pylori* and CMV. Associations carried wide confidence intervals.

**Model/system & primary method:** Prospective human birth cohort; multiplex serology, plasma p-tau217, Aβ-PET.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218584

### Progressive neurodegeneration of the locus coeruleus drives rapid eye movement sleep fragmentation
**Zelinger, N. & Nir, Y. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Graded caspase-3-mediated locus coeruleus (LC) ablation in adult female DBH-Cre mice (~25% neuronal loss by day 8, ~50% by day 18), tracked with longitudinal sleep recording and LC calcium photometry. REM sleep fragmentation emerged already at the mild degeneration stage; by the moderate stage, light-phase REM sleep increased while REM theta power declined progressively across stages. The design offers causal evidence that LC loss alone can produce the REM disturbances seen early in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease, though it uses chemical ablation (not tau-driven degeneration) in female mice only.

**Model/system & primary method:** DBH-Cre mouse LC ablation; EEG sleep staging and fiber photometry.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.12.758491

### The Alzheimer's disease risk gene ABCA7 regulates microglial lysosomal and lipid homeostasis and promotes phagocytosis to maintain neuronal health
**Heuer, S. E. & Young-Pearse, T. L. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Using isogenic ABCA7-knockout iPSC-derived microglia, astrocytes, and neurons, ABCA7 loss produced the largest proteomic changes in microglia: downregulated lysosomal signatures, reduced DQ-BSA degradative activity and LAMP1+ area, impaired phagocytosis, and altered lipid metabolism, with downstream harm to neurons. Correcting the common AD-risk variant rs3752246 in patient-derived iPSCs reversed phenotypes, tying a risk allele directly to microglial dysfunction. Findings are from human iPSC-derived cells in vitro.

**Model/system & primary method:** Isogenic CRISPR/Cas9 iPSC-derived microglia/astrocytes/neurons; unbiased proteomics, lysosomal and phagocytosis assays, variant correction.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.16.759681

### A humanized mouse model of DRPLA recapitulates epilepsy and demonstrates antisense oligonucleotide-mediated disease modification
**Q. Wu & Melissa Barker-Haliski | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Fully humanized ATN1 knock-in mice carrying human mutant ATN1 with 112 CAG repeats (Atn1Q112/+) were highly seizure-prone with ataxia, early mortality, and spontaneous seizures on video-EEG. An investigational antisense oligonucleotide targeting CAG-expanded human ATN1, dosed at postnatal day 2 and again at 4 weeks, normalized seizure threshold, decreased ataxia, abolished spontaneous seizures, and protected against early death. This is preclinical proof-of-concept for ASO lowering in a polyglutamine repeat disorder, using early and chronic dosing in mice.

**Model/system & primary method:** Humanized ATN1 knock-in mouse; acute seizure testing, ataxia profiling, chronic video-EEG; ASO intervention.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.12.758592

### Comparative analysis of plasma pTau217, NfL and eight modifiable risk scores for 11-year dementia prediction in the HUNT Study
**J. Stubs & Geir Selbaek | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 3,935 participants of the Norwegian HUNT study (mean age 68), demographics plus plasma pTau217 predicted 11-year all-cause dementia (AUC 0.773); adding any of eight modifiable dementia risk scores gave small but statistically significant gains (AUC up to 0.783), while adding NfL did not improve discrimination. Performance was stronger in women and APOE4-positive individuals and weaker below age 65. Combined pTau217-by-risk-score tertiles produced graded, 5–11-fold differences in dementia odds. Single-population, observational.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cohort; nested logistic regression, net reclassification, risk stratification.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.15.26365615

### Sex shapes capillary flows and oxygen extraction in the middle-aged mouse brain
**E. Gutierrez-Jimenez & Leif Østergaard | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** At 15 months, cortical blood flow was higher in female than male mice despite similar pial-vessel diameter and comparable estrogen levels. Capillary transit-time heterogeneity scaled more steeply with mean transit time in females (consistent with a passively regulated network) alongside a lower oxygen extraction fraction but preserved arterial oxygen delivery. Spatial transcriptomics revealed region-specific sex programs, most prominent in white matter (a male-associated myelin/axonogenesis signature with reduced white-matter volume in females), coinciding with higher hippocampal microglial reactivity and an altered spatial search strategy in females. The work localizes sex differences in brain aging within the cerebrovascular hierarchy; mouse data.

**Model/system & primary method:** 15-month-old mice; two-photon indicator-dilution and intravascular oxygen measurement, spatial transcriptomics, behavior.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.12.758495
