1Cryo-EM structure of ex vivo Sup35 yeast prion and the factors defining prion phenotype

Nat Commun  Alexander A Dergalev & Vitaly V Kushnirov
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Prion and amyloid strains fold into distinct conformers that dictate different pathologies, but what sets one strain apart from another has been hard to pin down. Working in the tractable Sup35 yeast system, the authors show that the difference between "strong" and "weak" strains — which differ in fibril fragmentation frequency and thus in nonsense-suppression efficiency — is governed by fibril stability rather than by how efficiently chaperones bind. They solve a 2.7 Å cryo-EM structure of a strong variant purified directly from yeast (ordered core residues 2–64) and find that the fully protease-resistant segment (2–32) is the key determinant of prion maintenance, while the partially resistant stretch (33–72) matters less. The ex vivo fold also diverges substantially from Sup35 amyloid assembled in vitro, a reminder that in-cell prion conformers are not reliably reproduced by test-tube fibrillization.
Model & method
Sup35 prion purified from S. cerevisiae; single-particle cryo-EM (2.7 Å), protease-resistance mapping, and fragmentation/stability assays.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77823-3

Plasma Proteomic Classification of Alzheimer Disease, Parkinson Disease, and Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis Using a Single Neurodegeneration Panel: A Diagnostic Cohort Study

medRxiv  Okada, K. & Ito, D.
Key findings
Using a single off-the-shelf plasma panel (Olink Target 48 Neurodegeneration, 42 assays retained after QC) with three disease-specific Elastic Net classifiers, the authors achieved disease-selective discrimination in a 302-participant single-center cohort (healthy controls, AD continuum, PD, ALS, and other neurological disorders). In a locked hold-out set (n=91), AUCs were 0.974 (AD), 0.926 (PD), and 0.995 (ALS). Plasma DOPA decarboxylase was excluded a priori because its levels mainly reflect dopaminergic treatment. The result argues that one shared multiplex panel, rather than separate single-analyte tests, can support classification across several neurodegenerative diseases — though this is a single-center diagnostic cohort with modest PD and ALS sample sizes (n=39 and 27) and awaits external and population-level validation.
Model & method
Human plasma (Keio University Hospital cohort); Olink proximity-extension proteomics with diagnosis-stratified discovery/hold-out split and balanced Elastic Net models.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.19.26365782

Spatial proximity to AD pathology defines cell-type and genotype-specific characteristics in human AD hippocampus

bioRxiv  Shi, C. H. & Huang, T. Y.
Key findings
The authors built a pipeline that concurrently annotates amyloid-β plaques, AT8 phospho-tau, and cell-specific MERFISH expression in human control and AD hippocampus, then read out transcriptional signatures of glia and neurons as a function of their physical distance from pathology. A notable genotype-specific effect emerged: astrocytic APOE expression was suppressed specifically near Aβ plaques in APOE4/E4 donors, whereas other APOE genotypes showed no such proximity effect. Spatially defined signatures were used to infer pathology-associated states in larger snRNA-seq datasets lacking spatial context, and 120 AD risk loci were examined for spatially sensitive, cell-type-specific expression. The work is human and observational, linking local pathological microenvironment to cell-state and genetic variation rather than establishing causation.
Model & method
Human post-mortem hippocampus (control and AD); MERFISH spatial transcriptomics with concurrent Aβ/AT8 pathology annotation, integrated with snRNA-seq and AD GWAS loci.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.15.759523
Preprint · not peer reviewed

Multi-level repression ensures tight translation control of C9ORF72

bioRxiv  Haizel, S. & Vogel, C.
Key findings
The intronic repeat expansion in C9ORF72 — the most common genetic cause of ALS/FTD — both generates toxic repeat-RNA/dipeptides and suppresses the major transcript (Variant 2), producing a loss-of-function arm. Dissecting the Variant 2 mRNA leader by structure, ribosome occupancy, and a quantitative mutant screen, the authors find baseline translation is held low by a cooperating set of elements: near-cognate start codons in all three frames, a frame-2 start–stop element, a frame-1 upstream translon, and secondary structure, arranged as a buffered feedforward repressor. Critically, ADAR-based RNA editing of specific leader sites raised translation of the gene — a candidate strategy to restore C9ORF72 expression and counter the loss-of-function component. Mechanistic work on reporter and endogenous leader sequences; therapeutic relevance remains to be tested in disease models. (Revision of a preprint first posted August 2025.)
Model & method
C9ORF72 Variant 2 mRNA leader; mutational/reporter screen, ribosome-occupancy analysis, and site-directed ADAR RNA editing.
https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.08.09.669471
Preprint · not peer reviewed

A new epigenetic framework for DNA methylation, hydroxymethylation, and gene expression during brain aging

bioRxiv  Ocañas, S. R. & Freeman, W. M.
Key findings
By resolving 5-methylcytosine and 5-hydroxymethylcytosine separately across the major cell types of the mouse hippocampus, the authors argue aging reshapes rather than dismantles epigenetic control: cell identity and regulatory logic are preserved with age, while cell-type-specific changes accumulate at loci governing each cell's specialized functions. They further show the relationship between a DNA modification and gene expression is not uniform but depends on modification type, genomic context, sequence specificity, and gene length. This counters the common framing of aging as a global loss of epigenetic identity, instead favoring a targeted remodeling model. Mouse-only and descriptive, but the cell-type and modification-type resolution addresses a real confound in bulk-tissue methylation studies.
Model & method
Mouse hippocampus, cell-type-resolved; parallel 5mC/5hmC profiling integrated with gene expression.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.15.757659
Preprint · not peer reviewed

Polyglutamine-expanded TBP rewires neuronal and muscle proteomes beyond transcription in a Drosophila model of SCA17

bioRxiv  Prifti, M. V. & Todi, S. V.
Key findings
SCA17 is caused by a polyglutamine expansion in the TATA-binding protein (TBP), a core transcription factor — yet the authors show pathology extends well beyond transcription. Expressing wild-type or pathogenic TBP in Drosophila neurons or skeletal muscle and profiling with TMT proteomics, eight post-translational modifications, and RNA-seq, they find shared TBP-dosage responses (ribosomes, mitochondria, repressed innate immunity) dominate, while narrower disease-specific effects are tissue-dependent: SCA17 activated xenobiotic, retinoid, and glutathione-redox pathways in the CNS but attenuated anabolism in muscle. Many proteomic changes occurred without matching mRNA changes, implicating post-transcriptional mechanisms, and knockdown of selected SCA17-regulated proteins reduced cytotoxicity, validating them as modifiers. An invertebrate model, so conservation of the identified targets to mammalian disease remains to be established.
Model & method
Drosophila expressing WT or pathogenic (polyQ) TBP in neurons or muscle; TMT quantitative proteomics with eight PTMs, RNA-seq, and targeted knockdown of candidate modifiers.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.20.760399
