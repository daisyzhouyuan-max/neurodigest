### Blood proteomics of menopause map to brain aging and dementia risk
**Madeline Wood Alexander & Kaitlin B Casaletto | Nat Med**

**Why it matters:** Menopause is implicated in later neurodegenerative risk, but the mediating biology has been unclear. Using serum NULISAseq proteomics in 80 rigorously STRAW+10-staged pre-, peri- and postmenopausal women (43–58 years), spontaneous menopause was characterized by dysregulated inflammatory, synaptic, metabolic and Alzheimer's-associated proteins that tracked more tightly with reproductive hormones than with chronological age; an age-matched 2,814-woman plasma Olink cohort replicated the shifts and revealed broader inflammatory/catabolic upregulation. The result nominates hormone-driven inflammatory and synaptic remodeling — rather than age per se — as a route from the menopause transition to dementia vulnerability. The design is cross-sectional and female-only, so the ordering is inferred across women rather than followed longitudinally.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human serum proteomics (NULISAseq discovery, n=80; plasma Olink validation, n=2,814); STRAW+10 menopause staging mapped onto brain-aging and AD biomarker processes.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04648-4

### Compartmentalized B cell–microglia crosstalk drives ischemic white matter demyelination
**Lulu Xu & Chuan Qin | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** White matter injury underlies much vascular cognitive impairment, but the role of B cells has been unclear. Under chronic cerebral hypoperfusion in adult male mice, B cells expanded and were activated in the dura mater and adopted an IgG-secreting phenotype; microglia recruited these meningeal B cells via MIF–CD74/CXCR4 signaling, and the secreted IgG drove microglial ferroptosis through Fcγ receptors, worsening myelin thinning, neuroinflammation and cognition. B cell depletion alleviated demyelination and cognitive deficits, placing a meningeal-B-cell/microglia ferroptosis axis causally upstream of ischemic white matter damage. Evidence is mouse-only and male-only, but the loss-of-function and the defined signaling arms are clean.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse chronic cerebral hypoperfusion model; B cell depletion, MIF–CD74/CXCR4 and Fcγ-receptor/ferroptosis dissection with meningeal immunophenotyping.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76817-5

### Sensory neuronal mTORC1 signaling establishes a neuroimmune memory that initiates allergic immunity
**Xueping Zhu & Caroline L Sokol | Immunity**

**Why it matters:** Allergic sensitization accrues from repeated subthreshold allergen exposures, but how neurons store that exposure history was unknown. In mouse skin, protease-allergen exposure induced sustained mTORC1 signaling and PGC-1α-associated mitochondrial remodeling in cutaneous sensory neurons, establishing a metabolically primed state with heightened responsiveness; upon re-exposure these neurons drove enhanced itch and CD301b+ dendritic-cell recruitment to skew Th2 immunity. The work defines a sensory-neuron-intrinsic form of "neuroimmune memory," giving a concrete neuronal substrate to allergic priming. The evidence is mechanistic and preclinical, in a defined cutaneous allergen model.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse cutaneous protease-allergen model; sensory-neuron mTORC1/PGC-1α manipulation with behavioral itch and dendritic-cell recruitment readouts.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2026.08.012

### Genetic resilience and resistance in Alzheimer's disease diagnosis and pathology
**Ganna Leonenko & Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative | Ann Neurol**

**Why it matters:** Some individuals avoid AD pathology as they age, or retain cognition despite substantial pathology, and educational attainment is protective for reasons that remain unclear. Partitioning educational-attainment polygenic scores into cognitive (intelligence-linked) and non-cognitive (behavioral/social) components across four cohorts — two with living-participant biomarkers (BioHermes-1, n=770; ADNI, n=1,361) and two postmortem — the authors found the components differentially associated with AD diagnosis and neuropathology, dissociating resistance to pathology from resilience of cognition. This gives a genetically grounded handle on why education tracks protection. The findings are associational and cohort-based, without direct mechanism.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multi-cohort genetics (two biomarker cohorts, two postmortem); component-partitioned polygenic scores tested against AD diagnosis and neuropathology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78367

### An anterior zona incerta to ventral tegmental area circuit controls compulsive eating in a binge-eating model
**Zongming Chen & Wei L Shen | Neuron**

**Key findings:** In a mouse binge-eating-disorder model, GABAergic anterior zona incerta (aZI) neurons became hyperexcitable after intermittent high-fat-diet training, and their activity was required for the development of compulsive, aversion-resistant intake. These neurons project to the ventral tegmental area, where training weakened their inhibitory control over dopamine neurons through both direct and indirect pathways, enhancing dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens. The circuit was dissected causally with optogenetics and genetic manipulation alongside single-cell RNA sequencing, defining an aZI→VTA node that sustains compulsive binge eating.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse binge-eating-disorder model; fiber photometry, optogenetics, circuit tracing and single-cell RNA sequencing.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.014

### Cross-ethnic replication of intersectin-1 (ITSN1) as a Parkinson's disease susceptibility gene
**Chunyu Li & Huifang Shang | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** Rare loss-of-function ITSN1 variants, recently linked to Parkinson's disease in individuals of European ancestry, were tested in 2,063 unrelated Chinese PD patients against 3,298 controls plus East Asian reference datasets. Three previously unreported frameshift loss-of-function variants — all absent from controls and classified likely pathogenic — drove significant gene-based enrichment of rare damaging variants, replicating ITSN1 (an endocytic scaffolding protein) as a PD risk gene across ancestries. As a rare-variant burden analysis, the association rests on a small number of carriers.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human whole-exome sequencing case-control (Chinese cohort); SKAT-O rare-variant burden testing.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70554

### MRI subtypes reveal distinct biological substrates and disability profiles in multiple sclerosis
**Paolo Preziosa & Maria A Rocca | Brain**

**Key findings:** Applying the unsupervised Subtype and Stage Inference (SuStaIn) framework to multimodal 3T brain MRI — lesion volume, white-matter mean diffusivity and atrophy — from 1,017 MS patients and 548 controls, the authors resolved data-driven imaging subtypes that mapped onto distinct disability, cognition, age-at-onset, genetic-severity and relapse-independent-progression profiles, capturing heterogeneity that conventional clinical phenotypes miss. The subtyping offers a biologically anchored stratification for prognosis and trial enrichment. The staging inference is cross-sectional and needs longitudinal confirmation of subtype trajectories.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multimodal brain MRI (n=1,017 patients, 548 controls); SuStaIn machine-learning subtype-and-stage inference.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag320

### Prosocial helping behaviors attenuate APOE ε4-driven accelerated cognitive decline
**Sae Hwang Han & Jessica Danielle Faul | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In a multiethnic US sample (N=17,972) followed for over two decades, within-person adoption and sustained maintenance of formal volunteering attenuated the accelerated cognitive decline typically observed in APOE ε4 carriers, with protective associations evident at modest engagement. The multilevel models isolate within-person change and address key sources of endogeneity, positioning prosocial engagement as a modifiable buffer against a genetic risk that engages overlapping neuroinflammatory, vascular and stress-regulatory pathways. The design is observational, so residual confounding and reverse causation cannot be fully excluded.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human longitudinal cohort; multilevel within-person models of volunteering and informal helping moderating APOE ε4-related decline.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71877

### Integrated multi-omics nominates the APP–CD74 axis as an immunoregulatory target in pediatric high-grade glioma
**Zhuang Wang & Rachid Drissi | Acta Neuropathol**

**Key findings:** Combining bulk RNA sequencing of 26 diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG) autopsy specimens with single-cell data from eight patients and integrated pediatric high-grade glioma datasets plus transwell and flow-cytometry assays, the authors showed that mesenchymal-like tumor cells chemotactically recruit monocyte-derived tumor-associated macrophages and identified an APP–CD74 signaling axis as a node in the immunosuppressive microenvironment. This nominates APP–CD74 as a candidate immunotherapeutic target in a tumor with almost no options. The mechanistic links are largely correlative and in vitro, without in vivo target validation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human DIPG/pediatric high-grade glioma bulk and single-cell transcriptomics with transwell and flow-cytometry validation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03089-0

### Kidney function tracks cognitive and structural brain health across cohorts
**Min Li & Hao Wang | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Across two population-based cohorts (META-KLS and UK Biobank; 6,847 adults) plus 426 CKD patients, higher cumulative estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) associated with better MoCA scores, whereas greater eGFR variability associated with worse cognition and with white-matter and cortical structural signatures on MRI. The data implicate both the level and the instability of renal function in brain aging, arguing eGFR variability is an under-recognized vascular contributor to cognitive risk. The associations are cross-sectional, so the causal direction between renal and brain aging remains open.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multi-cohort epidemiology with brain MRI; eGFR level and variability versus MoCA and brain structure.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71840

### The United States CADASIL Consortium: baseline findings from a natural-history study
**J S Paulsen & The United States CADASIL Consortium | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** This study establishes baseline clinical, functional and multimodal-biomarker characteristics of the first US cohort of CADASIL — a monogenic (NOTCH3) small-vessel disease used here as a single-cause model for sporadic vascular contributions to cognitive impairment and dementia (VCID), the second leading cause of dementia. ### The value of a family history to distinguish monogenic from polygenic amyotrophic lateral sclerosis
**P Beele & W van Rheenen | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Because both rare Mendelian variants and polygenic burden can cause familial clustering of ALS, the authors used current estimates of ALS genetic architecture to model how accurately a positive family history predicts the underlying genetic basis. The analysis indicates that familial recurrence is an unreliable proxy for monogenic inheritance — reduced-penetrance pathogenic variants can present as sporadic in small modern pedigrees, while polygenic risk can mimic Mendelian clustering — with consequences for genetic testing, counseling and gene-discovery strategy. This is a genetic-epidemiology modeling study rather than new sequencing.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human genetic-epidemiology modeling of ALS/FTD family history against monogenic versus polygenic architecture.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.16.26362994

### Peripheral immunoinflammatory markers in LRRK2 G2385R and R1628P Parkinson's disease
**H X Ding & L C Lit | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 240 participants, plasma immunoinflammatory markers were profiled across PD carriers of the Asian-prevalent LRRK2 G2385R and R1628P variants, idiopathic PD and controls. Several markers were paradoxically lower in variant carriers than in idiopathic PD or controls (TNF, CCL2, CX3CL1 and CCL5 in G2385R; IL-6, TNF, CCL2 and CCL5 in R1628P), and higher monocyte LRRK2 kinase activity (pRab10-Thr73) correlated with lower TNF — suggesting variant-specific peripheral immune signatures rather than uniform inflammation. The study is cross-sectional and correlational in a moderate sample.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human plasma multiplex immunoassay with monocyte LRRK2 kinase (pRab10) readouts across PD genotypes.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.20.26363505

### Cell-type-specific transcriptional risk scores and longitudinal multiple sclerosis outcomes
**M Upcott & K L Kreft | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Integrating the largest MS GWAS (14,802 patients) with blood and brain eQTL data via summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR) and the HEIDI test, and with single-cell expression from ~1.27 million PBMCs, the authors constructed cell-type-specific transcriptional risk scores and tested them against longitudinal MS outcomes, arguing these expression-anchored scores may distinguish clinical phenotypes better than standard polygenic risk scores. ### Paired CSF and plasma metabolomics define a CSF-centered signature in idiopathic normal-pressure hydrocephalus
**D Visentin & Z Maglica | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In a small exploratory case-control study (10 iNPH patients, 10 controls) using paired CSF and plasma HILIC-based metabolomics, iNPH — a potentially treatable dementia — showed a robust CSF-centered metabolic phenotype that was only partly reflected in peripheral blood, indicating compartment-specific alterations. The findings are hypothesis-generating given the very small sample and exploratory design.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human paired CSF/plasma metabolomics (HILIC-LC-MS) case-control.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.21.26363535
