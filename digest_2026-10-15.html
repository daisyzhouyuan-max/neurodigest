1Preclinical and first-in-human evaluation of VG-3927/SAR448851, an oral small-molecule TREM2 agonist for Alzheimer's disease

Sci Transl Med  Borislav Dejanovic & Christian Mirescu
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Loss-of-function TREM2 variants are among the strongest genetic risk factors for late-onset Alzheimer's, but prior agonism efforts relied on antibodies that can be sequestered by soluble TREM2. VG-3927 is an orally dosed, CNS-penetrant small molecule that binds the transmembrane domains of TREM2 and its adaptor DAP12 to promote higher-order receptor assembly, cooperating with endogenous ligands including Aβ and restoring signalling in disease-associated variants. In 5xFAD mice expressing common-variant human TREM2 the tool compound reprogrammed plaque-niche microglia toward a neuroprotective state, enhanced Aβ phagocytosis and raised brain glucose metabolism, whereas an agonist antibody failed to engage plaque microglia. A first-in-human phase 1 trial (n=115) found it well tolerated to 140 mg once daily with high CNS penetration (CSF-to-unbound-plasma ratio 0.91) and dose-dependent reductions in CSF sTREM2, a target-engagement biomarker.
Model & method
5xFAD;hTREM2CV mice and iPSC-derived neuron–microglia–astrocyte cultures; randomized double-blind placebo-controlled phase 1 (NCT06343636).
https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aec5740

2Preclinical and clinical investigation of VES001, an oral small-molecule sortilin receptor inhibitor for frontotemporal dementia

Nat Commun  Louise Klem & Mads Kjolby
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
GRN haploinsufficiency lowers progranulin and causes a genetic form of frontotemporal dementia (FTD-GRN), for which raising progranulin is the leading therapeutic rationale. VES001 is an oral small molecule that inhibits the progranulin-clearing receptor sortilin without downregulating it, and preclinically it bound sortilin selectively and raised progranulin in plasma, CSF and hippocampal interstitial fluid in rats. In a first-in-human phase 1a study, 79 healthy adults received single ascending doses (10–990 mg) or 7-day multiple ascending doses; the primary safety and tolerability endpoints were met with no deaths, serious adverse events or treatment-related discontinuations. This is a healthy-volunteer trial, so efficacy in FTD-GRN carriers remains to be shown, but it establishes a tractable oral route to progranulin elevation.
Model & method
Male rats (biodistribution/pharmacodynamics) and a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 1a in healthy adults.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77638-2

3Suppressing neural stem cell PLA2G7 restores inhibitory circuit function and improves cognition in aging mice

Nat Aging  Fuling Wu & Nannan Guo
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Age-related cognitive decline tracks with altered inhibitory signalling in hippocampal circuits, but the upstream regulators are unclear. The authors show that PLA2G7, a secreted lipid-metabolizing enzyme expressed in adult hippocampal neural stem cells, negatively regulates inhibitory circuit function in the dentate gyrus through a non-neurogenic, lipid-dependent mechanism. Conditional deletion of Pla2g7 in NSCs enhanced inhibitory input onto dentate granule cells and improved hippocampal cognition, lipidomics revealed PLA2G7-dependent phospholipid remodelling, and phosphatidylcholine supplementation rescued the inhibitory-synapse deficit. In aged mice, PLA2G7 suppression partially normalized circuit imbalance and improved cognition, nominating it as an aging-cognition target. Evidence is mouse-only and causal via genetic deletion.
Model & method
Conditional NSC-specific Pla2g7 knockout and aged wild-type mice; electrophysiology, lipidomic profiling and behavioural cognition assays.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-026-01202-2

4Mild amyloid burden in the brains of cognitively unimpaired individuals is associated with long-term cognitive impairment

Ann Neurol  Chengjie Xiong & John C Morris
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Standard amyloid-PET thresholds (≈20 Centiloid) define "positivity," but whether subthreshold amyloid carries risk has been unclear. Across 1,896 cognitively unimpaired participants followed for up to 20.3 years, amyloid-positive individuals (Centiloid >20) were 154% more likely to develop cognitive impairment (HR 2.54, p<0.0001), and the analysis of subthresholds indicated that burden below the conventional positivity cutoff still predicted later impairment and faster decline. The finding argues for reconsidering where the clinically meaningful amyloid threshold lies in preclinical Alzheimer's. This is observational cohort evidence, so it establishes risk association rather than causation.
Model & method
Human longitudinal cohort (n=1,896) with amyloid PET; Cox proportional-hazards and linear mixed-effects modelling of subthreshold Centiloid values.
https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78392

5A microexon regulatory code reveals autism-linked genetic variation

Nature  Guillermo E Parada & Benjamin J Blencowe
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Neuronal microexons are a conserved class of alternative splicing events enriched in nervous-system genes and frequently disrupted in autistic brains, yet the full repertoire and the impact of genetic variation on them were unknown. Deep transcriptome profiling uncovered hundreds of new microexons, including neuronal-subtype-specific events and some that trigger nonsense-mediated decay, and a machine-learning model defined a sequence code predictive of neuronal splicing and its regulatory elements. Applying the code identified genomic variants predicted to alter microexon splicing in autistic individuals, with rare variants having the strongest estimated effect, enriched in idiopathic cases, associated with autism status, and converging on synaptic and other autism-linked genes as well as genes not previously implicated. The variant effects are predicted rather than functionally validated.
Model & method
Human transcriptome profiling across tissues/cell types with machine-learning sequence modelling and autism cohort variant analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11119-w

Symmetries and continuous attractors in disordered neural circuits

Nat Neurosci  David G Clark & Haim Sompolinsky
Key findings
Classical continuous-attractor models require symmetric weights, yet real head-direction cells are markedly heterogeneous. Building recurrent networks directly from head-direction-cell data, the authors show this heterogeneity is compatible with quasicontinuous attractors: although the weights look disordered, they carry a hidden circular symmetry whose eigenvalue degeneracies could be detected in connectome data. Dynamical mean-field theory recovers the classical ring-attractor ingredients—Mexican-hat interactions and a spontaneously broken symmetry producing bump states—suggesting continuous-attractor mechanisms can operate in complex mammalian circuits.
Model & method
Theory/computation; data-constrained recurrent network models of rodent head-direction cells with dynamical mean-field analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02452-4

Elp1 is required for myeloid differentiation and brain repopulation

Nature  Marius Marc-Daniel Mader & Marius Wernig
Key findings
Reconstituting the microglial niche with haematopoietic-stem-cell-derived myeloid cells is a candidate brain cell therapy, but the cell-intrinsic regulators were uncharacterized. Using ex vivo–expanded cultured HSPCs amenable to large-scale screening (~20,000 perturbations), the authors found neonatal astrocytes provide strong signals for myeloid specification and identified Elp1 (elongator complex subunit 1) as essential for generating—but not maintaining—myeloid cells, acting by suppressing the unfolded protein response during specification. The work establishes a genetic-screening platform for HSPC-derived microglial replacement.
Model & method
Cultured mouse HSPCs with pooled genetic screening and transplantation-based microglial repopulation assays.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11112-3

Targeted stimulation of motor cortex ensembles drives learned movements

Nature  Qiyu Chen & Takaki Komiyama
Key findings
Combining two-photon calcium imaging with holographic optogenetics in mice performing a skilled lever-press, brief synchronous stimulation of ~20 M1 layer 2/3 neurons whose activity normally precedes movement onset induced movements resembling the learned action and reproduced the learned population activity pattern. Trial-to-trial variability of the triggered activity and movements was predicted by M1's initial state, stimulation could evoke the learned activity without movement (so the pattern is not merely a motor reflection), and stimulation failed in task-disengaged mice or when movement-following neurons were targeted. This provides causal evidence that a small, defined M1 ensemble, read out from an appropriate preparatory state, drives learned movement.
Model & method
Mice; in vivo two-photon imaging with holographic optogenetic stimulation of functionally defined ensembles.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11174-3

Divergent genomic evolution in astrocytomas and oligodendrogliomas

Nature  Tamrin Chowdhury & Roel G W Verhaak
Key findings
Whole-genome and whole-exome sequencing of longitudinally sampled tumours from 183 patients with IDH-mutant glioma (GLASS Consortium) revealed distinct evolutionary trajectories: recurrent oligodendrogliomas newly acquired PI3K and NOTCH1 mutations with increased tumour mutational burden, whereas recurrent astrocytomas acquired focal oncogene amplifications and CDKN2A/B homozygous deletions associated with chromosomal instability (chromothripsis, whole-genome doubling), largely irrespective of treatment. The divergent progression routes refine how the two IDH-mutant glioma subtypes should be monitored and targeted.
Model & method
Human longitudinal tumour cohort (n=183); whole-genome and whole-exome sequencing.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11150-x

Structural basis of ligand recognition and gating in mosquito IRs

Nature  Li Xu & Xiaojing Pan
Key findings
Ionotropic receptors mediate insect olfaction and mosquito host-seeking, but their gating was structurally undefined. Cryo-EM of the IR8a–IR75k carboxylic-acid-sensing complex from the malaria mosquito Anopheles stephensi, captured in apo, ligand-bound-open and ligand-bound extracellular-domain states, shows specificity is set by alkyl-chain length and carboxylate geometry in a bilobed IR75k pocket; ligand binding closes the domain and acts as a mechanical lever that propagates through conserved linkers to dilate the pore, corroborated by molecular dynamics and electrophysiology. The atomic view offers a foundation for host-seeking-targeted vector control.
Model & method
Cryo-EM structures with molecular-dynamics simulation and electrophysiology of a mosquito olfactory receptor complex.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11239-3

Highly replicable multisite patterns of adolescent white matter maturation

Neuron  Steven L Meisler & Theodore D Satterthwaite
Key findings
The ABCD-BIDS Community Collection release 3.1.0 provides more than 24,000 fully processed diffusion-MRI datasets from the ABCD study (ages 8–17) with person-specific tractography of 62 white-matter bundles. Across bundles, advanced microstructural measures—NODDI intracellular volume fraction, DKI mean kurtosis, and MAP-MRI return-to-origin probability—were more sensitive to development and more robust to image-quality variation than tensor-derived metrics, and ComBat-GAMM longitudinal harmonization markedly improved cross-vendor generalizability. The curated resource enables reproducible study of adolescent white-matter development at scale.
Model & method
Human longitudinal neuroimaging resource; harmonized diffusion-MRI microstructure and bundle tractography.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.021

Multi-omic profiling of 85 iPSC lines from familial dementia reveals cellular diversity and regulators of organoid development

Neuron  Le Qi & Daniel H Geschwind
Key findings
Profiling 85 MAPT-mutant iPSC lines and isogenic controls with whole-genome, long-read, bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing and methylation arrays, the authors identified four reproducible iPSC states with candidate surface markers, and uncovered unintended editing events and recurrent 20q11.21 duplications linked to increased ID1 expression and impaired cortical-organoid patterning. The results show how genomic integrity and editing history confound disease-modelling and argue for using unconverted lines as isogenic controls—a practical caution for familial-dementia iPSC studies.
Model & method
Human MAPT iPSC lines and cortical organoids; integrated multi-omics (WGS, long-read, scRNA-seq, methylation).
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.022

Extracellular vesicle α-synuclein topology reveals disease-associated conformers in brain and blood

Brain  Suman Dutta & George K Tofaris
Key findings
Parkinson's disease, dementia with Lewy bodies and multiple system atrophy share α-synuclein aggregation but diverge in cellular distribution and phenotype. Combining EV isolation with electrochemiluminescence, quantitative mass spectrometry and single-vesicle dSTORM imaging of post-mortem PD, DLB, MSA and control brains, the authors mapped the topology, epitope exposure and cellular origin of EV-associated α-synuclein and found disease-specific conformational signatures, then assessed their tractability in blood. The work supports EV α-synuclein conformers as candidate biomarkers that distinguish synucleinopathies, though clinical blood-based validation is still early.
Model & method
Human post-mortem brain and blood EVs; mass spectrometry and nanoscale single-vesicle super-resolution imaging.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag356

Safety and efficacy of delpacibart zotadirsen in Duchenne muscular dystrophy amenable to exon 44 skipping (EXPLORE44): a randomised, placebo-controlled, phase 1/2 trial

Lancet Neurol  Aravindhan Veerapandiyan & EXPLORE44 Study Investigators
Key findings
No exon-skipping therapy is approved for DMD amenable to exon 44 skipping. Del-zota (AOC 1044) is a first-in-class antibody–oligonucleotide conjugate delivering an exon-44-skipping morpholino to muscle to restore dystrophin. In this two-part randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 1/2 trial in healthy adult men (part A) and ambulatory and non-ambulatory participants with DMD44 aged 7–27 (part B) across ten US sites, the conjugate was evaluated for safety, pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics (exon skipping, dystrophin restoration) and efficacy. It represents a targeted delivery strategy aimed at overcoming the poor muscle uptake that has limited oligonucleotide exon-skipping drugs.
Model & method
Human randomized controlled phase 1/2 trial (healthy adults and DMD44 participants).

Association between epilepsy and young-onset dementia: nationwide data from France and Sweden

Neurology  Quentin Calonge & Vincent Navarro
Key findings
Epilepsy is linked to late-onset dementia, but evidence for young-onset dementia (YOD, <65) was limited. Across three nationwide analyses—a French nested case-control study of incident YOD (ages 40–64, diagnosed 2016–2018) with a Swedish replication (1987–2016) and an assessment of YOD risk after midlife epilepsy—epilepsy diagnoses and antiseizure-medication dispensations in the preceding decade were associated with increased YOD risk. The concordant cross-country findings strengthen epilepsy as a risk marker for young-onset dementia, though the registry design cannot establish whether the association is causal or reflects shared early neurodegenerative pathology.
Model & method
Human nationwide nested case-control analyses (France and Sweden) with conditional logistic regression.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218700

Microglial Presenilin-1 modulates neuronal function and memory-associated behaviors

J Neurosci  Paula da Silva Frost & Jose Henrique Ledo
Key findings
Presenilin-1 is central to learning and memory, and loss of neuronal PS1 impairs synaptic plasticity—but its microglial role was unexamined. Male mice lacking PS1 selectively in microglia (PS1ΔMG) showed enhanced memory. Single-cell-deconvolved RNA-seq indicated PS1ΔMG expanded a proliferative Cd52-lo microglial population enriched in AP-1 responsive genes and raised chemokines Ccl7 and Ccl8, with in silico prediction that microglial CCL7 acts via neuronal CCR5, a negative regulator of plasticity. The findings implicate microglial PS1 in a non-cell-autonomous chemokine pathway shaping memory; evidence is mouse-only and the CCL7–CCR5 link is computationally inferred.
Model & method
Microglia-specific PS1 knockout mice; behaviour with single-cell-deconvolved transcriptomics.
https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0310-26.2026

Neurofilament light chain and mutant huntingtin across Huntington's Disease Integrated Staging System stages: a cross-sectional multimodal analysis

Mov Disord  Jesús Pérez-Pérez & HDClarity investigators
Key findings
The HD-ISS stages Huntington's disease but does not incorporate fluid biomarkers. In the Barcelona HD cohort (n=78) with external replication in the multicentre HDClarity study, CSF and plasma neurofilament light chain, CSF mutant huntingtin and total tau were quantified across HD-ISS stages 0–3 and related to genetic burden, striatal volumes and clinical measures. Mapping how these biomarkers track the staging system supports their integration into HD staging and trial design. The analysis is cross-sectional, so it characterizes stage-associated levels rather than individual progression.
Model & method
Human HD gene-expansion carriers; CSF/plasma biomarker quantification with neuroimaging and clinical correlation.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70550

Reverse engineering Lewy bodies suggests alpha-synuclein pathology as condensatopathy

bioRxiv  Sansevrino, R. & Milovanovic, D.
Key findings
How soluble monomeric α-synuclein first transitions into a Lewy body, without pre-existing fibril seeds, has been unresolved. Combining in vitro reconstitution, computational modelling and a minimal cellular system, the authors show that phase separation of α-synuclein with an interacting scaffold can recapitulate Lewy-body-like structures. Proteomics, live-cell imaging, correlative light-electron microscopy and soft X-ray tomography showed these condensates redistribute actin and membrane organelles, with mitochondria accumulating at the periphery in an actin-dependent manner. The framing of Lewy pathology as a "condensatopathy" offers a seed-independent nucleation model; it is an in vitro and minimal-cell reconstitution rather than in vivo pathology.
Model & method
In vitro reconstitution, computational modelling and a minimal cellular system; multimodal imaging.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.09.757996

Temporal RNA splicing dysregulation in a human multicellular model of C9orf72 ALS

bioRxiv  Aliya, B. & Andersen, J.
Key findings
The early, temporal evolution of molecular changes in ALS is hard to study in patients. The authors established human spinal cord organoids from C9orf72-ALS patient iPSCs that recapitulated hallmark C9orf72 pathologies, increased cell death and progressive neuronal dysfunction over time. Longitudinal profiling identified RNA splicing dysregulation as an early and persistent feature, with organoid splicing signatures matching patient-associated changes. The system provides a tractable platform for mapping ALS disease trajectory, with the usual caveat that organoids only partially model mature spinal-cord cell types and circuitry.
Model & method
Human C9orf72-ALS iPSC-derived spinal cord organoids; longitudinal transcriptomic and splicing analysis.

One night of sleep deprivation reduces spatially patterned human brain clearance: implications for simulated tau propagation

bioRxiv  Brennan, G. S. & Rognes, M. E.
Key findings
Using Bayesian fitting of a kinetic enhancement/clearance model to longitudinal CSF tracer-enhanced MRI in 21 subjects, the authors estimated region-wise clearance rates across the living human brain. Clearance was spatially patterned and consistent between subjects, highest in entorhinal cortex and decreasing across regions in Braak-stage order; one night of sleep deprivation lowered mean clearance while preserving the spatial pattern. Linking reduced clearance to simulated tau propagation connects sleep loss to neurodegeneration-relevant protein accumulation, though the clearance–tau link here is modelled rather than measured directly and the cohort is small.
Model & method
Human tracer-enhanced MRI (n=21); Bayesian kinetic modelling with simulated tau propagation.

APOE4 reduction in vascular-associated fibroblasts decreases cerebral amyloid angiopathy and associated vascular dysfunction

bioRxiv  Saadi, F. & Holtzman, D. M.
Key findings
APOE4 is the strongest genetic risk factor for cerebral amyloid angiopathy, but the cellular source driving vascular amyloid was undefined. In post-mortem APOE4/E4 CAA brain, APOE colocalized with COL6+ vascular-associated fibroblasts around Aβ-laden vessels. Using 5xFAD mice with humanized floxed APOE4 and Col1a2-CreERT to selectively suppress APOE4 in VAFs, the authors show VAF-derived APOE4 mediates CAA and cerebrovascular dysfunction independently of parenchymal sources. This nominates a specific vascular cell type as a cell-type-targeted route to reduce CAA; findings are mouse genetic plus human post-mortem correlation.
Model & method
Humanized APOE4 5xFAD mice with cell-type-specific conditional suppression; human post-mortem CAA tissue.

Neuronal and vascular genome-maintenance states organize opposing multicellular architectures in the aging brain

bioRxiv  Koshboresh, A. & Paul, A.
Key findings
Genome maintenance is usually treated as cell-intrinsic, but brain cells age within multicellular neighbourhoods. Using MERFISH to map DNA-damage-response programs across 604,252 cells in matched 2–3- and 22-month-old mouse sections, the authors found regional DDR expression formed an age-dependent mosaic accompanied by immune and oligodendroglial shifts. Low-DDR neurons occupied immune- and OPC/oligodendroglial-rich niches, whereas DDR-high vascular anchors were associated with vascular-cell and (in cortex and cerebral nuclei) oligodendroglial enrichment, with immune cells enriched around DDR-low vascular anchors. The results argue genome-maintenance state is spatially embedded in aging-brain tissue architecture; the study is descriptive and associative.
Model & method
Young vs aged mouse brain; spatial transcriptomics (MERFISH) with metacell and distance-resolved niche analysis.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.07.749801
