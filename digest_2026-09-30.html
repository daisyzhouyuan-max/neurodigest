1Cervical lymphadenectomy impairs brain lymphatic clearance and synaptic proteostasis

Neuron  Zohaib N Khan & Laura Santambrogio
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Retrospective analysis of cancer patients who underwent neck lymph node dissection without chemo-radiation found up to 25% developed mild-to-severe cognitive impairment, with accelerated temporal-horn enlargement after bilateral surgery. Mouse and rat models phenocopying abrupt disruption of cervical and submandibular drainage reproduced a CSF signature of metabolic stress and inflammation and drove hippocampal redox stress, oxidative post-translational protein modifications and microaggregation that were biochemically and structurally distinct from amyloid. Gradual — rather than abrupt — drainage reduction spared these changes, implicating the kinetics of clearance failure. The work links surgical interruption of meningeal lymphatic outflow to a non-amyloid proteostasis pathology; the human evidence is observational, with causal mechanism established in rodents.
Model & method
Retrospective human cohort with neuroimaging; mouse and rat cervical/submandibular lymphadenectomy; CSF metabolomics/proteomics and hippocampal biochemistry.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.005

2Gut microbiota primes stroke severity via the AHR in intestinal dendritic cells

Cell  Rosa Delgado Jiménez & Corinne Benakis
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Enrichment of indole-producing Escherichia coli was associated with worse ischemic stroke outcomes in both mice and patients. Microbial indole triggered an aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR)-dependent response in dendritic cells; DC-specific AHR deletion improved stroke outcomes, preserved intestinal conventional type 1 DCs and shifted DCs toward immunoregulatory, migratory states. Pharmacological AHR inhibition promoted gut-derived DC accumulation in the meninges with increased regulatory T cells and reduced neuroinflammation, and Treg depletion abolished this protection. Faecal transplant from stroke patients and indole administration recapitulated the harm, which was attenuated in DC-AHR-deficient mice. The study defines a microbiota–AHR–DC axis that programs the intestinal immune response before brain injury and nominates microbial tryptophan metabolism as a tractable target.
Model & method
Mouse ischemic stroke with patient-microbiome association and faecal transplant; DC-specific AHR knockout; pharmacological AHR inhibition; Treg depletion.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.09.013

3An African ancestry-enriched H1 sub-haplotype challenges MAPT haplotype assignment and reveals distinct tau regulatory programs

Neuron  Sarah A Weitzman & Chiara Pedicone
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Integrating long-read sequencing with transcriptomics from postmortem brain, iPSC-derived neurons and astrocytes, the authors identify H1_A, a previously unrecognized non-inverted H1 sub-haplotype at the 17q21.31 tau locus that is enriched in individuals of African ancestry. Critically, H1_A carries rs8070723 — the variant widely used to define the protective H2 haplotype — exposing systematic MAPT haplotype misassignment in non-European populations. Ancestry and haplotype shaped MAPT expression, tau isoform composition, extracellular-matrix, immune and oxidative-stress pathways, with functional effects on astrocyte migration and glutathione regulation. The findings provide a corrected framework for interpreting MAPT-associated tauopathy risk across ancestries.
Model & method
Long-read genome sequencing; postmortem human brain plus iPSC-derived neuron and astrocyte transcriptomics; functional astrocyte assays.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.008

4Acute viral infection accelerates neurodegeneration in a mouse model of ALS

Nat Commun  Art Marzok & Matthew S Miller
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
In SOD1-G93A mice, a single sublethal viral infection before symptom onset markedly accelerated disease, with rapid loss of hindlimb function, lumbar-cord gliosis and upregulation of inflammatory, metabolic and muscular transcriptional programs. Suppressing gliosis with an anti-inflammatory small molecule, or administering a direct-acting antiviral, restored a disease course resembling uninfected animals. This provides causal, mechanistic evidence that infection-elicited immune responses can alter ALS trajectory and nominates a modifiable environmental trigger — though the causal evidence is mouse-only.
Model & method
SOD1-G93A ALS mouse model; acute viral infection; anti-inflammatory and antiviral intervention; spinal-cord transcriptomics.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77353-y

5Comprehensive profiling of brain dynamics during anesthesia across phylogeny

Nat Neurosci  Andrea I Luppi & Bratislav Misic
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Compiling multiscale neural activity across human, macaque, marmoset, mouse, zebrafish and nematode and extracting >6,000 time-series features, the authors define a conserved dynamical signature of anesthesia — shorter intrinsic timescales of neural activity and dampened inter-regional synchrony, amounting to spatiotemporal isolation of local activity. Deep-brain stimulation of the macaque centromedian thalamus reversed this profile and restored behavioral responsiveness. The phenotype covaried with conserved transcriptional profiles of excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmission, and biophysical modeling linked the macroscale dynamics to synaptic E/I timescales. The result is a cross-species, cross-scale account of how anesthetics disconnect neural activity, with a causal thalamic reversal.
Model & method
Cross-species multiscale electrophysiology/imaging; massive time-series feature extraction; macaque centromedian-thalamus DBS; biophysical modeling.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02460-4

A neural architecture for imagined and overt speech motor dynamics

Nat Neurosci  Zehao Zhao & Jinsong Wu
Key findings
Using high-resolution electrocorticography, articulatory kinematic trajectories were robustly encoded in frontoparietal cortex during both imagined and overt speech. Supramodal populations in middle premotor, subcentral and postcentral–supramarginal regions showed spatiotemporally stable shared planning representations, whereas modality-specific populations for imagery versus articulation were somatotopically interleaved in primary sensorimotor cortex. A generalized decoder achieved 80.4% accuracy for imagined speech (chance 16.7%), comparable to overt speech (78.3%), revealing a somato-cognitive organization that couples supramodal planning with modality-specific representation and directly informs imagery-based brain–computer interfaces.
Model & method
Human intracranial ECoG; linear encoding models; cross-modal decoding.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02456-0

OpenFISH enables same-section spatial transcriptomics and MALDI-MSI integration

Neuron  Xinyang Li & Lihui Duan
Key findings
OpenFISH is an open-source, low-cost, modular spatial transcriptomics platform made compatible with same-section MALDI mass-spectrometry imaging, enabling cell-type-aware co-mapping of transcripts and metabolites at single-cell resolution. It revealed cell-type-specific metabolic heterogeneity in the CNS and compartment-level metabolic zonation in hippocampus; exploratory 5xFAD experiments showed concordant cell-type metabolic changes, including microglial phospholipid shifts, excitatory-neuron changes masked in bulk analysis, and elevated lysophosphatidylethanolamine features in oligodendrocytes. The platform also quantified transposable-element activation after immune challenge and candidate striatal D1-neuron patterning changes in Reeler mutants.
Model & method
Mouse tissue; integrated spatial transcriptomics with MALDI-MSI; 5xFAD and Reeler models.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.007

Time-resolved spectral parameterization: recommendations and guidelines

Neuron  Dillan Cellier & Bradley Voytek
Key findings
This methodological primer provides recommendations for time-resolved and event-related spectral parameterization — disentangling oscillatory from aperiodic (non-oscillatory) neural activity over short, noisy time windows. It catalogues the practical and statistical hazards of deploying these tools, gives guidance on optimizing and assessing model fits, sets reporting standards for reproducibility, and advises how to handle cases where oscillations are not detected. A reference for studying the distinct dynamics of periodic and aperiodic activity in cognition, behavior and disease at fine timescales.
Model & method
Methods review/primer on spectral parameterization of periodic versus aperiodic neural activity.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.009

Microglia with distinct origins and non-canonical phenotypes coexist within the border-like niche of the brain's circumventricular organs

Immunity  Hannah Van Hove & Kiavash Movahedi
Key findings
Multi-omic characterization of two circumventricular organs (area postrema and median eminence) — brain regions lacking a blood–brain barrier — revealed border-like immune niches with a distinct macrophage compartment. CVO microglia were heterogeneous, including a CLEC7A-high myelin-associated subset in the median eminence shaped by ageing and metabolism, and ontogenically diverse: perinatally entering fetal monocytes generated long-lived monocyte-derived microglia that fully adopted a yolk-sac-derived identity, while after depletion the area postrema was repopulated partly by monocytes. The work establishes CVOs as specialized immune niches whose local macrophages reflect regional metabolic and immune demands.
Model & method
Mouse; multi-omics with fate-mapping and depletion analyses of CVO macrophages.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2026.09.002

The YTHDF proteins modulate Alzheimer's disease-associated brain gene signatures

Mol Neurodegener  Shinya Tasaki & Yanling Wang
Key findings
A machine-learning model integrating cellular and molecular features to explain differential gene expression in AD nominated YTHDF proteins — the canonical readers of m6A RNA modification — as among the most influential predictors of AD brain gene signatures. YTHDF-containing protein modules were downregulated in human AD brains, and knockdown or pharmacological inhibition of YTHDFs in iPSC-derived 2D and 3D neuronal models recapitulated key AD gene signatures. eCLIP-seq showed altered YTHDF binding in AD brains at both m6A-dependent and m6A-independent sites, implicating epitranscriptomic reader dysfunction in shaping the AD transcriptome.
Model & method
Human postmortem brain; iPSC-derived neuronal models; eCLIP-seq; machine learning.
https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00986-6

Excitation-inhibition imbalance links amyloid pathophysiology to cognition in non-demented individuals

Alzheimers Dement  Shaoshi Zhang & B T Thomas Yeo
Key findings
Biophysical modeling of resting-state fMRI was combined with CSF and plasma biomarkers in two non-demented cohorts (N=302 with CSF Aβ42; N=240 with plasma p-tau217). Elevated excitation–inhibition (E/I) ratio was associated with lower CSF Aβ42 and higher plasma p-tau217, most sensitively in sensory-motor regions, whereas elevated E/I in association cortices tracked worse memory. Mediation analyses indicated the E/I ratio partially accounted for the biomarker–memory relationship, positioning E/I imbalance as an intermediate, systems-level circuit mechanism along the AD continuum. The cross-sectional mediation design limits causal interpretation.
Model & method
Human; biophysical E/I modeling of resting-state fMRI; CSF/plasma AD biomarkers; mediation analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71891

Injury-transduced oligodendrocytes modulate neuroinflammation and glial activation in diseased and non-diseased central nervous system

Nat Commun  Yan Wang & Fuzheng Guo
Key findings
In mouse models, oligodendrocytes responded to demyelination — and also to stroke, endotoxin-induced injury, neurodegeneration, traumatic injury and healthy aging — by inducing and secreting SERPINA3N, defining a "SerpinOL" state. Direct injury to oligodendrocytes, rather than inflammation alone, drove the transition, which required STAT3 activation. Functionally, SerpinOLs amplified neuroinflammation and pushed glial activation toward pro-inflammatory, neurodegenerative states, identifying a common injury-responsive oligodendrocyte population that contributes to CNS pathology beyond its myelinating role.
Model & method
Mouse models and tissue analyses across multiple injury/disease paradigms; STAT3-dependence testing.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76159-2

Disease progression in iatrogenic cerebral amyloid angiopathy

Neurology  Simon Fandler-Höfler & David John Werring
Key findings
Across 82 patients from four European countries meeting modified Queen Square criteria for iatrogenic cerebral amyloid angiopathy (iCAA) — likely caused by amyloid-β transmission during prior medical procedures, with a median 39-year latency — symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) recurred at ~19 per 100 patient-years. Initial presentation with ICH predicted higher recurrence (hazard ratio 3.14), whereas spinal rather than brain surgical exposure carried lower risk (HR 0.34). Mortality was low (7.3%) despite frequent ICH, and cognitive impairment was recorded in ~51% at follow-up. The series characterizes the long-term prognosis of this emerging transmissible amyloid vasculopathy.
Model & method
Multinational human case series (n=82); Cox regression; observational.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218511

In-silico solute transport via perivascular networks in the human intracranial space

Nat Commun  Marius Causemann & Marie E Rognes
Key findings
A mixed-dimensional mathematical framework, combined with multimodal MRI and high-performance computing, simulates the spatiotemporal spread of a solute across the subarachnoid space, ventricular system, brain parenchyma and surface perivascular spaces in subject-specific geometries. Applied to intrathecal tracer scenarios, it evaluates how CSF production, intracranial pulsatility and perivascular flow and structure jointly shape transport from the vascular mesoscale to the organ macroscale over seconds to hours. The openly available framework offers a way to integrate glymphatic/perivascular clearance mechanisms across scales; its predictions remain to be validated experimentally.
Model & method
In-silico modeling on human MRI-derived geometries; computational.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77174-z

Gantenerumab reduces brain amyloid-β proteoforms: biochemical evaluation in dominantly inherited Alzheimer disease

medRxiv  Mukherjee, S. & Aguillon, D.
Key findings
Using high-resolution mass spectrometry on postmortem brain from a dominantly inherited AD (DIAD) trial, ~4 years of gantenerumab (anti-fibrillar Aβ; n=4) produced 60–80% reductions in insoluble plaque-associated Aβ42 and Aβ40 and lower pyroglutamate Aβ3pGlu versus untreated DIAD carriers (n=40), though not down to non-DIAD control levels (n=18). Gantenerumab-treated brains also showed regionally lower insoluble phospho-tau (pT181, pT205, pT208) and microtubule-binding-region tau species without a change in total insoluble tau. Solanezumab (anti-soluble Aβ; n=4) showed no significant differences. The postmortem biochemistry corroborates that fibrillar-Aβ removal mitigates downstream tau changes, on a very small treated sample.
Model & method
Human DIAD postmortem brain homogenates; high-resolution mass spectrometry of Aβ and tau proteoforms.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.22.26363432

Plasma immune signatures associate with neurodegeneration in frontotemporal lobar degeneration

medRxiv  van Schaik, M. & Malpetti, M.
Key findings
Plasma multiplex proteomics (NULISA; 130 CNS-related and 250 inflammation-related proteins) in 177 FTLD-spectrum patients (bvFTD, PPA, PSP, CBS) and 67 controls. Neurofilament light was the strongest single case–control discriminator (AUC 0.91), and a multivariate CNS signature reached AUC 0.93 versus 0.82 for the inflammation signature; however, inflammatory proteins better separated some syndrome pairs (e.g., PPA vs PSP). SPP1 and CD276 correlated with NfL and with higher mortality, whereas higher Aβ42 associated with lower mortality, and a TNF/immune-weighted signature tracked lower frontotemporal and limbic volumes. The data suggest complementary neuroaxonal and immune biomarker dimensions across FTLD.
Model & method
Human plasma proteomics (NULISA); multivariate classification; MRI-volume canonical correlation; survival analysis.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.28.26364155

Toxicity of MAPT 4R RNA contributes to motor neuron degeneration in ALS

bioRxiv  Crerar, H. & Patani, R.
Key findings
In iPSC-derived motor neurons from VCP-mutant and sporadic ALS, mRNA isoforms encoding 4-repeat (4R) tau were upregulated and cytoplasmically enriched without a corresponding rise in tau protein. Splice-switching antisense oligonucleotides and isoform-specific siRNAs showed that increased 4R expression reduces motor-neuron viability while its knockdown improves survival, with kinetics more consistent with an RNA-intrinsic than a protein-mediated effect; exon-10-containing MAPT RNA showed increased predicted secondary structure, self-association and altered tau biocondensation in vitro. In postmortem ALS cervical cord, higher relative exon-10 usage was associated with a higher-risk phenotype and shorter disease duration, nominating 4R MAPT RNA as a therapeutic target.
Model & method
iPSC-derived motor neurons; ASO/siRNA manipulation; in-vitro biophysics; postmortem ALS spinal cord.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.22.753400

Endogenous SMN heterogeneity defines dynamic states of motor neuron vulnerability and resilience

bioRxiv  Thomas, J. & Rodriguez-Muela, N.
Key findings
Longitudinal live-cell imaging of human SMN-Clover reporter motor neurons showed that neurons with high endogenous SMN survive significantly longer than low-SMN neighbors across healthy, SMA and ALS genetic backgrounds, and that these vulnerability states are plastic — a substantial fraction of neurons progressively raise SMN and shift toward resilience. Low-SMN neurons also showed increased spontaneous activity, and in vivo, selectively vulnerable populations displayed corresponding SMN shifts during disease. Integrated transcriptomics/proteomics linked resilience to RNA metabolism, proteostasis, cytoskeletal and stress programs, and the anti-apoptotic regulator LMO3 emerged as a resilience mediator whose overexpression reduced p53-responsive genes, increased survival and pushed neurons toward higher-SMN states.
Model & method
Human iPSC-derived motor neurons with SMN reporter; longitudinal live-cell imaging; transcriptomics/proteomics; in-vivo validation.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.23.753804

The astrocytic secretome increases adult hippocampal neurogenesis and spatial memory in health and disease

bioRxiv  Vilademunt, M. & Toni, N.
Key findings
Astrocyte-conditioned solution (ACS) increased adult hippocampal neurogenesis and improved object-location memory in young and aged wild-type mice. In THY-Tau22 (tau pathology) mice, ACS restored neurogenesis and cognitive performance; in APP/PS1 (amyloid pathology) mice, ACS reduced gliogenesis and rescued deficits in neurogenesis and spatial memory while attenuating circulating pro-inflammatory cytokines and plaque-associated microglial reactivity. The results position the astrocytic secretome as a niche-targeted strategy to support hippocampal plasticity across ageing and AD models; the work is mouse-only and does not resolve the active secretome components.
Model & method
Young, aged, THY-Tau22 and APP/PS1 mice; astrocyte-conditioned medium; neurogenesis quantification and behavior.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.26.754656
