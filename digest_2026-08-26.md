### Oligodendrocyte dysfunction in human age-related cognitive decline
**Georgina A. Craig & Véronique E. Miron | Nature Medicine**

**Why it matters:** Individual variance in cognitive aging is usually attributed to neuronal or synaptic loss; this work instead assigns a causal contribution to oligodendrocytes. In human white matter, worse cognitive trajectories tracked with *smaller* myelinated axons, *thicker* myelin, and *more* oligodendrocytes — the opposite of the demyelination phenotype one might predict — alongside downregulation of NRF2. Oligodendrocyte-specific NRF2 knockout in aged mice reproduced both the white matter pathology and blunted cognitive improvement, converting a human correlation into a causal claim in mouse. NRF2 is pharmacologically tractable, which makes this a plausible target for preserving cognition in aging rather than only a descriptive finding.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem white matter with individual cognitive-decline trajectories (neuropathology + transcriptomics); aged oligodendrocyte-conditional *Nrf2*-knockout mice.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04608-y

### Distinct filament conformation for receptor-bound amyloid-β from Alzheimer's disease brain
**Mikhail A. Kostylev & Stephen M. Strittmatter | Nature Communications**

**Why it matters:** The synaptotoxic "oligomer" has been the field's central conjecture for two decades without a structure. By eluting Aβ from its receptor with a prion protein antagonist and purifying it from AD brain, the authors find the receptor-bound pool is ~10× more abundant than free Aβ and consists of 65 nm filaments, not a distinct oligomeric fold — two symmetric S-shaped monomers per rung, broadly resembling plaque filaments from the same brain. The species is nonetheless distinguishable from plaque material by subunit tilt angle, N-terminal conformation, length, and seeding behavior, and its filament tips bind PrP and drive synapse loss in human neurons. This reframes synaptotoxicity as a property of short filament ends rather than a structurally separate oligomer.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human AD brain tissue; receptor-antagonist elution and purification, cryo-EM, human neuron synapse-loss assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75895-9

### Loss of neuronal population organization links pathology to behavior in a model of Alzheimer's disease
**Douglas A. Ruff & Marlene R. Cohen | PNAS**

**Why it matters:** This bridges the scale gap between molecular pathology and cognition using longitudinal recordings in a macaque model of early-stage disease. Single-neuron tuning and basic feature encoding stayed intact while *coordination* within and between visual and parietal populations degraded, and visually guided behavior became more variable despite preserved performance on simple tasks. Notably, functional disruption appeared in regions that provide feedback to visual cortex rather than where pathology was densest, and methylphenidate transiently restored behavioral organization. Caveat: n = 2 animals, so the effect sizes are illustrative rather than population estimates.

**Model/system & primary method:** Macaque model of early-stage AD (two animals); longitudinal multi-electrode population recordings in visual and parietal cortex with paired fluid and histological biomarkers.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2614164123

### APOE-guided lecanemab dosing dissociates ARIA-E from ARIA-H: the K-ROAD study
**Heekyoung Kang & Sang Won Seo | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Why it matters:** ARIA is the principal safety constraint on anti-amyloid immunotherapy, and APOE ε4 homozygotes carry the steepest risk. Under genotype-guided Cmax targets across 508 patients at eight Korean centers, the ε4/ε4-versus-non-carrier ARIA-E odds ratio was 3.25, against 8.49 in Clarity AD and 8.32 in Japanese post-marketing surveillance. ARIA-H, by contrast, was predicted by baseline microbleeds, white matter hyperintensity, age, and ε4 dose — i.e. by pre-existing cerebrovascular burden rather than by dose. The authors are appropriately careful that a non-randomized comparison against historical cohorts is compatible with, but does not establish, attenuation; the dual-axis safety hypothesis needs a randomized test.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multicenter cohort (n = 508, early AD); APOE-genotype-guided lecanemab escalation with MRI-based ARIA surveillance.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71765

### Cryo-EM structures of biopsy-derived TTR fibrils in hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis
**Yuxin Zheng & Yang Shi | Nature Communications**

**Why it matters:** Nineteen fibril structures at 1.9–3.4 Å from gastrocnemius biopsies and vitreous humor of ten *living* ATTRv patients across nine mutations — a marked expansion over the handful of post-mortem-derived amyloid structures available. Deep-learning analysis of cryo-EM density allowed semi-quantitative assignment of mutant versus wild-type TTR within individual fibrils, and the composition tracked with age of disease onset, suggesting which species nucleates deposition shapes clinical timing. The biopsy-based workflow is the durable contribution: it makes living-patient tissue accessible for structural work on heterogeneous hereditary amyloidoses generally.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human patient biopsy tissue (muscle, vitreous); cryo-EM with deep-learning-based density analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75850-8

### Social isolation recruits amygdala–medial prefrontal cortex projections to escalate alcohol drinking in male mice
**Reesha R. Patel & Kay M. Tye | Nature Neuroscience**

**Key findings:** Isolation escalated alcohol drinking in males but suppressed it in females, and BLA→mPFC excitability moved in opposite directions by sex, mirroring the behavioral split. In males, calcium imaging showed BLA-mPFC activity encodes and predicts drinking; optogenetic activation raised intake and photoinhibition reduced it in isolated animals. Combined optogenetics with imaging showed pathway stimulation reproduces the isolation-induced mPFC response pattern to alcohol, tying the circuit manipulation to the naturally occurring state. Mechanistic work was deliberately restricted to males, so the female phenotype remains unexplained.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse; whole-cell recordings, cellular-resolution calcium imaging, and simultaneous optogenetics–imaging.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02413-x

### Motor neurons organize *Drosophila* feeding sequences via a disinhibitory cascade
**Xiu-Wen Sui & Dong-Gen Luo | Nature Neuroscience**

**Key findings:** Motor neurons are conventionally treated as the terminal relay of a premotor command; here they actively pattern the sequence. Quadruple-electrode recordings during feeding show a leading motor neuron releases glutamate that simultaneously drives its muscle and disinhibits a premotor neuron, recruiting the next motor neuron and propagating a millisecond-precision wave down the chain. The pattern held independently of pumping rate, indicating the circuit sustains sequence order across changing action contexts rather than being entrained by a rate signal.

**Model/system & primary method:** *Drosophila* during feeding behavior; simultaneous quadruple-electrode recordings, behavioral analysis, computational modeling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02412-y

### Integrative spatial profiling of 3D genome organization and gene expression in tissue
**Pengfei Guo & Yanxiang Deng | Cell**

**Key findings:** Spatial Hi-C-RNA maps genome-wide chromatin contacts and transcriptomes from the *same* tissue section at near-single-cell resolution, so architecture and expression no longer have to be inferred across serial sections or separate assays. Applied to mouse brain, A/B compartments, TADs, and loops tracked region- and cell-type-specific transcriptional programs, and in mouse embryos the method resolved coordinated chromatin and transcriptional remodeling during neuronal maturation. In human melanoma, chromatin architecture separated intratumoral subregions that RNA alone did not distinguish — a useful demonstration that the chromatin channel carries non-redundant spatial information.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse brain and embryo, human melanoma tissue sections; multimodal spatial Hi-C plus RNA profiling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.07.039

**Henry Kim & Zhenyu Yue | Nature Neuroscience**

**Key findings:** A synthesis of autophagy across neuronal and glial compartments, organized around how the pathway intersects membrane trafficking to maintain CNS quality control from development through maturity. ### Retinal microvascular alterations and glymphatic dysfunction mediate the association between APOE ε4 and cognitive decline in Alzheimer's disease
**William Robert Kwapong & Zhen Wang | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** In 76 AD participants, ε4 carriers showed reduced retinal fractal dimension, increased vessel tortuosity, and lower periventricular diffusivity. Sparser retinal microvasculature tracked with impaired glymphatic markers (DTI-ALPS, PVeD), greater small-vessel-disease burden, abnormal plasma biomarkers, and worse cognition, and mediation analysis placed retinal FD and PVeD partially between ε4 genotype and cognitive performance. The attraction is a non-invasive retinal proxy for a vascular-glymphatic axis; the limitation is that cross-sectional mediation cannot establish the causal ordering it models, and n = 76 is small for a mediation design.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human AD cohort (n = 76); retinal OCT-A imaging, diffusion MRI (DTI-ALPS), plasma biomarkers.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71785

### Factors associated with intracerebral hemorrhage in confirmed cerebral amyloid angiopathy
**Anas Chaachou-Charradi & Mar Hernández-Guillamon | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** Across 205 autopsy-confirmed CAA brains from two Spanish cohorts, independent predictors of lobar ICH were younger age at death, APOE ε2 carriage, and vascular-remodeling-type CAA. Informatively, ε2 associated with hemorrhage but *not* with vascular remodeling, implying two at least partly separable routes to bleeding rather than a single severity axis. CAA severity was most strongly tied to ICH when concomitant AD neuropathologic change was present, and ε2/ε4 carried the highest ICH frequency. Neuropathological confirmation is the strength here — most CAA-ICH risk data rest on imaging-based probable diagnoses.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human autopsy cohort (n = 205); Vonsattel and VCING CAA grading with APOE genotyping.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71786

### *Ank3* loss in adult forebrain excitatory neurons disrupts behavior, neuronal activity, membrane proteome, and myelination
**Sehyoun Yoon & Peter Penzes | PNAS**

**Key findings:** Prenatal and adolescent-onset deletion of *Ank3* in forebrain excitatory neurons converged on the same adult phenotype — hyperactivity, reduced anxiety- and depression-like behavior — indicating the requirement is ongoing rather than purely developmental. Ankyrin-G loss lowered spontaneous and evoked activity, and membrane proteomics showed broad synaptic remodeling including upregulated Taok2 and, unexpectedly, downregulated myelin basic protein. Chronic lithium restored *Mbp* expression alongside its known behavioral rescue, linking a neuron-intrinsic scaffold to an oligodendrocyte-derived structural protein and offering a route by which *ANK3* risk variants could act through myelination.

**Model/system & primary method:** Conditional *Ank3* knockout mice (Emx1-Cre, CaMKIIα-Cre); calcium imaging in culture and slices, quantitative membrane proteomics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2606900123

### Accelerated brain atrophy in epilepsy: a prospective population-based study
**Marian Galovic & John S. Duncan | Annals of Neurology**

**Key findings:** Prior evidence that epilepsy is structurally progressive came largely from tertiary centers, where severely affected patients are preferentially rescanned. Scanning a community-based UK cohort (116 with epilepsy, 89 controls) twice, 3.5 years apart and regardless of seizure status, removes that ascertainment bias — and progression survives it, though modestly: global grey matter loss 2.7 vs 2.4 ml/year. The heterogeneity is the substantive result: acceleration concentrated in focal epilepsy, MRI-identifiable lesions, and ongoing seizures, while generalized epilepsy and seizure-free intervals looked stable. Vulnerable cortical regions thinned at 5.3 vs 3.4 µm/year across most phenotypes.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human prospective population-based cohort; longitudinal structural MRI morphometry.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78319

### When treatment becomes risk: polypharmacy and inappropriate prescribing in cognitive impairment
**Xavier Morató & Sergi Valero | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** Among 2,379 consecutive memory-clinic patients, 67% of those with CDR ≥ 0.5 met polypharmacy criteria and 21% hyperpolypharmacy, with odds rising across the CDR scale and higher education protective. Roughly a fifth of polypharmacy patients had impaired renal function, compounding exposure risk. Prescribing profiles differed between Aβ-positive and Aβ-negative patients and between the clinic population and trial participants — relevant to how far trial safety data generalize. Observational and retrospective, so the direction between drug burden and cognitive stage is not resolved; medication extraction used an LLM over health records, which the authors position as a scalable review tool.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human retrospective observational cohort (n = 2,379); LLM-based medication extraction from electronic health records stratified by AD biomarkers, ApoE, and eGFR.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71708

### Breakdown in the synaptic vesicle cycle defines early and reversible cortical pathogenesis in ALS
**Z. I. Laszlo & C. M. Henstridge | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The authors exploit the late involvement of primary visual cortex (BA17) to read out *early* disease-associated change in end-stage human ALS tissue — a neat workaround for the usual problem that autopsy material captures only terminal pathology. BA17 showed neuropil compaction, presynaptic terminal shrinkage, and synaptic degeneration with local neuronal populations preserved, and synaptoneurosome proteomics identified disrupted presynaptic vesicle cycling that resembled the inducible TDP-43 rNLS8 mouse. Suppressing TDP-43 in vivo restored the proteomic signature, which is the reversibility claim — demonstrated in mouse, not in human tissue, and at the level of protein abundance rather than synaptic function.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem ALS cortex plus inducible TDP-43 rNLS8 mice; quantitative synaptoneurosome proteomics with structural analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.746168

### Synaptic endocytic failure drives dopamine deficiency and protein transfer-mediated striatal dopamine-like neuron compensation
**Z. Li & M. Cao | bioRxiv** *(v4 revision of a preprint first posted May 2025)*

**Key findings:** In dopamine-neuron-specific *Synaptojanin 1* knockouts, terminal dystrophy and striatal dopamine deficiency develop while motor coordination holds up, and the proposed reason is a compensating population of striatal dopamine-like neurons. These cells reportedly do not arise by transcriptional reprogramming; instead they acquire dopaminergic proteins from midbrain dopamine neurons via selective long-range transfer favoring soluble cargo — which, if correct, would also provide a route for α-synuclein and tau to move between neurons. The compensation appeared across PD-linked endocytic mutants, was developmentally constrained, and was not reproduced by acute denervation. Inter-neuronal protein transfer is a strong claim; treat it as provisional pending peer review.

**Model/system & primary method:** Dopamine-neuron-specific *Synj1* knockout mice and other PD-linked endocytic mutants; circuit tracing, protein-transfer assays, motor behavior.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.05.14.653914

### Cell type-resolved chromatin accessibility clocks for brain aging
**P. Yu & W. S. Noble | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Existing chromatin-accessibility clocks run on bulk ATAC-seq, averaging over exactly the cell-type heterogeneity that makes brain aging asynchronous. Building pseudobulk snATAC-seq clocks per cell type from prefrontal cortex of 357 human donors aged 15–100, the authors report clocks that predict chronological age, register rejuvenation under genetic perturbation, and detect age acceleration in age-associated neurodegenerative disease. Relative to methylation clocks the appeal is interpretability — accessible regions sit closer to gene regulatory networks. Whether cell-type resolution yields a real gain over bulk, rather than a better-annotated one, is the claim to scrutinize on peer review.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human prefrontal cortex, 357 donors; single-nucleus ATAC-seq with pseudobulk per-cell-type regression models.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.22.746439

### An epigenetic signature of vulnerable neurons is under selective pressure associated with longevity across placental mammals
**G. Abdelhady & A. R. Pfenning | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Comparative genomics is used here as a natural experiment on brain aging: predicted open-chromatin levels at >3 million orthologous loci across 18 cortical cell types were associated with lifespan across 240 placental mammals via the TACIT method. Lifespan-associated regions stratified sharply by cell type — regions near mitochondrial genes under differential selection in energetically demanding layer V ET neurons, regions near inflammatory genes in glia. The authors then separate intrinsic aging programs from systemic effects in human prefrontal cortex and define an aging signature that predicts cell-type-specific vulnerability, which they report is engaged in AD. Evolutionary association is not mechanism, and open chromatin is predicted rather than measured in most species.

**Model/system & primary method:** Comparative epigenomics across 240 placental mammals plus human prefrontal cortex snATAC data; TACIT machine-learning association and representation learning.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.20.745782

### Chronic trazodone treatment consolidates sleep, improves memory, and reduces amyloid pathology in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease
**M. Arai & B. A. Kent | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Chronic trazodone in *App*<sup>NL-F</sup> knock-in mice increased NREM duration and slow-wave power during rest while promoting wake in the active phase — i.e. consolidated the sleep–wake cycle rather than simply sedating. Treatment from 14 to 16 months was associated with 45% lower hippocampal plaque burden, reduced regional glial activation, and better short- and long-term recognition memory, with effects concentrated in males. The repurposing case is helped by trazodone's existing safety record, but this is a knock-in mouse with amyloid as the readout, and the design does not separate sleep-mediated from direct drug effects on amyloid.

**Model/system & primary method:** *App*<sup>NL-F</sup> knock-in mice; chronic dosing with EEG/EMG sleep recording, amyloid and glial histology, recognition memory tasks.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.20.746036
