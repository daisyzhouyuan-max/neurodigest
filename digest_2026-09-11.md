### TMEM106B is a selective modulator of TDP-43 pathology in Alzheimer's disease
**Madison M Reeves & Mercedes Prudencio | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** In 2,604 autopsy brains with a primary neuropathological diagnosis of AD, the common *TMEM106B* coding variant rs3173615 was enriched specifically in cases carrying TDP-43 co-pathology (raising odds of the AD TDP-43 subtype α) but was not associated with Lewy body or vascular co-pathology. *APOE* ε4, by contrast, was broadly permissive, raising the risk of multiple co-pathologies. The finding argues that individual genetic modifiers steer *which* secondary proteinopathy accumulates in AD rather than acting uniformly, which is relevant to stratifying the clinical and pathological heterogeneity of AD. As a cross-sectional autopsy association study it establishes genetic correlation, not mechanism.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem cohort (n = 2,604 AD brains); genotyping of *TMEM106B* rs3173615 and *APOE* diplotype with binary logistic regression against co-pathology status.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71713

### A brain-to-bone marrow circuit drives neutrophil mobilization and exacerbates ischaemic brain injury
**Shenghan Gao & Yun Xu | Brain**

**Why it matters:** Using transient middle cerebral artery occlusion in mice, the authors trace a defined neural circuit linking a focal stroke to a harmful systemic immune response: ischaemia activates excitatory neurons in the insular cortex that engage CRH neurons in the hypothalamic paraventricular nucleus, driving sympathetic norepinephrine release in the bone marrow and β2-adrenergic-dependent neutrophil mobilization. Silencing the insula→PVN pathway or blocking peripheral sympathetic signalling suppressed neutrophil egress and reduced infarct size. Consistent with the mouse circuit, stroke patients with insular lesions had higher peripheral neutrophil counts, though the human data are correlational. The causal manipulations are mouse-only, but the circuit nominates concrete peripheral targets for limiting post-stroke neuroinflammation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse tMCAO with viral tracing, optogenetic/chemogenetic manipulation, sympathetic blockade, flow cytometry and neutrophil depletion; human stroke sera for clinical correlation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag307

### Lipid-driven membrane remodeling engages a lysosome-dependent adaptive repair program during retinal aging
**Emily Tom & Dorota Skowronska-Krawczyk | Mol Neurodegener**

**Why it matters:** In the retinal pigment epithelium — a CNS tissue whose dysfunction underlies age-related macular degeneration — reduced activity of the PUFA elongase ELOVL2 shifts plasma-membrane lipid composition and compromises membrane integrity. Rather than undergoing apoptosis, RPE cells mount a lysosome-dependent plasma-membrane repair program that preserves barrier function but, through spatially polarized lysosomal exocytosis, promotes extracellular remodeling and sub-RPE deposits characteristic of aging and AMD. Restoring ELOVL2-derived lipid products reversed the membrane abnormalities and suppressed the remodeling phenotype, providing causal evidence in the model that an adaptive repair response can itself become pathogenic. The work defines a tractable ELOVL2–lipid–lysosome axis linking lipid metabolism to age-related neurodegenerative deposits.

**Model/system & primary method:** RPE cell and retinal aging models with lipidomics, membrane biophysical assays, and ELOVL2 loss- and gain-of-function manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00990-w

### Architecture of the serotonergic projectome in the mouse brain
**Jun Ho Song & Liqun Luo | Cell**

**Why it matters:** Systematic whole-brain axon tracing of dorsal and median raphe serotonin neurons shows the serotonergic projectome is organized by functional relatedness of targets rather than their physical proximity. Neurons partition into five projectomic groups, each preferentially innervating the hippocampus, basal ganglia, cortex, medial interbrain, or brainstem/lateral thalamus, and each group maps onto a distinct combination of transcriptomic clusters, with transcriptome and soma position jointly predicting projection identity. Projection-specific serotonin depletion produced dissociable behavioral phenotypes, tying anatomical logic to function. The result reframes a diffusely projecting neuromodulatory system as a set of molecularly defined, target-specific channels — a reference map for interrogating serotonergic contributions to psychiatric and neurological disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse; whole-brain single-axon tracing integrated with spatial transcriptomics and projection-specific serotonin depletion.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.022

### Cell-type-specific disruption of nucleus accumbens social representations in a mouse model of autism
**Pingping Zhao & Peyman Golshani | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** Longitudinal miniscope calcium imaging shows nucleus accumbens (NAc) core populations selectively encode social interaction, and that D1- and D2-medium spiny neurons contribute in opposite directions — D1-MSNs promote sociability while D2-MSNs suppress it. In *Cntnap2*⁻/⁻ mice, an autism model, both acute and cross-day social representations degrade, with selective depletion of socially excited D1-MSNs and socially inhibited D2-MSNs. Optogenetic D2-MSN inhibition improved social behavior in mutants, whereas D1-MSN activation did not, providing cell-type-resolved causal evidence linking a specific accumbens deficit to social dysfunction. The findings are mouse-only and specific to the *Cntnap2* model, but they point to D2-MSN modulation as a candidate intervention node.

**Model/system & primary method:** *Cntnap2*⁻/⁻ and control mice; longitudinal single-cell calcium imaging with cell-type-specific optogenetic manipulation and population decoding.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.007

### Feature interference underlies a neuronal basis for the behavioral cost of task uncertainty
**Cheng Xue & Marlene R Cohen | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Combining monkey electrophysiology, human psychophysics and artificial neural network modeling, the authors show that both humans and monkeys — unlike a network trained purely for accuracy — make less accurate perceptual decisions when the task is uncertain. Comparing a network trained for correct choices with one trained to reproduce participants' choices implicates feature interference: under uncertainty, irrelevant stimulus features are represented more strongly and neuronal representations of different features become entangled, and causal manipulations reproduced the resulting errors. The work offers a concrete representational-geometry account in which cognitive capacity limits arise from interference between neural representations rather than from noise alone.

**Model/system & primary method:** Non-human primate electrophysiology plus human psychophysics and artificial neural network modeling, with behavioral and causal perturbations.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02430-w

### Representational learning by optimization of neural manifolds in an olfactory memory network
**Bo Hu & Rainer W Friedrich | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Recording population activity in zebrafish telencephalic area pDp (the homolog of piriform cortex) during odor-discrimination learning, the authors found no clear attractor dynamics; instead, training selectively increased the separation of neural manifolds representing task-relevant odors, consistent with autoassociative networks under precise excitation–inhibition balance. Using manifold-capacity theory, they showed that geometric modifications of the representations supported classification of task-relevant information, and that manifold capacity predicted discrimination performance across individuals. The study links experience-dependent changes in representational geometry directly to behavior and supports a model in which recurrent olfactory circuits store information in manifold geometry rather than in discrete attractor states.

**Model/system & primary method:** Juvenile and adult zebrafish; population activity imaging in area pDp during an odor-discrimination task, analyzed with neural-manifold/manifold-capacity frameworks.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02429-3

### Voltage imaging: A practical guide
**Adam E Cohen & Daozheng Gong | Neuron**

**Key findings:** This primer distills the practical choices in setting up a voltage-imaging experiment with genetically encoded voltage indicators — indicator selection, excitation wavelengths and microscopy configuration, cell-type-specific expression, and signal extraction. It lays out the physical determinants of signal-to-noise ratio, the trade-offs between one- and two-photon excitation, and the use of structured illumination to improve contrast in tissue, spanning scales from single ion channels to population dynamics in behaving animals. A methods reference rather than new data, it is a useful orientation for labs adopting an increasingly accessible technique for resolving subthreshold dynamics and spikes optically.

**Model/system & primary method:** Methods review/primer on genetically encoded voltage indicators and associated optical instrumentation and analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.002

## NEURODEGENERATION & AGING — TOP 5

### Retromer-targeted therapy for neurodegenerative diseases
**Michele Persico & David K Simon | Mol Neurodegener**

**Key findings:** This review positions the retromer complex — a conserved regulator of endosomal-lysosomal trafficking — at the crossroads of proteostasis, mitochondrial homeostasis, neurotransmission and neuroinflammation shared across AD, PD and ALS. It synthesizes in vitro and in vivo evidence that enhancing or restoring retromer function (via VPS35 and pharmacological retromer stabilizers) augments macroautophagy, chaperone-mediated autophagy and endosomal-lysosomal degradation, lowering misfolded-protein burden and, in some models, rescuing mitochondrial function and dampening neuroinflammation, with disease-modifying effects reported in PD and ALS models. The authors weigh the challenges of targeting the retromer, including potential off-target effects of enhancement. A conceptual and translational overview rather than new experimental data.

**Model/system & primary method:** Narrative review spanning cellular and animal models of AD, PD and ALS and retromer-enhancing pharmacology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00971-z

### Th17 effector cytokines induce shared and distinct microglial and endothelial cell responses in a mouse model for post-streptococcal encephalitis
**Charlotte R Wayne & Dritan Agalliu | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** In a mouse model of Group A Streptococcus-triggered neuropsychiatric disease, single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics revealed inflammatory transcriptional programs in microglia and brain endothelial cells, with loss of blood-brain barrier gene expression in female mice and GAS-responsive microglia localizing near infiltrating CD4⁺ T cells. Dissecting the Th17 axis, deletion of GM-CSF in CD4⁺ T cells partially reduced microglial chemokine expression without restoring the barrier, whereas IL-17A neutralization partially rescued BBB transcriptional changes, and microglia-specific deletion of IL-17 receptor A partially restored BBB integrity. Several microglial chemokines elevated in mice were also raised in patient sera. The data implicate microglial IL-17A–IL17RA signalling as a mediator of BBB dysfunction and neuroinflammation after peripheral streptococcal infection.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse genetic models with single-cell RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics, cell-type-specific gene deletion and cytokine neutralization; patient sera for correlation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76232-w

### Metabolic Markers of MRI-Confirmed Lacunar Stroke: Observational and Mendelian Randomization Analyses
**Wen Hui Ng & Eric L Harshfield | Neurology**

**Key findings:** Across 2,408 participants (1,456 MRI-confirmed lacunar stroke cases, 952 controls), NMR profiling of 250 serum metabolites found 211 associated with lacunar stroke observationally. Bidirectional Mendelian randomization supported causal roles for two: higher glycine was inversely associated with isolated lacunar infarcts (OR 0.70) and a higher proportion of cholesterol in medium LDL was inversely associated with multiple infarcts/leukoaraiosis (OR 0.54). Five HDL- and lactate-related metabolites tracked executive function and processing speed observationally, and genetically determined ω-3/total fatty acid levels were linked to diffusion-tensor markers of small-vessel disease. The MR design strengthens causal inference for glycine and medium-LDL cholesterol, nominating metabolomic candidates for small-vessel disease pathogenesis and biomarker development.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human case-control cohort with serum NMR metabolomics, GWAS per metabolite, and bidirectional Mendelian randomization.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218409

### Dynamic frailty exposure and cognitive aging trajectories: A multicohort study
**Xingjie Wang & Jiling Liang | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In 19,586 older adults pooled from three nationally representative cohorts (China, England, United States), baseline pre-frailty and frailty were associated with faster annual global cognitive decline versus robust status (pooled β ≈ −0.023 SD/year), as was worsening from robust to a frailer state (β = −0.025 SD/year) and higher mean frailty burden. Trajectory modeling likewise linked baseline frailty, rising frailty burden and frailty worsening to less-favorable cognitive-aging groups, while frailty improvement trended toward more-favorable trajectories. The associations are observational and cannot establish causation, but the consistency across rate-based and trajectory analyses in three cohorts supports repeated frailty assessment as an informative marker of cognitive-aging risk.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multicohort longitudinal study (n = 19,586) with linear mixed-effects models, random-effects meta-analysis and group-based trajectory modeling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71821

### Inflammatory neuropathy in mouse and primate models of colorectal cancer
**Caitlyn M Gaffney & Andrew J Shepherd | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Mice bearing colorectal tumors developed peripheral neuropathy with subtle locomotor deficits but no overt hypersensitivity, accompanied by widespread changes in pro-inflammatory cytokines and lipid metabolites in peripheral nerves. Nerves showed macrophage accumulation, myelin decompaction and ryanodine-receptor oxidation linked to disrupted calcium homeostasis and reduced sensory-neuron spike amplitude. Parallel plasma inflammatory and lipid changes, with peripheral-nerve macrophage accumulation, were seen in rhesus macaques with colorectal cancer, indicating a conserved, tumor-driven subacute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy across species. The cross-species concordance implicates tumor-induced neuropathy — independent of chemotherapy — as an under-recognized contributor to neurological dysfunction in cancer.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse and rhesus macaque colorectal cancer models with peripheral-nerve histology, electrophysiology, cytokine and lipidomic profiling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76683-1
