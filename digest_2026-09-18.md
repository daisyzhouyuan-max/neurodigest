### Levetiracetam therapeutically targets GABAergic synapses in diffuse midline glioma
**Tara Barron & Michelle Monje | Nat Med**

**Why it matters:** Building on the discovery that GABAergic neuron-to-glioma synapses drive diffuse midline glioma (DMG) growth, this work shows the common antiseizure drug levetiracetam attenuates low-frequency GABAergic synaptic currents specifically in DMG cells — not healthy neurons — through a mechanism independent of its canonical SV2A target. In patient-derived orthotopic xenografts, levetiracetam reduced proliferation and tumour burden and extended survival in DMG but not hemispheric high-grade glioma, matching retrospective real-world data showing longer overall survival in children with DMG taking the drug. The subtype specificity makes this an immediately testable repurposing candidate.

**Model/system & primary method:** Retrospective clinical survival analysis; patient-derived orthotopic xenografts; electrophysiology of glioma synaptic currents.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04646-6

### Genomic and transcriptomic quality control for an autologous iPSC-derived cell therapy for Parkinson's disease
**Andres M Bratt-Leal & Xiaokui Zhang | Cell Stem Cell**

**Why it matters:** Autologous iPSC-derived dopaminergic grafts could avoid the immunosuppression that allogeneic Parkinson's cell therapies require, but per-patient manufacturing demands per-patient quality control. The authors establish a reproducible pipeline pairing whole-genome sequencing across the fibroblast→iPSC→dopaminergic-precursor lineage with NeuriTest, an RNA-seq classifier trained on animal engraftment data to predict graft quality before implantation, and report efficacy in a PD rodent model and safety in a 9-month GLP toxicology study. This is a manufacturing and qualification framework rather than a new biological discovery, but it addresses a genuine bottleneck for autologous cell therapy.

**Model/system & primary method:** Multi-donor patient iPSC-derived dopaminergic precursors; whole-genome sequencing and RNA-seq QC; rodent efficacy and GLP toxicology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2026.08.014

### Transcriptome-based classification in mice with ASD-risk mutations
**Junyeop Daniel Roh & Eunjoon Kim | Science**

**Why it matters:** Across 1008 sex-balanced prefrontal RNA-seq profiles from 17 mouse lines carrying different ASD-risk mutations, the heterogeneous genetic causes collapse onto two opposing transcriptomic states that differ in sex bias, regional specificity, developmental stability, cell-type remodeling, and response to fluoxetine and lithium. Single-nucleus profiling shows broader cell-type remodeling in one group, and cell-type-specific modules mirror the bulk signatures. The framework classifies independent lines and identifies subgroups with conserved synaptic directionality, offering a molecular stratification that could organize an otherwise fragmented genetic landscape — though it remains a mouse-cortex resource needing human validation.

**Model/system & primary method:** 17 ASD-risk mouse lines; bulk and single-nucleus prefrontal RNA-seq with transcriptomic classification.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adz6688

### Brainstem sensing of multiple body signals during food consumption
**Rachel A Essner & Mark L Andermann | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** The lateral parabrachial nucleus (LPBN) is a central interoceptive hub, but how it organizes diverse bodily signals has been hard to resolve. Using dense calcium imaging of hundreds of LPBN neurons in mice, the authors captured a seconds-long activity wave during eating whose dynamics tracked food transiting the upper gastrointestinal tract (confirmed by X-ray videofluoroscopy). Imaging the same neurons across days revealed spatially clustered subsets encoding oral signals, stomach filling, visceral malaise, arousal, and body movement, with only certain subsets modulated by cortical input. The work establishes a platform for dissecting how peripheral and central interoceptive signals are integrated to guide behavior.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse LPBN; longitudinal calcium imaging with X-ray videofluoroscopy.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.015

### Post-inhibitory rebound firing promotes hypothalamic activation during male mating in mice
**Wen Zhang & Xiaohong Xu | Neuron**

**Key findings:** Mount initiation requires activation of the medial preoptic area (mPOA) despite dominant inhibitory input from the bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (BNST). In vivo recordings show mount-active mPOA neurons are suppressed before behavioral onset, and the magnitude of that pre-behavioral inhibition predicts subsequent activation — a signature of post-inhibitory rebound (PIR). A conductance-based model implicates T-type Ca²⁺ currents; bidirectional manipulation of T-type channel expression scaled PIR, mPOA activity, and mating (knockdown reduced all three, overexpression enhanced them), establishing PIR as a mechanism that converts inhibition into behaviorally relevant hypothalamic activation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse mPOA/BNST; in vivo recording, conductance-based modeling, T-type channel manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.019

### Retinal microvascular changes are related to indicators of glymphatic dysfunction and small vessel disease in frontotemporal dementia
**William Robert Kwapong & Min Chu | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In 34 patients with clinically diagnosed FTD (predominantly behavioral variant) versus 34 controls, sparser retinal microvasculature (lower fractal dimension on fundus photography) tracked with lower DTI-ALPS (a diffusion proxy for glymphatic function), greater small-vessel-disease burden, poorer cognition, and greater disease severity. The correlations position the retina as an accessible readout of cerebral microvascular and perivascular-clearance changes in FTD, though the cross-sectional design and small sample preclude causal or temporal inference.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human FTD cohort (n=34 + 34 controls); color fundus photography and multimodal MRI (DTI-ALPS).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71844

### Operationalizing Alzheimer's disease trials in the era of targeted therapies
**Jagan A Pillai & David A Wolk | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** This review argues that the clinical adoption of anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies should reshape how AD observational cohorts and trials are designed — capturing antibody type, dose, duration, degree of amyloid clearance, and downstream effects on neurodegeneration; comparing treated and untreated populations; and stratifying beyond traditional biomarkers to identify treatment-resistant subgroups. It emphasizes stage-specific combination-therapy trials, long-term follow-up for durability and post-treatment effects, and digital biomarkers with responder-enrichment. A design/opinion framework rather than new data.

**Model/system & primary method:** Narrative review of AD trial and cohort design.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71829

### A complex of MAST1 and 14-3-3η regulates Tau phosphorylation in the developing cortex
**Sumire Antonioli & Thomas A Leonard | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** MAST-family kinases are implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders but poorly understood mechanistically. The authors show the neuronal 14-3-3η paralog binds MAST1 at two regulatory serines (S90, S161), with PAK phosphorylation controlling that interaction, and identify microtubule-associated protein Tau as a candidate MAST1 substrate via whole-brain phosphoproteomics in a Mega-Corpus-Callosum syndrome mouse model. Pathogenic MAST1 mutations impair function through misfolding or reduced kinase activity, linking a PAK–MAST1–14-3-3 axis to Tau phosphorylation and microtubule remodeling during cortical neuron differentiation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse MCC models and biochemistry; phosphoproteomics and kinase–substrate mapping.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2617534123

### Blood-brain barrier-penetrant nanotherapy inhibits haem oxygenase-1-mediated ferroptosis for post-resuscitation neuroprotection
**Mao Zhang & Jiefeng Xu | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** In mouse cardiac-arrest/CPR models, the authors identify HMOX1-mediated ferroptosis as a driver of post-resuscitation neuronal injury and deliver the neuroprotectant artesunate using zwitterionic OPDEA micelles that cross the intact blood-brain barrier via adsorptive-mediated transcytosis, accumulating in hippocampal neurons and microglia. The formulation suppressed HMOX1 activity, restored iron homeostasis, reduced lipid peroxidation, and preserved mitochondrial function and neuronal viability. Evidence is preclinical and mouse-only, but it nominates a tractable target (the HMOX1–ferroptosis axis) and a BBB-penetrant delivery platform.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse cardiac-arrest/CPR and in vitro hypoxia-reoxygenation; OPDEA–artesunate micelle nanotherapy.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76830-8

### Divergent associations of estimated cardiorespiratory fitness with cognitive trajectories in Chinese and U.S. middle-aged and older adults
**Jiangping Ma & Huifang Shang | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Comparing latent cognitive trajectories in CHARLS (China, n=3458) and HRS (US, n=6905), higher estimated cardiorespiratory fitness (a sex-specific non-exercise MET algorithm) was associated with lower odds of a moderate-decline trajectory in the US (OR 0.94 per 1-MET increase) but, unexpectedly, higher odds of the low and moderate trajectories in China (OR 1.28 and 1.10). The paradoxical cross-national divergence — robust across sensitivity analyses — cautions against generalizing fitness–cognition relationships across populations, though estimated (rather than directly measured) fitness and the observational design limit interpretation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Two harmonized human cohorts; latent class growth modeling and multinomial regression.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71871

### Laromestrocel preserves brain volume and cognitive function in Alzheimer's disease by inhibiting neuroinflammation
**B. G. Rash & J. M. Hare | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 49 subjects with mild AD, the mesenchymal stem cell therapy laromestrocel reduced progressive neuroinflammation most strongly in core AD regions (left hippocampus, temporal and parietal cortex, medulla) at 39 weeks versus placebo. Reduced neuroinflammation correlated with less brain atrophy (hippocampus R=-0.33) and better cognition (hippocampal inflammation × MoCA R=-0.40), and plasma neurogranin fell, consistent with reduced synaptic loss. Interpret cautiously: small per-arm samples (N≈9–11), many region- and dose-specific comparisons, and correlational links between inflammation, atrophy, and cognition.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human mild-AD trial (n=49); MRI neuroinflammation and volumetrics, plasma biomarkers.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.16.26363132

### Molecular subgroups of ALS patients with distinct survival outcomes identified through plasma proteomics
**I. S. Aksoylu & A. Manberg | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Profiling ~5400 plasma proteins (Olink Explore HT) in 235 ALS patients, consensus clustering resolved three subgroups with significantly different survival. Network and gene-ontology analysis linked the shortest-surviving group to cellular stress response and Rho-GTPase activity, with the circulating proteome drawing on nervous-system, vascular, immune, and muscle cells. The work supports plasma proteomics for prognostic stratification in ALS, though it is single-cohort and requires external replication before any clinical use.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human ALS plasma (n=235); Olink proteomics, consensus clustering, WGCNA.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.16.26362998

### Integration of cell-specific gene expression and chromatin accessibility facilitates localization of neurodegenerative risk in microglia
**X. Reed & M. R. Cookson | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Differentiating 135 iPSC donor lines into neurons and microglia with single-cell RNA and ATAC profiling, the authors mapped expression/accessibility QTLs and colocalized them with GWAS loci for Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and Lewy body dementia. Many risk loci resolved specifically to microglia, and a perturb-seq test at the BIN1 locus confirmed one nominated enhancer controls microglial BIN1 (and neighbouring genes). The results argue that common variants acting on microglial gene expression contribute directly to neurodegeneration risk rather than merely reflecting a secondary response.

**Model/system & primary method:** iPSC-derived neurons/microglia (135 donors); single-cell multiome QTL mapping, GWAS colocalization, perturb-seq.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.15.751821

### Phosphoproteome modifications and cortical circuit dysfunction are linked to the early-stage progression of alpha-synuclein aggregation
**S. Dutta & C. Rochet | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** (v3 revision of a preprint first posted January 2025.) Intrastriatal α-synuclein preformed-fibril (PFF) seeding produced phosphoproteomic — but not global proteomic or lipidomic — changes in rodent sensorimotor cortex by 3 months, enriched for synaptic-transmission and cytoskeletal pathways and implicating CK2, PKA, and ERK signaling. PFF-treated mice showed increased cortical firing and spike–spike coherence, and pERK1/2-positive granulovacuolar-body-like structures appeared specifically in aggregate-bearing neurons, tying early synuclein pathology to cortical circuit dysfunction relevant to the non-motor symptoms of Parkinson's disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** Rat/mouse α-synuclein PFF model; phosphoproteomics and in vivo electrophysiology.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.01.24.634820

### Decline in mitochondrial health and lysosomal acidification in human neurons during normal aging
**E. M. Legault & J. Drouin-Ouellet | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** (v2, first posted April 2026.) Using direct reprogramming of fibroblasts from donors aged 11–91 into induced neurons — which retains cellular age signatures — the authors found neurons from older donors show mitochondrial impairment in neurites and, after mitophagy induction, accumulate damaged mitochondria in autophagosomes and LAMP1-positive lysosomes with incomplete clearance despite preserved lysosomal enzyme activity. This provides rare direct evidence of age-related mitophagy impairment in human neurons, relevant to why aging predisposes to Parkinson's and Alzheimer's; findings are imaging-based and correlated with donor age.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human induced neurons (donors 11–91 y); confocal mitochondrial/mitophagy imaging.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.09.717473
