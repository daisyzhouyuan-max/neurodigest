1A second-generation "hypoxia in a pill" rescues neurodegenerative phenotypes across distinct mouse models

Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A  Hong Wang & Vamsi K Mootha
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Chronic continuous hypoxia is therapeutic in several preclinical disease models but impractical and unsafe to deliver as a gas. The authors optimise an oral surrogate pairing a hemoglobin-oxygen affinity enhancer (GBT601) with a HIF-2α inhibitor (PT2399) to blunt compensatory erythropoiesis. Begun after symptom onset, GBT601 monotherapy extended median lifespan of Ndufs4-knockout (Leigh syndrome) mice from 62 to 105 days, and the GBT601/PT2399 combination to 158 days; the combination halted motor progression in a Friedreich's ataxia model and reversed motor deficits, brain hyperoxia and lipid peroxidation in an α-synuclein Parkinson's model. This moves the hypoxia-mimetic concept toward a deliverable small-molecule regimen spanning mitochondrial and proteinopathy-driven disease.
Model & method
Three mouse models (Ndufs4 KO, Friedreich's ataxia, α-synuclein PD); oral small-molecule dosing with lifespan, motor and brain-oxygenation readouts. Causal evidence is mouse-only.
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2618654123

2Dystrophic changes of nigrostriatal axons harboring a SYNJ1 Parkinson's mutation suggest catastrophic failure of endocytic mechanisms

Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A  Yumei Wu & Pietro De Camilli
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
The SYNJ1 R258Q mutation, which selectively impairs the Sac1 phosphoinositide-phosphatase domain, causes early-onset familial Parkinsonism. Knock-in mice show brain-wide synaptic vesicle trafficking defects but strikingly selective dystrophy of dopaminergic axons in the dorsolateral striatum. Correlative light microscopy–FIB-SEM resolved these lesions in 3D as clusters of focal axonal dilations containing massive onion-like, DAT-enriched plasma-membrane infoldings, accompanied by a regional dopamine-release deficit. The restriction to a subset of axons argues for a vulnerability threshold at which expanded plasma membrane outstrips compensatory endocytosis — a concrete structural link between an endocytic lesion and selective nigrostriatal degeneration.
Model & method
Synj1 R258Q knock-in mice; correlative light microscopy and FIB-SEM volume EM of striatal axons with dopamine-release measurement.
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2622215123

3Centrum Semiovale Perivascular Spaces Predict First Hemorrhagic Lesion in Asymptomatic Amyloid-Positive Individuals

Ann Neurol  Baris Alten & Brett L Cucchiara
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Identifying cerebral amyloid angiopathy before its first hemorrhage is a major unmet need. In a secondary analysis of 932 cognitively unimpaired, amyloid-positive, hemorrhage-free participants in the A4 trial, severe enlarged perivascular spaces in the centrum semiovale (EPVS-CSO) at baseline predicted incident lobar microbleeds or cortical superficial siderosis over a median 5.1 years (33.7% vs 13.6%; adjusted HR 2.85, 95% CI 1.91–4.25). Severe basal ganglia perivascular spaces — a topographic negative control tied to hypertensive small-vessel disease — carried no such risk, strengthening specificity for the CAA pathway. The finding supports a defined prehemorrhagic CAA stage and integration of amyloid biomarkers into MRI-based Boston criteria.
Model & method
Human secondary analysis of a phase-3 trial cohort; baseline MRI EPVS rating with longitudinal hemorrhagic-lesion and cognitive outcomes. Observational within a trial population.
https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78388

4Global and regional amyloid-β in explaining APOE-ε4 and sex associations with medial temporal tau: a neuropathology study

Acta Neuropathol  Gemma Salvadó & Oskar Hansson
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
APOE-ε4 and female sex both raise medial temporal lobe (MTL) tau, but whether they act through amyloid-dependent routes was unresolved. Across 2550 autopsy-characterised participants in two cohorts (ROS/MAP, AZSAND) spanning the AD continuum, adjusting jointly for global and regional MTL amyloid-β abolished the APOE-ε4–MTL tau association (p ≥ 0.19 in both cohorts), whereas the female-sex association remained robust (p ≤ 0.004). Models including regional plus global amyloid explained substantially more MTL tau variance than global amyloid alone. The data dissociate two risk factors: APOE-ε4 appears to drive MTL tau largely via regional amyloid, while female vulnerability implicates amyloid-independent mechanisms — with direct implications for who benefits from amyloid-targeting therapy.
Model & method
Human neuropathology in two cohorts; mediation analysis of neuropathological amyloid-β on MTL tau burden. Cross-sectional autopsy associations.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03095-2

5Lateral sclerosis in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis is an upper motor neuron distal axonopathy ("dying back"): a clinical-neuropathological study

Acta Neuropathol  Jianguo Liu & Fei Song
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Despite being ALS's defining upper motor neuron (UMN) lesion, the "sclerosis" of the lateral corticospinal tract (CST) has rarely been correlated with clinical phenotype since Charcot. Comparing spinal cords from predominantly-UMN versus predominantly-LMN ALS, the authors found pronounced myelin and axonal loss across all fibre calibres in the lateral CST of UMN-predominant cases, more severe lumbar than cervical — a length-dependent gradient supporting a dying-back process. Severely affected regions harboured a distinct foamy inflammatory microglial population, and axonal TDP-43 was increased in the CSTs. The work grounds the UMN phenotype in quantitative pathology and implicates glio-axonal interactions in UMN degeneration, though the microglia–degeneration link remains correlative.
Model & method
Human post-mortem spinal cord; quantitative histopathology of myelin, axons, microglia and TDP-43 across spinal levels with clinical phenotyping.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03072-9

Stepwise engagement of cingulate ensembles underlies motivational decline in chronic pain

Neuron  Wenying Xu & Wei-Guang Li
Key findings
Using whole-brain activity-dependent tagging, projection-specific perturbation, in vivo Ca²⁺ imaging and single-unit recording in male mice with neuropathic injury, the authors resolve a temporally staged anterior cingulate (ACC) circuit. ACC→basolateral amygdala neurons are engaged early, respond to allodynic stimulation and drive mechanical hypersensitivity, whereas ACC→lateral habenula neurons engage later and support effortful persistence, with persistence-related coding preferentially disrupted after injury. The scheme separates the sensory and motivational consequences of chronic pain into distinct, sequentially recruited projection classes.
Model & method
Male mouse neuropathic pain; projection-specific tagging/perturbation with calcium imaging and electrophysiology. Causal evidence is mouse-only.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.018

Tau phosphorylation from neuronal adaptation to Alzheimer's disease

Neuron  Ryan Trouvé & Marieke M B Hoekstra
Key findings
This review argues that pathological tau phosphorylation reflects dysregulation of a shared, normally adaptive response rather than a distinct disease pathway. Synthesising data from human, rodent and fly brain and neuronal models across neurodevelopment, sleep, hibernation and cellular stress, the authors note that physiological phospho-tau changes — affecting synaptic transmission, mitochondrial activity, RNA metabolism and genome integrity — are transient and reversible, whereas aging, repeated stress and amyloid-β-associated stress promote persistent accumulation, impaired clearance and aggregation. The proposed determinants of pathology are phosphorylation persistence, subcellular localisation, proteoform composition and the capacity to restore tau homeostasis.
Model & method
Review spanning human-to-fly brains and neuronal cell models.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.032

Orbitofrontal value dynamics and striatal population geometry during interruption of automatic behavior

Neuron  Thomas W Elston & Joni D Wallis
Key findings
Monkeys performed a task pitting competing value representations to evoke an automatic-then-controlled response, giving a window into how initial responses are interrupted. Simultaneous OFC–caudate Neuropixels recordings showed OFC population dynamics vacillating between competing value states during corrective responses; this appeared to be transmitted to the caudate via enhanced local field potential synchronisation that coincided with disruption of structured caudate population activity — crucially, without changes in striatal firing rate. The result suggests automatic responses can be interrupted through top-down disruption of striatal population geometry rather than firing-rate suppression, relevant to disorders of cognitive control.
Model & method
Non-human primate; simultaneous OFC–caudate Neuropixels recordings during a value-conflict task.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.016

ApoE lipidation state directs immunometabolic reprogramming of human microglia

Alzheimers Dement  Tsion G Shiferaw & Kimberley D Bruce
Key findings
Treating human microglia with lipid-free or lipid-bound ApoE3/ApoE4 revealed isoform- and lipidation-specific effects. ApoE4 produced fewer but enlarged lipid droplets and increased mitochondrial fragmentation versus ApoE3, effects enhanced by lipid-bound ApoE4; proteomics showed a strong type I interferon response to lipid-free ApoE, exacerbated by lipid-free ApoE4. The findings dissociate two ApoE4 liabilities — lipid-bound ApoE4 driving metabolic reprogramming, lipid-free ApoE4 driving inflammatory signalling — nominating ApoE lipidation as a modifier of ApoE4-associated AD risk.
Model & method
Cultured human microglia; live-cell holotomography and global proteomics. In vitro.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71882

Multiple motor proteins regulate TDP-43 anterograde axonal transport

Acta Neuropathol  Monica Feole & Gorazd B Stokin
Key findings
In axons of H9-derived human neurons, TDP-43 undergoes active anterograde transport and interacts with multiple kinesins — all three KIF5 isoforms via the adaptor KLC1, plus the synaptic-vesicle motor KIF1A. This multi-motor engagement implies a flexible transport system for delivering TDP-43-associated mRNA to distal axons. The authors propose that in ALS, where TDP-43 mislocalises to the cytoplasm, simultaneous disruption of several transport mechanisms could compromise this flexibility, contributing to accumulation of non-functional granules and impaired local translation — framing axonal TDP-43 transport as a therapeutic target.
Model & method
Human iPSC-derived (H9) neurons; live-axon transport imaging and protein–protein interaction assays. Mechanistic/correlative with respect to disease.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03071-w

The genetic landscape of myotonic dystrophy type 1: insights from nearly one million genomes

Brain  Rezbieara P Rahman & Emma Matthews
Key findings
Using ExpansionHunter on whole-genome data from 985,026 participants (Genomics England, UK Biobank, All of Us), pathogenic DMPK CTG expansions (≥50 repeats) were found in 645 individuals (~1 in 1,527), two-to-fivefold above traditional estimates, across all ancestries. In UK Biobank, carriers had increased odds of any cardiac diagnosis (OR 2.06) and especially conduction abnormalities (OR 4.86); ~1 in 10 had cataract before age 55. Most carriers had no prior DM1 diagnosis, revealing a large reservoir of undiagnosed multisystem disease and supporting routine DMPK analysis within cardiac and ophthalmic diagnostic pathways.
Model & method
Human population genomics across three biobank cohorts with linked health records. Observational.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag350

Histone neutralization protects the ischemic brain against the consequences of stroke-associated pneumonia

Acta Neuropathol  Dongpei Yin & Dirk M Hermann
Key findings
Stroke-associated pneumonia predicted poor outcome and long-term neutrophil deregulation in patients. In mice, S. pneumoniae pneumonia induced three days after transient middle cerebral artery occlusion worsened recovery and increased blood-brain barrier breakdown, neutrophil infiltration, microvascular thrombosis and brain atrophy, with a peripheral degranulation/NETosis signature. An antibody neutralising extracellular histones reduced injury and prevented long-term atrophy, whereas DNase-I NET degradation, NETosis blockade (LDC7559) and myeloperoxidase inhibition (AZD4831) did not — identifying extracellular histones, rather than NETs per se, as key drivers and a candidate therapeutic target in post-stroke pneumonia.
Model & method
Human association plus mouse MCAO with experimental pneumonia; proteomics and targeted interventions. Causal evidence mouse-only.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03097-0

A neuronal GPCR modifies neuropeptide signaling to suppress proteostasis in distal tissues

Sci Adv  Joud Hirbawi & Ehud Cohen
Key findings
Building on prior work that knocking down the neuronal GPCR gtr-1 mitigates Aβ toxicity in C. elegans, the authors show gtr-1 knockdown differentially remodels gene expression — including neuropeptide-coding genes — and dissimilarly modifies aggregation of distinct proteotoxic proteins across tissues, while also enhancing protein degradation. The work positions neuropeptides as organismal coordinators of proteostasis under different proteotoxic challenges, relevant to the intertissue, neuron-governed regulation of aggregation implicated in Alzheimer's and Huntington's disease.
Model & method
C. elegans expressing proteotoxic transgenes; neuronal GPCR knockdown with transcriptomic and aggregation assays. Invertebrate model.
https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.ady6048

Mitochondrial NAD+ import gates neuronal resilience to active SARM1

bioRxiv  Barbar, L. & DiAntonio, A.
Key findings
SARM1 is the inducible NADase that executes pathological axon degeneration, and cytosolic NAD⁺ loss has been assumed the lethal lesion. Here SARM1 activation rapidly depleted both cytosolic and mitochondrial NAD⁺ and collapsed ATP in both compartments. Enhancing mitochondrial NAD⁺ import via the transporter SLC25A51 preserved mitochondrial NAD⁺ and function and delayed axon degeneration and neuronal death despite continued SARM1 activity; unexpectedly it also restored cytosolic NAD⁺-dependent metabolism and reversed a glycolytic block, and glycolytic inhibition attenuated protection. The data implicate coupled compartmental metabolic failure — not cytosolic NAD⁺ loss alone — in SARM1-driven degeneration. Interpret cautiously pending peer review.
Model & method
Neuronal cultures with SARM1 activation; compartment-resolved NAD⁺/ATP measurement and SLC25A51 manipulation.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.05.756890

ASC specks and α-synuclein amplify microglial NLRP3 inflammasome activation and co-localize with Lewy body pathology

bioRxiv  Thakur, K. & Sandhu, J. K.
Key findings
Extending work linking extracellular ASC specks to amyloid-β, this study reports that ASC specks and α-synuclein together amplify microglial NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and that ASC specks co-localise with Lewy body pathology. The results support a self-amplifying loop in which the inflammasome adaptor, released as a damage-associated molecular pattern, cooperates with aggregated α-synuclein to sustain microglial inflammation in Parkinson's disease and related synucleinopathies. Findings are cell-level and associative pending peer review.

Microglial inflammasome assays with α-synuclein and ASC specks; co-localisation with human Lewy body pathology.

DAP12-deficient microglia adopt a TREM2-independent antigen-presenting state with a glial-to-neuronal cascade in Nasu-Hakola disease

bioRxiv  Leon, J. & Morimoto, S.
Key findings
Nasu-Hakola disease, caused by loss of DAP12 (TYROBP) or its receptor TREM2, offers a window into what DAP12-deficient microglia become. Single-nucleus RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics of an NHD donor's prefrontal cortex and hippocampus showed microglia adopting a CIITA-driven, MHC class II-high antigen-presenting state with complement, iron and glycolytic programs — but not the TREM2-dependent lipid program. Astrocytes became pan-reactive and lost metallothionein, oligodendrocytes lost mature cells and cholesterol synthesis, and excitatory neurons lost presynaptic/activity programs, outlining a glia-to-neuron cascade. Single-donor data warrant caution pending peer review.
Model & method
Human post-mortem NHD brain; single-nucleus RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics (n=1 donor vs control).
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.07.757387

Fine-tuning genomic models on rare splicing events identifies a novel biomarker of TDP-43 pathology

bioRxiv  Martin-Linares, C. P. & Ling, J. P.
Key findings
The authors test whether genomic foundation models can predict rare disease-associated splicing after fine-tuning, using TDP-43 cryptic exons as a benchmark. Fine-tuned OpenSpliceAI detected held-out cryptic exons in human and mouse and captured allele-specific effects at the disease-associated UNC13A locus, while a prediction head on AlphaGenome's frozen backbone failed — full-weight fine-tuning was required for AlphaGenome to generalise. The work frames cryptic-splicing detection as a route to TDP-43-pathology biomarkers for ALS/FTD, contingent on fine-tuning strategy. Methodological claims await peer review.
Model & method
Computational; genomic foundation-model fine-tuning benchmarked against OpenSpliceAI on TDP-43 cryptic exons.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.07.757366

A Blood-Based Synaptic Biomarker in Neuronally-Enriched Extracellular Vesicles Predicts Disability Progression in Progressive Multiple Sclerosis

medRxiv  Jank, L. & Bhargava, P.
Key findings
Progressive MS lacks blood biomarkers of the neurodegeneration driving disability. Measuring the postsynaptic protein synaptopodin in neuronal-enriched extracellular vesicles, a 1-SD higher baseline level was associated with ~32% lower risk of confirmed disability progression across the SPRINT-MS trial (n=184), strongest in secondary progressive MS (~57% lower odds), and replicated in a real-world validation cohort (n=268; ~60% lower odds). Higher baseline synaptopodin also tracked slower subsequent whole-brain atrophy, nominating a scalable blood readout of synaptic integrity. Associative and pending peer review.
Model & method
Human; synaptopodin quantification in neuronal-enriched EVs across a trial and validation cohort with progression and atrophy outcomes.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.07.26364982
