1Single-cell, multi-region profiling of the macaque brain across the lifespan

Cell  Wei Yang & Noah Snyder-Mackler
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
This transcriptomic atlas of 5.3 million cells from 582 samples spanning 11 regions of 55 rhesus macaques (0.5–21 years) resolves age-depleted and vulnerable neuronal populations and subtle age-associated glial compositional shifts detectable only at single-cell resolution. The authors chart convergent and divergent age-associated molecular signatures across regions and cell types, including sex-specific signatures that could underlie sex biases in neurological disease. Critically, these aging signatures overlap those of Alzheimer's disease, supporting the view that aging and AD share molecular roots that emerge at different ages within region- and sex-specific windows of vulnerability. As a primate resource, it narrows the translational gap left by rodent aging studies.
Model & method
55 rhesus macaques across the lifespan; single-nucleus RNA-seq atlas (5.3M cells, 11 regions), with region- and sex-stratified aging analyses.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.09.014

2Neuronal cell-cycle re-entry stratifies clinical trajectories in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis

Brain  Jonathan Plessis-Belair & Roger B Sher
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Integrating AnswerALS cohort transcriptomics with human iPSC-derived spinal motor neurons, the authors show that the depth of aberrant cell-cycle engagement stratifies both neuronal fate and patient outcome. A subgroup with the highest G1/G2-phase cyclin expression declined more slowly (ALSFRS-R), whereas S-phase marker engagement tracked faster progression. Mechanistically, impaired nuclear import initiates TDP-43 pathology and a transient wave of cell-cycle activity immediately preceding death, and DNA-replication initiation marks a pathological point of no return with replication-associated DNA damage. Selective CDK4/6 inhibition blocked S-phase entry and conferred neuroprotection, nominating a stratification axis and a druggable node, though causal claims rest on iPSC models.
Model & method
AnswerALS transcriptomic cohort plus human iPSC-derived spinal motor neurons; nuclear-import manipulation and CDK4/6 inhibition.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag391

3Sub-second dopamine reinforcement shapes juvenile social play

Cell  Xinfeng Chen & Ying Li
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Using Social-seq (occlusion-robust 3D pose estimation with unsupervised clustering), the authors found nucleus accumbens dopamine tightly coupled to sub-second social syllables during naturalistic juvenile interactions. Wild-type males showed a sharper dopaminergic contrast between proactive play and forced submission than females, consistent with sex-divergent play strategies. In Shank3-deficient autism models this was corrupted — proactive-play dopamine responses were abolished in males and sign-inverted in females — and a reinforcement-learning model parameterized by the disrupted dopaminergic values recapitulated the mutant phenotypes. Closed-loop optogenetic stimulation of VTA→NAc dopamine projections, time-locked to proactive play, restored reciprocal play in Shank3-deficient rats, providing a behaviorally contingent interventional framework.
Model & method
Juvenile rodents including Shank3-deficient rats; Social-seq behavioral sequencing, dopamine recording, and closed-loop optogenetics.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.10.040

4A coronavirus protease activates sensory neurons via TRPA1 to drive sneeze, cough, and pain

Neuron  Sonali S Mali & Diana M Bautista
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
The coronavirus papain-like protease (PLpro) directly activates airway-innervating nociceptors in mice and human iPSC-derived nociceptors, triggering sneezing, coughing, acute pain, and CGRP release. Both PLpro proteolytic activity and the host channel TRPA1 are required for neuronal activation and sneezing, and infection with mouse-adapted SARS-CoV-2 or murine coronavirus MHV-A59 induces TRPA1-dependent sneezing. SARS-CoV-2 infection of the trigeminal ganglia remodeled nociception-associated transcriptional programs, including TRPA1–CGRP upregulation. The work defines a direct molecular mechanism linking a viral enzyme to airway reflexes and pain and identifies PLpro and TRPA1 as therapeutic targets.
Model & method
Mice (including TRPA1-null) and human iPSC-derived nociceptors; coronavirus infection, calcium imaging, and behavioral reflex assays.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.017

5Enhancer-based AAV approach for selective AADC delivery reduces motor symptoms and dyskinesia in Parkinson's mouse models

Cell Reports  Ananya Chowdhury & Tanya L Daigle
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
AAV-mediated striatal AADC expression combined with L-DOPA is a promising symptomatic strategy for Parkinson's disease but currently requires invasive delivery. Here, enhancer-driven AAVs restrict AADC to defined cell types and are paired with a blood-brain-barrier-penetrant capsid. Across two delivery routes and PD mouse models, cell-type-specific AADC expression improved survival and ameliorated motor deficits and L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia, with rescue similar to or greater than ubiquitous expression, and enhancer specificity was conserved in non-human primates. Combined with L-DOPA, this points toward a less invasive, better-tolerated gene-therapy approach, pending clinical translation.
Model & method
Parkinson's disease mouse models with non-human primate validation; enhancer-driven AAV with a BBB-penetrant capsid, across systemic and parenchymal delivery.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118132

Key findings
A meta-analytic GWAS of multidimensional pain across 1,235,695 European-ancestry individuals identified 343 independent loci (92 new), with sex-specific analyses revealing 115 loci in males and 12 in females. Understanding apolipoprotein E as a predominant cause of Alzheimer's disease

Brain  Dylan M Williams & Emma L Anderson
Key findings
This review argues that APOE's contribution to Alzheimer's disease has been underestimated because the common ε3 allele itself raises risk relative to ε2. When both ε3 and ε4 are considered, the majority of late-onset AD in European-ancestry populations — and much early-onset and genetically driven disease, including trisomy 21 — appears attributable to APOE function or expression, positioning APOE as a component cause spanning most disease aetiologies and arguing that nearly all prospective cases could benefit from APOE-directed therapies.

Insight discordance differentiates behavioural variant frontotemporal dementia from false positives

Brain  Joshua Flavell & Peter J Nestor
Key findings
In 27 FTLD patients presenting as bvFTD and 25 psychiatric false-positives, patients with bvFTD endorsed fewer symptoms than false-positives while clinicians observed markedly more, so a model combining patient-clinician and informant-clinician discrepancy achieved near-perfect cross-validated discrimination (AUC 0.99). bioRxiv  L Yang & D Liu
Key findings
In mouse cortex, extended wakefulness selectively suppressed neuronal autophagic flux while sleep rapidly restored it, shifting autophagic cargo from mitochondrial components toward synaptic proteins and upregulating ATG7.
