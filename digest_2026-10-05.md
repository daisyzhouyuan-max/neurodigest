### Prediagnosis plasma neurofilament light and ALS risk in the Danish EPIC cohort
**Aline Davias & Marc G Weisskopf | Ann Neurol**

**Why it matters:** In a nested matched case-control study (166 ALS cases, 332 controls) with plasma banked a median 14 years before diagnosis, NfL was elevated in future cases up to ~14.6 years prediagnosis — far earlier than the ~5-year window previously documented. The per-SD odds ratio for ALS rose steeply as diagnosis approached (OR 3.8 within 5 years, 1.7 at 5–14.6 years, non-significant 1.3 beyond 14.6 years), and discrimination was correspondingly modest far out (AUC 0.63 at 5–14.6 years vs 0.78 under 5 years). The finding implies a much longer ALS prodrome than assumed and raises, cautiously, the prospect of earlier at-risk identification — though the early-window effect is small and NfL is non-specific, so this is a window on disease latency rather than a usable screening test.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human nested case-control within a prospective cohort; plasma NfL by Olink Target 96; conditional logistic regression and ROC analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78382

### Progressive remodeling of protein interaction networks in a mouse tauopathy model
**Weiwei Lin & Benjamin Wolozin | Cell Rep**

**Why it matters:** The study builds a temporally resolved, systems-scale map of multi-protein complex remodeling across tauopathy progression, integrating co-fractionation mass spectrometry, quantitative phosphoproteomics and machine learning to track phosphorylation-dependent shifts in interactomes rather than mere protein abundance. It resolves specific disease-associated assemblies — including MAPT–Dpysl2, Cyfip1–actin, and ptk2b-containing complexes — that modulate early phenotypes in vivo, framing neurodegeneration as progressive network instability driven by phosphorylation. Causal evidence is from a single mouse tauopathy model and the atlas is primarily a resource, but it reframes proteostatic collapse as reorganization of complex architecture and nominates tractable nodes.

**Model/system & primary method:** Transgenic mouse tauopathy model; co-fractionation MS interactomics + phosphoproteomics + ML, with in vivo phenotyping.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118084

### Altered lysosomal sphingolipid catabolism tracks lysosomal dysfunction in the aging brain
**Chinmoy Sarkar & Marta M Lipinski | Cell Rep**

**Why it matters:** Longitudinal lipidomic and proteomic profiling of lysosomal fractions from mouse cortex (3–24 months), analyzed by multi-omics factor analysis, reveals an age-dependent accumulation of myelin-derived lipid and protein components and a catabolic shift favoring sphingomyelin degradation over glycosphingolipids. The authors experimentally corroborate that age-dependent glycosphingolipid accumulation is associated with lysosomal enlargement and dysfunction, most pronounced in microglia — linking normal brain aging to a lysosomal-storage-disease-like phenotype in glia. The evidence is murine and largely associative for the functional readouts, but the organelle-resolved design pins the changes to the lysosome itself rather than bulk tissue.

**Model/system & primary method:** Aging mouse cortex; lysosomal-fraction lipidomics/proteomics with MOFA, plus cell-type validation implicating microglia.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118065

### Memory performance and plasma pTau217 refine Alzheimer prognosis in Down syndrome
**Casey R Vanderlip & Alzheimer's Biomarker Consortium—Down Syndrome (ABC‑DS) Study Team | Ann Neurol**

**Key findings:** Using ABC-DS data, combining plasma pTau217 with the modified cued recall task identified a subgroup of currently cognitively stable individuals with Down syndrome at markedly elevated risk of clinical impairment over the following 7 years. The low-burden, multimodal framework improves individual-level prognosis and could enrich prevention trials in this high-risk, under-served population. The report is a brief communication and the predictive gain is described qualitatively; prospective validation of the risk thresholds is still needed.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human longitudinal cohort (Down syndrome); plasma pTau217 immunoassay plus modified cued recall, prognostic modeling over 7 years.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78366

### Cryo-EM structures of human endosomal exchangers NHE6 and NHE9
**Jesper S Hansen & Kilian V M Huber | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Cryo-EM structures of human NHE9 and two NHE6 splice variants (differing by inclusion of a β-hairpin loop between TM2 and TM3) define the architecture of the only two SLC9 members targeted to endosomes. Mapping the sodium-binding site yields a mechanistic model of ion transport, and for NHE6 the data support a conserved PIP2-mediated regulatory mechanism. Because NHE6/NHE9 dysfunction is linked to neurological and neurodegenerative disorders (including Alzheimer's disease), the structures provide a framework for understanding endosomal pH homeostasis and a template for targeting it — though the disease relevance here is structural inference rather than functional disease modeling.

**Model/system & primary method:** Recombinant human transporters; single-particle cryo-EM with structure-guided interpretation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77254-0

### Medium-term cognition after CAR T-cell therapy for CNS lymphoma
**Sirine Mersali & Caroline Houillier | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In a retrospective single-center series of 30 patients with isolated CNS lymphoma treated with CAR T cells (21 followed to 12 months), MoCA scores improved significantly over baseline at 6 and 12 months (mean 25.4 vs 22.1 at M12, p < 0.001), with language gains and stability across other domains — even among the 63% who had acute neurotoxicity. Early trajectory (improvement vs worsening at 6 weeks) tracked with neurotoxicity occurrence and severity. The reassuring medium-term cognitive picture is tempered by small n, retrospective design, and survivorship: only patients without tumor progression and with follow-up were assessed.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human retrospective cohort; serial neuropsychological testing (MoCA and domain measures), paired t-tests.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218561

### Light-based bioprinting for patient-specific neurovascular thrombosis models (perspective)
**Linda Dei-Awuku & Lining Arnold Ju | Sci Adv**

**Key findings:** This perspective argues for digital light processing and volumetric bioprinting as a route to perfusable, high-fidelity vessel-on-chip models of cerebral venous sinus thrombosis and carotid web — cerebrovascular disorders that disproportionately affect women and are poorly captured by conventional platforms. Integrated with clinical imaging and computational fluid dynamics, such constructs could reproduce pathological flow stasis, shear-stress gradients and site-specific cell aggregation for personalized risk assessment. It is a forward-looking review rather than new experimental data, so the claims are programmatic.

**Model/system & primary method:** Review/perspective on light-based biofabrication and vessel-on-chip modeling; no primary data.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeh3952

### In vivo seeding evidence for mixed α-synuclein and TDP-43 pathology in a GRN mutation carrier
**Paola Caroppo & Grazia Devigili | Mol Neurodegener**

**Key findings:** In a single patient carrying the GRN p.Thr272Serfs*10 mutation who presented with parkinsonism and later prodromal frontotemporal dementia, seed amplification assays on olfactory mucosa together with skin analyses suggested co-existing TDP-43 and α-synuclein pathology in vivo. The report illustrates how combining peripheral seeding assays across tissues might non-invasively capture mixed proteinopathy during life. This is a single case report (published as correspondence), so it is hypothesis-generating evidence of feasibility, not a validated diagnostic finding.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human single-case report; seed amplification assay on olfactory mucosa and skin.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-01000-9

### REM sleep EEG slowing signals basal forebrain integrity in older adults with cognitive concerns
**Lam, A. & Naismith, S. L. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 116 older adults with subjective or mild cognitive impairment (65% MCI), greater REM-sleep EEG slowing (delta+theta / alpha+sigma+beta power ratio) was associated with smaller nucleus basalis of Meynert volume on MRI (all p < 0.05), and smaller NbM volume tracked with longer sleep latency and poorer episodic memory. The association appeared stronger within the MCI subgroup, though moderation effects did not survive FDR correction. The authors position REM EEG slowing as a non-invasive proxy of early cholinergic basal-forebrain degeneration; the design is cross-sectional, so causal and prognostic claims await longitudinal follow-up.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cross-sectional cohort; polysomnography-derived REM EEG spectral ratio and T1-MRI NbM volumetry, with regression/moderation analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.30.26363850

### ALS/FTD-linked TDP-43 alterations block HSV-1 infection via disrupted cell adhesion
**Jackvony Infurna, S. & Orr, A. G. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** ALS/FTD-associated TDP-43 forms conferred cell-autonomous resistance to herpes simplex virus type-1 across multiple TDP-43 mutations, sporadic and C9ORF72 ALS, neural and non-neural cells, and patient-derived and animal models, while also blunting inflammatory and stress responses to HSV-1. TDP-43 knockdown (but not overexpression) reproduced protection, implicating loss of function; mutant neurons showed disrupted adhesion pathways, altered splicing, and impaired focal adhesion kinase (FAK/PTK2), with reduced early viral binding/entry. FAK inhibition alone reduced infection and restoring functional FAK rescued it, placing adhesion machinery between TDP-43 dysfunction and virus-host interaction. Evidence is mechanistic and multi-model but the disease implication of altered viral susceptibility remains to be established.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived neurons, patient cells and animal models; HSV-1 infection assays with knockdown/overexpression, splicing analysis, and FAK perturbation.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.28.755069

### No association between rare TMEM106B variants and Parkinson's disease risk
**Chaparro-Solano, H. M. & the Global Parkinson's Genetics Program (GP2) | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Leveraging whole-genome sequencing from 22,010 cases and 40,550 controls across diverse ancestries (GP2, AMP-PD, UK Biobank), the study found no pathogenic/likely-pathogenic ClinVar variants in TMEM106B in any cohort and no association of rare coding variants with PD risk, individually or by burden. This well-powered negative result argues that, despite reported reductions in TMEM106B expression in PD brain, rare coding variation in the gene does not materially drive PD susceptibility — a useful constraint on interpreting the locus. As a rare-variant coding analysis it does not address common non-coding regulatory variation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multi-cohort whole-genome sequencing; single-variant and gene-burden association testing.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.28.26363833

### ANGPTL7 shapes resident immune responses driving glaucomatous optic nerve neurodegeneration
**Vollmer, T. R. & Wilson, C. W. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Beyond its known trabecular-meshwork role, ANGPTL7 was found expressed in prelaminar optic nerve head astrocytes. Angptl7-knockout mice showed no intraocular-pressure difference from wild-type yet were protected from IOP-induced retinal ganglion cell damage in a rodent glaucoma model, dissociating neuroprotection from pressure lowering. The data suggest ANGPTL7 acts through LAIR1-positive immune cells to promote pro-inflammatory signaling and modulate resident immune cells, with oligomerization required for activity — implicating neuroinflammation, rather than IOP alone, in glaucoma progression. Evidence is murine and the immune signaling mechanism is correlative.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse Angptl7 knockout and rodent glaucoma neurodegeneration model; human posterior-eye expression analysis and immune-cell signaling studies.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.27.751872

### An LDL-cholesterol–pericyte axis in cerebral microvascular dysfunction after ischemic stroke
**Wang, F. & Liu, Y. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Across two acute ischemic stroke cohorts (n=97, n=86), higher serum soluble PDGFRβ — a pericyte-injury marker — correlated with greater neurological deficit, circulating NfL, and LDL-cholesterol. In Apoe-knockout mice, high-fat diet reprogrammed cerebral pericytes (suppressed type-I interferon signaling, raised ROS programs), and LDL-C exposure of cultured human brain pericytes induced oxidative-stress, lipid-handling and ferroptosis-associated programs while reducing surface PDGFRβ and increasing its soluble release — effects attenuated by N-acetyl-L-cysteine. Together the clinical and experimental data nominate an LDL–pericyte oxidative-stress axis, with exploratory hints of larger effects in females. Human data are associative and the sex effects are exploratory.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human AIS serum cohorts; Apoe-knockout mouse single-cell RNA-seq of pericytes and cultured human brain pericytes with LDL-C ± NAC.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.27.754749
