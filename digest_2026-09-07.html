1Priming of CD8+ T cells by peripheral dendritic cells exacerbates tau-mediated neurodegeneration

Nat Neurosci  Hao Hu & David M Holtzman
Why it matters
Brain CD8+ T cell infiltration tracks tau pathology severity in Alzheimer's disease and primary tauopathies, but where these T cells get primed has been unclear. The authors show that conventional type-1 dendritic cells (cDC1s) cross-present brain-derived antigen in secondary lymphoid tissue to prime CD8+ T cells peripherally, and that tauopathy mice lacking cDC1s or cross-presentation are protected from tau-driven neurodegeneration, with reduced brain T cell infiltration and glial activation. This identifies a peripheral, targetable checkpoint upstream of the neuroinflammatory cascade that damages neurons in tauopathy.
Model & method
Tauopathy mouse models (cDC1-deficient and cross-presentation-deficient) with brain and lymphoid T cell profiling.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02427-5

2An Integrated Single-Nucleus Atlas Resolves Cell-Type-Specific Programs and Molecular Subtypes in Alzheimer's Disease

bioRxiv  N Rahimzadeh & V Swarup
Why it matters
Single-cell AD studies have been hard to reconcile across cohorts. This atlas pools >3 million nuclei from 791 individuals across 13 studies spanning AD, MCI and normal aging, and finds a reproducible cell-type-specific molecular architecture: co-expression modules that track neuropathology and cognition, GWAS risk genes acting largely downstream of hub transcription factors (e.g., microglial SPI1), and disease-stage remodeling of intercellular communication. A new patient-subtyping method (MONET) resolves four molecular subtypes — metal-ion stress, neuroinflammatory, synaptic integrity, and tissue remodeling — that dissociate pathology burden from cognitive impairment and point to largely non-overlapping candidate therapeutics, suggesting AD may require stratified rather than one-size-fits-all treatment.
Model & method
Meta-analysis of human single-nucleus transcriptomic data (13 studies, 791 individuals); masked variational autoencoder for patient subtyping.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.747935

3Oligodendroglial somatic SNCA copy number gains are associated with inclusions and disease onset in multiple system atrophy

Acta Neuropathol  Caoimhe Morley & Christos Proukakis
Why it matters
MSA is a synucleinopathy of unknown cause. Extending earlier work on somatic SNCA copy-number variation, the authors show oligodendrocyte-specific SNCA gains (and losses) are significantly elevated in MSA brains versus controls, concentrated in the regions preferentially affected in each MSA subtype (putamen in striatonigral degeneration, cerebellum in olivopontocerebellar atrophy), and associated with a twofold increase in co-localized α-synuclein inclusions. Higher gain burden correlated with earlier disease onset. Elevated γH2AX (a DNA double-strand break marker) in MSA oligodendrocytes, especially in inclusion-bearing cells, points to genomic instability as a potential upstream driver — a mechanism distinct from the aggregation-focused models that dominate the synucleinopathy field.
Model & method
Postmortem human brain (13 SND, 12 OPCA, 15 control), FISH for SNCA copy number combined with α-synuclein/SOX10 immunofluorescence and γH2AX staining.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03077-4

4Familial, neuropathological and cellular analysis identify ARPP21 as a major amyotrophic lateral sclerosis associated gene in French cohorts

Acta Neuropathol  Sibylle de Bertier & Stéphanie Millecamps
Why it matters
In a French cohort of 1,190 ALS cases, recurrent ARPP21 variants (p.P529L and p.P713L) account for 2.7% of familial and 0.1% of sporadic ALS, making ARPP21 the most frequent rare ALS gene after the four major known loci, with age-dependent but incomplete penetrance (45% by age 50). The p.P713L mutant aggregates with hyperphosphorylation and co-localizes with p62 in cellular models, and neuropathology from a p.P529L carrier showed typical TDP-43 pathology alongside ARPP21-positive deposits (though these may reflect general neuronal stress rather than a mutant-specific signature). The authors argue for incorporating ARPP21 into routine familial ALS genetic panels.
Model & method
Familial/clinical genetics (1,190 ALS cases, 50 family members), cellular aggregation assays, human neuropathology.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03075-6

5Large-scale single-molecule analysis of tau proteoforms

Nat Methods  James Joly & Parag Mallick
Why it matters
Tau exists as a heterogeneous population of proteoforms shaped by splicing and post-translational modification, but conventional methods can't resolve this complexity at the single-molecule level. The authors introduce Iterative Mapping, which uses repeated cycles of fluorescently labeled antibody probing to interrogate up to billions of individual protein molecules, detecting proteoforms at 0.1% abundance with high reproducibility. Applied to organoids, mouse brain, and human AD tissue, it resolved 130 distinct tau proteoform groups with up to six phosphorylation events, and the nonrandom distribution of these events suggests ordered, site-specific modification rather than stochastic accumulation — a resource-generating advance for dissecting tau pathobiology at a resolution not previously accessible.
Model & method
Organoids, mouse brain, and human AD brain tissue; single-molecule iterative antibody-probing assay (12 site-specific tau antibodies).
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-026-03188-6

Neuromodulation for restoring and amplifying brain function

Nat Neurosci  Shrey Grover & Robert M G Reinhart
Key findings
This review formalizes two complementary neuromodulation strategies: "restorative normalization" (RN), which aims to restore normal neural dynamics, and "compensatory amplification" (CA), which instead strengthens repurposed compensatory processes. The authors identify four factors enabling effective CA — multiple realizability, multiscale neuroplasticity, precision readiness, and activity selectivity — and argue CA is particularly underexploited in neurodegeneration, psychiatry, and aging, where it could delay decline or extend processing capacity even without restoring "normal" dynamics.
Model & method
Conceptual review/framework synthesizing cognitive neurophysiology and clinical neuromodulation literature.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02434-6

Structured experience shapes strategy learning and neural dynamics in the medial entorhinal cortex

Nat Neurosci  John C Bowler & James G Heys
Key findings
Training recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and mice on an odor-timing task, the authors show that the structure of early experience — not just its content — determines whether flexible, generalizable strategies emerge. Without structured early training, both RNNs and mice developed rigid, error-prone strategies; structured curricula instead produced neural activity in medial entorhinal cortex that reflected the task's temporal structure, and dynamical-systems analysis linked specific training curricula to network dynamics that supported abstraction as task complexity increased.
Model & method
Mouse behavior with medial entorhinal cortex electrophysiology, validated against RNN models trained on the same task.

Progress towards a biotypic biomarker profile for amyotrophic lateral sclerosis-frontotemporal spectrum disorders

Brain  Michael J Strong & David A Wolk
Key findings
Summarizing a 2025 international workshop, this review argues that ALS and FTD form a clinicopathological spectrum unified by TDP-43 cytoplasmic aggregation, with phenoconversion often preceded by years to decades of silent pathobiology. The authors propose "biotypic biomarkers" — measures that capture underlying pathophysiology independent of eventual clinical presentation — as necessary to detect ALS/FTD pathobiology before symptom onset and to time disease-modifying therapy correctly; no single such marker yet exists, but a multimodal profile incorporating genetic risk and biological-aging markers is proposed as the near-term path forward.
Model & method
Narrative review/workshop consensus synthesizing biomarker literature across ALS and FTD.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag297

Diagnostic performance of plasma pTau217 in genetically admixed South American populations

Nat Commun  Pamela V Martino-Adami & Alfredo Ramírez
Key findings
Plasma pTau217 evidence has come mostly from European-ancestry cohorts. In memory-clinic cohorts from Brazil (n=353) and Argentina (n=134), pTau217 and pTau217/Aβ42 accurately identified CSF-defined amyloid pathology and showed high concordance with clinical diagnosis; two-cutoff approaches improved classification under diagnostic uncertainty. APOE-ε4, lower BMI, and reduced kidney function were associated with higher pTau217, and African ancestry attenuated the effect of APOE-ε4 on CSF (but not plasma) biomarkers — a reminder that ancestry-specific calibration will matter as plasma biomarkers are deployed in resource-limited settings.
Model & method
Human memory-clinic cohorts (Brazil, Argentina), Simoa-based plasma biomarker assays with CSF and clinical diagnosis as reference standards.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76434-2

Non-invasive characterization of perivascular subarachnoid spaces

Nat Commun  Nina E Fultz & Lydiane Hirschler
Key findings
Prior invasive intrathecal contrast imaging suggested CSF is compartmentalized into a perivascular subarachnoid space (PVSAS) that enhances distinctly along major cerebral arteries, separated from the broader subarachnoid space by a semipermeable membrane. Using a non-invasive CSF-mobility MRI technique (CSF-STREAM), the authors observe high CSF mobility adjacent to the middle and anterior cerebral arteries with a steep drop-off into the surrounding subarachnoid space in healthy controls — supporting the existence of the PVSAS without contrast injection and opening a route to studying CSF compartmentalization and glymphatic-adjacent clearance pathways in disease non-invasively.
Model & method
Healthy human controls; CSF-STREAM (CSF-selective T2-prepared MRI with mobility encoding).
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76306-9

Targeting m6A writer METTL3 with engineered nanovesicles reduces neuroinflammation in vitro and in vivo

Nat Commun  Liangfu Xu & Lang Rao
Key findings
The authors engineer siMETTL3-hNVs, hybrid nanovesicles combining natural microglia-derived nanovesicles with liposomal siRNA against the m6A writer METTL3, to deliver epigenetic silencing across the blood-brain barrier via CCR2-CCL2 chemotaxis and caveolae-mediated transcytosis. The particles also act as cytokine decoys via surface cytokine receptors, and combined with METTL3 knockdown drive microglial M2 repolarization. In female mouse models of acute neuroinflammation and radiation-induced brain injury, treatment reduced cytokine levels, hippocampal damage, and cognitive deficits — a proof-of-concept for brain-targeted epigenetic nanotherapeutics.
Model & method
Female mouse models of acute neuroinflammation and radiation-induced brain injury; engineered hybrid nanovesicle drug delivery.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75862-4

Spatiotemporal Tau Accumulation and its Clinical Impact on Progressive Supranuclear Palsy: Longitudinal Florzolotau (18F) Positron Emission Tomography

Mov Disord  Yoshikazu Chishiki & Hironobu Endo
Key findings
In 26 PSP patients (18 Richardson's syndrome, 8 non-RS) followed for one year with florzolotau PET, baseline tau deposition was most prominent in globus pallidus and midbrain, but longitudinal increases were concentrated in globus pallidus, frontoparietal cortex, and cerebellar white matter, with minimal further midbrain progression. Globus pallidus tau accumulation correlated with clinical decline (ρ=0.688) and showed the strongest association with progression in multivariate modeling, supporting florzolotau PET as a candidate biological outcome measure for tau-targeted PSP trials — though the cohort is small and non-RS PSP subtypes were underrepresented.
Model & method
Human longitudinal cohort (26 PSP, 10 controls); florzolotau (18F) tau-PET and MRI at baseline and 1 year.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70468

GLP-1 receptor agonist use is associated with shorter survival in patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and diabetes mellitus

medRxiv  Iris Lee & Neil Shneider  v2 revision of a preprint first posted May 2025
Key findings
In a single-institution retrospective cohort of 85 ALS patients with comorbid diabetes (15 on GLP-1 receptor agonists), GLP-1RA use was associated with shorter tracheostomy-free survival after adjusting for age, sex, bulbar onset, BMI, and riluzole use (HR 2.5, 95% CI 1.1–5.3). GLP-1RA-treated patients were younger at onset and heavier at diagnosis, and the unadjusted survival difference was only a trend (p=0.08), becoming significant after covariate adjustment — a retrospective, single-center signal that warrants replication given the rapidly increasing use of GLP-1RAs, but should not yet be read as causal.
Model & method
Retrospective single-institution clinical cohort (n=85 ALS+diabetes); Kaplan-Meier and Cox proportional hazards survival analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.05.05.25326993

Nucleus basalis of Meynert integrity is associated with cognitive dysfunction in Parkinson's disease independently of locus coeruleus degeneration

medRxiv  A Bailey & M Sharp  v3 revision of a preprint first posted July 2026
Key findings
In 112 PD patients (disease duration <10 years) and 46 controls, mean diffusivity of the cholinergic nucleus basalis of Meynert (nbM) — a proxy for microstructural degeneration — did not differ from controls overall, but higher nbM diffusivity was associated with worse executive function within PD patients, independent of neuromelanin-indexed degeneration in the substantia nigra and locus coeruleus, and this relationship was stronger in PD than in controls. Attention showed a similar but non-significant trend; memory was unaffected. The cross-sectional design limits causal inference, but the finding supports a basal forebrain cholinergic contribution to early PD executive dysfunction distinct from noradrenergic (locus coeruleus) degeneration.
Model & method
Human cross-sectional cohort (112 PD, 46 controls); diffusion-weighted and neuromelanin MRI with neuropsychological testing.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.26358398

Network analysis of α-synuclein pathology progression reveals p21-activated kinases as regulators of vulnerability

bioRxiv  N Vatsa & M X Henderson  v3 revision of a preprint first posted October 2024
Key findings
Mapping whole-brain α-synuclein pathology in wildtype mice from 3 days to 9 months after pathology seeding, the authors combine network-diffusion modeling of anatomical connectivity with a new brain-wide spatial gene expression atlas (PANGEA) to show that regional vulnerability to synucleinopathy is not fully explained by connectivity alone — specific transcriptional programs, including group II p21-activated kinases (PAK5/6), mark vulnerable regions. Pharmacological PAK inhibition reduced α-synuclein aggregation and protected against neuron loss in primary neurons even when given after pathology onset, and suppressed pathology in vivo; genetic PAK5/6 depletion reproduced the protective effect — nominating group II PAKs as a disease-modifying drug target for Parkinson's disease, with causal evidence still limited to mouse and cellular models.
Model & method
Wildtype mouse α-synuclein seeding model with whole-brain pathology mapping and spatial transcriptomics; primary neuron pharmacology and PAK5/6 knockout validation.
https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.10.22.619411

Genotoxic and metabolic stress drive divergent senescence programs in human microglia

bioRxiv  B Platt & S Janssens
Key findings
Using the human microglial line HMC3, the authors show that chronic DNA-damaging stress (doxorubicin) and chronic metabolic stress (high glucose) both induce classic senescence features — hypertrophy, senescence-associated β-galactosidase, reduced metabolic viability without cell loss — but through partly divergent pathways: metabolic stress additionally induced p16 and peripheral nuclear p21 localization, and produced an interferon-associated inflammatory secretory phenotype versus a chemokine-rich one under genotoxic stress. Both converged on mitochondrial dysregulation with only partially compensatory antioxidant responses. The findings suggest brain-aging-associated microglial senescence is not a single program but a family of stressor-specific states, each with a distinct inflammatory signature.
Model & method
Human microglial cell line (HMC3) under chronic genotoxic (doxorubicin) or metabolic (high-glucose) stress; senescence phenotyping and transcriptional/mitochondrial profiling.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.31.748228

ApoE4 impairs astrocyte lipid droplet formation via endolysosomal dysfunction

bioRxiv  I Ralhan & M S Ioannou
Key findings
Neuron-derived lipids are normally transferred to astrocytic lipid droplets for safe storage; failure of this pathway exacerbates neurodegeneration. The authors show ApoE4 particles impair astrocyte endolysosomal function and promote lipofuscin accumulation, disrupting lipid trafficking into droplets in cultured primary astrocytes. Critically, this defect was reversible: blocking ApoE4 internalization with PCSK9 (which prevents LDLR recycling), or directly repairing endolysosomal function, restored lipid droplet formation — pointing to endolysosomal repair as a potential therapeutic strategy for ApoE4-associated AD risk, though the work is limited to cultured astrocytes.
Model & method
Cultured primary mouse astrocytes exposed to ApoE4 particles; endolysosomal and lipid droplet imaging with pharmacological rescue (PCSK9).
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.01.748585
