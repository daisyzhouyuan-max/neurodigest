1Progenitor transcriptional alterations link fetal and adult pathology in Huntington's disease

Brain  Mattia Battistella & Elena Cattaneo
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Huntingtin is expressed from conception, yet symptoms emerge in mid-adulthood, leaving open whether HD has a neurodevelopmental component. Single-cell profiling of a rare, anatomically intact human HD fetal brain (lateral ganglionic eminence and cortex) against matched controls found that neural progenitors carried the largest burden of differentially expressed genes, with altered synaptic-gene programs — and these progenitor signatures were moderately recapitulated in an allelic series of HD mouse embryos and overlapped with adult post-mortem and organoid data. The work supplies direct single-cell evidence that transcriptional vulnerability begins in fetal progenitors and persists into adult pathology, reframing HD as partly a disorder of neurodevelopment rather than purely adult-onset degeneration.
Model & method
One intact human HD fetal brain + controls, HD mouse embryos, cross-referenced to human post-mortem and organoid datasets; single-cell RNA-seq. Human evidence is necessarily n-limited (single fetal specimen).
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag364

2Prevalence of α-synuclein seeding activity in the olfactory mucosa following SARS-CoV-2 infection

Acta Neuropathol  Beatrice Heim & Werner Poewe
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Hyposmia is shared by post-COVID syndrome and prodromal Parkinson's disease, but whether SARS-CoV-2 triggers local α-synuclein misfolding has been untested. In this observational seed-amplification-assay study of olfactory mucosa (PD n=51, post-COVID n=95, controls n=42), αSyn-SAA positivity was 80.4% in PD versus 24.2% in post-COVID and 11.9% in controls, and within the post-COVID group was higher in those with persistent olfactory dysfunction (35.7%) than normosmic individuals (15.1%). The elevated seeding signal in post-COVID participants — well above controls though far below PD — raises the possibility that infection can seed peripheral synucleinopathy, though this is a cross-sectional pilot with no longitudinal conversion data.
Model & method
Human olfactory mucosa samples; α-synuclein seed-amplification assay with clinical phenotyping (Sniffin' Sticks, MDS-UPDRS, RBD inventory).
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03104-4

3Blood-based biomarkers as predictors of all-cause dementia: a 13-year follow-up in the Whitehall II cohort

Neurology  Séverine Sabia & Archana Singh-Manoux
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Plasma biomarkers track amyloid well in research settings, but their long-horizon predictive value in community populations is uncertain. In 5,225 participants (mean age 69) with samples assayed for Aβ42/40, GFAP, NfL and p-tau217 and a median 12.2-year follow-up, p-tau217 carried the strongest association with incident all-cause dementia (HR 3.74, 95% CI 3.12–4.50), far exceeding the Aβ42/40 ratio (HR 1.88). The result quantifies how much prognostic signal a single mid-life p-tau217 measurement retains over more than a decade, supporting its use for long-range risk stratification while the modest performance of amyloid ratios tempers expectations for the others.
Model & method
Human population-based prospective cohort (Whitehall II); stored-plasma immunoassays with Cox regression for incident dementia.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218681

4Education reduces dementia incidence and mitigates genetic risk

Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A  Silvia H Barcellos & Patrick Turley
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Causal evidence for modifiable dementia risk factors is scarce because education correlates with many confounders. Exploiting a compulsory-schooling reform as a regression-discontinuity instrument — with dementia ascertained from medical, hospital and death records to limit attrition bias — the authors find that additional education causally lowers incidence of Alzheimer's disease and related dementias, and that the reform weakened the association between polygenic risk and dementia. The second finding is the more striking: it implies genetic risk is not fixed and can be attenuated by a social-policy intervention, strengthening the case for education as a population-level prevention lever rather than a mere correlate.
Model & method
Human quasi-experiment; regression-discontinuity around a schooling-age reform with linked registry outcomes and polygenic-risk interaction analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2529506123

5TSPO-PET binding in vivo reflects increased phagocytic microglia at post mortem in frontotemporal dementia

Acta Neuropathol  Davi S Vontobel & Maura Malpetti
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
TSPO-PET is widely read as an in vivo index of neuroinflammation, but what cell type drives the signal has remained contested, and had not been resolved in FTD with matched ante-mortem imaging and tissue. Pairing [11C]PK11195 PET with quantitative post-mortem neuropathology in 10 FTD cases (5 FTLD-tau, 5 FTLD-TDP) and 5 controls, CD68+ phagocytic microglial burden — concentrated in white matter — scaled with regional TSPO binding (β=8.66, P<0.001) with comparable slopes across tau and TDP pathology, and among four candidate cell markers only the CD68–TSPO co-localized fraction tracked the signal. The study grounds TSPO-PET interpretation in a specific cellular substrate, validating it as a phagocytic-microglia readout in FTD though in a small autopsy series.
Model & method
Human matched ante-mortem [11C]PK11195 TSPO-PET and post-mortem tissue; CD68 immunohistochemistry, multiplex immunofluorescence, 3D single-microglia reconstruction.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03103-5

Sustained antidepressant-like effects of psychedelics through 5-HT2AR–β-arrestin signaling

Nat Neurosci  Licong He & Sheng Wang
Key findings
Structure-guided design of β-arrestin-biased 5-HT2AR agonists identified β-arrestin, rather than Gαq, signaling as the driver of prolonged antidepressant-like effects in mice. The slow dissociation of LSD lets repeated low doses accumulate receptor occupancy approaching an acute high dose while avoiding its peak concentration, sustaining β-arrestin signaling with limited Gαq-mediated Ca²⁺ responses; repeated low-dose LSD or psilocybin pretreatment produced durable behavioral effects without psychedelic-associated behavioral disruption in mice or cynomolgus macaques. The dissociation of therapeutic from hallucinogenic signaling offers a rational template for non-hallucinogenic psychedelic therapeutics, though efficacy remains behavioral and preclinical.
Model & method
Mouse behavioral pharmacology and macaques; structure-guided biased-agonist design with receptor-signaling assays.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02497-5

Identification of candidate markers for the isolation of neuron-specific extracellular vesicles

Nat Neurosci  Dmitry Ter-Ovanesyan & David R Walt
Key findings
Isolating cell-type-specific extracellular vesicles from biofluids has been a bottleneck for minimally invasive brain monitoring. Combining gene-expression and EV proteomic data to nominate transmembrane surface markers, the authors catalogued EV surface proteins in human CSF and plasma, computationally predicted neuron-, astrocyte-, oligodendrocyte- and microglia-specific markers, and experimentally confirmed NRXN3 as present on neuronal EVs — then built an immuno-isolation workflow to capture NRXN3+ vesicles. The methodology provides a generalizable route to cell-type-resolved EV biomarkers for brain disease, with NRXN3 as a validated neuronal handle.
Model & method
Human CSF and plasma; EV proteomics, computational marker selection, ultrasensitive immunoassays and immuno-isolation.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02466-y

Network geometry shapes the transformation of task representations across the human cortex

Nat Neurosci  Lakshman N C Chakravarthula & Michael W Cole
Key findings
Analyzing fMRI across 16 cognitive tasks with activity-flow modeling, the dimensionality of intrinsic connectivity predicted how representations change between regions: low-dimensional connectivity compressed representations and raised cross-task similarity, whereas high-dimensional connectivity expanded them and supported conjunctive coding. Activity-flow modeling reproduced the compression-to-expansion gradient along the sensory–association–motor hierarchy and generated geometries matching targets better than sources, consistent with active transformation rather than passive transfer of information. The work identifies connectivity geometry as a systems-level principle organizing cortical representation.
Model & method
Human task fMRI; representational geometry analysis with activity-flow modeling.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02486-8

Behavioral genomics identifies Creld1 as a cross-modal regulator of somatosensation

Nat Neurosci  Yan Jiang & Bailong Xiao
Key findings
An AAV9-based postnatal CRISPR-Cas9 knockout platform for dorsal root ganglion genes enabled a targeted 20-gene behavioral screen that flagged Creld1. Postnatal knockout or Cre-mediated deletion of Creld1 in DRG neurons produced profound deficits across touch, temperature, pain and itch, while overexpression enhanced touch and thermal responses; mechanistically CRELD1 acts as an auxiliary regulator of voltage-gated sodium channels controlling DRG excitability. The study establishes a scalable somatosensory behavioral-genomics pipeline and names Creld1 a broad-spectrum regulator of somatosensation.
Model & method
Mouse DRG; AAV9 postnatal CRISPR knockout, conditional deletion, somatosensory behavioral assays and electrophysiology.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02484-w

A vagal gut–brain axis for feeding-induced gastric secretion in mice

Neuron  Zhuang Wang & Jinfei D Ni
Key findings
Dissecting vagal gut–brain–gut pathways for meal-induced gastric secretion, stomach-innervating vagal afferents were necessary and sufficient for secretion; among them Glp1r+ and Sst+ afferents drove acid, pepsin and gastrin, whereas Oxtr+ and Vip+ neurons had little effect. Piezo2 marked Glp1r+ mechanosensory afferents and its conditional deletion attenuated distension-evoked secretion, while efferent Cck+ motor neurons broadly increased secretion and Grp+ preferentially drove acid; enteric Calb2+ neurons drove secretion with minimal motility effect. The result is a cell-type-resolved vagal–enteric wiring diagram for gastric control.
Model & method
Mouse; intersectional genetic circuit dissection with afferent/efferent manipulation and secretion assays.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.033

MCT6 is an intestinal Lac-Phe exporter required for metformin-associated weight loss

Cell  Shuke Xiao & Jonathan Z Long
Key findings
The anorexigenic metabolite Lac-Phe requires export from cells to acquire systemic activity. The orphan transporter MCT6/SLC16A5 transports Lac-Phe in vitro, mediates its cellular efflux, and is required to maintain circulating Lac-Phe — particularly after metformin or high-intensity exercise. Mice with global or intestinal-epithelial MCT6 deletion resisted metformin-associated weight loss, a phenotype rescued by exogenous Lac-Phe, pinpointing transporter-mediated release as the step converting an intracellular metabolic state into a circulating effector. The finding links gut glycolytic flux to the systemic action of a feeding-suppressing metabolite relevant to appetite neurobiology.
Model & method
Mouse (global and tissue-specific knockouts) and cell assays; transport biochemistry with metabolic phenotyping.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.09.058

Ultrarapid decision-making in an avian brain

Science  Philipp Schmidbauer & Andreas Nieder
Key findings
Carrion crows performing a visual motion-discrimination task reported decisions in ~235 ms on average — roughly twice as fast as monkeys or humans — without sacrificing accuracy. Recordings from the pallial endbrain showed direction-selective neurons encoding the upcoming choice as early as ~70 ms post-stimulus and ~170 ms before the response, with ramping activity scaling with sensory evidence and converging at response onset. The data reveal evidence-accumulation decision circuitry in a non-mammalian brain operating at exceptional speed, consistent with evolutionary pressure for rapid visuomotor control.
Model & method
Carrion crows; psychophysics with single-neuron recordings from the pallial endbrain.
https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aed6459

α-synuclein pathology drives region-specific tau deposition

Alzheimers Dement  Seyed Hani Hojjati & Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative
Key findings
In 531 ADNI participants, α-synuclein-positive individuals showed significantly greater tau-PET deposition in basal ganglia, insula and medial temporal lobe, and in these regions tau burden correlated with more severe psychiatric symptoms and slower reaction times — associations present only in the α-synuclein-positive group. The pattern suggests co-pathology shapes where tau accumulates and maps onto features considered distinctive of synucleinopathy, offering a mechanistic bridge between Lewy and Alzheimer pathologies, albeit from cross-sectional imaging associations rather than causal manipulation.
Model & method
Human (ADNI); tau-PET compared by α-synuclein status with clinical correlations.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71932

TIA1 is required for microglial activation in tauopathy

Cell Rep  Chelsea J Webber & Benjamin Wolozin
Key findings
Crossing microglia-specific TIA1 conditional-knockout mice (Cx3cr1-Cre) with PS19 P301S tau mice, loss of microglial TIA1 suppressed microglial activation — with striking reductions in Tnf-α, Il-1β and sensome markers (Clec7a, Trem2, Itgax) — and, importantly, these reductions were accompanied by decreased tau pathology. Proteomics implicated TIA1 interaction with C1Q. The result positions the stress-granule RNA-binding protein TIA1 as a cell-intrinsic driver of microglial neuroinflammation whose removal is protective against tau pathology, nominating it as a glial therapeutic target (mouse causal evidence).
Model & method
Mouse (microglial TIA1 cKO × PS19 tauopathy); conditional genetics, cytokine/marker profiling, proteomics.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118199

Outdoor extreme heat-related dementia risk and the role of socioeconomic disparities

Alzheimers Dement  Eun Young Choi & Jennifer A Ailshire
Key findings
Using 20 years of US Health and Retirement Study data (N=28,512) with census-tract heat exposure and competing-risk models, individuals with a moderate burden of "Danger+" heat days (≥103 °F) had 14% higher dementia risk and those with a high burden 23% higher risk versus low exposure, with no association for milder "Extreme Caution" heat. Associations were stronger in socioeconomically disadvantaged areas with low air-conditioning prevalence. The study provides large-scale longitudinal human evidence that extreme-heat exposure is an environmental dementia risk factor modulated by structural inequities — an observational association requiring causal confirmation.
Model & method
Human longitudinal cohort; Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard models with geographic heat-exposure linkage.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71857

Latent class trajectory modeling identifies CSF protein signatures of motor progression in Parkinson's disease

Mov Disord  Shunyao Qi & Peigen Xiao
Key findings
In 396 early-PD PPMI participants, latent-class trajectory modeling of longitudinal MDS-UPDRS III scores separated distinct motor-progression subtypes, and LASSO-selected CSF proteomic signatures (Olink) discriminated rapid from slow progressors, with validation in 184 SOMAscan-profiled samples. By moving beyond a single average trajectory, the approach identifies candidate fluid biomarkers for prognostic stratification that could enrich trials for rapid progressors, though the signatures remain discovery-stage and need external clinical validation.
Model & method
Human (PPMI); latent-class trajectory modeling with CSF proteomics and machine-learning feature selection.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70589

Tau isoform mapping across tissues and biofluids reveals a signature distinguishing Alzheimer's disease from ALS

Nat Commun  Samir Abu-Rumeileh & Markus Otto
Key findings
Blood p-tau217 flags AD pathology but is confounded by peripheral "big" tau released from muscle, which is also elevated in ALS. Measuring serum brain-derived (BD-) tau and p-tau217 by direct immunoassay and after BD-tau immunoprecipitation (ALS n=139, AD n=101, controls n=75), plus targeted mass spectrometry of brain, spinal cord and muscle, the authors show that after BD-tau-IP, BD-tau and BD-p-tau217 were elevated in AD only, improving diagnostic specificity; mass spectrometry revealed a central-to-peripheral shift from BD-tau to big-tau predominance, with exclusively big p-tau217 in muscle. Selective detection of brain-derived tau species reduces peripheral confounding and sharpens blood AD biomarkers against ALS.
Model & method
Human serum and post-mortem tissue; immunoassays with brain-derived-tau immunoprecipitation and targeted mass spectrometry.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78390-3

Prevention of prion disease symptoms in mice with deep PrP lowering

bioRxiv  J. E. Gentile & E. V. Minikel
Key findings
A potent C16-conjugated PrP-lowering siRNA delivered into mouse CSF reduced whole-brain PrP to ~12% residual. Early chronic treatment kept prion-infected mice symptom-free through 500 days post-inoculation, suppressing prion replication ~100-fold and minimizing neuropathology; treating already-symptomatic mice markedly extended survival and cut seeding activity ~10-fold. Strains differing in response to modest (~50%) lowering showed indistinguishable replication kinetics but were both controlled by deep knockdown, and the siRNA durably lowered PrP in humanized mice and macaque brain/CSF. If it holds, this is the first demonstration that a therapeutic can prevent prion-disease symptoms, and it argues that depth of PrP lowering is decisive — but the evidence is preclinical.
Model & method
Mice (incl. humanized) and cynomolgus macaques; CSF-delivered C16-siRNA with survival, prion-replication and neuropathology readouts.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.22.761034

Splanchnic-spinal transmission of gut-initiated α-synucleinopathy

bioRxiv  B.-K. Jung & G. S. B. Suh
Key findings
Testing whether body-first Parkinson's pathology spreads by routes beyond the vagus, the authors combined intracolonic rotenone and gastroduodenal α-synuclein PFF models with pathway-specific surgical interruption. In the rotenone model, interrupting the prevertebral-ganglia/splanchnic-spinal axis attenuated behavioral deficits and α-synuclein pathology more strongly than vagotomy; in the PFF model both vagal and splanchnic-spinal routes contributed, with early pathology detected in peripheral structures of both, and spinal pathology corroborated in human PD tissue. The work nominates the splanchnic-spinal pathway as a key conduit for peripheral-to-central disease progression, broadening the vagus-centric body-first model (causal evidence is rodent; human data are observational).
Model & method
Mouse gut-to-brain models with surgical pathway interruption and anatomical mapping; human PD tissue corroboration.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.19.760019
Preprint · not peer reviewed

Somatic DNA-repeat expansions initiate distinct yet analogous changes in Huntington's disease cortical and striatal neurons

bioRxiv  S. Kashin & S. A. McCarroll
Key findings
Striatal neurons degenerate in HD as their HTT CAG tract expands somatically beyond ~150 repeats. Here cortical pyramidal neurons were found to undergo biologically analogous changes — the same sequence of pathological phases, with toxicity also commencing near 150 CAGs — yet then diverging in their downstream gene expression. A neuron's precise molecular identity, rather than its cortical layer or area, predicted the extent of somatic expansion, which varied up to 20-fold across pyramidal subtypes, and both cell classes shared HTT nuclear inclusions once expansions ran well beyond 150 CAGs. The data recast cortical and striatal degeneration as parallel consequences of the same somatic-expansion threshold rather than one region driving the other.
Model & method
Human HD post-mortem cortex and striatum; single-cell transcriptomics with somatic CAG-length measurement.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.09.688862
Preprint · not peer reviewed

Astrocytic ablation of truncated TrkB receptors reduces amyloid plaques and prevents synaptic loss in 5xFAD mice

bioRxiv  R. Bass & B. Xu
Key findings
Truncated TrkB (TrkB.T), expressed mainly in astrocytes, is thought to blunt BDNF–TrkB.FL signaling. Astrocyte-specific deletion of Ntrk2 at 5 weeks cut cortical TrkB.T by >80% without changing TrkB.FL, enlarged Bassoon+ presynaptic active zones, and reduced β-amyloid plaque burden by >60% across cortex, hippocampus and subiculum in 6-month-old 5xFAD mice while preventing loss of PSD-95+ synapses — notably without detectable change in micro- or astrogliosis. The result implies that relieving astrocytic suppression of BDNF signaling can lower amyloid and protect synapses, pointing to TrkB.T as a glial therapeutic target (mouse model, single AD genetic background).
Model & method
5xFAD mice; astrocyte-specific conditional knockout with plaque quantification and synaptic marker analysis.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.20.760443

Dissecting cellular and molecular networks of aging and senescence in the human hippocampus

bioRxiv  M. B. De Los Santos & H. Phatnani
Key findings
Profiling post-mortem human hippocampi across adulthood with spatial transcriptomics, single-nucleus RNA-seq and high-plex immunofluorescence, the authors resolved aging trajectories that were specific to cell type and anatomical layer — for example, inhibitory-neuron synaptic genes declined with age selectively in the CA4 layer. A microglial and neurovascular program enriched in females carried many senescence-associated genes including CDKN1A/p21, and imaging showed divergence between p16- and p21-defined senescence phenotypes. The study maps how anatomical context, cell type and sex jointly structure hippocampal aging and senescence, cautioning that single canonical senescence markers can disagree.
Model & method
Human post-mortem hippocampus; spatial transcriptomics, single-nucleus RNA-seq and high-plex immunofluorescence.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.23.761418
