1Antibodies against MLC1 found in patients with NMOSD-like disease mediate astrocytopathy in rodent models

Science Translational Medicine  Hoi Kiu Wong & Simone Mader
Why it matters
A substantial minority of patients in the NMOSD/MOGAD spectrum are seronegative for both AQP4 and MOG, leaving them without a target antigen or a mechanistic account. Screening 297 patients with inflammatory CNS autoimmune disease identified four with IgG against MLC1, a membrane protein whose extracellular epitopes are concentrated at astrocytic end feet — the same compartment AQP4 antibodies attack. Crucially the authors did not stop at association: a monoclonal MLC1 antibody induced astrocytopathy in mouse cerebellar slice cultures and in a rat encephalitis model, which converts MLC1 from a candidate marker into a plausibly pathogenic one. Four patients is a small denominator, so prevalence and clinical spectrum remain open.
Model & method
Human patient sera (n = 297 screened) with cell-based antibody assay; mouse cerebellar slice culture and rat encephalitis model for pathogenicity.
https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.ady0403

2Aging limits neuronal regeneration from glia in the mouse retina

PNAS  Jugasmita Deka & Levi Todd
Why it matters
Glia-to-neuron reprogramming is among the more credible regenerative strategies for neurodegeneration, but essentially the entire literature rests on young animals — while age is the dominant risk factor for the diseases it targets. Across three independent transcription-factor strategies, aged Müller glia reprogrammed consistently worse, and single-cell transcriptomics showed why: rather than activating progenitor programs, aged glia adopt reactive and inflammatory states, against a background of exacerbated neuroimmune response to injury. Dexamethasone partially restored neurogenesis, implicating the inflammatory milieu rather than an irreversible glial deficit. This is a direct warning about the translational gap in a field that mostly tests in young tissue.
Model & method
Aged mouse retina in vivo; three transcription-factor reprogramming strategies with single-cell transcriptomics and immunomodulation.
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2612369123

3Distinctive properties of the prion protein in the brain and retina in the amyloidosis associated with the PRNP F198S mutation

Acta Neuropathologica  Bernardino Ghetti & Gianluigi Zanusso
Why it matters
Two tissues from the same patients, carrying the same mutation, handle the same protein differently — which is a clean internal control on the question of what makes a deposit amyloid. In GSS F198S retina, PrP deposits were confined to the outer plexiform layer, lacked the tinctorial properties of amyloid, yet were still detergent-insoluble, assembled into large aggregates, and seeding-competent by RT-QuIC. The sharpest difference is proteolytic: the 8 kDa internal fragments constitutive in brain were absent from retina, though they could be generated experimentally with proteinase K, pointing to a tissue-specific processing step rather than a difference in the protein itself. Retinal PrP glycosylation was also more complex than control.
Model & method
Post-mortem brain and retina from PRNP F198S carriers in one pedigree; neuropathology, biochemical fractionation, RT-QuIC seeding assay.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03060-z

4Hormone replacement therapy and dementia risk among postmenopausal women: identifying responsive subgroups in the UK Biobank

Alzheimer's & Dementia  Steven Squires & Anne-Marie Minihane
Why it matters
The HRT-and-dementia literature is notoriously contradictory, and this analysis argues the contradictions come from averaging over subgroups that respond differently. Across 183,450 postmenopausal women and 2.43 million person-years, HRT use associated with lower all-cause dementia (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.84–0.96) — a modest overall effect that concentrated markedly in surgical menopause (0.74, 0.65–0.84), APOE ε4 carriers (0.87, 0.80–0.95), and women with lower lifetime endogenous estrogen exposure (0.84, 0.77–0.93), with initiation between 46 and 56 most favourable. The pattern is coherent with an estrogen-deficiency model. It remains observational, so healthy-user bias and confounding by indication are not excluded by design.
Model & method
Human prospective cohort (UK Biobank, n = 183,450, mean 13.3 years follow-up); multivariable Cox regression with stratification by menopause type, APOE ε4, and estrogen exposure.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71679

5EOAD-signature atrophy predicts dementia in early-onset MCI due to Alzheimer disease

Neurology  Thiago Paranhos & Alexandra Touroutoglou
Why it matters
Early-onset AD progresses heterogeneously and lacks the prognostic biomarkers established for late-onset disease, which complicates both counselling and trial enrolment. In 130 biomarker-confirmed EOAD patients at the MCI stage, baseline atrophy within a predominantly parieto-temporal EOAD signature predicted progression to dementia (HR 1.24 per 1-SD, 95% CI 1.13–1.37), and adding it to a model containing baseline clinical severity improved fit (ΔAIC = −4.5; likelihood-ratio p = 0.011). The incremental value over clinical severity is the operative result — structural MRI is already acquired in these patients, so the marginal cost of the marker is near zero. Effect size is moderate and the cohort is multisite natural-history rather than a trial population.
Model & method
Human multisite cohort (LEADS, n = 130 MCI due to EOAD, 97 controls); baseline structural MRI with Cox proportional hazards modelling.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218519

A 3D human retina model reveals a non-cell-autonomous and non-neuronal mechanism of photoreceptor loss in a lysosomal storage disorder

Science Translational Medicine  Jimin Han & Ruchira Singh
Key findings
Photoreceptor loss in CLN3 disease turns out to be driven from the neighbouring epithelium rather than the neuron itself. Building a 3D hPSC retina organoid–RPE model that reconstitutes the photoreceptor–RPE interface, the authors showed the common CLN3Δex7-8 mutation lowers acid ceramidase and sphingosine-1-phosphate and causes outer-segment loss, and that RPE dysfunction alone is sufficient to drive it. Findings were corroborated in CLN3 miniswine and in donor eyes from two patients, with retinal imaging in two living patients suggesting reduced RPE autofluorescence early in disease. Recombinant human acid ceramidase rescued photoreceptor degeneration in both the organoid model and miniswine eyes, making the enzyme a direct therapeutic candidate. Note the abstract states both a "non-cell-autonomous" mechanism in the title and a "cell autonomous role of the RPE" in the results — the consistent reading is that the defect is autonomous to RPE and non-autonomous with respect to photoreceptors.
Model & method
Human iPSC-derived retina organoid–RPE co-culture, CLN3 miniswine, and patient donor eyes; sphingolipid profiling with recombinant enzyme rescue.
https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.ady7616
Neurodegeneration & aging

An axon-intrinsic loop restricts nerve regeneration through axonal protein synthesis

Science Advances  Courtney N. Buchanan & Jeffery L. Twiss
Key findings
Regeneration failure here is not passive damage but an actively maintained brake. Axotomy raises axoplasmic Ca²⁺, which drives local translation of KHSRP, an RNA-binding protein that accelerates mRNA decay and slows regrowth; KHSRP stays elevated even after Ca²⁺ normalises by 16 hours. The mechanism sustaining it is a translational loop — axonal REG3A triggers ER Ca²⁺ release, activating PERK and eIF2α phosphorylation to keep Khsrp translation running. Depleting Reg3a damped growth-cone Ca²⁺ oscillations, lowered KHSRP, reduced retraction events, and accelerated sciatic nerve regeneration in vivo, which makes the loop a tractable target rather than a descriptive finding.
Model & method
Rodent sciatic nerve crush and cultured neurons; axoplasmic Ca²⁺ imaging, local translation assays, Reg3a depletion in vivo.
https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aed0049

Elucidating the neuropathological and molecular heterogeneity of amyloid beta and tau in Alzheimer's disease through machine learning and transcriptomic integration

Alzheimer's & Dementia  Kanhao Zhao & the ADNI and PREVENT-AD study groups
Key findings
Functional network changes in AD are hard to read because amyloid-driven effects sit on top of aging and non-amyloid biology. Using contrastive deep learning on resting-state fMRI from 289 older adults split by CSF amyloid status (A+ n = 129, A− n = 160), the authors separated A+-specific network dimensions from variation shared with A− individuals. The A+-specific signature localised to right superior temporal and anterior cingulate cortex, tracked attention and memory, and showed transcriptomic enrichment for synaptic dysfunction and glial activity; the shared dimensions instead involved language regions and generic aging pathways. The separation is the contribution — whether it generalises beyond these cohorts is untested.
Model & method
Human resting-state fMRI (n = 289) stratified by CSF amyloid; contrastive graph deep learning with transcriptomic enrichment.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71757

Longitudinal effects of physical activity on sleep in association with hippocampal-amygdala subfield structure and functional connectivity in Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer's & Dementia  Ravi Dadsena & Alexa Haeger
Key findings
In a randomised 6-month multimodal exercise intervention (n = 46 on the AD continuum; 25 exercise, 21 psychoeducation) followed to 18 months, the intervention group showed increased hippocampus–amygdala connectivity and attenuated subfield atrophy immediately after the intervention. A year later both groups had progressed in cognitive decline and hippocampal atrophy, so the structural benefit did not persist once the intervention stopped — the honest reading is short-term preservation rather than disease modification. Subfield volumes and connectivity tracked sleep, activity, and cognitive measures. Small n for a randomised imaging trial.
Model & method
Human RCT (Dementia-MOVE, n = 46); high-resolution T1 and resting-state fMRI with hippocampal/amygdala subfield segmentation, actigraphy and PSQI.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71651

Identification of chemicals targeting dementia genes and pathways in the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database

Alzheimer's & Dementia  Scarlet Cockell & Kelly M. Bakulski
Key findings
A systematic screen of 1,008 chemicals against dementia gene sets from Agora and CTD across nine dementia-related pathways found 817 (81.1%) enriched for at least one pathway and 71 for all nine, including benzo(a)pyrene, ethanol, paraquat, and particulate matter. Alzheimer's-specific enrichment implicated 295 chemicals (sodium arsenite, PRR = 57.9) and tauopathy enrichment 305 (bisphenol A, PRR = 37.7). The very high hit rate is itself informative and cautionary: enrichment in a curated literature database reflects what has been studied as much as what is biologically true, so this is best read as hypothesis generation for exposure epidemiology, not evidence that four-fifths of tested chemicals cause dementia.
Model & method
Computational; Fisher exact tests and proportional reporting ratios over curated chemical–gene interactions, FDR < 1 × 10⁻⁶.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71775

De-escalating and discontinuing immunotherapies in patients with NMOSD and MOGAD

Neurology  Yael Hacohen & Romain Marignier
Key findings
Effective targeted immunotherapy has turned relapse prevention into a question of how long to continue treating, and this review argues the answer differs sharply by disease. In AQP4-Ab NMOSD, relapses are severe and reactivation risk after withdrawal is high across observational studies, so discontinuation is never recommended, though de-escalation has succeeded in selected patients with prolonged remission. MOGAD is heterogeneous — many children are monophasic and adult relapse risk declines over years — so de-escalation of anti-CD20 and IVIG is reasonable, and cohort data suggest discontinuation after 2–5 years of remission may be feasible, particularly in those who become MOG-IgG seronegative. Seronegative NMOSD has essentially no evidence base; the suggested 5-year threshold rests on expert opinion. MRI, OCT, and fluid biomarkers are candidates for detecting early recurrence but none is validated for routine use.
Model & method
Narrative review of observational cohorts and registry data; no primary data.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218482

Syndecan-4 exerts canonical heparan sulfate-dependent and noncanonical heparan sulfate-independent functions that regulate Aβ amyloid homeostasis

bioRxiv  K. H. Shim & T. Golde
Key findings
Heparan sulfate proteoglycans are conventionally treated as amyloid accelerants, and Syndecan-4 — the most upregulated transmembrane HSPG in AD brain and in amyloid-depositing mice — behaves that way in part: it associates spatially with deposits by proximity ligation, and its ectodomain accelerates Aβ fibril formation in an HS-dependent manner in vitro. The unexpected result runs the other way. Sdc4 overexpression reduced Aβ production in CHO cells, hiPSC-derived neurons, and mouse organotypic brain slices, accompanied by marked decreases in sAPP and C83 and accumulation of immature APP — an HS-independent effect on APP processing rather than on aggregation. Two opposing activities in one protein complicate any simple therapeutic direction; the in vivo CRND8 arm will determine which dominates.
Model & method
CHO cells, hiPSC-derived neurons, mouse organotypic brain slice cultures, and CRND8 mice; proximity ligation, in vitro aggregation, structure-function overexpression.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.743990

Context-dependent regulatory networks connect Alzheimer's disease genetics to microglial inflammatory responses

bioRxiv  T.-T. Fu & L. Hou
Key findings
AD risk variants concentrate in microglial regulatory elements, but which variant acts through which element in which activation state has been largely unresolved. The authors build context-dependent epigenomic networks from bulk and single-nucleus ATAC-seq spanning inflammatory, genetic-perturbation, and disease contexts, using TF footprinting and graph embedding to predict circuits in unseen contexts. Applied to a SORL1-marked inflammatory microglial state that expands with AD progression, the framework annotates risk variants at the SORL1 locus and identifies variants associated with cellular-state abundance across donors. It also nominates ZBTB14, whose inflammation-associated program links variant-bearing regulatory elements to target genes, with donor-level footprint activity negatively associated with AD pathology. Predictive network inference, not experimental validation of the circuits.
Model & method
Human bulk and single-nucleus ATAC-seq across microglial contexts; TF footprinting with graph-embedding network inference.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.24.746572

Development and validation of highly selective monoclonal antibodies for the detection of huntingtin neoepitopes

bioRxiv  E. M. Doherty & I. Munoz-Sanjuan
Key findings
Pathogenic N-terminal huntingtin fragments are central to HD pathology, yet their detection has depended on a thin antibody repertoire with reproducibility problems. Recombinant rabbit monoclonals were raised against reciprocal neoepitopes flanking the HTT exon1/exon2 junction at P90 and K91. The P90 clones (1B12, 11G2) recognise the fragment-length-selective C-terminal neoepitope with no detectable binding to full-length huntingtin, and — pointedly — a side-by-side comparison of the widely used MW8 antibody from two sources revealed measurable lot-to-lot drift in fragment selectivity that the sequence-defined recombinants did not show. The K91 antibody addresses a genuinely open question: P90-positive fragments can arise from HTT1a mis-splicing, whereas the reciprocal K91 neoepitope would require site-specific proteolysis, a mechanism previously untestable for want of a reagent. A reagent paper, but one that bears on a mechanistic dispute.
Model & method
Recombinant rabbit monoclonal antibody generation with epitope-specificity characterisation against huntingtin fragments and full-length protein.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.746138

Glycogen synthase kinase-3β regulates cellular prion protein levels

bioRxiv  K. S. Beauchemin & S. Supattapone
Key findings
PrPC is the obligate substrate for prion conversion and a receptor for Aβ oligomers, so lowering it is attractive on two fronts, but its regulation is poorly mapped. Following up a whole-genome knockout screen, the authors show that siRNA knockdown or CRISPR knockout of Gsk3b — but not Gsk3a — reduces both surface and total PrPC, with PrPC the 60th most reduced protein out of 7,227 detected by whole-cell mass spectrometry. Two clinical-grade GSK-3 inhibitors, laduviglusib and AZD-1080, lowered PrPC in mouse CAD5 and human BE(2)-C cells in both undifferentiated and differentiated states, and reduced PrPSc across three rodent prion strains. Cycloheximide experiments place the mechanism post-translationally. Cell models only; no in vivo prion challenge yet.
Model & method
Mouse CAD5 and human BE(2)-C cells; siRNA and CRISPR knockout, whole-cell mass spectrometry, pharmacological GSK-3 inhibition in prion-infected cells.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.746199

Movement-responsive deep brain stimulation reinforces motor circuits in Parkinson's disease

medRxiv  D. J. Lawrence & S. J. Little
Key findings
Conventional DBS delivers constant stimulation regardless of what the patient is doing. In four patients with sensing-enabled systems, stimulation was gated on volitional movement decoded from cortical activity. During structured tasks this improved average forearm speed and blunted the progressive bradykinetic slowing seen under constant-amplitude DBS, alongside a cumulative rise in sensorimotor cortical beta activity and connectivity. In unconstrained daily activity, bradykinesia severity fell and continued improving over hours of therapy, then gradually reversed on switching back to constant stimulation — a time course suggesting circuit reinforcement rather than an immediate stimulation effect. Four patients, so this is a mechanism-and-feasibility demonstration, not an efficacy estimate.
Model & method
Human, four patients with sensing-enabled DBS; cortically decoded movement-responsive stimulation with structured motor tasks and at-home monitoring.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.20.26360021
