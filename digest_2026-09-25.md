### A pericyte-to-myofibroblast transition links APOE4 to cerebrovascular degeneration
**Braxton R Schuldt & Joel W Blanchard | Cell**

**Why it matters:** APOE4 is the strongest genetic risk factor for Alzheimer's disease and is tied to cerebrovascular degeneration, but the cellular route has been unclear. Building a single-cell transcriptomic atlas of human brain vasculature, the authors find that APOE4 carriers lose pericytes and gain a myofibroblast-like population co-expressing contractile and extracellular-matrix genes; these transitioned cells secrete fibronectin that promotes vascular amyloid deposition. Elevated TGF-β signaling drives the switch, and TGF-β inhibition restored pericyte coverage and lowered vascular fibrosis and amyloid to APOE3 levels, nominating a targetable mechanism. Causal manipulation is mouse and cell-based; the human data are cross-sectional and associative.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human brain vascular single-cell transcriptomic atlas with immunostaining validation, plus APOE4 mouse models and TGF-β pathway perturbation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.058

### Spatiotemporal characterization of Alzheimer disease pathology in living human brain tissue
**Aram Aslanyan & Ross W Paterson | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** AD pathology is almost always studied postmortem or indirectly through fluid and imaging biomarkers. Here small cortical biopsies taken during ventriculoperitoneal shunt surgery for suspected normal-pressure hydrocephalus (n=18) were used to examine amyloid and tau in living tissue, alongside matched ventricular and lumbar CSF and postmortem control brain. Amyloid plaques were present in 9 of 18 biopsies while mature tau tangles were rare, and MALDI mass-spectrometry imaging mapped amyloid species, opening a window on protein turnover during life. The cohort is small and confined to a surgical population, so generalization to sporadic AD is limited.

**Model/system & primary method:** Living human cortical biopsies from shunt surgery with matched CSF and postmortem tissue; MALDI imaging mass spectrometry and protein-turnover analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76494-4

### TMEM59 regulates microglial Aβ phagocytosis in a TREM2-dependent manner
**Jian Meng & Yun-Wu Zhang | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** Microglial clearance of amyloid-β is central to AD, but its molecular brakes are poorly mapped. Microglia-specific deletion of TMEM59 in 5xFAD mice attenuated cognitive deficits, plaque burden and synapse loss, with single-cell RNA-seq linking the benefit to enhanced, TREM2-dependent phagocytosis—implying TMEM59 normally restrains amyloid uptake. To translate this, the authors built a chaperone-mediated-autophagy-targeting chimera (CMATAC) peptide that degrades TMEM59 and reported therapeutic efficacy, offering a degrader-based strategy for Aβ amyloidosis. Evidence is entirely mouse and cell-based.

**Model/system & primary method:** 5xFAD mice with microglia-specific TMEM59 deletion; single-cell RNA-seq and a CMATAC degrader peptide.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71873

### Dynamic changes in plasma biomarkers of Alzheimer's disease in patients treated with lecanemab
**Rui Mu & Carlos Cruchaga | Lancet Neurol**

**Why it matters:** Anti-amyloid antibodies slow decline in early AD, yet how blood markers move during treatment is largely unmapped. In a longitudinal real-world cohort of patients receiving biweekly lecanemab, the authors tracked a panel of 130 plasma proteins and related their changes to conversion to amyloid-PET-negative status and to cognitive trajectories. The design surfaces candidate response and neurodegeneration markers that shift on therapy, potentially enabling blood-based monitoring of amyloid clearance. As an observational clinical-care cohort rather than a randomized trial, treatment effects are not isolated from selection and confounding.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human longitudinal cohort on lecanemab; 130-protein plasma proteomic panel with amyloid-PET and cognitive endpoints.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00284-X

### Neural evidence that dogs segment speech with a humanlike consonant bias
**Boglárka Morvai & Attila Andics | Science**

**Why it matters:** Humans, unlike nonhuman primates, weight consonants over vowels when segmenting speech despite vowels' greater acoustic salience. Using noninvasive EEG in both humans and dogs, the authors found enhanced word-level neural tracking—via intertrial coherence and event-related potentials—for consonant-structured over vowel-structured streams in both species. This indicates that a consonant bias, previously thought human-specific, can emerge in a non-speaking species exposed to human speech, suggesting linguistic regularity–based processing biases are not uniquely human. The finding is a neural-entrainment signature rather than a behavioral demonstration of word learning.

**Model/system & primary method:** Noninvasive EEG in dogs and humans; neural entrainment (intertrial coherence) and ERP measures on consonant- vs vowel-structured speech streams.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adw7709

### Reconciling global collaboration and data sovereignty in neuroscience
**Kimberly L Ray & Franco Pestilli | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** This perspective argues that intensifying national controls on data collection, use and cross-border transfer—driven by the strategic value of AI-ready data—are colliding with neuroscience's dependence on large, internationally distributed brain datasets. As population-scale neuroimaging, neurotechnology and computational modeling raise the value of neural data, the authors contend that fragmented governance risks narrowing representation, slowing innovation and entrenching global inequities in brain-health research, and they call for an international coordinating body for neuroscience data governance. This is a policy argument, not empirical research.

**Model/system & primary method:** Perspective/review on international neuroscience data governance.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02468-w

### An in-body networking system for communication between wearable and implantable therapeutics
**Ramy Ghanim & Alex Abramson | Science**

**Key findings:** The authors engineered a communication platform that uses body tissue as a conductive medium to pass signals among implants and wearables across epidermal, subcutaneous, intraperitoneal and gastrointestinal spaces. Devices emit transient electric fields that selectively switch on target devices' transistor circuits; implants are syringe-injectable, draw negligible power while listening, and achieve more than tenfold greater tissue-communication coverage than Bluetooth. In rats, coordinated full-body networks of sensors and neural interfaces enabled wireless dual-limb motor control, a step toward closed-loop bioelectronic therapies with neural interfaces. Demonstrations are in rodents.

**Model/system & primary method:** Bioelectronic implants/wearables using tissue as a conductive medium; in vivo rat networks with neural interfaces for wireless motor control.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adz5300

## NEURODEGENERATION & AGING — TOP 5

### Aberrant phase separation from a rare ABI3 mutation drives microglial dysfunction and Alzheimer's risk
**Shengnan Li & Lili Cui | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** The ABI3 S209F variant is a recognized AD risk factor of unclear mechanism. Across APP/PS1 and 5xFAD mice with microglia-specific ABI3 knockdown or wild-type/S209F expression, plus primary microglia and HMC3 cells, the authors show the mutation alters liquid-liquid phase separation properties of ABI3, linking aberrant condensate behavior to microglial dysfunction and disease pathology. This ties a phase-separation defect in a specific microglial protein to inherited AD risk. Causal evidence is mouse- and cell-based.

**Model/system & primary method:** APP/PS1 and 5xFAD mice with microglia-specific ABI3 manipulation; primary microglia, HMC3 cells and recombinant-protein LLPS assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71812

### A cell-type-resolved meta-analysis reveals glial DNA methylation changes associated with aging and Alzheimer's disease
**Uchit Bhaskar & Melanie A Carless | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Epigenome-wide studies implicate DNA methylation in AD but rarely resolve glial contributions. Pooling methylation data across 13 cohorts and using in silico cell-type deconvolution, the authors identify pronounced age-associated methylation signatures in prefrontal-cortex astrocytes and AD-associated changes across glial cell types, refining which epigenetic shifts track biological aging versus disease. Because cell-type signals are computationally deconvolved from bulk data rather than measured in sorted cells, the assignments are inferential.

**Model/system & primary method:** Meta-analysis of human DNA-methylation data across 13 cohorts with in silico glial cell-type deconvolution.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71864

### Platelet count polygenic scores may modify the effect of aspirin in primary prevention of dementia
**Peter D Fransquet & Paul Lacaze | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In the ASPREE randomized trial of aspirin in older adults (n=13,541), the authors screened thousands of polygenic risk scores for interactions with aspirin on dementia and major bleeding. Platelet-related polygenic scores were strongly enriched among interactions (18 of 112 nominally significant hits; enrichment OR ≈54.7), suggesting genetically defined platelet subgroups might differ in whether aspirin affects dementia risk. This is a hypothesis-generating pharmacogenomic interaction analysis, not evidence that aspirin prevents dementia in any subgroup.

**Model/system & primary method:** Secondary analysis of the ASPREE RCT; aspirin × polygenic-score interaction testing with Cox models.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71817

### Personalized single-cell transcriptomics reveals molecular diversity in Alzheimer's disease
**Pramod Bharadwaj Chandrashekar & Daifeng Wang | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Applying a knowledge-guided graph neural network to population-scale single-nucleus RNA-seq spanning over 1,900 brains across cohorts, the authors learn per-donor functional-genomic representations that classify AD phenotypes and resolve molecularly defined patient subpopulations, capturing donor-level cell-type interactions and gene-regulatory networks. The framework offers a route to personalized molecular stratification of AD from brain transcriptomes. Findings rest on postmortem cross-sectional tissue, so the subtypes are molecular rather than clinically validated trajectories.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem single-nucleus RNA-seq (>1,900 brains); knowledge-guided graph neural network for donor-level functional genomics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72310-1

### Regulation of protein kinase pathways in a primary neuron model of anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis
**Yuyoung Joo & David R Benavides | J Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Anti-NMDAR encephalitis, the most common autoimmune encephalitis, is driven by antibodies that cross-link and internalize the GluN1 subunit, but the downstream signaling is unclear. Using a human GluN1 monoclonal antibody (5F5) in primary hippocampal neurons, the authors map protein-kinase pathway changes engaged when NMDARs are antibody-targeted, linking receptor internalization to specific intracellular signaling rather than to loss of surface receptors alone. Evidence is from a reductionist primary-neuron model with a single monoclonal antibody.

**Model/system & primary method:** Primary hippocampal neurons with a human GluN1 monoclonal antibody; protein-kinase pathway and receptor-trafficking assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0708-25.2026
