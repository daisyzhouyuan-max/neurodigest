### Generational gains in cognitive capacity and dementia incidence rates in Western countries
**Anders M Fjell & Kristine B Walhovd | Nat Med**

**Why it matters:** Dementia incidence has fallen ~13% per decade in Europe and the US, but it was unclear whether later-born cohorts start old age cognitively better off, decline more slowly, or both. Pooling ~783,000 episodic-memory assessments from ~219,000 people across five longitudinal cohorts followed up to 30 years, the authors find a "double dividend": later-born generations both enter old age with higher memory and decline more slowly, an advantage large enough to plausibly account for the observed drop in incidence. The gain concentrates among the fastest decliners and is mirrored by slower hippocampal atrophy and lower rates of clinical impairment. Because baseline memory is set largely in early life while decline rate is shaped in adulthood, the data argue for prevention spanning the whole lifespan rather than late-life intervention alone.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; harmonized multi-cohort longitudinal mixed-effects modeling of episodic memory with linked MRI hippocampal volumetry (observational, generational comparison).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04759-y

### Brain border-associated macrophages rhythmically scavenge amyloid-β and regulate vascular amyloid pathology
**Helena J Barr & Beth Stevens | Immunity**

**Why it matters:** Circadian disruption is an epidemiological risk factor for Alzheimer's disease, but a cellular link to amyloid handling has been missing. Single-cell RNA-seq of the brain immune compartment across the day–night cycle identifies brain border-associated macrophages (BAMs) as strongly rhythmic, with the rest phase a dedicated window of enhanced endocytosis and uptake of fluid-borne Aβ. The rhythm depends on the clock gene Bmal1 and the scavenger receptor CD206 and degrades with age; BAM-specific Bmal1 deletion worsened perivascular and leptomeningeal Aβ burden in an AD mouse model. This nominates timed border-macrophage scavenging as a mechanism by which circadian disruption could promote cerebral amyloid angiopathy.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse (AD model + conditional Bmal1 deletion in BAMs); single-cell RNA-seq across circadian time, in vivo uptake assays, amyloid quantification.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2026.10.014

### Microglia mediate motor memory decay through activity-dependent phagocytosis of myelin sheaths
**Zhechun Hu & Lang Wang | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Learning-induced myelination helps consolidate motor memories, but why those memories fade has been unclear. Here motor memory decay tracks the loss of memory-associated myelin sheaths to microglial phagocytosis. Mechanistically, reduced activity of motor-memory engram cells lowers oligodendrocyte CD47 — a "don't-eat-me" signal — licensing microglial engulfment of their myelin. Oligodendrocyte-specific CD47 knockout accelerated both myelin loss and memory decay, while CD47 overexpression protected both; microglia engulfed myelin and synapses through independent routes. The work frames forgetting as an active, activity-gated glial process and, because many memory types require myelination, suggests a broadly relevant mechanism.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse; motor learning paradigms with engram labeling, cell-type-specific CD47 gain/loss of function, and imaging of microglial myelin phagocytosis (causal in mouse).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02505-8

### Dual augmentation of STMN2 and GRN delays disease progression through coordinated axonal and neuroimmune support in ALS models
**Chen Liang & Fei Teng | Cell Rep**

**Why it matters:** Loss of stathmin-2 (STMN2) is a direct consequence of TDP-43 dysfunction and drives axonal degeneration in ALS, while progranulin (GRN) supports lysosomal function and restrains neuroinflammation. A combined gene therapy restoring both factors extended survival, preserved motor performance, and maintained neuromuscular integrity in TDP-43^A315T mice, and promoted axonal regeneration in human iPSC-derived motor neurons. Mechanistically the dual therapy restored axonal/synaptic programs in motor neurons while upregulating lysosomal pathways in microglia and support genes in astrocytes, indicating coordinated neuronal and glial rebalancing. Evidence is preclinical (mouse survival plus human iPSC neurons), but the two-target logic addresses complementary ALS pathologies.

**Model/system & primary method:** TDP-43^A315T transgenic mice and human iPSC-derived motor neurons; dual-gene (STMN2 + GRN) augmentation with survival, behavioral, and transcriptomic readouts.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118305

### A cell-type-resolved proteomic atlas reveals intra- and intercellular signaling in Alzheimer's disease
**Xue Zhang & Junmin Peng | Neuron**

**Key findings:** Deep proteomics of sorted or proximity-labeled microglia, astrocytes, OPCs, and neurons from two AD mouse models (5xFAD, AppNL-G-F) across ages quantified 13,411 proteins and 3,028 differentially abundant proteins — most invisible to bulk proteomics. ~30% of proteomic changes were discordant with transcriptomics, underscoring the value of protein-level measurement. Cell-type networks nominated hub and cross-cell-type signaling proteins conserved in human AD brain, including pleiotrophin (PTN), which is transcribed in astrocytes but accumulates in microglia; recombinant PTN activated human induced microglia, supporting a functional astrocyte-to-microglia signaling axis.

**Model/system & primary method:** AD mouse models (5xFAD, AppNL-G-F) with human AD cross-validation; cell-type-resolved mass-spectrometry proteomics, network analysis, iMG functional assay.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.019

### Pericyte PDGFR-β deficiency contributes to Aβ pathology through impaired glymphatic transport and altered AQP-4 localization
**Hai-Chao Chen & Yu-Hong Jing | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Pericyte PDGFR-β signaling is tied here to glymphatic clearance. Both APP/PS1 and Pdgfr-β^+/− mice showed pericyte loss and glymphatic dysfunction; Pdgfr-β^+/− mice additionally lost perivascular AQP-4 polarization and basement-membrane laminin-α2/β1. Pericyte-derived factors promoted astrocytic AQP-4 in vitro, and the AQP-4 facilitator TGN-073 partially restored CSF tracer distribution. Crossing the two models (Pdgfr-β^+/−/APP/PS1) exacerbated amyloid pathology, positioning pericyte PDGFR-β as an upstream regulator of perivascular waste clearance and a candidate target. Evidence is mouse-model based.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse (APP/PS1, Pdgfr-β^+/−, crosses) plus astrocyte culture; CSF tracer glymphatic imaging, deep cervical lymph node ligation, AQP-4 immunolocalization, pharmacological rescue.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71991

### Ten protein panel predicts ALS from plasma with high accuracy
**Woo Jae Kim & Eleanor V Thomas | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Plasma from 632 ALS patients and 160 controls was profiled on NULISA CNS and Inflammation panels and modeled with gradient boosting and SHAP, yielding a 10-protein panel (NEFL, FOLR1, pTau-181, TREM1, VEGFA, NEFH, CALCA, NRGN, LAMP3, PTX3) that reached balanced accuracy 98.3% (AUC 0.992) in an independent validation cohort (144 ALS, 37 controls). Performance was driven by the proteomic signature rather than clinical variables and did not depend on neurofilament light alone, supporting a multiplexed blood screen beyond single-marker approaches. As a case–control classification study, prospective and early-stage diagnostic performance remain to be established.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human plasma (>750 ALS cases across discovery and validation); multiplexed proximity proteomics with XGBoost/SHAP and nested cross-validation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78766-5

### Intranasal delivery of a ROS-activatable myeloperoxidase inhibitor alleviates Alzheimer's-like symptoms and pathology
**Jing Wang & John W Chen | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** Genetic deletion of myeloperoxidase (MPO) — an oxidative-inflammation enzyme released by activated microglia — improved memory and reduced amyloid in 5XFAD mice, validating the target. The authors then built LERAMI, a liposome-encapsulated MPO inhibitor released on demand by a ROS-sensitive linker in oxidative microenvironments, delivered intranasally. LERAMI improved cognition, reduced microglial activation and oxidative DNA damage, preserved synaptic and neuronal density, lowered amyloid burden, and reprogrammed the hippocampal transcriptome toward synaptic-plasticity pathways. The strategy couples target validation with a brain-targeted, condition-activated delivery approach; evidence is mouse-only.

**Model/system & primary method:** 5XFAD mice (plus MPO knockout); ROS-activatable liposomal MPO inhibitor, intranasal delivery, behavior, histopathology, hippocampal RNA-seq.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2603280123

### Neuroimmune transcriptional signatures across repeat expansion ataxias in the human brain
**Zhongbo Chen & Mina Ryten | Acta Neuropathol**

**Key findings:** Paired cerebellar and frontal-cortex RNA-seq from 23 donors with repeat-expansion ataxias (SCA1/2/6, Friedreich's ataxia, and undiagnosed cases) versus 22 matched controls, with single-nucleus validation, revealed extensive transcriptional change extending beyond the cerebellum to frontal cortex. Across genetic subtypes, immune and inflammatory pathways were consistently activated — localized by snRNA-seq to non-neuronal cells (microglia, astrocytes) — alongside downregulation of neuronal/synaptic programs and relative glial expansion with neuronal loss. Shared signatures spanned molecularly diagnosed and undiagnosed cases, nominating neuroinflammation as a convergent, potentially targetable feature of these otherwise heterogeneous neurodegenerative disorders.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem brain (two brain banks); bulk and single-nucleus RNA-seq across regions and ataxia subtypes.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03115-1

### APOE ε4 carriership status enhances age-related changes in Alzheimer's disease plasma biomarker concentrations
**Rebecca Z Rousset & Charlotte E Teunissen | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** In 5,913 population-based participants aged 30–80 (Netherlands Twin Register), APOE ε4 carriage — but not an APOE-excluded AD polygenic score — steepened age trajectories of AD plasma biomarkers: Aβ-42/40 decline accelerated after age 50, and p-tau217 and GFAP rises steepened after ages 55 and 60, respectively, versus non-carriers. A separate longitudinal cohort (EMIF-AD) confirmed that ε4-associated p-tau217 change reflects AD pathology. The results map when preclinical amyloid-related biomarker shifts emerge by genotype and reinforce p-tau217 as an early detection marker, with cross-sectional age modeling as the main design caveat.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human population cohort plus longitudinal validation; plasma p-tau217, Aβ-42/40, NfL, GFAP with gene × age × sex interaction modeling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78723-2

### In silico designed small molecule targets misfolded TDP-43 to rescue function and ALS pathology
**M Kokes & V Mathur | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The authors report ACE-339, an orally bioavailable small molecule discovered via a machine-learning platform that directly engages aggregated TDP-43. In vitro it interacts with TDP-43 hexamers and disaggregates recombinant protein; in cells it rescues both gain-of-function (cytoplasmic aggregation) and loss-of-function (mis-splicing, synaptic activity) phenotypes; and it reportedly reverses disease-associated phenotypes in two independent mouse models of TDP-43 pathology. If the disaggregation mechanism and in vivo rescue hold under peer review, a direct TDP-43 disaggregator would address a largely undruggable driver common to ALS, FTD, and a subset of AD. Claims rest on preprint data from an industrial discovery program; target engagement specificity and translatability remain to be independently confirmed.

**Model/system & primary method:** Recombinant protein, cell lines, and two mouse models of TDP-43 proteinopathy; ML-guided small-molecule discovery with biochemical disaggregation and splicing/behavioral readouts.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.14.766688

### Human Endogenous Retrovirus K RNA triggers cytoplasmic TDP-43 aggregation by initiating pathological nuclear condensates
**Y-H Chang & J Dubnau | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Using CRISPR-engineered cells expressing Halo-tagged TDP-43 at endogenous levels, the authors propose that HERV-K RNA nucleates TDP-43 proteinopathy. HERV-K activation drove cytoplasmic TDP-43 aggregation and nuclear loss of function (aberrant cryptic exon inclusion), but was preceded by phosphorylated TDP-43 condensates in the nucleus ("PETANS"), from which cytoplasmic inclusions appeared to originate. A minimal UG-rich HERV-K element was sufficient to induce the cascade. The model inverts the usual cytoplasm-first view, placing an endogenous retroviral RNA upstream as a nuclear nucleator. Findings are cell-based and await validation in neurons and patient tissue.

**Model/system & primary method:** CRISPR-engineered human cell lines; super-resolution and live-cell imaging, cryptic-exon splicing assays, structure–function mapping of HERV-K RNA.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.16.767080

### A harmonized functional reference framework for GBA1
**S Glendinning & E Sammler | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** A multiplexed assay of variant effect (MAVE) measured lysosomal GCase activity for >10,400 GBA1 variants, covering 97% of possible single-amino-acid substitutions. The atlas recapitulated known structural determinants, agreed with expert pathogenicity calls, and reclassified 94 of 186 variants of uncertain significance as functionally reduced. Integration with the Global PD Genetics Program linked greater functional impairment to earlier Parkinson's onset and higher odds ratios, and stable-line assays validated subtle deficits near wild-type, including known PD risk alleles. This provides a quantitative variant-to-function map to aid interpretation in Gaucher disease and PD, pending peer review and clinical-grade validation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Deep mutational scanning in cells (lysosome-specific GCase activity) with stable-line validation and PD genetics integration.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.15.766795

### A large-scale multi-omics study of cortical miRNA dysregulation in Alzheimer's disease
**E Pishva & K Lunnon | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Integrating cortical small-RNA-seq from seven datasets across six post-mortem cohorts (1,738 individuals) with transcriptomic, proteomic, genetic, and single-nucleus data, the authors identified 16 microRNAs associated with advanced neurofibrillary-tangle pathology, with miR-132-3p significant at both intermediate and advanced stages. Five miRNAs (including the miR-132/212 family and miR-146b-5p) showed evidence of mediating mRNA/protein–tau associations, and network analysis linked miR-132/212 to opposing neuronal/synaptic versus oligodendrocyte/myelination programs, with mediated targets enriched in astrocytes. The work prioritizes a compact set of cortical miRNAs and glial programs for mechanistic follow-up. Associations are from post-mortem human cortex (correlational).

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem cortex multi-omics meta-analysis; miRNA–mRNA–protein mediation and co-expression network analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.16.767087

### Rest promotes microglial clearance of degenerating neurons
**Z Xiang & Y U Liu | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Longitudinal two-photon imaging in mice with cytosolic TDP-43 proteinopathy showed microglia remove neuronal corpses through an ordered reach–approach–isolate–digest sequence, with clearance higher in the light (rest) phase and lower in the dark (active) phase. The pattern reversed with light–dark inversion and was blunted by sleep disruption. Chemogenetic inhibition of locus coeruleus noradrenergic neurons increased daytime engulfment, and β2-adrenergic (ADRB2) activation/inhibition suppressed/enhanced clearance, identifying noradrenergic signaling as a brake — though sustained microglial ADRB2 deletion impaired clearance, indicating a more complex chronic role. The findings tie a core neuroimmune maintenance function to the rest–activity cycle. Mouse, preprint.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse (cytosolic TDP-43 proteinopathy); in vivo longitudinal two-photon imaging, circadian manipulation, chemogenetics, ADRB2 pharmacology and deletion.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.16.766874
