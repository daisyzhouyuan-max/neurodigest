### UNC13A splice correction rescues synaptic dysfunction induced by TDP-43 loss
**Matthew J Keuss & Pietro Fratta | Nature**

**Why it matters:** TDP-43 nuclear loss drives cryptic splicing in hundreds of transcripts across ALS and FTD, but its functional consequence for synapses had been unclear. The authors define a predegenerative state in which neurons stay structurally intact yet lose synaptic communication, and show that correcting a *single* cryptic splicing event in UNC13A is sufficient to reverse it. Splice-switching antisense oligonucleotides restored spontaneous and evoked vesicle release, per-synapse release probability and network synchrony in human cortical and spinal motor neurons and a humanized mouse. This nominates UNC13A splice correction as a mechanistically defined, genetically validated target relevant to ~97% of ALS and ~50% of FTD with TDP-43 pathology.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived cortical and spinal motor neurons and a humanized UNC13A mouse; splice-switching ASOs with electrophysiology and synaptic-release imaging.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11256-2

### The blood-brain barrier imports diverse neuroprotective proteins through pathways impaired early in Alzheimer's disease models
**Haoyue Zhou & Andrew C Yang | Cell**

**Why it matters:** Using bioorthogonal proteomics, the authors recast the BBB as a selective importer rather than only a barrier: the healthy barrier samples 191 plasma proteins and admits 92 into parenchyma, homing to specific cell types. APOA1 crosses via SR-B1 and engages astrocytic ABCA1 (an AD risk gene) to drive cholesterol efflux supporting synapse integrity. Early-stage 5xFAD mice show selective transport remodeling — reduced APOA1 and increased SERPINA1 uptake — distinct from nonspecific leakage, and hepatic APOA1 overexpression restored brain APOA1 and rescued astrocytic, synaptic and behavioral deficits.

**Model/system & primary method:** 5xFAD mice and hepatic APOA1 overexpression; in vivo bioorthogonal (cell-type-resolved) proteomics of blood-to-brain protein transport.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.10.039

### Sleep-like slow waves during wakefulness are associated with hallucinations and poorer cognition in Parkinson's disease
**Fosco Bernasconi & Olaf Blanke | Science Translational Medicine**

**Why it matters:** Sleep-like slow waves (SLSWs) intrude into wakefulness in healthy people during lapses of vigilance; whether they are altered in PD was unknown. In 84 nondemented patients with PD and 30 healthy older adults, SLSWs occurred more frequently in PD, and higher frontocentral SLSW amplitude tracked hallucinatory traits while more prominent SLSWs tracked poorer cognition. The work links a measurable waking-EEG signature to two of PD's most disabling non-motor features, suggesting diurnal slow-wave intrusion as a correlate of PD neuropathology. Associations are cross-sectional, so causal direction remains open.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cross-sectional cohort (84 PD, 30 controls); high-density waking EEG with slow-wave detection and cognitive/clinical phenotyping.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aec1215

### Targeting peripheral cathepsin E restricts CD8+ T-cell neuroinvasion and ameliorates Alzheimer's disease pathology
**Zhen Xie & Junjun Ni | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Why it matters:** Age-dependent CD8+ T-cell infiltration contributes to AD, but the molecular cue driving recruitment was undefined. Cathepsin E (CatE) is selectively and age-dependently upregulated in peripheral CD8+ T cells of 5xFAD mice and patients; adoptive transfer of AD-derived CD8+ T cells accelerated soluble Aβ accumulation, glial activation and memory deficits in young recipients. Mechanistically CatE raises vinculin/fibronectin and β1-integrin activation to stabilize adhesion to brain endothelium, and a brain-impermeable CatE inhibitor mitigated pathology — implicating a peripherally druggable node in neuroimmune trafficking.

**Model/system & primary method:** 5xFAD mice, patient CD8+ T cells, adoptive transfer; 4D-DIA proteomics, microfluidic migration under flow, and a brain-impermeable CatE inhibitor.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71994

### The role of network connectivity and transcriptomic vulnerability in shaping gray matter atrophy in multiple sclerosis
**Mar Barrantes-Cepas & Giuseppe Pontillo | Science Advances**

**Why it matters:** Across MRIs from 2187 participants, regional gray-matter atrophy in MS aligned with functional cortical hubness (nodal-stress hypothesis) and was constrained by anatomical and functional connectivity (consistent with transneuronal spread), whereas lesional disconnection and transcriptomic vulnerability played only marginal roles. Atrophy anchored to connectivity profiles of disease epicenters in visual, sensorimotor and temporal cortices plus hippocampi, amygdalae and thalami, and network measures improved prediction of future atrophy — supporting connectome-based spread as a driver of MS neurodegeneration rather than cell-intrinsic gene expression.

**Model/system & primary method:** Large human MRI cohort (n=2187) with normative modeling; network-based (connectome) and transcriptomic-vulnerability analyses of atrophy progression.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeg3412

### Glial progenitor-like state is preferentially co-opted in glioblastoma and sustained by POU3F3
**Susana I Ramos & Nadejda M Tsankova | Neuron**

**Key findings:** A spatiotemporal multi-omic atlas of glioblastoma resolved preferential enrichment of a glial intermediate progenitor cell (gIPC)-like state most resembling third-trimester germinal-matrix gIPCs, across tumor core, infiltrative margin and recurrence. EGFR and the lncRNA NRXN1-DT emerged as co-opted gIPC markers, and POU3F3 was prioritized as a driver regulating NRXN1-DT enhancer activity, EGFR expression and stem/progenitor-like maintenance — nominating oncofetal gliogenic programs as therapeutic targets.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human primary/recurrent GBM; spatial multi-omic profiling with functional validation of POU3F3.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.012

### Antisense oligonucleotide silencing rescues neurodevelopmental deficits in AMPA receptor gain-of-function mice
**Chih-Ming Chen & Shu-Ling Chiu | Neuron**

**Key findings:** A recurrent GluA1 variant (GRIA1 p.A636T) from ASD/intellectual-disability patients, modeled as a knock-in, produced core ASD/ID-like behaviors and selective hippocampal vulnerability with progressive dendritic atrophy and neuronal loss. Synaptic AMPARs were hyperexcitable and failed the developmental switch from calcium-permeable to calcium-impermeable receptors, causing persistent excitotoxicity. A single neonatal allele-specific ASO silencing the mutant transcript prevented pathology and rescued behavior — proof of concept for RNA-targeted precision therapy in AMPAR channelopathy.

**Model/system & primary method:** Gria1-A636T knock-in mice; allele-specific ASO with electrophysiology, histology and behavior.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.013

### Slow-timescale regulation of dopamine release governs recovery of a motivational drive over days
**Praneel S Sunkavalli & Stephen X Zhang | Neuron**

**Key findings:** Using sexual satiety and recovery in male mice as a model of multi-day motivation, deep-brain fluorescence-lifetime imaging in the medial preoptic area showed tonic dopamine transmission declined after mating and re-emerged over a week. Two mechanisms acted on different timescales: a transient drop in tonic firing of hypothalamic dopamine neurons suppressed drive for 1–2 days, while a slower decline and recovery in the neurons' capacity to produce and release dopamine paralleled the week-long return of drive.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse mating-drive paradigm; in vivo fluorescence-lifetime dopamine imaging in MPOA with circuit manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.022

### Dissociable cerebellar pathways for reach correction and learning
**Courtney I Dobrott & Abigail L Person | Neuron**

**Key findings:** During mouse reaching, feedback-driven corrective submovements occurred spontaneously or after neural perturbation. Closed-loop perturbation of red nucleus-projecting neurons in the anterior interposed nucleus was readily compensated and produced learned shifts in future submovement timing, whereas broader IntA perturbation was neither compensated nor learned — showing that distinct IntA output channels separately permit accurate online correction versus corrupt it, with divergent consequences for subsequent learning.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse reaching with closed-loop optogenetic perturbation and electrophysiology of cerebellar interposed-nucleus outputs.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.010

### Advancing brain organoids from disease phenotypes to mechanisms and translation across developmental, infectious, and neurodegenerative diseases
**Jürgen A Knoblich & Sally Temple | Neuron**

**Key findings:** This review argues that organoid studies are moving beyond phenotype recapitulation toward defining when pathogenic changes emerge, how they evolve, and which cell types drive or resist disease across neurodevelopmental, infectious and neurodegenerative contexts. ### Information formatting in neuronal populations predicts differential functional contributions to behavior
**Douglas A Ruff & Marlene R Cohen | Nature Neuroscience**

**Key findings:** In monkeys combining motion and reward information, both middle temporal area (MT) and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) encoded both variables but *formatted* them differently: MT represented them separably, dlPFC in a decision-related combined format. A recurrent network trained to reproduce this formatting predicted distinct stimulation effects, confirmed experimentally — MT microstimulation biased choices while dlPFC stimulation produced winner-take-all outcomes — supporting a view that areas reformat shared information to enable complex behavior.

**Model/system & primary method:** Non-human primate electrophysiology and microstimulation during a motion-reward task; recurrent network modeling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02514-7

### Mitochondria-derived extracellular vesicles mediate Alzheimer's disease pathology through transmitting microglial Ggt1
**Yun Li & Jialin C Zheng | Nature Aging**

**Key findings:** Mitovesicles (mtVs), a mitochondria-derived EV subset, were enriched in brain from AD patients and 5xFAD mice. Administering 5xFAD brain-derived mtVs induced AD-like behavioral and pathological changes in wild-type mice and accelerated disease in AD mice, and blocking mtV release alleviated phenotypes. Proteomics identified Ggt1 as a highly enriched mtV cargo whose inhibition mitigated 5xFAD pathology and delayed mtV-induced progression — nominating mtVs and Ggt1 as therapeutic targets.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human AD brain and 5xFAD mice; mitovesicle isolation/transfer, proteomics and Ggt1 inhibition.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-026-01263-3

### Plasma P-Tau217 as a prognostic, monitoring, and risk-stratification biomarker of clinical progression in Lewy body disease
**Sara A Lorkiewicz & Kathleen L Poston | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In a prospective longitudinal AD Research Center cohort, baseline plasma pTau217 and its longitudinal change predicted cognitive (MoCA) and functional (CDR-SB) decline in Lewy body disease. Using an amyloid-PET-derived, LBD-specific cut-point, participants with abnormal pTau217 declined faster, supporting plasma pTau217 — a marker of co-occurring AD pathology — as a prognostic and monitoring tool for stratifying clinical progression in LBD, not only AD.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human longitudinal cohort (LBD, AD, controls); plasma pTau217 with linear mixed-effects modeling against cognition and function.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218686

### Diabetes mellitus is associated with altered Alzheimer's disease pathology
**Min Wu & Benyan Luo | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** Across 2784 participants from three cohorts (CPAS, ADNI, HABS-HD), diabetes was associated with *lower* global amyloid burden and lower tau accumulation in the two cohorts with more cognitive impairment, and weakened the coupling of Aβ and plasma pTau217 to subsequent tau accumulation, with reduced tau propagation from early to later Braak stages. The findings complicate the view of diabetes as uniformly accelerating AD proteinopathy and are observational, leaving mechanism and confounding (including metformin use, explored here) unresolved.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multi-cohort observational study; amyloid/tau PET, plasma pTau217, longitudinal cognition.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.72002

### A single-nucleus multiome map of the human cerebellum with Alzheimer's disease and related dementias
**Yayan Feng & Feixiong Cheng | Nature Communications**

**Key findings:** Single-nucleus RNA+ATAC profiling of 103,861 nuclei from cerebellum and frontal cortex across ADRD cases and controls yielded 431,834 peak-to-gene linkages and disease-associated regulatory changes, with trajectory analysis implicating STMN1 and CLU in cerebellar granule cells. Integrating genetics and 3D genome data nominated causal variants linked to SEZ6L2 (Purkinje cells) and KANSL1 (granule cells); CRISPR interference of the variant-containing elements in patient-derived cerebellar organoids suppressed these genes and perturbed neurodegeneration-associated programs.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem cerebellum/cortex single-nucleus multiome; patient-derived cerebellar organoids with CRISPRi validation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78232-2

### TNIP1 acts as an aggrephagy receptor that assembles clearance machinery for protein aggregate removal
**Hyungsun Park & Seongju Lee | Cell Reports**

**Key findings:** TNIP1 was identified as a selective-autophagy (aggrephagy) receptor that accumulates at polyubiquitinated aggregates under proteotoxic stress and assembles SQSTM1/p62 and TAX1BP1 onto them. TNIP1 depletion reduced aggregate clearance and increased axonal degeneration; TNIP1 expression is lowered in ALS with TDP-43 pathology, and an ALS-associated TNIP1 mutant failed to associate with TDP-43 aggregates or rescue their degradation — establishing TNIP1 as a proteostasis node and candidate ALS target.

**Model/system & primary method:** Cell-based proteotoxic-stress models with ALS patient expression data; aggrephagy receptor assembly and axonal degeneration assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118283

### Evaluation of submandibular gland biopsies with real-time quaking-induced conversion in clinical Parkinson disease
**Wiebke M Jürgens-Wemheuer & Walter J Schulz-Schaeffer | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In a prospective study, α-synuclein seeding activity by RT-QuIC on fresh-frozen submandibular gland biopsies was tested as an in vivo diagnostic in clinically diagnosed PD versus surgical controls, benchmarked against immunohistochemistry and PET-blot on formalin-fixed tissue from the same biopsy. The work evaluates an accessible extracerebral tissue seed-amplification assay for antemortem confirmation of PD; biopsy-based sampling carries procedural and tissue-adequacy constraints relevant to clinical adoption.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human prospective diagnostic study; submandibular gland RT-QuIC seed amplification with IHC/PET-blot reference methods.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218704

### Tau-vulnerable medial temporal lobe connectivity drives a functional-to-synaptic cascade in Alzheimer's disease
**Y. Zhou & Z. Zhao | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Separating medial temporal lobe subregions by neurofibrillary-tangle burden using histological atlases, the authors mapped seed-based functional connectivity in ADNI (N=386, 144 with 2-year follow-up) and an independent cohort (N=100). ### Time-resolved proteomics of a polyglutamine-expanded huntingtin in vitro cell model reveals early RNA-regulatory compensation and late respiratory-chain remodeling
**E. Sonmez & H. Cimen | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Proteomic profiling of a mutant-huntingtin cell model at 72 h and 144 h revealed a two-stage, non-linear response: early enrichment of RNA-processing, splicing and protein-synthesis proteins (a putative compensatory response) followed by their depletion and dominance of mitochondrial energy-metabolism changes. Strikingly, ~85% of proteins altered at both timepoints reversed direction between stages, with respiratory-chain subunits reduced early (Complex IV, ATP synthase) then broadly increased late — arguing that mutant huntingtin reorganizes rather than simply amplifies the proteome. (Revision of a preprint first posted August 2026; an in vitro cell model, not neurons.)

**Model/system & primary method:** Huntingtin-expressing cell line; time-resolved quantitative proteomics with subunit-level respiratory-chain analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.746221

### A shared slowing axis and phenotype-specific rhythms link clinical EEG to FDG-PET dementia phenotypes
**N. Satyadev & D. T. Jones | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 455 patients with EEG and FDG-PET within one year, six data-driven EEG latent factors (nonnegative tensor decomposition) were compared across 14 FDG-PET-defined diagnostic classes (assigned by the StateViewer AI framework). After regressing out a subject-level slowing index, four oscillatory factors (posterior alpha, Rolandic beta, frontotemporal delta-theta, posterior theta-slow alpha) varied by phenotype, with loss of fast and gain of slow rhythms most pronounced in dementia with Lewy bodies and dysexecutive/posterior AD — supporting low-cost EEG as an adjunct to imaging-based dementia subtyping.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human EEG–FDG-PET cohort (n=455); tensor-decomposition EEG factors benchmarked against AI-defined PET phenotypes.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.09.764237
