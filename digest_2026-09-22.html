1Tibial bone compression promotes recovery after brain injury through osteocyte PIEZO1

Nat Neurosci  Zhiqing Cai & Xiaochun Bai
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Traumatic brain injury is known to accelerate bone healing, and this work turns that observation around to ask whether loading bone can help the injured brain. Dynamic compressive tibial axial loading improved survival and motor/cognitive recovery after stroke and TBI in mice and after TBI in pigs, reducing neuron loss, chronic inflammation and astrogliosis. Causality is well supported: deleting the mechanosensory channel Piezo1 selectively in osteocytes abolished the benefit, while transferring serum from loaded mice reproduced it — defining a mechanically tunable bone-to-brain endocrine axis. The evidence is entirely preclinical, but the cross-species reproducibility and clean loss-of-function argue this is more than a mouse artifact.
Model & method
Mouse stroke/TBI and pig TBI models; tibial axial loading, osteocyte-specific Piezo1 knockout, serum transfer, with IL-1R2/APOL11a/HSP70 and serum BDNF/PF4/dopamine as candidate mediators.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02422-w

2The Alzheimer's disease risk gene SORL1 is a regulator of excitatory neuronal function

Alzheimers Dement  C Andrew Williams & Jessica E Young
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
SORL1 is one of the strongest AD risk genes, but its cell-biological consequences at the synapse have been unclear. In human iPSC-derived cortical excitatory neurons, loss of SORL1 broadly reshaped synaptic protein-protein interactions and AMPA-receptor–related composition, and produced network hyperactivity and impaired plasticity on multi-electrode arrays. Critically, the hyperactivity was largely amyloid-β independent, positioning endosomal recycling failure as a driver of synaptic dysfunction upstream of, or parallel to, amyloid. The work is in a reductionist human-neuron system without circuit or in vivo context, but it gives a concrete synaptic mechanism to a genetic risk factor.
Model & method
Human iPSC-derived excitatory cortical neurons; mesoscale synaptic proteomics, immunocytochemistry and multi-electrode array recording.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71862

3Changes in serum β-synuclein precede blood biomarkers of Alzheimer pathology in Down syndrome

Alzheimers Dement  Alba Cervantes González & Olivia Belbin
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Adults with Down syndrome have near-inevitable AD pathology, making them an informative window on biomarker sequencing. Measuring the presynaptic protein β-synuclein by immunoprecipitation–mass spectrometry across the AD continuum (n=131; 88 DS, 43 euploid controls), the authors found β-synuclein elevated in DS well before symptom onset and rising several years ahead of p-tau217, neurofilament light and GFAP. Higher levels tracked cortical atrophy, hypometabolism and reduced episodic memory. This nominates synaptic dysfunction as one of the earliest measurable blood signals in the AD cascade. The design is cross-sectional, so the ordering is inferred across individuals rather than followed longitudinally.
Model & method
Human cross-sectional cohort spanning the DS-AD continuum; serum β-synuclein quantified by immunoprecipitation–mass spectrometry against established plasma biomarkers.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71860

4Urine-derived neuronal extracellular vesicle α-synuclein seed amplification assay for synucleinopathies

Mov Disord  Hoang Bao Tram Tran & Young Eun Kim
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Seed amplification assays for pathogenic α-synuclein are transforming synucleinopathy diagnosis, but they largely depend on CSF, limiting repeat and large-scale use. Here, neuronal extracellular vesicles were immunocaptured from urine with L1CAM beads and run through RT-QuIC, distinguishing PD from disease controls with 73.1% sensitivity/84% specificity and MSA with 76.9%/84% (rising to ~94% specificity versus healthy controls). If it replicates, a fully non-invasive seed assay would be a meaningful practical advance for screening and monitoring. The cohort is small (26 PD, 13 MSA, 25 controls) and sensitivity trails CSF assays, so this is a proof-of-concept rather than a finished test.
Model & method
Human biofluid study; L1CAM immunoprecipitation of urine-derived neuronal EVs followed by real-time quaking-induced conversion (RT-QuIC).
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70533

Striatal dopaminergic reserve shapes the longitudinal impact of peripheral immune activation on motor progression in Parkinson's disease

Mov Disord  Osman Kahveci & Tamer Demiralp
Key findings
In 335 drug-naive PD patients and 305 controls followed for 7 years in PPMI, a higher neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) predicted faster motor progression — each 1 SD higher baseline NLR added ~0.35 UPDRS-III points/year — yet NLR was not consistently tied to dopamine transporter decline. Baseline striatal DAT binding moderated the effect, with the NLR–motor relationship strongest where caudate/putamen dopaminergic reserve was higher. The dissociation suggests peripheral inflammation drives clinical worsening partly through mechanisms beyond nigrostriatal denervation. Findings are observational and correlational.
Model & method
Human longitudinal cohort (PPMI); linear mixed-effects modeling of NLR, UPDRS-III and DAT-SPECT with moderation analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70549

Ursodeoxycholic acid and Parkinson's disease risk: an emulated target trial in UK electronic health records

Mov Disord  Chengsheng Ju & Li Wei
Key findings
UDCA has mitochondrial and neuroprotective effects and has been proposed for PD, but population evidence was lacking. Emulating a target trial in UK primary care data (1990–2023), the authors matched 17,295 UDCA initiators to 51,885 non-initiators within a hepatobiliary-disease population. The 15-year PD risk was 1.70% versus 2.16% (risk difference −0.46 percentage points; RR 0.79), a modest protective association consistent across secondary and dose-response analyses. Confounding by indication and the hepatobiliary-restricted population temper generalizability, but the target-trial framing strengthens the causal read relative to standard observational designs.
Model & method
Human pharmacoepidemiology; target-trial emulation with propensity-score matching and pooled logistic regression on linked EHR data.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70535

Behavior determines times to care home admission and death in semantic dementia: insights from a cohort of 90 cases

Alzheimers Dement  Marc Melkonian & Serge Belliard
Key findings
Across a 31-year, 90-patient semantic dementia cohort, behavioral disturbance — not the side or degree of anterior temporal atrophy — emerged as the sole baseline predictor of institutionalization and death. Half were admitted to care 7.5 years and half died 8.4 years after diagnosis; even mild behavioral disorders were associated with markedly earlier care-home admission (by ~3.0 years) and shorter survival (by ~3.6 years). The result gives clinicians a simple, usable prognostic marker in a disorder where predictors have been elusive, though the single-cohort design limits generalization.
Model & method
Human retrospective clinical cohort; survival and risk-factor analysis of sociodemographic, neuroimaging, neuropsychological and behavioral variables.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71859

Post mortem MRI of cholinergic white matter pathways across neurodegenerative diseases

Alzheimers Dement  Francisco J López-González & Daniel Ferreira
Key findings
Using post mortem in situ 3T MRI in 55 brain donors with standardized neuropathology, cholinergic white matter pathway (CWMP) diffusivity correlated strongly with a visual FLAIR-based rating (CHIPS), independent of global white matter damage. AD and mixed AD+Lewy body pathology showed greater CWMP degeneration than Lewy body disease, other dementias and controls, and hippocampal sclerosis plus vascular co-pathology were the strongest neuropathological predictors. The imaging–pathology validation supports CHIPS as a clinically accessible proxy for cholinergic tract integrity, within the constraints of a modest, cross-sectional autopsy sample.
Model & method
Human post mortem study; diffusion tensor imaging and FLAIR CHIPS rating validated against standardized neuropathological assessment.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71851

Baseline CSF complement balance is associated with longitudinal memory decline in relation to plasma p-tau181 in mild cognitive impairment

Alzheimers Dement  Zhinan Ye & Dehao Yang
Key findings
In 123 ADNI MCI participants, the baseline CSF C3/factor-H ratio interacted with time-varying plasma p-tau181 to predict memory trajectory (three-way interaction p=0.023): when p-tau181 exceeded ~0.76 SD, a higher C3/FH ratio was associated with slower decline, whereas no cross-sectional complement–memory association was seen in any group. The pattern suggests complement regulation modifies tau-related cognitive decline specifically in the prodromal, higher-tau phase — a hypothesis-generating signal from a conditional statistical interaction rather than a direct mechanistic test.
Model & method
Human longitudinal cohort (ADNI-MCI); linear mixed-effects and Johnson–Neyman moderation analysis of CSF complement, plasma p-tau181 and memory.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71836

Centenarians maintain cognition by resisting amyloid-β or decoupling it from tau propagation

medRxiv  Rohde, S. K. & Holstege, H.
Key findings
In 112 centenarians with medial temporal lobe pathology and antemortem cognition, amyloid-β was associated with increased parahippocampal (but not hippocampal) p-tau and lower cognition — yet most centenarians stayed cognitively high-performing via two distinct routes: ~44% resisted amyloid-β accumulation, while ~28% carried substantial amyloid but resisted downstream p-tau propagation. The data point to separable natural resilience mechanisms that uncouple amyloid from tau, each a potential therapeutic entry point. Interpret cautiously: this is a cross-sectional, survivor-enriched autopsy-adjacent cohort, so the "mechanisms" are inferred from pathology–cognition associations.
Model & method
Human centenarian cohort; subregional amyloid-β and p-tau mapping in the medial temporal lobe related to cognitive performance.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.18.26363376
Preprint · not peer reviewed

Selective abundance of miR-290-295 in adult substantia nigra dopamine neurons is neuroprotective via preservation of protein synthesis

bioRxiv  Li, Z. & Vinnikov, I. A.
Key findings
The miR-290-295 cluster is normally a stem-cell microRNA switched off during differentiation, but the authors report it remains selectively expressed in adult substantia nigra pars compacta dopamine neurons. Global knockout reduced SNpc dopamine-neuron numbers and lowered dopamine-biogenesis proteins (tyrosine hydroxylase, DDC, DAT), producing locomotor deficits — arguing the cluster sustains protein synthesis and survival in these vulnerable neurons. The claim of neuroprotection rests on constitutive knockout phenotypes in mice; whether restoring or mimicking the cluster protects against PD-relevant insults remains to be shown.
Model & method
Mouse genetics; miR-290-295 cluster knockout with characterization of SNpc dopamine-neuron number, dopaminergic protein expression and locomotion.
https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.08.05.668587

The impact of alpha-synuclein overexpression on nigrostriatal transmission

bioRxiv  Bergum, N. & Ford, C. P.
Key findings
Selectively overexpressing wild-type human α-synuclein in SNc dopamine neurons — modeling the accumulation that precedes overt degeneration — robustly reduced dopamine release in dorsolateral striatum while postsynaptic transmission to medium spiny neurons stayed intact, and impaired glutamate co-release onto MSNs and cholinergic interneurons. The findings suggest α-synuclein disrupts specific presynaptic outputs early, before neuron loss, correlating with locomotor impairment. As an overexpression model in mice, it isolates a candidate early pathophysiology rather than demonstrating it in human disease.
Model & method
Mouse model with AAV-driven α-synuclein overexpression in SNc; slice electrophysiology of nigrostriatal and somatodendritic transmission with locomotor assays.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.15.751842

Region-dependent regulation of tau phosphorylation in a mouse model of tauopathy

bioRxiv  Jimenez-Ornelas, C. & Sockanathan, S.
Key findings
In PS19 tauopathy mice, genetic disruption of the cell-surface enzyme GDE2 shifted tau phosphorylation in a region-specific way, transiently delaying phosphorylation at pro-aggregation sites (S202/T205, T212, S396) in cortex while accelerating the anti-aggregation site S262, with a marked reduction in S202/T205 and T212 at 6 months and a lowered aggregation propensity. The work implicates GPI-anchor cleavage and local kinase environments in tau regulation, offering a mechanistic handle on where and how tau hyperphosphorylation is set. Effects are model- and site-specific in mice; behavioral and neurodegeneration outcomes are not yet the focus.
Model & method
Mouse genetics; PS19;Gde2-knockout crosses with region-resolved phospho-tau biochemistry and aggregation assays.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.14.751492
Preprint · not peer reviewed

Smartphone passive digital phenotyping in frontotemporal lobar degeneration

medRxiv  Paolillo, E. W. & Wong, B.
Key findings
In 567 adults spanning clinically normal, prodromal and symptomatic FTLD (ALLFTD cohort), passively collected smartphone signals — battery-percentage as a proxy for phone use, plus step count — tracked disease severity at baseline and over follow-up and added incremental value beyond app-based cognitive tests. For a rare, heterogeneous disease that is hard to monitor at scale, low-burden passive sensing could enrich trials and remote care. The features are indirect behavioral proxies, and their specificity to FTLD progression versus general aging or comorbidity will need further validation.
Model & method
Human observational study; ALLFTD Mobile App passive smartphone metrics related to clinical severity, longitudinally.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.18.26363439
