### Cerebellar optogenetic stimulation restores motor cortex activity and behaviour after stroke
**Richard J Kopchock Iii & Gary K Steinberg | Brain**

**Why it matters:** Remote neuromodulation of the contralesional cerebellum is an emerging stroke-recovery strategy, but its effect on cortical population dynamics was unknown. Longitudinal one-photon calcium imaging of ipsilesional M1 layer V neurons showed stroke depressed population activity (reduced calcium peak frequency and amplitude), with the deficit scaling to lesion size. Optogenetic CaMKII–ChR2 stimulation of the contralesional lateral cerebellar nucleus on post-stroke days 5–14 reversed these deficits, improved rotating-beam recovery, and — most strikingly — decoupled both cortical activity and behavioural outcome from lesion burden.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse transient MCAO (n = 13); longitudinal in vivo one-photon calcium imaging of M1 with optogenetic dentatothalamocortical stimulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag396

### Longitudinal plasma proteomic signatures following lecanemab treatment in Alzheimer's disease
**Yujing Chen & Qianhua Zhao | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** Monitoring of amyloid-targeting therapy currently leans on the ATN framework, which does not capture the broader molecular response to amyloid clearance. Using the NULISAseq CNS panel across 209 plasma samples (95 participants, up to 18 months), the authors derive a candidate 12-protein lecanemab-response signature extending beyond ATN — ACHE, Aβ40, Aβ42, CCL3, CCL4, CSF2, ICAM1, IL12p70, IL16, KDR, TREM2 and YWHAZ — implicating myeloid activation, chemokine signalling, endothelial regulation and cholinergic pathways. ACHE tracked clinical severity and amyloid-PET burden; early IL16 and IL12p70 changes showed exploratory associations with later trajectory. Observational (67 lecanemab vs 30 Aβ-positive and 15 Aβ-negative controls), so the candidate markers await validation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; longitudinal plasma NULISAseq proteomics in lecanemab-treated versus untreated amyloid-positive and amyloid-negative controls.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71995

### Disrupting a cargo receptor entrapment complex is a therapeutic node for distinct proteinopathies
**Magdalena Riedl Khursigara & Anna Greka | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** Toxic accumulation of mutant proteins in the secretory pathway underlies several proteinopathies. The authors define a cargo-receptor entrapment complex (TMED7, TMED2, TMED9, TMED10) that retains structurally diverse mutant proteins in the early secretory pathway through TMED7 binding to the Golgi protein GRASP55. Breaking this interaction — by inducible TMED7 deletion or the small molecule BRD7635 — promotes clearance of accumulated cargoes, reverses histopathology and rescues function across distinct proteinopathies in vivo, including mitigating vision loss in a mouse model of retinitis pigmentosa. It nominates a shared, druggable node across otherwise unrelated diseases; evidence is preclinical.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse models and cells; inducible genetic TMED7 deletion and small-molecule (BRD7635) disruption of the TMED7–GRASP55 interaction.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.023

### ER-associated biogenesis of PINK1 preprotein for neuronal mitophagy
**J Tabitha Hees & Angelika B Harbauer | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** PINK1 governs mitochondrial quality control, and its loss causes recessive Parkinson's disease, but where neuronal PINK1 is locally translated and processed was unclear. Live-cell imaging shows neuronal PINK1 biogenesis switches between a mitochondrially anchored co-translational route and an ER-associated post-translational one: on metabolically regulated untethering from mitochondria, Pink1 mRNA relocates to ribosomes near the ER and endolysosomes, where the ER chaperone DNAJB6 shields the PINK1 preprotein in transit (the ER-SURF pathway). Loss of DNAJB6 leaves persistent ER/endolysosome-associated precursor stores and failed mitophagy after mitochondrial damage.

**Model/system & primary method:** Neuronal cell models; live-cell imaging of PINK1 translation sites and preprotein dynamics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-79754-5

### Anticoagulants compared with aspirin in embolic strokes of undetermined source: an individual participant data meta-analysis
**Aristeidis H Katsanos & Ashkan Shoamanesh | Lancet Neurol**

**Why it matters:** Pooling individual participant data from four randomised trials (13,970 patients), direct-acting oral anticoagulation did not reduce recurrent stroke versus aspirin in embolic stroke of undetermined source (adjusted HR 0.94, 95% CI 0.78–1.14; no heterogeneity), and major bleeding did not differ significantly (HR 1.19, 95% CI 0.52–2.73; substantial heterogeneity). A possible benefit in patients with left atrial enlargement was explicitly hypothesis-generating. The analysis consolidates the case against empirical anticoagulation after ESUS and sharpens where a targeted trial might still find benefit.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; individual-participant-data meta-analysis of four randomised controlled trials.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00366-2

### Spectral envelopes of facial movements predict intention, cortical representations, and neural prosthetic control
**Richard Hakim & Bernardo L Sabatini | Neuron**

**Key findings:** Introduces face-rhythm, an unsupervised pipeline combining markerless point-tracking, spectral analysis and non-negative tensor decomposition to reduce facial video to a small set of interpretable components. Across Pavlovian conditioning, a brain–machine-interface task and free behaviour in mice, it recovers whisking, sniffing, licking and subtler motifs that are consistent across animals, decode task variables and internal states, and explain cortical activity with a low-rank representation. Facial motor cortex neurons were well predicted by a phase-invariant spectral transform of movements above ~0.5 Hz, whereas slower movements retained a phase-variant representation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mice; markerless facial point-tracking with spectral and non-negative tensor decomposition, linked to cortical recordings.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.016

### Human cardiac organoids innervated by autonomic neurons using a mixoid approach model myocardial infarction under hypoxia
**Hsueh-Fu Wu & Nadja Zeltner | Cell Stem Cell**

**Key findings:** A "mixoid" method mixes human PSC-derived neural and cardiomyocyte progenitors to generate autonomically innervated cardiac organoids. Sympathetic innervation promoted functional coupling, improved architecture, transcriptional metabolic maturation and a shift toward atrial cardiomyocyte identity; under hypoxia, sympathetic mixoids recapitulated myocardial-infarction features (adrenergic overload, calcium mishandling, fibrosis, apoptosis) that were reduced by the beta-blocker propranolol. Parasympathetic innervation likewise enhanced metabolic maturation and promoted an atrial-conduction-system identity. The platform enables study of autonomic regulation of heart development and offers a general route to innervated organoids.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human PSC-derived sympathetic and parasympathetic innervated cardiac organoids (mixoids).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2026.10.018

### Heparan sulfate sulfatases are essential for the patterning of human stem cell-derived midbrain dopaminergic neurons
**Chiara Tremolanti & Ernest Arenas | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Identifies the heparan-sulfate-modifying enzymes SULF1 and SULF2 as essential extracellular regulators of midbrain dopaminergic (mDA) neuron identity. In CRISPR/Cas9-engineered iPSCs, loss of SULF1/2 increased 6-O-sulfated heparan sulfate and disrupted anteroposterior and dorsoventral patterning; double-knockout cells failed to acquire ventral midbrain fates and instead adopted hindbrain, spinal cord and neural-crest-like identities (single-nucleus RNA-seq). The defect was partly attributable to aberrant FGF signalling, and FGF inhibition redirected cells toward midbrain fates without restoring ventral identity. Directly relevant to generating bona fide mDA neurons for Parkinson's cell-replacement therapy.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived mDA cultures; CRISPR/Cas9 knockout and single-nucleus RNA-seq.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-79485-7

### Targeted high-fidelity sequencing identifies CCG interruptions as critical determinants of spinocerebellar ataxia type 8 pathogenicity
**Sheng Zeng & Beisha Tang | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** Screening 14,130 probands and 4,307 controls with ExpansionHunter and validating by long-range HiFi sequencing, repeat length alone did not distinguish SCA8 patients from controls — no difference in carrier frequency or repeat size across groups, and no relationship between repeat size and age at onset. Instead, CCG interruptions (within the 100–300 repeat range) were restricted to SCA patients, while expansion-positive patients without CCG interruptions largely carried co-occurring ataxia-related pathogenic variants. Reframes a two-decade debate: sequence architecture, not expansion length, determines SCA8 pathogenicity.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cohorts; short-read whole-genome screening plus targeted long-range HiFi sequencing.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70608

### Ancestry-specific effects of apolipoprotein E on Alzheimer's disease endophenotypes
**Ana I Boeriu & HABS‐HD Study Team | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In 2,733 participants of the Health and Aging Brain Study–Health Disparities, genetically inferred ancestry modified APOE ε4 associations with AD biomarkers. ε4-positivity associated with higher plasma pTau217 and pTau181, lower Aβ42/Aβ40 and worse global cognition (CDR-SB) within ancestry groups, but the ε4 effect on pTau217 was ~2.5-fold larger in EUR-like than AFR-like participants, with African ancestry attenuating the association. The cross-sectional analysis argues that plasma-biomarker-based AD evaluation must account for both APOE genotype and genetic ancestry to avoid diagnostic misclassification.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; ancestry-stratified, global-compositional and local-ancestry regression of plasma AD biomarkers.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71987

### Clearance of erythrocytes after subarachnoid hemorrhage: cranial lymphatic and dural pathways
**Tangtang Xiang & Steven T Proulx | Sci Adv**

**Key findings:** Using murine arterial-perforation and low-rate intracisternal-injection SAH models, the authors map red-blood-cell distribution and clearance. While RBCs accessed peripheral lymphatics via cranial nerves, an extensive population of extravasated erythrocytes was sequestered within non-endothelial, collagen-lined interstitial channels flanking the major dural venous sinuses; by day 2 post-SAH, regional macrophages actively cleared some trapped RBCs. The spatiotemporal framework identifies dural and lymphatic routes as anatomical and cellular targets to accelerate clearance of toxic blood products after stroke.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse SAH models; anatomical mapping and imaging of RBC distribution and macrophage clearance.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeg4194

### Investigation of parkinsonism-associated genes in a large cohort of individuals with early-onset or familial predominant parkinsonism
**Kilian Rousvoal & PDG group | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** Whole-exome sequencing of 1,488 probands with early-onset (<45 years) or familial predominant parkinsonism who lacked variants in seven known PD genes, screening 118 expert-curated parkinsonism-associated genes (PAGs). 5.4% carried a pathogenic PAG variant, and where clinical review was possible the phenotype was compatible in 91.5%. Six genes (ATP13A2, ATXN2, FIG4, GCH1, PLA2G6, PPP2R5D) accounted for 40% of compatible cases. PAGs explain roughly 5% of otherwise genetically unexplained early-onset or familial parkinsonism, and clinical correlation remains essential to establish causality.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; whole-exome sequencing cohort with expert gene-panel analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70604

### A GPNMB-associated lysosomal program tracks neurodegeneration across the DAM continuum
**Werner, G. & Schultze | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In a progranulin/TMEM106B-deficient mouse model recapitulating frontotemporal lobar degeneration with lysosomal impairment, CSF1R inhibition reduced microgliosis yet accelerated neurodegeneration, implying a protective microglial role under lysosomal stress. Single-cell transcriptomics showed rising Gpnmb within disease-associated microglia (DAM) marks a shift from inflammatory toward lysosomal, autolysosomal and lipid-catabolic programs; engagement of this GPNMB program correlated with neurodegenerative severity, was TREM2-dependent, and was reversed by lysosomal rescue. Amyloid-driven models with limited neurodegeneration showed DAM activation but low GPNMB engagement. CSF proteomics converged on a signature overlapping human FTD. Interpret cautiously: mouse models with convergent human CSF correlation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse FTLD models (PGRN/TMEM106B-deficient); single-cell transcriptomics, CSF proteomics and CSF1R inhibition.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.20.767890

### Early locus coeruleus tauopathy features potassium channel dysfunction and is attenuated by Kv7 activation
**Zhang, Y.-C. & He, K.-W. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In PS19 tau transgenic mice and wild-type mice receiving locus coeruleus (LC) inoculation of human tau fibrils, young (but not aged) PS19 LC neurons showed elevated tonic firing and intrinsic excitability with reduced low-threshold potassium current, alongside pre-tangle phospho-tau; single-cell profiling suggested increased nuclear REST and reduced Kcnq3/Kcnd1 (Kv7.2/Kv7.3). Hyperexcitability depended on endogenous tau; combined Kv4/Kv7 modulation attenuated it, while GSK-3 inhibition reduced tau phosphorylation and restored potassium currents. Chronic treatment with the Kv7 opener retigabine lowered bilateral AT8-positive tau burden and preserved tyrosine-hydroxylase-positive neuron density after seeding. Causal within the model; proposes excitability regulation as an early-tauopathy intervention.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse (PS19; LC tau-fibril seeding); electrophysiology, single-cell RNA-seq, sleep-EEG and pharmacology.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.19.767760

### Identification of a human Anxa1-associated dopaminergic population linked to Parkinson's disease vulnerability and sensorimotor putaminal networks
**Schonfeld, E. & Awatramani, R. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Mouse Anxa1+ substantia nigra dopaminergic neurons are highly vulnerable in PD models but lacked a defined human counterpart. Transferring the mouse taxonomy to human with a hierarchical mixture-of-experts model recovers a human Anxa1-associated population that was the most depleted subtype in PD brains. A ligand–receptor algorithm (validated against mouse tracing) infers preferential innervation of the caudal putamen, where DaT-SPECT shows the earliest and most severe dopaminergic axon loss, placing this region within the somato-cognitive action network. The work nominates CACNA1G as a candidate neuroprotective target. Computational cross-species inference from human single-nucleus data.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human single-nucleus RNA-seq with cross-species taxonomy transfer; connectivity and ligand–receptor inference.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.20.766782

### Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis progression is tracked in plasma by systemic proteins independent of motor neurons
**Finney, C. & Shvetcov, A. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In the Answer ALS cohort (245 patients, 76 controls) with serial SomaScan plasma proteomics and patient-matched iPSC-derived motor neurons, 77 baseline proteins separated patients from controls, dominated by muscle proteins (PDLIM3, ACTN2, MYOM2). A two-stage longitudinal design identified 72 progression markers whose within-patient change tracked functional decline (led by CLEC3B, ANTXR2, ART3) — largely secreted/shed, non-muscle proteins that replicated in Target ALS. Motor-neuron-expressed proteins were underrepresented and iMN expression did not predict tracking. Baseline NfL predicted decline rate but its change did not track decline, whereas the progression markers did within every NfL stratum, positioning NfL for prognosis and systemic proteins for monitoring.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human; serial plasma SomaScan proteomics with patient-matched iPSC-derived motor neurons.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.15.26368153

### An atlas of the aging glycoproteome reveals how glycosylation modulates autoantibody binding in the brain
**Schoof, M. & Wyss-Coray, T. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Mapping the N-glycoproteome of brain, liver, spleen, kidney and plasma across nine ages spanning the mouse lifespan (79,517 N-glycopeptides on 4,397 glycoproteins), organ-specific differences exceeded age-related changes, but aging still remodeled many glycosites. Brain aging increased short paucimannose glycans, driven partly by hexosaminidase B (HEXB); in white matter, aging increased paucimannosylation of myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein (MOG), a major autoimmune target. MOG-reactive autoantibodies were detected in aged animals and in MOG-antibody-associated disease (MOGAD) patients, and MOG glycosylation modulated autoantibody binding; comparable changes followed experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis. Positions N-glycosylation as both an aging marker and a regulator of disease-relevant interactions.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse multi-organ lifespan glycoproteomics; human MOGAD samples and EAE.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.28.762617
