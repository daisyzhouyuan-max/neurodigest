### Dysregulated cholesterol trafficking through ABCA1 drives neurodegeneration in Parkinson's disease
**Feng-Xia Ma & Lei Cao | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Brain cholesterol is long implicated in Parkinson's disease, but lowering synthesis has disappointed therapeutically; this work reframes the problem as one of trafficking. ABCA1 is elevated in the substantia nigra of postmortem PD brain and in a PD mouse model, and the authors dissect opposing cell-type roles: astrocytic ABCA1 drives excessive cholesterol efflux, while in neurons ABCA1 localizes to lysosomes and imports cholesterol, producing overload, autophagy impairment and α-synuclein pathology. Genetic silencing of Abca1 in either astrocytes or dopaminergic neurons attenuated neurodegeneration and behavioural deficits, and the authors identify magnesium citrate as an ABCA1 inhibitor that reduced dopaminergic loss. The causal evidence is mouse-based, but it nominates ABCA1 inhibition as a disease-modifying direction distinct from cholesterol-synthesis targeting.

**Model/system & primary method:** Postmortem human nigra plus PD mouse models; cell-type-specific Abca1 silencing (astrocytes, dopaminergic neurons), lysosomal cholesterol assays and a candidate small-molecule inhibitor.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02487-7

### H3K9me3 inhibition reverses Alzheimer's progression by restoring synaptic and immune proteostasis across the brain–retina axis
**Dieu-Trang Fuchs & Maya Koronyo-Hamaoui | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** The repressive histone mark H3K9me3 rose early across the AD continuum in both postmortem brain and retina, and brain–retina levels were tightly correlated (r = 0.72), suggesting the retina could serve as an accessible readout of brain epigenetic state. Pharmacologically, the epidithiodiketopiperazine ETP69 inhibited SUV39H1 and lowered cerebral H3K9me3 in vivo; in two transgenic AD models it mitigated pathology, preserved synapses and improved cognitive and visual performance, with proteomics showing re-established proteostasis and remodelled innate-immune and BDNF/VGF-granin signalling. The data frame H3K9me3 accumulation as a shared and reversible driver rather than a bystander, though efficacy rests on transgenic mouse models.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem brain and retina across the AD spectrum; APPswe/PS1dE9 and APPswe/tauP301L/PS1 mice; methyltransferase inhibition (ETP69), behavioural testing and proteomics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71918

### Parkinson's disease LRRK2 mutations dysregulate iron homeostasis and promote oxidative stress and ferroptosis in human neurons and astrocytes
**Adamantios Mamais & Matthew J LaVoie | Mol Neurodegener**

**Why it matters:** Nigral iron accumulation precedes clinical onset in LRRK2-associated PD, but the responsible cells and mechanism were unclear. Across isogenic human iPSC-derived glutamatergic neurons, dopaminergic neurons and astrocytes, pathogenic heterozygous LRRK2 mutations consistently raised lysosomal ferrous iron, elevated lipid peroxidation and ROS, and these phenotypes were reduced by the selective LRRK2 inhibitor MLi-2 or by iron chelation. CRISPR loss of Rab8a reproduced the iron phenotype and exogenous Rab8a normalized lysosomal iron in R1441C cells, implicating Rab8a-dependent trafficking. The study ties LRRK2 kinase signalling to lysosomal iron dyshomeostasis and ferroptosis-related stress in a human cellular setting.

**Model/system & primary method:** Isogenic human iPSC-derived neurons and astrocytes; ICP-MS and iron-probe super-resolution imaging, LRRK2 inhibition, iron chelation and Rab8a perturbation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00979-5

### CX3CL1 (fractalkine)–CX3CR1 interface drives CD8⁺ T cell-induced neurodegeneration in human brain organoids
**Woo Sub Yang & Insoo Kang | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Why it matters:** How adaptive immunity injures neurons in AD-relevant settings has been hard to model in human tissue. In human cortical organoids, amyloid-β exposure increased neuronal CX3CL1 (fractalkine), which engages CX3CR1 on CD8⁺ T cells, and upregulated IL-15; transwell assays showed enhanced recruitment of CX3CR1⁺ CD8⁺ T cells toward Aβ-treated organoids. Infiltrating CD8⁺ T cells caused neuronal injury (MAP2 loss, cleaved caspase-3) with cell-death and inflammation signatures, conditioned medium from activated CX3CR1-high CD8⁺ T cells reproduced the damage, and neutralizing CD8⁺ cytokines and cytotoxic molecules rescued it. The work supplies a defined axis linking amyloid stress to CD8⁺ T-cell neurotoxicity and a human organoid platform to probe it.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived cortical organoids with CD8⁺ T cells; Aβ challenge, transwell migration, conditioned-medium transfer and cytokine/cytotoxin neutralization.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2605209123

### Early lineage divergence segregates sensory and motor-associative identities in the thalamus
**Lorenzo Puche-Aroca & Guillermina López-Bendito | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Combining a spatiotemporal single-cell multiomic and spatial-transcriptomic atlas of the embryonic mouse thalamus with barcoding-based lineage tracing, the authors resolve two major glutamatergic lineages — sensory-related and motor-associative — arising from spatially and molecularly distinct progenitor domains under different gene-regulatory programs, indicating early segregation of functional identities. Within the sensory lineage, first-order visual and somatosensory trajectories branch from a shared higher-order-like state, and in silico prediction plus in vivo perturbation identify Sp9 as a regulator of visual thalamic fate. The result defines the developmental architecture underlying thalamic modality specification.

**Model/system & primary method:** Embryonic mouse thalamus; single-cell multiomics, spatial transcriptomics, lineage barcoding and in vivo perturbation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02485-9

### More than just accuracy: a pathway-level framework for implementing blood and digital biomarkers for Alzheimer's disease care
**Robert Perneczky & Anna Hofmann | Neuron**

**Key findings:** This review argues that the clinical value of plasma AD tests — now gaining FDA clearances and European certifications — will hinge on pathway-level outcomes rather than diagnostic accuracy alone: diagnostic confidence, appropriate use of CSF and PET, time to diagnosis, treatment-eligibility assessment and trial recruitment. It positions digital biomarkers (cognition, speech, mobility) as complementary but more heterogeneous in construct validity, device dependence, adherence and regulatory status, and lays out how combined blood–digital workflows should be evaluated through context of use, downstream decisions, governance and post-market monitoring. A framework piece, not primary data, but a useful map for implementation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Review/framework; synthesis of blood and digital biomarker evidence and implementation considerations.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.006

### Direction-specific biomarker profiles and clinical significance of plasma p-tau217/amyloid PET discordance in cognitively normal adults
**Yanlu Huang & Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In cognitively normal adults from CPAS (n = 594) and ADNI (n = 475), plasma p-tau217/amyloid-PET discordance carried direction-specific meaning. Relative to double-negative participants, p-tau+/PET− showed higher GFAP and NfL across cohorts, p-tau−/PET+ showed a lower plasma Aβ42/40 in ADNI, and double-positive participants had the broadest abnormalities. Longitudinally, both p-tau+ groups had higher clinical-progression risk and faster decline in selected domains, while both PET+ groups accumulated amyloid faster; faster accumulation in p-tau+/PET− emerged only at lower specificity thresholds. Interpretation should account for both the discordance pattern and the p-tau217 cutoff, and the operating points require validation before clinical use.

**Model/system & primary method:** Two cognitively normal cohorts (CPAS, ADNI); plasma p-tau217, amyloid PET with PET-anchored cutoffs, and mixed-effects/Cox longitudinal modelling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71922

### Why diabetes matters in dementia studies: failure to account for diabetes status masks regional mitochondrial DNA copy number changes in human hippocampus, amygdala and cerebellum in Alzheimer's disease
**Aakruti Kaikini & Afshan N Malik | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Absolute qPCR of mtDNA copy number (mtDNA-CN) in postmortem hippocampus, amygdala and cerebellum (N = 66–77) showed region-dependent baselines (amygdala > hippocampus > cerebellum). Donors with diabetes had markedly higher mtDNA-CN than non-diabetic donors across all three regions (+30% to +49%), an effect also present within AD. Only after removing diabetic donors did AD associate with 19–30% lower mtDNA-CN, reaching significance in amygdala and cerebellum after correction. The finding casts diabetes as an effect modifier that can mask AD-related mitochondrial changes — a methodological caveat for dementia cohorts — though the functional meaning of elevated mtDNA-CN in the diabetic brain is unresolved and the sample is modest.

**Model/system & primary method:** Postmortem human brain (control, AD, with/without diabetes); absolute qPCR quantification of mtDNA copy number by region.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71870

### Heterogeneous amyloid β dimerization revealed by single-molecule FRET and MD simulation
**Fanjie Meng & Hoi Sung Chung | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** Dimerization is the first step of Aβ aggregation yet has been hard to characterize. Using single-molecule FRET with MD simulation, the authors find dimerization is extremely rare (dissociation constant > 16 mM) but populates multiple distinct dimer states with different FRET efficiencies and stabilities, some persisting milliseconds to seconds. Dimer population rose with ionic strength and lower pH, pointing to non-specific electrostatic interactions, and simulations reproduced diverse bound states including Greek-key-like mixed parallel/antiparallel β-strand motifs. This structural and kinetic heterogeneity at the earliest aggregation step reflects a rugged energy landscape that may seed the polymorphism of mature fibrils.

**Model/system & primary method:** In vitro Aβ; single-molecule FRET spectroscopy with enhanced-sampling molecular dynamics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2618695123

### Genome-wide cross-trait analysis to compare genetic architecture between Parkinson's disease and kidney-related traits
**Le Chang & Sarah A Gagliano Taliun | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Testing the epidemiological kidney–PD link at the genetic level, the authors combined GWAS for PD and five UK Biobank kidney traits (European ancestry) across correlation, pleiotropy mapping, colocalization and enrichment. Genome-wide correlations were modest — significant only for the urinary potassium-to-creatinine ratio — yet shared loci appeared in 9 of 15 trait pairs, more often reflecting distinct causal variants within a shared region than a single shared variant. Four loci carried a shared causal variant, implicating BIN3, MSRA and TNK2 in protein quality control. The work provides specific loci and pathways for investigating a kidney–brain axis in neurodegeneration rather than asserting a causal relationship.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human GWAS (PD and five kidney traits, European ancestry); genetic-correlation, colocalization and pleiotropy analyses, sex-combined and sex-stratified.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77920-3

### Differential trajectories of brain atrophy in multiple sclerosis, MOGAD and AQP4⁺ NMOSD: the role of disease activity in MOGAD
**Rosa Cortese & MAGNIMS Study Group | Neurology**

**Key findings:** In a retrospective multicenter MAGNIMS cohort (98 MS, 72 MOGAD, 41 AQP4⁺ NMOSD, 278 controls; ≥2 same-scanner MRIs), each demyelinating disease showed a characteristic atrophy pattern. MS had accelerated global atrophy (β = −2.03 cm³/year vs controls); MOGAD showed white-matter loss and ventricular enlargement; AQP4⁺ NMOSD showed pallidal reduction. Within MOGAD, relapsing patients and those with new lesions had greater ventricular expansion than controls, MS and NMOSD, supporting an event-driven, inflammation-linked pattern of tissue injury in MOGAD rather than the diffuse neurodegeneration characteristic of MS. Limitations include the small NMOSD sample and heterogeneous follow-up.

**Model/system & primary method:** Multicenter longitudinal human MRI; lesion-filled T1 volumetry (SAMSEG) with linear mixed-effects atrophy modelling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218673

### PODXL regulates the neuronal entry of α-synuclein aggregates
**Neupane, S. & Aguzzi, A. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Cell-to-cell spread of α-synuclein (αSyn) fibrils is central to PD, but the neuronal surface factors governing uptake are undefined. A flow-cytometry CRISPR-activation screen across 68 PD-genetics-prioritized cell-surface genes in human iPSC-derived cortical neurons identified podocalyxin-like protein 1 (PODXL) as a strong suppressor of αSyn preformed-fibril uptake. PODXL overexpression reduced fibril uptake and pSer129-seeded accumulation across structurally distinct recombinant and disease-derived fibrils, while knockdown increased uptake; domain-deletion and glycosylation mutants implicated the ectodomain and N-glycosylation. If it holds, PODXL defines a surface gatekeeper of pathogenic αSyn entry, though this is an in vitro screen awaiting in vivo and peer-reviewed confirmation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived cortical neurons; CRISPR-activation screen, PODXL over/underexpression and preformed-fibril uptake/seeding assays.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.15.759470

### Astrocytic sEH–EET signaling regulates microglial lysosomal function and suppresses the STING–type I interferon pathway in Alzheimer's disease
**Ghosh, B. & Zheng, H. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Soluble epoxide hydrolase (sEH), which degrades anti-inflammatory epoxyeicosatrienoic acids (EETs), is elevated in AD brain. Using snRNA-seq across wild-type, sEH-knockout, 5xFAD and 5xFAD;sEH-KO mice plus primary and iPSC-derived microglia, the authors show genetic sEH disruption in 5xFAD reduced a type-I-interferon-responsive microglial subcluster and lowered phospho-STING, and 11,12-EET supplementation attenuated STING activation and IFN target-gene expression in microglia. Cell-type-specific (astrocyte- and endothelial-specific) knockouts were generated to localize the contribution. The data position astrocytic sEH–EET signalling as an upstream brake on microglial STING–interferon activity, in a mouse/in-vitro mechanistic study.

**Model/system & primary method:** 5xFAD and sEH-knockout mice (incl. cell-type-specific KO); single-nucleus RNA-seq, primary mouse and human iPSC-derived microglia with EET supplementation.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.16.759679

### High-throughput identification of protein turnover modulators in human neurons
**Dembska, J. & Suter, D. M. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Proteostasis decline is a hallmark of aging and neurodegeneration, but screening protein turnover in post-mitotic human neurons has been impractical. A fluorescent-timer live-imaging platform quantified global synthesis and degradation rates in human ESC-derived neurons at screening scale; of 5,897 compounds, 199 increased both rates, and three selected compounds upregulated translation-associated genes. All three cleared pathogenic α-synuclein aggregates in mouse primary neurons and one did so in human dopaminergic neurons, suggesting enhanced turnover can reduce Lewy-body-like pathology. A methods-and-screen advance awaiting target deconvolution; this is a later version of a preprint first posted May 2026.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human ESC- and iPSC-derived neurons plus mouse primary neurons; fluorescent-timer live-cell imaging for turnover and α-synuclein aggregate-clearance assays.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.06.723176

### Too early for LATE? Age at onset is associated with the clinical and neuroimaging expression of limbic-predominant age-related TDP-43 pathology in Alzheimer's disease
**Zhu, N. & Rabinovici, G. D. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 196 individuals with autopsy-confirmed intermediate-to-high AD neuropathology, enriched for younger onset, LATE-NC co-pathology occurred in 31% overall but only 19% of those with symptom onset ≤65 years, with frequency rising with older onset age. LATE-NC presence was associated with greater episodic-memory impairment and smaller amygdala and hippocampal volumes; among LATE-NC cases, older onset tracked with memory-first presentation and greater medial-temporal involvement (higher inferior-to-medial temporal FDG-PET metabolism ratios). The study argues LATE-NC should still be considered in early-onset AD, integrating neuropathology with antemortem clinical and imaging data in a single-sample autopsy cohort.

**Model/system & primary method:** Autopsy-confirmed AD cohort with antemortem data; neuropathological LATE-NC staging linked to cognition, MRI volumetry and FDG-PET.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.15.26365384

### Reproducible cerebrospinal fluid proteomic signatures across early neuronal α-synuclein disease stages in PPMI
**Weber, S. & Mollenhauer, B. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Using proximity-extension-assay CSF proteomics in PPMI (discovery n = 721; validation n = 974), the authors profiled early neuronal α-synuclein disease stratified by the NSD integrated staging system. Prodromal strata (hyposmia, iRBD) were near-indistinguishable from controls (≤16 proteins), whereas manifest disease showed broader changes (up to 305 proteins). DOPA decarboxylase (DDC) replicated consistently, mitochondrial creatine kinase CKMT1 peaked prodromally (suggesting a transient bioenergetic response), and a reproducible set of synaptic/neurosecretory proteins (RPH3A, STX1B) separated controls from more advanced stages. The results point to stage-dependent CSF signatures for early PD, albeit limited by the subtlety of prodromal changes.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human CSF (PPMI discovery and validation cohorts); Olink proximity-extension proteomics across NSD disease stages.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.18.26365832
