1A pericyte-to-myofibroblast transition links APOE4 to cerebrovascular degeneration

Cell  Braxton R Schuldt & Joel W Blanchard
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
APOE4 is the strongest genetic risk factor for Alzheimer's disease and is tied to cerebrovascular degeneration, but the cellular route has been unclear. Building a single-cell transcriptomic atlas of human brain vasculature, the authors find that APOE4 carriers lose pericytes and gain a myofibroblast-like population co-expressing contractile and extracellular-matrix genes; these transitioned cells secrete fibronectin that promotes vascular amyloid deposition. Elevated TGF-β signaling drives the switch, and TGF-β inhibition restored pericyte coverage and lowered vascular fibrosis and amyloid to APOE3 levels, nominating a targetable mechanism. Causal manipulation is mouse and cell-based; the human data are cross-sectional and associative.
Model & method
Human brain vascular single-cell transcriptomic atlas with immunostaining validation, plus APOE4 mouse models and TGF-β pathway perturbation.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.058

2Spatiotemporal characterization of Alzheimer disease pathology in living human brain tissue

Nat Commun  Aram Aslanyan & Ross W Paterson
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
AD pathology is almost always studied postmortem or indirectly through fluid and imaging biomarkers. Here small cortical biopsies taken during ventriculoperitoneal shunt surgery for suspected normal-pressure hydrocephalus (n=18) were used to examine amyloid and tau in living tissue, alongside matched ventricular and lumbar CSF and postmortem control brain. Amyloid plaques were present in 9 of 18 biopsies while mature tau tangles were rare, and MALDI mass-spectrometry imaging mapped amyloid species, opening a window on protein turnover during life. The cohort is small and confined to a surgical population, so generalization to sporadic AD is limited.
Model & method
Living human cortical biopsies from shunt surgery with matched CSF and postmortem tissue; MALDI imaging mass spectrometry and protein-turnover analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76494-4

3TMEM59 regulates microglial Aβ phagocytosis in a TREM2-dependent manner

Alzheimers Dement  Jian Meng & Yun-Wu Zhang
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Microglial clearance of amyloid-β is central to AD, but its molecular brakes are poorly mapped. Microglia-specific deletion of TMEM59 in 5xFAD mice attenuated cognitive deficits, plaque burden and synapse loss, with single-cell RNA-seq linking the benefit to enhanced, TREM2-dependent phagocytosis—implying TMEM59 normally restrains amyloid uptake. To translate this, the authors built a chaperone-mediated-autophagy-targeting chimera (CMATAC) peptide that degrades TMEM59 and reported therapeutic efficacy, offering a degrader-based strategy for Aβ amyloidosis. Evidence is entirely mouse and cell-based.
Model & method
5xFAD mice with microglia-specific TMEM59 deletion; single-cell RNA-seq and a CMATAC degrader peptide.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71873

4Dynamic changes in plasma biomarkers of Alzheimer's disease in patients treated with lecanemab

Lancet Neurol  Rui Mu & Carlos Cruchaga
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Anti-amyloid antibodies slow decline in early AD, yet how blood markers move during treatment is largely unmapped. In a longitudinal real-world cohort of patients receiving biweekly lecanemab, the authors tracked a panel of 130 plasma proteins and related their changes to conversion to amyloid-PET-negative status and to cognitive trajectories. The design surfaces candidate response and neurodegeneration markers that shift on therapy, potentially enabling blood-based monitoring of amyloid clearance. As an observational clinical-care cohort rather than a randomized trial, treatment effects are not isolated from selection and confounding.
Model & method
Human longitudinal cohort on lecanemab; 130-protein plasma proteomic panel with amyloid-PET and cognitive endpoints.
https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00284-X

5Neural evidence that dogs segment speech with a humanlike consonant bias

Science  Boglárka Morvai & Attila Andics
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Humans, unlike nonhuman primates, weight consonants over vowels when segmenting speech despite vowels' greater acoustic salience. Using noninvasive EEG in both humans and dogs, the authors found enhanced word-level neural tracking—via intertrial coherence and event-related potentials—for consonant-structured over vowel-structured streams in both species. This indicates that a consonant bias, previously thought human-specific, can emerge in a non-speaking species exposed to human speech, suggesting linguistic regularity–based processing biases are not uniquely human. The finding is a neural-entrainment signature rather than a behavioral demonstration of word learning.
Model & method
Noninvasive EEG in dogs and humans; neural entrainment (intertrial coherence) and ERP measures on consonant- vs vowel-structured speech streams.
https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adw7709

Reconciling global collaboration and data sovereignty in neuroscience

Nat Neurosci  Kimberly L Ray & Franco Pestilli
Key findings
This perspective argues that intensifying national controls on data collection, use and cross-border transfer—driven by the strategic value of AI-ready data—are colliding with neuroscience's dependence on large, internationally distributed brain datasets. As population-scale neuroimaging, neurotechnology and computational modeling raise the value of neural data, the authors contend that fragmented governance risks narrowing representation, slowing innovation and entrenching global inequities in brain-health research, and they call for an international coordinating body for neuroscience data governance. This is a policy argument, not empirical research.
Model & method
Perspective/review on international neuroscience data governance.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02468-w

An in-body networking system for communication between wearable and implantable therapeutics

Science  Ramy Ghanim & Alex Abramson
Key findings
The authors engineered a communication platform that uses body tissue as a conductive medium to pass signals among implants and wearables across epidermal, subcutaneous, intraperitoneal and gastrointestinal spaces. Devices emit transient electric fields that selectively switch on target devices' transistor circuits; implants are syringe-injectable, draw negligible power while listening, and achieve more than tenfold greater tissue-communication coverage than Bluetooth. In rats, coordinated full-body networks of sensors and neural interfaces enabled wireless dual-limb motor control, a step toward closed-loop bioelectronic therapies with neural interfaces. Demonstrations are in rodents.
Model & method
Bioelectronic implants/wearables using tissue as a conductive medium; in vivo rat networks with neural interfaces for wireless motor control.
https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adz5300
Neurodegeneration & aging

Aberrant phase separation from a rare ABI3 mutation drives microglial dysfunction and Alzheimer's risk

Alzheimers Dement  Shengnan Li & Lili Cui
Key findings
The ABI3 S209F variant is a recognized AD risk factor of unclear mechanism. Across APP/PS1 and 5xFAD mice with microglia-specific ABI3 knockdown or wild-type/S209F expression, plus primary microglia and HMC3 cells, the authors show the mutation alters liquid-liquid phase separation properties of ABI3, linking aberrant condensate behavior to microglial dysfunction and disease pathology. This ties a phase-separation defect in a specific microglial protein to inherited AD risk. Causal evidence is mouse- and cell-based.
Model & method
APP/PS1 and 5xFAD mice with microglia-specific ABI3 manipulation; primary microglia, HMC3 cells and recombinant-protein LLPS assays.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71812

A cell-type-resolved meta-analysis reveals glial DNA methylation changes associated with aging and Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimers Dement  Uchit Bhaskar & Melanie A Carless
Key findings
Epigenome-wide studies implicate DNA methylation in AD but rarely resolve glial contributions. Pooling methylation data across 13 cohorts and using in silico cell-type deconvolution, the authors identify pronounced age-associated methylation signatures in prefrontal-cortex astrocytes and AD-associated changes across glial cell types, refining which epigenetic shifts track biological aging versus disease. Because cell-type signals are computationally deconvolved from bulk data rather than measured in sorted cells, the assignments are inferential.
Model & method
Meta-analysis of human DNA-methylation data across 13 cohorts with in silico glial cell-type deconvolution.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71864

Platelet count polygenic scores may modify the effect of aspirin in primary prevention of dementia

Alzheimers Dement  Peter D Fransquet & Paul Lacaze
Key findings
In the ASPREE randomized trial of aspirin in older adults (n=13,541), the authors screened thousands of polygenic risk scores for interactions with aspirin on dementia and major bleeding. Platelet-related polygenic scores were strongly enriched among interactions (18 of 112 nominally significant hits; enrichment OR ≈54.7), suggesting genetically defined platelet subgroups might differ in whether aspirin affects dementia risk. This is a hypothesis-generating pharmacogenomic interaction analysis, not evidence that aspirin prevents dementia in any subgroup.
Model & method
Secondary analysis of the ASPREE RCT; aspirin × polygenic-score interaction testing with Cox models.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71817

Personalized single-cell transcriptomics reveals molecular diversity in Alzheimer's disease

Nat Commun  Pramod Bharadwaj Chandrashekar & Daifeng Wang
Key findings
Applying a knowledge-guided graph neural network to population-scale single-nucleus RNA-seq spanning over 1,900 brains across cohorts, the authors learn per-donor functional-genomic representations that classify AD phenotypes and resolve molecularly defined patient subpopulations, capturing donor-level cell-type interactions and gene-regulatory networks. The framework offers a route to personalized molecular stratification of AD from brain transcriptomes. Findings rest on postmortem cross-sectional tissue, so the subtypes are molecular rather than clinically validated trajectories.
Model & method
Human postmortem single-nucleus RNA-seq (>1,900 brains); knowledge-guided graph neural network for donor-level functional genomics.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72310-1

Regulation of protein kinase pathways in a primary neuron model of anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis

J Neurosci  Yuyoung Joo & David R Benavides
Key findings
Anti-NMDAR encephalitis, the most common autoimmune encephalitis, is driven by antibodies that cross-link and internalize the GluN1 subunit, but the downstream signaling is unclear. Using a human GluN1 monoclonal antibody (5F5) in primary hippocampal neurons, the authors map protein-kinase pathway changes engaged when NMDARs are antibody-targeted, linking receptor internalization to specific intracellular signaling rather than to loss of surface receptors alone. Evidence is from a reductionist primary-neuron model with a single monoclonal antibody.
Model & method
Primary hippocampal neurons with a human GluN1 monoclonal antibody; protein-kinase pathway and receptor-trafficking assays.
https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0708-25.2026
