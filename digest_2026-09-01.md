### Endogenous opioid dynamics in the dorsal striatum sculpt neural activity to promote goal-directed action
**Raajaram Gowrishankar & Michael R Bruchas | Cell**

**Why it matters:** Using conditional deletions, a neuropeptide biosensor, two-photon imaging and time-locked optogenetics, the authors show that dynorphin release from dorsomedial striatum medium spiny neurons is both necessary and sufficient for goal-directed behavior, driven by glutamatergic input from the basolateral amygdala. This gives a causal, millisecond-timescale account of how an endogenous opioid peptide shapes striatal circuit function, moving neuropeptide biology beyond slow, diffuse modulation into fast, spatially precise control of behavior.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse dorsomedial striatum; conditional gene deletion, neuropeptide biosensor imaging, two-photon calcium imaging, time-locked optogenetics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.07.058

### Spatial mapping of RNA turnover kinetics in the mouse brain
**Qi Qiu & Hao Wu | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Spatial NT-seq is a new method that co-maps newly synthesized and pre-existing RNA directly on spatial transcriptomics platforms without transgenesis, giving the field a spatially resolved "in vivo timescope" for RNA turnover. Applied to mouse brain, it identifies the dentate gyrus as a hotspot of coordinated RNA synthesis/decay and reveals rapid, region-specific transcriptional responses to electroconvulsive stimulation — a technique advance with direct relevance to any lab studying region-specific gene regulation in brain, including neurodegeneration.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse brain; transgenesis-free metabolic RNA labeling combined with in situ chemical recoding on spatial transcriptomics, plus computational modeling of mRNA stability determinants.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02420-y

### Cross-tissue immune profiling of APOE ε4 reveals early dysregulation in Alzheimer's disease
**Artur Shvetcov & Caitlin A Finney | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** Integrating proteomics across plasma (n=9,028), CSF (n=1,099) and two cortical regions, the authors identify a conserved, allele-dose-dependent pro-inflammatory immune signature in APOE ε4 carriers that is present independent of AD diagnosis and appears in patient-derived cortical organoids *before* amyloid-β or tau pathology — arguing for a genotype-driven immune mechanism upstream of classical proteinopathy. A 12-week medical ketogenic diet partially reversed the signature, giving a tractable early intervention target; the diet effect is from a small ancillary cohort and should be read as hypothesis-generating rather than a validated treatment effect.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human plasma, CSF and postmortem dorsolateral prefrontal/superior temporal cortex; cross-tissue proteomics with patient-derived cortical organoid validation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71714

### Human-specific sequence features in HTT exon 1 promote toxic misprocessing via splicing factor SRSF7
**Camilla Maffezzini & Elena Cattaneo | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** Beyond CAG-repeat length, the authors identify the human-specific proline-rich domain of HTT exon 1 as a cis-regulatory element that promotes production of the toxic HTT1a transcript, and pin the mechanism on loss of SRSF7 binding sites present in the mouse but not human sequence — mutating those sites in a humanized mouse ES cell platform confirms SRSF7's suppressive role. This reframes HTT toxicity as sequence-context-dependent, not just repeat-length-dependent, and nominates splicing-based, PRD-focused strategies as a therapeutic avenue for Huntington's disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** HuntEx1-engineered mouse embryonic stem cell platform with humanized HTT exon 1 sequence; motif analysis and targeted mutagenesis of SRSF7 binding sites.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76208-w

## NEURODEGENERATION & AGING — TOP 5

### Longitudinal functional network connectivity changes across the clinical stages of C9orf72 hexanucleotide repeat expansion carriers
**Liwen Zhang & ARTFL/LEFFTDS/ALLFTD Consortia | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In task-free fMRI and structural MRI across 36 asymptomatic, 17 prodromal and 29 symptomatic C9orf72 expansion carriers versus 107 controls, salience, sensorimotor, default-mode and medial pulvinar thalamic network connectivity showed longitudinal, stage-specific changes. In asymptomatic carriers, connectivity change already correlated with baseline neurofilament light (NfL); in prodromal/symptomatic carriers it tracked with both NfL and symptom severity — evidence that functional network decline begins before overt symptoms and scales with an established fluid biomarker of neurodegeneration.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human C9orf72 hexanucleotide repeat expansion carriers across disease stages; longitudinal task-free and structural MRI with plasma NfL correlation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71744

### Multimodal Characterization of Glymphatic-Related Magnetic Resonance Imaging Markers in Huntington's Disease: A Multi-Cohort Retrospective Study
**Alexia Solomon & Jingwen Yao | Ann Neurol**

**Key findings:** Across 2,731 MRI sessions from 880 participants in three large HD cohorts, the DTI-ALPS glymphatic index declined progressively with HD stage (stage 3: −6.43%, d=−0.46) while subcortical enlarged perivascular space burden rose sharply (up to +63.7% at stage 3), and lower ALPS correlated with greater motor impairment and reduced functional capacity. Both glymphatic markers improved prediction of HD progression beyond CAG-Age Product Score and striatal volume, though effect sizes were low-to-moderate and the design is cross-sectional/retrospective, so causal or purely fluid-clearance interpretations of the imaging signal remain provisional.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human HD gene carriers (HD-ISS stages 0–3) across three retrospective cohorts; DTI-ALPS and enlarged-perivascular-space MRI metrics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78344

### A machine learning framework for predicting and modulating condition-dependent protein phase separation
**Jangwon Bae & Yongwon Jung | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** LLPSense combines protein language model embeddings with environmental parameters (concentration, temperature, solvent) to predict condition-dependent phase separation, outperforming sequence-only models and correctly identifying previously unrecognized reentrant phase behavior in SGTA. Applied to Parkinson's disease-associated α-synuclein, the model accurately predicts which mutations enhance or suppress phase separation and enables model-guided mutagenesis to modulate condensate behavior — a computational tool directly applicable to mechanistic studies of aggregation-prone proteins in neurodegeneration.

**Model/system & primary method:** Computational/machine learning framework (protein language model embeddings + environmental parameters) with experimental validation of predicted phase-separation behavior, including PD-linked α-synuclein mutants.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76248-2

### Treatment of multiple sclerosis with peptide-coupled red blood cells induces antigen-specific T regulatory cells
**Andreas Lutterotti & Roland Martin | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** In a phase Ib dose-escalation trial, autologous red blood cells coupled to seven immunodominant myelin peptides were safe and well tolerated in MS patients, with reduced serum neurofilament light suggesting decreased CNS tissue inflammation. Single-cell RNA-seq showed expansion of antigen-specific CD4+ regulatory-phenotype memory T cells and contraction of proinflammatory cells by three months post-infusion, accompanied by an early rise in IL-10, Tr1 cells and tolerogenic antigen-presenting cells — mechanistic evidence for antigen-specific immune tolerance induction, though this is an open-label, small-n safety/mechanism trial without a placebo arm.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human phase Ib open-label dose-escalation clinical trial in MS; peptide-coupled autologous red blood cell infusion with clinical, imaging and single-cell transcriptomic readouts (plus mouse/human biodistribution studies).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2614908123

### Regional ventricular enlargement as an in vivo biomarker of cumulative biomechanical neurodegeneration in former American football players
**Brian Szekely & DIAGNOSE CTE Research Project | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In 170 former American football players versus 54 age-matched controls, manual segmentation showed larger left inferior lateral ventricle volume in former players, with the largest effects in former professional athletes, and greater cumulative linear/rotational head-acceleration exposure was associated with enlargement across lateral ventricular and inferior horn regions — proposing regional ventricular morphology as an in vivo structural correlate of cumulative repetitive head impact exposure. The design is cross-sectional and associative (no direct pathological confirmation of CTE), so this should be read as a candidate exposure biomarker rather than a diagnostic one.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human former American football players and matched controls; subject-specific manual MRI segmentation of ventricular structures correlated with quantified head-impact exposure history.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71764

### Meta-analysis of glucose metabolism across Alzheimer's, Parkinson's and ALS reveals distinct patterns of cortical hypometabolism versus subcortical hypermetabolism with disease-specific spatial signatures
**Raikes, A. C. & Brinton, R. D. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Pooling 130 FDG-PET studies (5,298 patients, 3,499 controls), coordinate-based meta-analysis finds that all three late-onset neurodegenerative diseases (AD, PD, ALS) show both cortical hypometabolism and subcortical/limbic/cerebellar hypermetabolism, with disease-specific spatial signatures tracking each disease's known functional networks. The hypermetabolic component — consistently under-emphasized relative to hypometabolism in the literature — is interpreted cautiously as possible compensatory upregulation, though neuroinflammation and other mechanisms cannot be excluded from PET coordinate data alone.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human FDG-PET literature (130 studies); activation likelihood estimation meta-analysis of hypo- and hypermetabolic coordinates across AD, PD and ALS.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.07.26350339

### Characterization and pharmacological modulation of Alzheimer's disease-associated human microglial states
**Garcia-Diaz Barriga, G. & Gruber-Schoffnegger, D. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Integrating single-nucleus transcriptomics across the AD spectrum, the authors define a disease- and lipid-associated microglia (DLaM) state enriched in AD genetic risk carriers and linked to neuropathology and cognitive decline, then reproduce this state in hiPSC-derived microglia using ferric ammonium citrate (lipid accumulation, lysosomal dysfunction, impaired Aβ phagocytosis). A transcriptomics-guided screen identifies LY2090314 as a compound that reverses the DLaM-like state and restores microglial function — a candidate therapeutic modulator, though efficacy is so far limited to an in vitro hiPSC model.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human single-nucleus transcriptomic datasets across the AD spectrum; hiPSC-derived microglia perturbation and transcriptomics-based compound screening.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.26.747247

### Early MATR3 loss and distinct neurodegenerative molecular signatures precede the onset of neuropathology in motor neurons and Purkinje cells of MATR3 S85C knock-in mouse model of ALS
**Maksimovic, K. & Park, J. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In a MATR3 S85C knock-in mouse model of early-stage ALS, loss of detectable MATR3 protein in both spinal motor neurons and cerebellar Purkinje cells precedes any motor dysfunction or overt neuropathology, marking it as the earliest detectable molecular event. RNA profiling reveals cell-type-specific downstream signatures — Ngfr induction and JNK-mediated cell death signaling in Purkinje cells versus elevated Fgf21 and integrated stress response genes in motor neurons (echoing prior SOD1-model findings) — suggesting distinct degenerative programs in the two ALS-vulnerable neuron types despite a shared initiating lesion.

**Model/system & primary method:** MATR3 S85C knock-in mouse model of ALS; bulk cerebellar and motor-neuron-specific RNA profiling with immunohistochemistry.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.26.747343

### Nigro-striatal deficits capture phenoconversion risk in isolated REM sleep behavior disorder
**Johansson, M. & Lehericy, S. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 56 patients with isolated REM sleep behavior disorder followed for up to 11 years (24 phenoconverted to PD, DLB or MSA), reduced striatal dopamine transporter binding conferred the expected phenoconversion risk (HR 3.1), and neuromelanin-MRI-based substantia nigra and locus coeruleus/subcoeruleus cell loss independently predicted conversion risk when modeled together (HR 2.8 and 2.3 respectively) — indicating combined dopaminergic and noradrenergic degeneration contribute independently to prodromal synucleinopathy, and that a single neuromelanin-MRI scan could substitute for separate nuclear-imaging dopaminergic and noradrenergic assessments. Sample size is modest (n=56) for a Cox regression with multiple predictors.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human isolated RBD patients (ICEBERG study), 11-year longitudinal follow-up; 123I-FP-CIT SPECT and neuromelanin-sensitive/diffusion MRI with Cox proportional hazards analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.26.26361210

### Cerebrospinal fluid YWHAG:NPTX2 ratio predicts clinical severity and future phenoconversion in frontotemporal lobar degeneration
**Oh, H. S.-H. & Pascual, B. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Across four independent cohorts, the CSF synaptic-protein ratio YWHAG:NPTX2 (previously identified as an AD cognitive-decline biomarker independent of amyloid/tau) is elevated across sporadic and familial FTLD syndromes and correlates with clinical severity independent of neurofilament light. In presymptomatic familial FTLD (C9orf72, GRN, MAPT carriers) the ratio is estimated to rise roughly a decade before symptom onset and improves prediction of imminent conversion 1.7-fold over plasma NfL alone, which the authors project could more than halve required sample sizes for FTLD prevention trials — a modeled trial-design benefit rather than a demonstrated one.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human CSF across four independent cohorts (UCSF-MAC, ALLFTD, GENFI, PDBP) spanning sporadic and familial FTLD; synaptic protein ratio biomarker assay.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.22.746470
