### Bone marrow myelopoiesis dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease limits monocyte homing to the brain and drives disease progression
**Miguel Angel Abellanas & Michal Schwartz | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Boosting monocyte-derived macrophage recruitment to the brain is known to slow pathology in amyloidosis mouse models, but what limits this recruitment in the first place was unclear. The authors show that in 5×FAD mice — and in circulating monocytes from AD patients — monocyte differentiation in the bone marrow is disrupted by a maladaptive type I interferon (IFN-I) response. Blocking IFN-I signaling with neutralizing antibodies, or reconstituting mice with IFN-I receptor-deficient bone marrow, restored normal myelopoiesis, increased monocyte homing to the brain, and ameliorated disease pathology. This is a causal demonstration (in mice) that a systemic, peripheral immune defect drives AD progression, and it nominates IFN-I blockade as a therapeutic strategy to restore protective monocyte trafficking.

**Model/system & primary method:** 5×FAD amyloidosis mice plus bone marrow transplant/reconstitution and IFN-I receptor blockade; parallel monocyte phenotyping in human AD patients.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02417-7

### Intestinal infections establish antigen-specific, long-lived memory CD4+ T cells in the brain and meninges
**Aaron Fleming & Menna R Clatworthy | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** The meninges are increasingly recognized as an immunological interface between the CNS and periphery, but how they acquire "memory" of prior systemic infections was unclear. This study shows that gastrointestinal infections (bacterial or parasitic) reprogram dural CD4+ T cell polarization (toward TH1, TH17 or TH2 states depending on pathogen class) via CXCR6–CXCL16-dependent trafficking, seeding long-lived, antigen-specific memory populations in dural lymphoid aggregates and the brain itself. These cells mount rapid recall responses on rechallenge. The finding establishes a direct gut–meninges immunological memory axis, relevant to how peripheral infection history could shape neuroinflammatory set-points and CNS immune surveillance over a lifetime — all evidence here is in mice.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse models of enteric bacterial and parasitic infection; meningeal/dural immune profiling, CXCR6-CXCL16 pathway manipulation, antigen-specific rechallenge.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02428-4

### Are infraslow oscillations the missing link between sleep and Alzheimer's?
**Demetrio Grollero & Michele Bellesi | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** Locus coeruleus (LC) dysfunction and impaired glymphatic clearance are both implicated in AD, and both have been linked separately to infraslow oscillations (ISOs) in sleep spindle (sigma) activity. This study analyzed NREM sleep EEG in AD patients versus controls and found selectively reduced ISO peak amplitude in AD, with intrinsic frequency and bandwidth preserved; ISO amplitude trended with plasma Aβ42/40, while ISO bandwidth correlated with neurofilament light chain and (as a trend) GFAP and verbal memory retention. The authors propose ISO integrity as a candidate electrophysiological marker linking LC-dependent sleep microarchitecture to glymphatic function and AD pathology — a plausible mechanistic bridge between two previously separate AD risk pathways, though the associations with fluid biomarkers are correlational and some are only trend-level.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human NREM sleep EEG in AD patients and controls; sigma-band infraslow oscillation extraction correlated with plasma AD biomarkers (Aβ42/40, pTau181, pTau217, NfL, GFAP).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71525

### Fibronectin inhibition restores myelination in endothelial TNFR2-dependent nonremitting experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis
**Aikaterini Nanou & George Kollias | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Why it matters:** Remyelination failure over the course of MS drives sustained neurodegeneration, but why some lesions never repair is poorly understood. Using EAE, the authors show that endothelial-specific (not immune or global) loss of TNFR2 is sufficient to reproduce the severe, nonremitting phenotype seen with global TNFR2 deletion, independent of immune infiltration or vascular permeability changes. Instead, endothelial TNFR2 loss drives fibronectin accumulation in demyelinating lesions, which traps oligodendrocyte progenitor cells and blocks myelin repair — a phenotype reproduced in the Cuprizone remyelination model. Systemic fibronectin inhibition restored remyelination and clinical remission in mice, identifying the vascular/perivascular matrix, rather than the immune compartment per se, as a druggable node for promoting CNS repair in MS.

**Model/system & primary method:** EAE and Cuprizone mouse models with endothelial cell-specific TNFR2 deletion; bulk/single-nucleus RNA-seq and pharmacological fibronectin inhibition.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2616799123

### Increased amyloidogenic neuronal injury in HIV-1-infected APP-KI Alzheimer's disease mice
**Shaurav Bhattarai & Howard E Gendelman | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** People living with HIV-1 show elevated dementia incidence, but no animal model previously allowed concurrent study of HIV-1 replication and AD pathology. The authors engineered a humanized AD mouse (NOG/APP-KI background reconstituted with human CD34+ HSCs, developing human microglia-like cells) that supports progressive brain HIV-1 replication. HIV-1 infection increased brain amyloid-β load and reduced synaptic/neuronal integrity; spatial transcriptomics showed that Aβ and HIV-1 drive distinct transcriptional programs individually, but their combination amplifies AD-like pathology, with neurons showing the largest changes in genes tied to neuroinflammation, protein trafficking, and synaptic dysfunction — a new model system for a growing, understudied dementia-risk population.

**Model/system & primary method:** Novel humanized APP-knock-in AD mouse model (hNAIL) with HIV-1ADA infection; spatial transcriptomics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71807

### Phosphorylation of α-synuclein directs trafficking of the v-SNARE synaptobrevin-2
**Elyas H Arvell & Sarah L Gordon | J Neurosci**

**Key findings:** α-Synuclein's native presynaptic function remains poorly defined despite its central role in Parkinson's disease pathology. Using molecular replacement in α-synuclein-knockout mouse neuronal cultures, the authors show that activity-dependent phosphorylation at Thr81 (one of two newly identified activity-regulated phosphosites, alongside Ser42) controls the endocytic retrieval route of synaptobrevin-2/VAMP2, a core fusogenic SNARE and known α-synuclein binding partner. Ablating Thr81 phosphorylation accelerated synaptobrevin-2 retrieval during stimulation; phosphomimetic Thr81 slowed it — without affecting global endocytosis, evoked exocytosis, or recycling pool size. This narrows in on a specific, phosphorylation-gated mechanism by which α-synuclein tunes presynaptic vesicle trafficking, a function whose disruption may be relevant to synaptic dysfunction in PD.

**Model/system & primary method:** Primary neuronal cultures from α-synuclein knockout mice; phosphovariant molecular replacement and live imaging of synaptobrevin-2/VAMP2 trafficking.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1860-25.2026

### Size-tailored nanoparticle-antibody conjugates overcome hepatic sequestration in Alzheimer's disease treatment
**Xuewei Du & Haiming Luo | Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A**

**Key findings:** Anti-amyloid antibody immunotherapy is limited by rapid hepatic sequestration and complement-driven liver toxicity. The authors conjugated an anti-Aβ42 antibody (1F12) to 450-nm PEGylated mesoporous silica nanoparticles and showed that, in APP/PS1 mice, intravenous dosing enhanced peripheral Aβ clearance via intestinal excretion, reshaped gut microbiota, reduced intestinal inflammation, and lowered hepatic Aβ burden — while also reducing brain Aβ deposition, microglial activation, and improving cognitive performance. This is a mouse proof-of-concept for engineering antibody biodistribution (rather than target affinity) to solve a major practical bottleneck in anti-Aβ immunotherapy.

**Model/system & primary method:** APP/PS1 amyloidosis mice; PEGylated mesoporous silica nanoparticle-antibody conjugates administered intravenously.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2603034123

### Non-contrast CT markers of hematoma expansion and andexanet response in factor Xa inhibitor-associated intracerebral hemorrhage: an analysis from the ANNEXa-I trial
**Koji Tanaka & Andrew M Demchuk | Ann Neurol**

**Key findings:** In this post hoc analysis of the ANNEXa-I trial (n=434), at least one non-contrast CT marker of hematoma expansion risk (heterogeneous density, hypodensity, black hole sign) was present in 72.1% of factor Xa inhibitor-associated intracerebral hemorrhage cases, and hematoma expansion (≥12.5 mL or ≥35% volume increase) occurred in 31.8% overall. These markers were independently associated with expansion risk, and andexanet's effect on limiting expansion was generally consistent across marker subgroups — supporting the use of simple CT features to risk-stratify patients for anticoagulant-reversal therapy, though as a post hoc trial analysis this is hypothesis-supporting rather than a prespecified primary endpoint.

**Model/system & primary method:** Post hoc analysis of a randomized clinical trial (ANNEXa-I, n=434) using non-contrast CT imaging markers.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78347

### Polyanionic peptibody ameliorates tau pathology and promotes neuronal proteostasis in a tau transgenic mouse model
**A. C. Caruso & R. M. Nisbet | bioRxiv** *(v2 revision of a preprint first posted August 2025, then titled "Targeting intracellular tau with a gene-encoded single-chain antibody promotes neuronal homeostasis and ameliorates tau pathology"; data and conclusions unchanged)*

**Key findings:** Intraneuronal tau aggregation depends on exposure of positively charged repeat domains that drive pathogenic tau-tau interactions. The authors engineered a polyanionic peptide fused to an inert antibody fragment (scFv) — the "peptibody" ACR12 — designed to electrostatically neutralize these charged domains, delivered via brain-penetrant AAV-PHP.eB. In K3 tau transgenic mice, long-term AAV-driven ACR12 expression reduced total and phosphorylated tau and restored deregulated neuronal proteins toward wild-type levels. As a preprint with mouse-only data (no human or clinical evidence), this should be read as an early-stage therapeutic proof-of-concept for electrostatic tau interactors rather than a validated treatment strategy.

**Model/system & primary method:** K3 tau transgenic mice; AAV-PHP.eB-delivered polyanionic peptibody (ACR12) targeting intracellular tau.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.08.13.665777

### Synonymous HTT CAA/CCA-loss variants associated with early Huntington disease onset enhance toxicity beyond somatic instability
**G. L. Sequiera & M. A. Pouladi | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Loss of CAA/CCA interruptions within the HTT polyglutamine-coding CAG tract is linked clinically to earlier Huntington disease onset, previously attributed to increased somatic CAG repeat instability. Using cellular models (including MSH3-knockout cells in which somatic instability is blunted), the authors show that CAA/CCA-loss variants retain significantly higher toxicity than canonical HTT independent of somatic instability, following a consistent toxicity hierarchy (CAA/CCA-loss > CCA-loss > CAA-loss > canonical) and producing elevated repeat-associated non-AUG (RAN) translation products. This points to sequence-context-intrinsic toxicity — not just repeat instability — as a contributor to disease-modifying effects in HD, evidence to date confined to cell-line models.

**Model/system & primary method:** HEK293-based and stable cell-line models of mutant HTT, including MSH3 knockout lines to isolate somatic-instability-independent effects.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.749261

### Hippocampal/medial temporal sclerosis is associated with clinical severity, regional TDP-43, and hippocampal atrophy in Alzheimer disease
**P. Sharma & S. Ramamoorthy | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Alzheimer disease neuropathologic change alone does not fully explain clinical severity. In a multicenter autopsy cohort (National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center), hippocampal/medial temporal lobe (HS/MTL) sclerosis was present in 13.0% of AD cases and was strongly associated with greater dementia severity in both AD (OR 2.56) and non-AD strata (OR 4.93), and with higher regional TDP-43 burden (OR 6.54) and hippocampal atrophy (OR 6.43). Among AD cases assessed within two years of death, absence of HS/MTL sclerosis identified a subgroup with substantial AD pathology but no dementia — i.e., clinical severity cannot be inferred from amyloid/tau burden alone without accounting for this co-pathology. This is cross-sectional/observational autopsy data, so directionality of the sclerosis-severity relationship is not established.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multicenter autopsy cohort (NACC), longitudinal clinical data linked to harmonized neuropathology staging (versions 8–10).

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.749099

### SPP1+ microglia are associated with neuroimmune rewiring and glutamatergic neuronal injury in ART-suppressed people living with HIV
**C. S. Shabangu & G. Jiang | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Antiretroviral therapy (ART) suppresses systemic HIV but not brain viral reservoirs. Using bulk, single-cell, and single-nucleus transcriptomics of postmortem human brain tissue, the authors identify an activated SPP1+ microglial population — expanded 5.8-fold under ART — that serves as the dominant CNS HIV reservoir, adopting an immune-evasive state (chronic type I interferon signaling, inflammasome activation, disrupted HLA-A/HLA-E presentation via nef) reproduced in primary human microglia with prolonged interferon-β exposure. This state is linked to TREM2-C1QC complement-mediated synaptic pruning and a marked loss of VGLUT1+/GAD67+ glutamatergic neurons that persists despite viral suppression — nominating SPP1, MHC-I, and complement pathways as targets for reversing HIV-associated neurocognitive impairment. Findings are from postmortem human tissue (necessarily correlational/cross-sectional) complemented by in vitro microglial modeling.

**Model/system & primary method:** Postmortem human brain tissue from ART-suppressed people living with HIV; bulk/single-cell/single-nucleus transcriptomics and primary human microglia interferon-exposure modeling.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.04.749241

### Analysis of east Asian Parkinson's disease genomes identifies novel susceptibility loci and functional regulatory variation
**Q. Sun & E.-K. Tan | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** PD genetics has been overwhelmingly derived from European-ancestry cohorts. This is the largest East Asian PD GWAS to date (5,825 East Asian participants, expanded to 74,716 in meta-analysis), identifying two novel genome-wide-significant loci (TLE4; HLA-V/HLA-G) and an East Asian-specific regulatory variant at the LRRK2 locus (rs1388594) that associates with LRRK2 expression in the basal ganglia and replicates across three independent East Asian cohorts — but shows no association in European-ancestry populations (P = 0.08), pointing to ancestry-specific regulatory variation at a locus already central to PD drug development (LRRK2 inhibitors). As a preprint, this awaits peer review before its two novel loci and the LRRK2 regulatory finding can be considered established.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human GWAS and meta-analysis (East Asian PD cohorts, n=74,716 combined) with brain eQTL integration at the basal ganglia.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.26362212
