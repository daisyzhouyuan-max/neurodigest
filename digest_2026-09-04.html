1Somatic mutations reveal the ontogeny of microglia in human ageing

Nature  Julia A Belk & Siddhartha Jaiswal
Why it matters
Using somatic-mutation and mitochondrial-DNA lineage tracing in 20 older individuals, the authors show that bone-marrow-derived myeloid cells infiltrate the human brain throughout life and can comprise a substantial fraction of the microglial pool with ageing — challenging the assumption, drawn largely from mouse work, that human microglia are maintained almost entirely by embryonic self-renewal. In cohort analyses, most forms of clonal haematopoiesis were associated with reduced Alzheimer's disease risk, linking bone-marrow clonal dynamics to microglial identity and AD susceptibility.
Model & method
Human post-mortem brain (n=20 older individuals); somatic/mitochondrial DNA lineage tracing and single-cell RNA-seq, with cohort-level clonal haematopoiesis–AD association analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10939-0

2Priming of CD8+ T cells by peripheral dendritic cells exacerbates tau-mediated neurodegeneration

Nat Neurosci  Hao Hu & David M Holtzman
Why it matters
Identifies a peripheral immune step upstream of the CD8+ T cell accumulation seen in tauopathy and Alzheimer's disease: conventional type 1 dendritic cells (cDC1s) cross-present brain-derived antigen in secondary lymphoid tissue to prime CD8+ T cells that later infiltrate the brain and track with tau pathology severity. Tauopathy mice lacking cDC1s or cross-presentation were protected from neurodegeneration, with reduced brain CD8+ T cell infiltration and glial activation — pointing to peripheral antigen priming, not just brain-intrinsic immunity, as a druggable node.
Model & method
Mouse tauopathy models genetically lacking cDC1s or antigen cross-presentation; immune profiling of brain and secondary lymphoid tissue.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02427-5

3Intracellular complement factor H protects neurons during CNS inflammation

Nature  Christina Mayer & Manuel A Friese
Why it matters
Reveals a non-canonical, secretion-independent function for complement factor H (CFH) inside neurons: inflammation- and oxidative-stress-induced CFH localizes to the endoplasmic reticulum and limits reactive oxygen species and lipid peroxidation — a key driver of neuronal ferroptosis — conferring resilience to retinal ganglion cell degeneration in multiple sclerosis. Protection depends on the C-terminal SCR20 domain and persists without complement component C3, distinguishing this mechanism from classical complement-pathway biology and suggesting a new neuroprotective target.
Model & method
Human donor retinal ganglion cell single-nucleus RNA-seq (MS vs. control) with mouse genetic and biochemical validation.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10981-y

4Vps35 p.D620N causes Lrrk2 kinase hyperactivity, chronic microglial activation and inflammation

bioRxiv  Deng, I. B. & Farrer, M. J.
Why it matters
Directly links a dominant Parkinson's-causing VPS35 mutation to microglial dysfunction: Vps35 p.D620N knock-in mice have constitutively hyperactive LRRK2 kinase in microglia, and single-cell RNA-seq reveals a chronic pro-inflammatory microglial state (upregulated S100 family genes and lipocalin-2, altered lysosomal stress sensing and phagocytosis, reduced homeostatic/synaptic-surveillance signaling) that is further exacerbated by peripheral LPS challenge. Mechanistically bridges three convergent PD genes (LRRK2, RAB32, VPS35) to innate immune dysregulation as a candidate driver of dopaminergic vulnerability.
Model & method
Vps35 p.D620N knock-in mice; microglial single-cell RNA-seq, immunohistochemistry, peripheral LPS challenge.

5Stroke and Alzheimer's disease have distinct consequences on neurovascular function but synergize to increase amyloid deposition

bioRxiv  Ismail, O. & Mishra, A.
Why it matters
In an amyloid mouse model, mild subcortical ischemia and Aβ pathology each chronically impair cerebrovascular reactivity and neurovascular coupling through largely distinct spatiotemporal trajectories, yet the combined ischemia+Aβ group showed the least residual cerebrovascular dysfunction but the greatest increase in Aβ deposition. Suggests stroke and AD pathology are mechanistically coupled but not through simple additive vascular damage, with direct relevance to understanding mixed vascular-amyloid dementia.
Model & method
Tg2576 amyloid mouse model with transient mild subcortical ischemia; chronic cerebrovascular reactivity imaging and post-mortem astrocyte/Aβ histology.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.29.746901

Structured experience shapes strategy learning and neural dynamics in the medial entorhinal cortex

Nat Neurosci  John C Bowler & James G Heys
Key findings
In an odor-timing task, both mice and recurrent neural networks trained with poorly structured early curricula developed rigid, error-prone strategies, whereas structured early training produced entorhinal cortex dynamics that reflected the task's temporal structure and supported abstraction and generalization as task complexity increased. Dynamical-systems analysis linked curriculum structure directly to how flexible, generalizable neural representations emerge.
Model & method

A descending glycinergic circuit drives opioid-resistant mechanical pain via spinal GluN3A excitatory glycine receptors in mice

Neuron  Dong Dong & Longzhen Cheng
Key findings
A descending circuit from GlyT2+ neurons in the rostral ventromedial medulla to somatostatin+ neurons in the spinal dorsal horn is necessary and sufficient for morphine-resistant mechanical allodynia. Glycine released from this pathway activates GluN3A-containing NMDA receptors acting as unconventional excitatory glycine receptors, bypassing opioid suppression and driving the opioid-resistant pain state, including craniofacial pain via trigeminal and limbic inputs — nominating GluN3A-containing NMDARs as a target for intractable pain.
Model & method
Mice; optogenetic/chemogenetic circuit manipulation, electrophysiology, behavioral pain assays.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.003

The organization of visual pathways in the Drosophila brain

Cell  Judith Hoeller & Michael B Reiser
Key findings
Using the full male Drosophila connectome, the authors trace photoreceptor signals through the optic lobes into the central brain, revealing a multilayered architecture of parallel pathway classes with distinct input mixtures. Signals from visual-input channels spread broadly but converge onto focal regions specialized for particular features and fine spatial sampling; receptive-field and feature-bias predictions matched physiological data and extended to thousands of previously uncharacterized neuron types.
Model & method
Drosophila connectome (electron microscopy); network-based analysis of the optic lobes and central brain, cross-validated against physiology.

Sexual dimorphism in the complete Drosophila male central nervous system connectome

Key findings
Presents the full connectome of the Drosophila male CNS (166,700 neurons, 11,710 neuron types) and the first comprehensive synaptic-resolution comparison to the female connectome: 8,069 isomorphic, 138 dimorphic, 289 male-specific, and 71 female-specific neuron types. Glutamate concentration tunes AMPA receptor function through conductance-state occupancy

Nat Neurosci  Gokulakrishna Banumurthy & Jacques I Wadiche
Key findings
At mouse interneuron synapses, synaptic glutamate concentration — not just AMPA receptor subunit composition — governs key biophysical AMPAR properties (current-voltage rectification, polyamine block, Ca2+ permeability); lower glutamate reduces these hallmark features via differential spermine affinity across conductance states, resolved through isolated-receptor recordings and numerical simulation. Identifies a previously unrecognized layer of control over excitatory synaptic signaling beyond subunit composition.

Key findings
Opioids and methamphetamine, but not natural rewards, trigger mitochondrial calcium influx via the mitochondrial calcium uniporter (MCU) specifically during high-intensity dopaminergic neuron activity in nucleus accumbens terminals, driving rapid ATP production that compensates for hyperactivity-induced energy deficits and sustains dopamine release. Genetic or pharmacological MCU inhibition in dopaminergic neurons selectively blunted drug-induced dopamine release and addictive behaviors while sparing natural reward processing.

Metabolic reallocation in spinal cord oligodendrocytes drives chronic pain in mice through neuronal Aβ42 production

Sci Transl Med  Yannick Fotio & Daniele Piomelli
Key findings
After peripheral injury, spinal cord oligodendrocytes downregulate myelin protein synthesis and upregulate lipid biosynthesis, disrupting spinal myelin composition and compromising axonal integrity; this drives neuronal APP accumulation, BACE1 upregulation, and increased insoluble Aβ42 during the critical window for transition to chronic pain. Blocking Aβ42 production (BACE1 inhibition) or clearing it (anti-Aβ antibody) prevented persistent pain, as did oligodendrocyte-specific deletion of NAAA — identifying an NAAA→Aβ42 axis linking spinal myelin metabolism to chronic pain and amyloid biology.

Changes in five cerebrospinal fluid and three plasma Alzheimer's disease biomarkers across increasing brain amyloid-beta and tau pathology burden

Acta Neuropathol  Andrea Mastrangelo & Piero Parchi
Key findings
In 250 participants with post-mortem-validated Aβ/tau pathology (prion and non-prion disease; median sampling-to-death interval 1–1.5 months), CSF Aβ42/Aβ40 declined earliest (by the second quartile of Aβ burden, with highest accuracy at low/intermediate Aβ, AUC 0.984), while CSF p-tau181/p-tau217 rose only from the third quartile and performed best at advanced Aβ/intermediate tau burden. Plasma p-tau217 and p-tau217/Aβ42 increased only in the highest Aβ/tau quartiles. Supports a sequential biomarker model across the AD neuropathological continuum but shows no current fluid biomarker captures the earliest phase of Aβ deposition.
Model & method
Human neuropathological validation cohort (ante-mortem CSF/plasma vs. post-mortem Aβ/tau staging); multivariable regression and sequential ROC analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03076-5

Cell Rep  Jiannan Zhang & Kang Zheng
Key findings
Inflammation aberrantly activates Wnt signaling in astrocytes, increasing astrocytic SREBF2 and intracellular cholesterol while suppressing APOE expression and cholesterol efflux. Mov Disord  Adrian L Asendorf & Merle C Hoenig
Key findings
In 28 controls, 60 prodromal, and 94 clinical PD patients, lower putaminal dopamine transporter binding was associated with higher "network attack tolerance" (a graph-theoretic measure of resilience to hub loss) selectively in the somatomotor network, and PD patients showed elevated somatomotor network attack tolerance relative to controls. Genotoxic and metabolic stress drive divergent senescence programs in human microglia

bioRxiv  Platt, B. & Janssens, S.
Key findings
In the human microglial cell line HMC3, chronic DNA damage (doxorubicin) and chronic high-glucose metabolic stress both induced hallmark senescence features (cellular/nuclear hypertrophy, elevated SA-β-galactosidase activity, reduced metabolic viability without cell loss) via p53-p21 activation, but diverged downstream: genotoxic stress produced a chemokine-rich senescence-associated secretory phenotype with NRF2-TFAM downregulation, while metabolic stress induced p16 expression, peripheral p21 localization, and an interferon-associated inflammatory signature with a constrained antioxidant response. Indicates that distinct age-related stressors drive mechanistically separable senescent microglial states relevant to chronic neuroinflammation in brain aging.
Model & method
Human microglial cell line (HMC3) exposed to genotoxic vs. metabolic stress; senescence phenotyping and transcriptomic/pathway analysis.

Proteoform-resolved neoGFAP as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker across the TBI–MCI–AD continuum in Veterans

medRxiv  Haskins, W. E. & Peskind, E. R.
Key findings
In Veterans spanning traumatic brain injury, mild cognitive impairment, and Alzheimer's disease, a calpain-cleaved GFAP neoepitope (neoGFAP) outperformed total GFAP for discriminating MCI+AD from non-AD (CSF AUROC 0.81 vs. 0.73 overall, reaching significance in an amyloid-positive subset) and for distinguishing MCI from chronic blast-exposed Veterans without cognitive impairment. In chronic blast injury, neoGFAP was paradoxically depleted versus controls, consistent with tissue sequestration of aggregated fragments. Plasma neoGFAP reached AUROC 0.90 (comparable to pTau217's 0.92), and adding CSF markers and age improved diagnostic accuracy from 71% to 80% — though the CSF benchmarking cohort was small (n=57).
Model & method
Human Veteran cohort (plasma triage n=367; CSF benchmarking n=57) spanning TBI–MCI–AD; proteoform-specific immunoassay plus unbiased proteomics.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.01.26361845
