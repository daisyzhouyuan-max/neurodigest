1EBV reactivation priming of the peripheral immune system in multiple sclerosis relapse

Nat Med  Devin A King & Tanuja Chitnis
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
In longitudinal blood from relapsing-remitting MS patients, a reproducible transcriptional signature in monocytes and B cells appeared up to three months before clinical relapse and was enriched for host genes responsive to Epstein-Barr virus lytic reactivation. RT-qPCR confirmed elevated EBV LMP-1 transcripts in pre-relapse B cells, and flow cytometry showed expansion of CD11c+ atypical B cells bearing the EBV surface protein gp350. Pre-relapse modules overlapped MS GWAS risk loci and EBNA-2-bound enhancers, suggesting inherited susceptibility and EBV-responsive programs share regulatory elements — a mechanistic bridge between the epidemiological EBV–MS link and relapse timing. The study is observational and how peripheral activation relates to CNS lesion formation is not established.
Model & method
Human RRMS longitudinal blood; single-cell and bulk RNA-seq, multiparameter flow cytometry, targeted EBV RT-qPCR.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04665-3

2Developmental xenocortication using human-derived organoids in mice

Nature  Konstantin Kaganovsky & Sergiu P Pașca
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
The authors genetically depleted glutamatergic neurons from mouse neocortex and hippocampus ("apallial" mice) and neonatally engrafted the vacated cortical cavity with human stem-cell-derived cortical organoids, generating xenocortical mice in which human tissue occupied most of the cortical volume. Grafts produced diverse human cortical cell types, including layer 5 extratelencephalic projection neurons, integrated with the mouse nervous system, and showed organized, developmentally patterned activity by graft-wide calcium imaging and electrophysiology. Behaviourally, apallial and xenocortical mice retained broadly normal locomotion with selective deficits in limb coordination and altered organization of spontaneous behaviour. By removing host circuit competition, the platform enables far more extensive human cortical integration than prior transplantation approaches, opening in vivo study of human neurodevelopment and disease.
Model & method
Human iPSC-derived cortical organoids transplanted into genetically apallial mice; in vivo calcium imaging, electrophysiology, behavioural phenotyping.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11032-2

3Predictive and instructive cerebellar encoding of dopamine reward drives motivated behavior

Nat Neurosci  Benjamin A Filio & Mark J Wagner
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
By training mice to push a lever for delayed dopamine delivered directly into the brain, the authors dissociated reward from the consummatory movements that normally confound cerebellar reward signals. Two-photon imaging showed granule cells predictively encoding dopamine reward with sustained activity that "stretched" to bridge 1- or 2-second delays and terminated at reward receipt, while climbing fibers spiked just after delivery — a predictive and an instructive code, respectively. Both were causal: chronic granule-cell inhibition disrupted self-stimulation learning, and climbing-fiber "rewards" alone drove operant learning in naive animals. Encoding of dopamine matched or exceeded that of water reward, placing the cerebellum within brain reward-prediction networks rather than purely motor control.
Model & method
Mice with intracranial dopamine self-stimulation; two-photon calcium imaging of granule cells and climbing fibers, chronic inhibition and climbing-fiber stimulation.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02449-z

4APOE genotypes differentially remodel the astrocytic lipid droplet proteome to shape lipid droplet dynamics

Nat Commun  Carla Cuní-López & Priyanka S Narayan
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Lipid droplets accumulate in astrocytes in Alzheimer's disease, but how the strongest genetic risk factor, APOE, shapes their handling has been unclear. Comparing oleic-acid-loaded human iPSC-derived astrocytes carrying APOE2, APOE3, or APOE4, the authors found each variant imposed a distinct lipid droplet proteome and lipidome and, critically, distinct degradation dynamics: APOE2 droplets underwent efficient lipophagy-mediated turnover whereas APOE4 droplets resisted autophagic clearance. This identifies impaired lipid droplet clearance as a candidate cell-autonomous mechanism linking APOE4 to Alzheimer's risk, in a defined human genotype-matched system. Causal evidence is in vitro and genotype effects on downstream neurodegeneration remain to be tested.
Model & method
Human iPSC-derived astrocytes, isogenic APOE2/3/4; lipid droplet proteomics, lipidomics, lipophagy/turnover assays.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76565-6

5Sex-specific biological aging clocks across organs and omics

Nat Med  MULTI Consortium & Junhao Wen
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Most biological aging clocks are trained on sex-pooled data and implicitly assume sex invariance. The authors built 38 sex-specific clocks across 15 organ systems and showed marked divergence between female and male models, with organ-specific aging liability and its links to cardiometabolic, endocrine and mental traits configured differently by sex. Proteomics resolved distinct synaptic, immune, vascular and metabolic networks tracking male versus female aging, and sex-specific clocks predicted systemic disease and all-cause mortality in a sex- and organ-dependent manner. For brain aging specifically, the brain clock showed sex-dependent associations with cognitive decline trajectory during a preclinical Alzheimer's trial, arguing that sex-appropriate normative references reveal signatures that pooled models obscure.
Model & method
Human multi-organ, multi-omics cohorts; sex-stratified machine-learning aging clocks, Mendelian randomization, proteomics, longitudinal survival analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04662-6

Hippocampal astrocytic sequences emerge during learning and memory recall

Nat Neurosci  Ryan A Senne & Steve Ramirez
Key findings
One-photon calcium imaging of dorsal CA1 astrocytes tracked across days in freely moving mice revealed that foot shock during contextual fear conditioning evoked robust astrocytic calcium-event sequences with a time-compressed structure resembling neuronal "time cell" activity. Similar sequences re-emerged on later exposure to the conditioned context without shock but were absent in a distinct context, and spontaneous astrocytic activity tracked behavioral-state transitions including freezing only in the conditioned context. The results implicate astrocytes in representing the temporal structure of associative memories, extending sequence coding beyond pyramidal neurons; the evidence is correlative imaging rather than causal manipulation.
Model & method
Mice, contextual fear conditioning; longitudinal one-photon calcium imaging of CA1 astrocytes.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02448-0

A retinoic acid autoregulatory loop governing prefrontal-motor arealization

Nature  Lin Yang & Nenad Sestan
Key findings
Defining a retinoic-acid-associated gene regulatory network in the developing human prefrontal cortex, the authors identified MEIS2 — a transcription factor linked to intellectual disability and autism — as a network hub. Conditional deletion of Meis2 in postmitotic cortical excitatory neurons in mice partially respecified prospective prefrontal association territory toward motor-like molecular and connectivity identity, showing postmitotic neurons actively maintain areal identity. Meis2 loss reduced the ALDH1A3-expressing neuron population and local RA signalling, revealing a conserved autoregulatory loop (RA → MEIS2 → ALDH1A3 → RA) that confines and reinforces RA signalling to pattern the frontal cortex along the association–motor axis.
Model & method
Developing human PFC (genomics) and conditional Meis2-knockout mice; gene regulatory network analysis, molecular and connectivity mapping.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11014-4

Topographic structure and function of locus coeruleus noradrenaline neurons

Nature  Zhixiao Su & Jeremiah Y Cohen
Key findings
Reconstructing single noradrenergic neurons in the mouse locus coeruleus, the authors found individual axons were extensive but largely confined to subsets of brain regions, with axonal projections and graded gene expression correlating with cell-body location — evidence for genuine topographic organization in a system long viewed as diffuse. Functionally, dorsal LC neurons projecting to isocortex activated when mice switched choices and carried reward-prediction-error signals during continuous learning, while ventral LC neurons showed higher background activity when mice ignored reward-predictive stimuli. The work maps structure onto function in a neuromodulatory system and identifies learning-related signals for flexible behaviour.
Model & method
Mice; single-neuron axonal reconstruction, spatial transcriptomics, and activity recording during a continuous-learning task.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11026-0

Inflection points and transitions in Alzheimer's disease

Nature  Bart De Strooper & Eric Karran
Key findings
This perspective reframes Alzheimer's disease away from a linear amyloid-to-tau-to-neurodegeneration cascade and toward a sequence of discrete molecular, cellular and network phases organized around biological inflection points — thresholds at which stress-driven loss of homeostasis produces qualitative shifts in cellular behavior that then spread across tissue domains and accumulate as a brain-wide mosaic. The induction of amyloid-associated tau phosphorylation is proposed as a pivotal inflection point separating amyloid-dominated states from tau-stress states. The framework is offered to reconcile the pathology–symptom dissociation and the shared architecture of familial and sporadic disease, foregrounding timing, thresholds and eroding homeostatic resilience rather than steady accumulation.
Model & method
Conceptual review/perspective synthesizing human and model AD data.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11110-5

H3K27M drives OPC stemness and intrathecal therapeutic vulnerability in brainstem glioma organoids

Cell Stem Cell  Luyang Xie & Liwei Zhang
Key findings
The authors established a patient-derived brainstem glioma organoid platform with high success rates that recapitulated parental H3K27M-mutant diffuse midline glioma features. Multi-omics and functional assays showed these tumors sustain oligodendrocyte-precursor-cell stemness through dysregulated ACTIVIN signaling. An intrathecal drug screen — chosen to bypass the intact blood–brain barrier that defeats systemic therapy — identified triple intrathecal therapy (methotrexate, cytarabine, dexamethasone) as potent against DMG, and organoid-avatar-guided personalized therapy produced sustained clinical responses in patients, with CSF proteomics tracking OPC-marker reductions. The clinical evidence is from organoid-guided individual cases rather than a controlled trial.
Model & method
Patient-derived brainstem glioma organoids and patient treatment; multi-omics, intrathecal drug screening, CSF proteomics.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2026.08.012

Local sympathetic signal enables the immune evasion of hepatic stellate cells

Neuron  Tianhao Li & Jing Yang
Key findings
Sympathetic axon density correlated with liver fibrosis severity in human patients, and in mice sympathetic ablation mitigated CCl4-induced fibrosis while chemogenetic activation of local sympathetic signals worsened it. Activated hepatic stellate cells, the main fibrogenic cell type, predominantly expressed the α1B-adrenergic receptor (ADRA1B); HSC-specific Adra1b deletion reduced fibrosis. Mechanistically, ADRA1B signaling reshaped NK-cell ligand expression on stellate cells — down on activating ligands, up on inhibitory ones — allowing immune evasion from NK cytotoxicity. The study defines a peripheral neuro-immune circuit through which the sympathetic nervous system governs organ fibrosis, with causal evidence in mice and correlative human histology.
Model & method
Human liver samples and mouse CCl4 fibrosis model; sympathetic ablation/chemogenetics, HSC-specific Adra1b knockout, NK-cell assays.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.016
Neurodegeneration & aging

Hippocampal snRNA-seq in Collaborative Cross reveals molecular signatures of cognitive resilience independent of chronological aging

Alzheimers Dement  Yu Chen & Catherine C Kaczorowski
Key findings
Single-nucleus RNA-seq of hippocampus from 107 genetically diverse Collaborative Cross mice across three ages and both sexes, with contextual fear memory as a continuous cognitive readout, dissociated the transcriptional signatures of aging from those of cognitive decline. Aging changes were modest and cell-type-specific — early shifts in excitatory neurons followed by glial remodeling — whereas cognitive decline was associated with widespread transcriptional alterations concentrated in excitatory neurons and enriched for proteostasis and glutathione/redox pathways. The dissociation argues cognitive decline is not merely a consequence of chronological aging and nominates proteostasis and redox regulation as candidate substrates of cognitive resilience.
Model & method
Collaborative Cross mice (3 ages, both sexes); hippocampal single-nucleus RNA-seq with contextual fear memory phenotyping, pseudobulk differential expression.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71825

Alzheimer's Co-Pathology Burden on Basal Forebrain Atrophy and Cognitive Impairment in Dementia with Lewy Bodies: A Cross-Sectional Pilot Study

Ann Neurol  Daniele Urso & Giancarlo Logroscino
Key findings
In 40 patients with dementia with Lewy bodies and 14 controls, DLB patients showed significant basal forebrain atrophy relative to controls, and those with Alzheimer's co-pathology (defined by in vivo biomarkers) had greater basal forebrain atrophy and reduced cortical thickness. Basal forebrain volume correlated with the pTau181/Aβ1-42 ratio and with MMSE, and mediation analyses linked AD pathology and basal forebrain atrophy to neurodegeneration and cognition. The findings suggest AD co-pathology amplifies cholinergic basal forebrain degeneration in DLB, but the sample is small, cross-sectional, and the mediation is correlational rather than causal.
Model & method
Human DLB cohort; 3T MRI volumetry, AD fluid biomarkers, ANCOVA and mediation analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78357

Rethinking tau-targeted therapy in Alzheimer's disease: Toward a dual biomarker strategy

Alzheimers Dement  Charlotte E Teunissen & Bruno P Imbimbo
Key findings
This review argues that interpreting reductions in soluble phosphorylated tau as therapeutic success is misleading, because different p-tau species report different biology: p-tau262 and p-tau356 mark sites within soluble pre-tangle assemblies, CSF p-tau262 may decline as neurofibrillary pathology advances, and marked p-tau217 lowering by posdinemab produced no clinical benefit. Drawing a limited analogy to the amyloid field, the authors propose a dual-domain framework evaluating tau therapies with complementary measures of aggregated tau burden and biologically defined soluble tau states — including seeding-related "active tau" assays — so that a treatment-induced change is judged favorable only alongside aggregate reduction and clinical evidence.
Model & method
Review/framework synthesizing human tau-therapy trial and fluid-biomarker data.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71867

Cholesterol maintains the degradative capacity of lysosomes during clearance and recycling of dysfunctional mitochondria

Nat Commun  Haoning Yang & Yasunori Saheki
Key findings
Clearing damaged mitochondria was tightly coupled to acute accumulation of PI4P on the lysosomal surface via PI4KIIα, which activated OSBP-mediated cholesterol transfer from ER to lysosome; the resulting ER cholesterol efflux activated SREBP-2 to boost cholesterol synthesis. Sustained lysosomal cholesterol maintained lysosomal acidity and membrane integrity required for efficient mitochondrial degradation, and the process released free fatty acids recycled into DGAT1-dependent lipid droplets. The work defines a phosphoinositide-regulated cholesterol-transport pathway sustaining lysosomal degradative capacity — a process whose impairment is closely linked to neurodegeneration, though this study establishes the mechanism in general cell models rather than neurons.
Model & method
Cultured cells; live-cell imaging of PI4P/cholesterol dynamics, OSBP/PI4KIIα and SREBP-2 manipulation, mitophagy/lysosome assays.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77423-1

Factor XIa Inhibitors for Secondary Prevention After Noncardioembolic Stroke: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Neurology  Aristeidis H Katsanos & Georgios Tsivgoulis
Key findings
Pooling three randomized trials of factor XIa inhibitors versus placebo in acute noncardioembolic ischemic stroke or TIA (14,239 participants, effects largely driven by one phase 3 trial of 12,327), the meta-analysis found FXIa inhibitors reduced any stroke (RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.66–0.84), ischemic stroke (RR 0.74, 0.66–0.84) and composite cardiovascular events (RR 0.83, 0.75–0.92) added to standard antiplatelet therapy, without increasing major bleeding (RR 1.12, 0.87–1.44), hemorrhagic stroke, intracranial hemorrhage or mortality. The favorable efficacy–safety separation supports FXIa inhibition as a mechanistically distinct antithrombotic strategy for secondary stroke prevention, with the caveat that estimates rest heavily on a single large trial.
Model & method
Human meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials; random-effects pooling of stroke and bleeding outcomes.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218521
