1Single-cell atlas of transcriptomic vulnerability across brain disorders

Nature  Donghoon Lee & Panos Roussos
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
A population-scale single-nucleus atlas of the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex from 1,494 donors (>6.3 million nuclei) spanning eight conditions—Alzheimer's disease, diffuse Lewy body disease, vascular dementia, Parkinson's, tauopathy, frontotemporal dementia, schizophrenia and bipolar disorder—separates shared from disease-specific transcriptional programs. Universal signatures converge on basic housekeeping (mRNA processing, protein localization); once those are discounted, AD, DLBD, vascular dementia and PD show stronger genetic and transcriptomic concordance, and more severe AD tracks with reduced neuronal abundance alongside expansion of immune and vascular populations. Deep-layer excitatory neuron abundance associates with neuropsychiatric symptoms. Being cross-sectional postmortem data, progression "trajectories" are inferred rather than observed.
Model & method
Human postmortem DLPFC; single-nucleus RNA-seq across 1,494 donors with genetic-concordance and pseudo-trajectory analyses.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09573-z

2Lifespan single-cell transcriptomic atlas of the human prefrontal cortex

Nature  Hui Yang & Panos Roussos
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
A single-nucleus atlas spanning the full human lifespan shows the DLPFC follows non-linear, cell-type-specific transcriptional trajectories—dynamic remodeling during development, relative stability in midlife, and selective molecular "reactivation" in late adulthood. Late-life glial programs tied to immune activation, stress responses and circadian reorganization are anatomically organized across cortical layers and grey/white-matter domains, distinct from early-life neuronal resilience programs. By defining normative aging trajectories, it provides a reference for distinguishing healthy cortical aging from the transition to disease vulnerability. Trajectories are inferred from cross-sectional sampling, not longitudinal follow-up.
Model & method
Human postmortem DLPFC across the lifespan; single-nucleus RNA-seq with trajectory modeling.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10271-7

3mTORC1 drives cell-autonomous astrocyte reactivity in tuberous sclerosis

Nature  Thomas L. Li & Helen S. Bateup
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Using patient-derived brain organoids to follow mutant-progenitor fate in the absence of seizures, the authors show TSC2 loss biases neural progenitors toward enlarged, pro-inflammatory reactive astrocytes in a cell-autonomous manner—downregulating glutamate transporters, secreting inflammatory cytokines, and upregulating neurodegenerative risk genes including APOE and CLU. This argues glial dysfunction is a primary consequence of the mutation rather than a downstream effect of chronic seizure activity, reframing reactive astrocytes as a therapeutic target in TSC. Evidence rests on organoids plus resected patient tuber tissue; in vivo circuit consequences were not directly tested.
Model & method
Human iPSC-derived brain organoids and resected patient tuber tissue; single-cell transcriptomics with cyclic immunostaining.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11054-w

4Kamuvudine-9 shows protective and therapeutic efficacy in a mouse model of multiple sclerosis

Sci Transl Med  Praveen Yerramothu & Jayakrishna Ambati
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
K-9, a safer nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI) derivative that blocks inflammasome activation, both prevented further neurological decline and reversed pre-existing paralysis and vision loss in experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, preserving spinal-cord myelin and axons and abolishing the rise in serum neurofilament light. Mechanistically it disrupted NLRP3–NEK7 and NLRP3–NLRC4 interactions to inhibit dual inflammasome activation. Supporting translational relevance, three independent human cohorts showed lower MS incidence among NRTI-treated individuals and a reduced relapse rate. Causal efficacy is mouse-only; the human evidence is observational pharmacoepidemiology.
Model & method
EAE mouse model plus three human pharmacoepidemiology cohorts; behavioral scoring, serum NfL, and inflammasome protein-interaction assays.
https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aei2870

5Spatial organization of AQP4 water-channel expression in the human brain

Sci Adv  Tahmineh Taheri & Bratislav Misic
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Reconstructing a brain-wide glymphatic-related topography from AQP4 gene expression, the authors find it peaks in subcortical, ventral and periventricular territories near CSF reservoirs and perivascular interfaces. AQP4-enriched regions show lower normative perfusion yet lie near small-vessel-disease territories, and—most relevant here—atrophy patterns colocalize with AQP4 expression, most strongly for tau and TDP-43 proteinopathies, an alignment reinforced by PET markers of neuroinflammation; glioma is also most frequent in AQP4-rich regions. Findings are spatial and correlational from group-level maps rather than causal.
Model & method
Human brain AQP4 transcriptomic maps integrated with perfusion, atrophy, PET neuroinflammation and connectome data.
https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeg8117

AI-based characterization of Alzheimer's disease phenotypes from population-scale single-cell data

Nat Med  Chenfeng He & Daifeng Wang
Key findings
PASCode, a graph-neural framework ensembling multiple statistical methods, scored single-cell phenotype associations across >6 million PsychAD prefrontal-cortex nuclei from >1,000 brains, flagging ~1.5 million phenotype-associated cells in 584 donors with AD-related phenotypes. It prioritized microglial subpopulations implicated in AD pathology, reactive astrocyte subtypes whose shifted neuroprotective/neurotoxic gene expression may confer cognitive resilience, and excitatory/inhibitory imbalance plus mitochondrial dysfunction in cognitively impaired donors; shared astrocyte states linked AD and depression. Released as an open-source tool with pretrained models and external validation.
Model & method
Human PsychAD single-nucleus RNA-seq; graph neural network phenotype-association scoring.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-04128-1

Massively parallel assessment of gene-regulatory activity at human cortical-structure-associated variants

Nat Neurosci  Nana Matoba & Jason L. Stein
Key findings
A massively parallel reporter assay tested 9,092 variants associated with human cortical structure in neural progenitor cells at baseline and during Wnt stimulation, identifying 918 variants with regulatory potential across 76% of studied loci, more than half showing allelic effects. Wnt exposed condition-dependent enhancers, and regulatory activity was largely driven by Alu elements, suggesting a contribution to cortical expansion. The work provides a genome-scale functional bridge from noncoding GWAS loci to cortical development.
Model & method
Human neural progenitor cells; massively parallel reporter assay ± Wnt stimulation.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02454-2

Single-nucleus transcriptome-wide association study of human brain disorders

Nature  Sanan Venkatesh & Panos Roussos
Key findings
Cell-type-specific models of genetically regulated expression, built from multi-ancestry PsychAD DLPFC nuclei, uncovered thousands of gene–trait associations undetectable in bulk tissue and resolved many to discrete neuronal, glial and immune populations. Cross-ancestry analysis in the Million Veteran Program confirmed conserved, pleiotropic effects and enabled mapping of candidate causal genes and pathways for neuropsychiatric and neurodegenerative disorders.
Model & method
Human DLPFC single-nucleus RNA-seq transcriptomic imputation; multi-ancestry TWAS with Million Veteran Program validation.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10836-6

Neuronal detection of social actions directs collective escape behaviour

Nature  Jo-Hsien Yu & Matthew Lovett-Barron
Key findings
In schooling glassfish, collective escape can be mediated by an individual's visual perception of others escaping. Imaging neural activity while fish viewed virtual conspecifics revealed midbrain optic-tectum and thalamic neurons that increased firing when virtual fish escaped—and also when they abruptly disappeared—but only for biologically plausible burst-and-glide motion; fish retreated accordingly. This identifies a neural representation of socially acquired danger information sufficient to trigger escape from social cues alone.
Model & method
Danionella cerebrum; neural activity imaging with virtual-conspecific visual stimuli.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11041-1

The lipidomic architecture of the mouse brain

Nature  Luca Fusar Bassini & Gioele La Manno
Key findings
Micrometer-scale mapping of membrane lipids across the adult mouse brain defines discrete "lipizones" that align with functional anatomy and partly mirror—but also diverge from—cell-type territories, capturing distal axon terminals. The atlas reveals a new axis of white-matter oligodendrocyte heterogeneity and biochemical zonation in the choroid plexus and ventricular walls, and shows pregnancy-associated cortical lipid remodeling most pronounced in layer 4. It is offered as a foundational resource for lipids in brain development, physiology and pathology.
Model & method
Adult mouse brain across sexes and pregnancy; spatial lipidomics (imaging mass spectrometry).
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11050-0

Projection-defined modules reveal mouse motor cortex architecture

Cell  Antonio Falasconi & Silvia Arber
Key findings
Subcortical projection patterns partition mouse motor cortex into 16 modules organized along two spatial axes: one distinguishing primary motor cortex (reciprocal with somatosensory cortex) from secondary motor cortex (coupled to frontal areas) via differential excitatory-neuron composition, the other stratified by somatosensory input and aligned cell-type signatures. The two-axis logic extends to subcortical targets, with striatum, thalamus and brainstem following distinct convergence–divergence rules, giving an anatomical framework for how motor cortex integrates into brain-wide networks.
Model & method
Mouse motor cortex; projection mapping integrated with cell-type composition and corticocortical connectivity.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.046

CXCL1–CXCR2 signaling mediates cancer cell growth and radiation response in leptomeningeal metastasis

Sci Transl Med  Ahmed M. Osman & Adrienne Boire
Key findings
In leptomeningeal metastasis, CSF proteomics linked cancer-cell-derived CXCL1 to disease growth, with higher pre-treatment CSF CXCL1 predicting worse response to proton craniospinal irradiation. In syngeneic mouse models, interrupting cancer-cell (but not host) Cxcl1 impaired leptomeningeal growth; a CXCR2+ cancer-cell subpopulation enriched over time and showed cell-cycle programs, and higher tumor CXCR2 predicted worse survival. Intrathecal CXCR2 antagonism slowed growth and radiosensitized tumors, nominating an actionable target.
Model & method
Patient CSF proteomics plus syngeneic mouse leptomeningeal-metastasis models; intrathecal CXCR2 blockade.
https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aeh7968
Neurodegeneration & aging

Long-term risk of dementia after TIA: a population-wide matched cohort study

Neurology  Raed A. Joundi & Moira K. Kapral
Key findings
Among 113,081 matched patients with a first TIA (mean age 70), 19.3% developed dementia over a mean 7.1 years—higher than matched controls at every horizon (2.71 vs 2.03 per 100 person-years). Dementia hazard was greatest in the first year (HR 1.75, 95% CI 1.66–1.85) and stayed elevated over 20 years (overall HR 1.34). Compared with stroke, risk started lower (1-year HR 0.67) but converged by 5 years. Dementia far outnumbered recurrent stroke, and adjusting for incident stroke barely attenuated the association.
Model & method
Ontario population-wide linked administrative cohort (2002–2022); matched cause-specific and competing-risk survival models.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218554

Synergistic association of insomnia and anxiety with cognitive impairment and dementia in older adults

Alzheimers Dement  Yan Chen & Xia Li
Key findings
In the cross-sectional SHAPE cohort, comorbid insomnia and anxiety were synergistically associated with cognitive impairment (significant additive interaction), a pattern validated longitudinally for incident dementia in the UK Biobank. Plasma-biomarker mediation implicated amyloid pathology—driven predominantly by p-tau217, with a smaller but significant independent GFAP (astrogliosis) contribution—offering a biological substrate for the combined risk. The mediation analysis rests on cross-sectional biomarker data, limiting causal ordering.
Model & method
Human SHAPE cohort with UK Biobank validation; regression, additive-interaction and plasma p-tau217/GFAP/NfL mediation analyses.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71853

Distinct single-nucleus RNA-seq changes among non-neuronal cells in ADNC, LATE-NC and mixed pathologies

Alzheimers Dement  Qi Qiao & David W. Fardo
Key findings
Stratifying SEA-AD middle-temporal-gyrus nuclei into LATE-NC-dominant, ADNC-dominant and mixed pathology, the authors found glial and vascular cells carry pathology-distinguishing transcriptional signatures: LATE-NC-dominant enriched for signaling and RNA regulation, ADNC-dominant for mitochondrial dysfunction, and mixed pathology for translational dysregulation and lipid remodeling—suggesting mixed pathology is a distinct biological state rather than a simple sum of the two. This is a secondary analysis of existing atlas data.
Model & method
Human middle temporal gyrus single-nucleus RNA-seq (SEA-AD); differential expression and gene-set enrichment across glial and vascular supertypes.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71810

Long-term exposure to air pollution and Parkinson's disease: an administrative cohort of older adults in South Korea

Mov Disord  Jongmin Oh & Youn-Hee Lim
Key findings
Across 5.3 million adults aged ≥65 (2010–2019; 143,557 incident PD cases), each interquartile-range increase in PM2.5 (HR 1.018, 95% CI 1.008–1.028) and ozone (HR 1.019, 95% CI 1.006–1.033) was associated with modestly higher PD incidence, whereas NO2 showed an inverse association after full adjustment. Effect sizes are small and the design is observational with district-level modeled exposures, but the scale lends weight to a possible contribution of particulate and ozone exposure to PD etiology.
Model & method
Korean National Health Insurance cohort; time-varying Cox proportional-hazards models with CMAQ-modeled pollutant exposures.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70518

Mapping sequential ATN cascade transitions via brain structural topography in Alzheimer's disease

Sci Adv  Dong Wang & Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative
Key findings
Reframing AD as dynamic cascades rather than static ATN categories, network-topology analysis of neuroimaging identified four reproducible subgroups corresponding to early-vulnerability, initiation, amplification and terminal stages, each with distinct biomarker signatures, proteomic profiles and longitudinal cognitive trajectories. In a post hoc analysis, slower cognitive decline after amyloid-targeting treatment was seen mainly in the initiation-stage group, suggesting structural staging could help time anti-amyloid therapy. The treatment analysis is exploratory and post hoc.
Model & method
ADNI multimodal neuroimaging with integrated proteomics; brain-network-topology subgrouping and longitudinal cognitive modeling.
https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aef3787
