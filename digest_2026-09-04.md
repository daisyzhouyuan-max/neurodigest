### Somatic mutations reveal the ontogeny of microglia in human ageing
**Julia A Belk & Siddhartha Jaiswal | Nature**

**Why it matters:** Using somatic-mutation and mitochondrial-DNA lineage tracing in 20 older individuals, the authors show that bone-marrow-derived myeloid cells infiltrate the human brain throughout life and can comprise a substantial fraction of the microglial pool with ageing — challenging the assumption, drawn largely from mouse work, that human microglia are maintained almost entirely by embryonic self-renewal. In cohort analyses, most forms of clonal haematopoiesis were associated with reduced Alzheimer's disease risk, linking bone-marrow clonal dynamics to microglial identity and AD susceptibility.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem brain (n=20 older individuals); somatic/mitochondrial DNA lineage tracing and single-cell RNA-seq, with cohort-level clonal haematopoiesis–AD association analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10939-0

### Priming of CD8+ T cells by peripheral dendritic cells exacerbates tau-mediated neurodegeneration
**Hao Hu & David M Holtzman | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Identifies a peripheral immune step upstream of the CD8+ T cell accumulation seen in tauopathy and Alzheimer's disease: conventional type 1 dendritic cells (cDC1s) cross-present brain-derived antigen in secondary lymphoid tissue to prime CD8+ T cells that later infiltrate the brain and track with tau pathology severity. Tauopathy mice lacking cDC1s or cross-presentation were protected from neurodegeneration, with reduced brain CD8+ T cell infiltration and glial activation — pointing to peripheral antigen priming, not just brain-intrinsic immunity, as a druggable node.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse tauopathy models genetically lacking cDC1s or antigen cross-presentation; immune profiling of brain and secondary lymphoid tissue.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02427-5

### Intracellular complement factor H protects neurons during CNS inflammation
**Christina Mayer & Manuel A Friese | Nature**

**Why it matters:** Reveals a non-canonical, secretion-independent function for complement factor H (CFH) inside neurons: inflammation- and oxidative-stress-induced CFH localizes to the endoplasmic reticulum and limits reactive oxygen species and lipid peroxidation — a key driver of neuronal ferroptosis — conferring resilience to retinal ganglion cell degeneration in multiple sclerosis. Protection depends on the C-terminal SCR20 domain and persists without complement component C3, distinguishing this mechanism from classical complement-pathway biology and suggesting a new neuroprotective target.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human donor retinal ganglion cell single-nucleus RNA-seq (MS vs. control) with mouse genetic and biochemical validation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10981-y

### Stroke and Alzheimer's disease have distinct consequences on neurovascular function but synergize to increase amyloid deposition
**Ismail, O. & Mishra, A. | bioRxiv**

**Why it matters:** In an amyloid mouse model, mild subcortical ischemia and Aβ pathology each chronically impair cerebrovascular reactivity and neurovascular coupling through largely distinct spatiotemporal trajectories, yet the combined ischemia+Aβ group showed the least residual cerebrovascular dysfunction but the greatest increase in Aβ deposition. Suggests stroke and AD pathology are mechanistically coupled but not through simple additive vascular damage, with direct relevance to understanding mixed vascular-amyloid dementia.

**Model/system & primary method:** Tg2576 amyloid mouse model with transient mild subcortical ischemia; chronic cerebrovascular reactivity imaging and post-mortem astrocyte/Aβ histology.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.29.746901

### Structured experience shapes strategy learning and neural dynamics in the medial entorhinal cortex
**John C Bowler & James G Heys | Nat Neurosci**

### A descending glycinergic circuit drives opioid-resistant mechanical pain via spinal GluN3A excitatory glycine receptors in mice
**Dong Dong & Longzhen Cheng | Neuron**

**Key findings:** A descending circuit from GlyT2+ neurons in the rostral ventromedial medulla to somatostatin+ neurons in the spinal dorsal horn is necessary and sufficient for morphine-resistant mechanical allodynia. Glycine released from this pathway activates GluN3A-containing NMDA receptors acting as unconventional excitatory glycine receptors, bypassing opioid suppression and driving the opioid-resistant pain state, including craniofacial pain via trigeminal and limbic inputs — nominating GluN3A-containing NMDARs as a target for intractable pain.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mice; optogenetic/chemogenetic circuit manipulation, electrophysiology, behavioral pain assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.003

**Judith Hoeller & Michael B Reiser | Cell**

### Mitochondrial calcium influx-driven bioenergetics selectively enable drug addiction
**Jun Gao & Xin Pan | Nat Neurosci**

### Metabolic reallocation in spinal cord oligodendrocytes drives chronic pain in mice through neuronal Aβ42 production
**Yannick Fotio & Daniele Piomelli | Sci Transl Med**

**Key findings:** After peripheral injury, spinal cord oligodendrocytes downregulate myelin protein synthesis and upregulate lipid biosynthesis, disrupting spinal myelin composition and compromising axonal integrity; this drives neuronal APP accumulation, BACE1 upregulation, and increased insoluble Aβ42 during the critical window for transition to chronic pain. Blocking Aβ42 production (BACE1 inhibition) or clearing it (anti-Aβ antibody) prevented persistent pain, as did oligodendrocyte-specific deletion of NAAA — identifying an NAAA→Aβ42 axis linking spinal myelin metabolism to chronic pain and amyloid biology.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse models of peripheral injury (hindpaw formalin injection, sciatic nerve ligation); single-nucleus RNA-seq, lipidomics, pharmacological and genetic intervention.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aea8738

### Changes in five cerebrospinal fluid and three plasma Alzheimer's disease biomarkers across increasing brain amyloid-beta and tau pathology burden
**Andrea Mastrangelo & Piero Parchi | Acta Neuropathol**

**Key findings:** In 250 participants with post-mortem-validated Aβ/tau pathology (prion and non-prion disease; median sampling-to-death interval 1–1.5 months), CSF Aβ42/Aβ40 declined earliest (by the second quartile of Aβ burden, with highest accuracy at low/intermediate Aβ, AUC 0.984), while CSF p-tau181/p-tau217 rose only from the third quartile and performed best at advanced Aβ/intermediate tau burden. Supports a sequential biomarker model across the AD neuropathological continuum but shows no current fluid biomarker captures the earliest phase of Aβ deposition.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human neuropathological validation cohort (ante-mortem CSF/plasma vs. post-mortem Aβ/tau staging); multivariable regression and sequential ROC analysis.

### Genotoxic and metabolic stress drive divergent senescence programs in human microglia
**Platt, B. & Janssens, S. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In the human microglial cell line HMC3, chronic DNA damage (doxorubicin) and chronic high-glucose metabolic stress both induced hallmark senescence features (cellular/nuclear hypertrophy, elevated SA-β-galactosidase activity, reduced metabolic viability without cell loss) via p53-p21 activation, but diverged downstream: genotoxic stress produced a chemokine-rich senescence-associated secretory phenotype with NRF2-TFAM downregulation, while metabolic stress induced p16 expression, peripheral p21 localization, and an interferon-associated inflammatory signature with a constrained antioxidant response. Indicates that distinct age-related stressors drive mechanistically separable senescent microglial states relevant to chronic neuroinflammation in brain aging.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human microglial cell line (HMC3) exposed to genotoxic vs. metabolic stress; senescence phenotyping and transcriptomic/pathway analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.31.748228

**Cao, X. & Wang, G. | medRxiv**

*v3 revision of a preprint first posted April 2025.*

### Proteoform-resolved neoGFAP as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker across the TBI–MCI–AD continuum in Veterans
**Haskins, W. E. & Peskind, E. R. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In Veterans spanning traumatic brain injury, mild cognitive impairment, and Alzheimer's disease, a calpain-cleaved GFAP neoepitope (neoGFAP) outperformed total GFAP for discriminating MCI+AD from non-AD (CSF AUROC 0.81 vs. 0.73 overall, reaching significance in an amyloid-positive subset) and for distinguishing MCI from chronic blast-exposed Veterans without cognitive impairment. In chronic blast injury, neoGFAP was paradoxically depleted versus controls, consistent with tissue sequestration of aggregated fragments. Plasma neoGFAP reached AUROC 0.90 (comparable to pTau217's 0.92), and adding CSF markers and age improved diagnostic accuracy from 71% to 80% — though the CSF benchmarking cohort was small (n=57).

**Model/system & primary method:** Human Veteran cohort (plasma triage n=367; CSF benchmarking n=57) spanning TBI–MCI–AD; proteoform-specific immunoassay plus unbiased proteomics.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.01.26361845
