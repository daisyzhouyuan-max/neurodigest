1A cross-species AAV platform for precise microglial interrogation

Neuron  Fengting Yu & Minmin Luo
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Microglia have resisted AAV-based manipulation because of poor transduction and neuronal off-target expression, forcing most causal microglial work into transgenic or CSF1R-dependent systems. This Cre-independent vector combines a 446-bp microglia-specific regulatory element, a high-yield AAV2/9.m capsid, miRNA-mediated neuronal de-targeting and microglia-tuned codon optimization to reach >90% microglial specificity after intracranial delivery, including in an Alzheimer's model. With doxorubicin-assisted delivery the same toolkit works in rat, rhesus macaque and human ex vivo slices, and supports calcium imaging, chemogenetics and CRISPR-Cas9 editing — a broadly useful resource for dissecting microglial contributions to neurodegeneration.
Model & method
Mouse (incl. AD model), rat, rhesus macaque and human ex vivo brain slices; engineered AAV with regulatory-element, capsid and codon-optimization components; functional payloads for imaging, chemogenetics and gene editing.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.017

2PTPRS mediates invasion of tetanus neurotoxin into the central nervous system

Cell  Pengsheng Chen & Min Dong
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Tetanus neurotoxin reaches the CNS by retrograde transport through motoneurons, but the host factor routing it there was unknown. Using pull-down/mass spectrometry and a luciferase-based in vivo transport assay, the authors identify receptor protein tyrosine phosphatase sigma (PTPRS) as the retrograde transport receptor: global or motoneuron-specific PTPRS knockout abolishes TeNT entry into the CNS, and a PTPRS-binding-deficient toxin fails to reach the spinal cord, instead producing botulism-like flaccid paralysis rather than spastic tetanus. Cryo-EM localizes the interaction to the PTPRS immunoglobulin domain. Beyond defining a post-entry intervention point for tetanus, PTPRS-mediated trafficking is a candidate route for targeted CNS drug delivery.
Model & method
Mouse genetic models (global and conditional PTPRS knockout); affinity proteomics, in vivo luciferase transport assay, structural (cryo-EM/biochemical) mapping.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.09.026

3CHCHD2 mitigates age-associated proteostatic collapse by maintaining the mitochondria–lysosome axis

Acta Neuropathol  Teresa R Kee & Jung-A A Woo
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
The Parkinson's-linked CHCHD2 T61I mutation is tied to Lewy body disorders by unclear mechanisms. Here the mutant binds and lowers cathepsin B and L, inducing lysosomal membrane permeabilization and impairing α-synuclein fibril clearance; in mice it exacerbates age-related axonal degeneration and cognitive deficits. Conversely, wild-type CHCHD2 — which the authors show declines in the human brain with age — protects against neuroinflammation, dopaminergic neuron loss, insoluble aggregation and mitochondrial/lysosomal deterioration. Reduced CTSB and CHCHD2 in human Lewy body disease brains support the model, nominating preservation of CHCHD2 as a strategy against age-related proteostatic failure.
Model & method
Mouse models expressing wild-type or T61I CHCHD2, with human Lewy body disorder postmortem validation; biochemical interaction mapping, lysosomal/cathepsin assays, α-synuclein fibril clearance and neuropathology.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03093-4

4A high-dimensional population code in the bat motor cortex during natural dexterous flight

Cell  Boaz Styr & Michael M Yartsev
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Prevailing views of motor cortical dynamics derive largely from constrained reaching tasks. Recording wirelessly from the wing motor cortex of freely flying bats alongside 3D wing tracking, the authors find that activity is not dominated by the wingbeat cycle: neurons are sparsely active with mixed selectivity for flight kinematics and variable, millisecond-scale entrainment to wingbeat phase. Successive wingbeats occupy distinct neural states, yielding a high-dimensional population code that standard low-dimensional accounts do not capture. The result reframes how cortex may organize naturalistic, high-degree-of-freedom movement.
Model & method
Freely flying Egyptian fruit bats; large-scale wireless electrophysiology of wing motor cortex with synchronized 3D wing kinematics and population-level analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.09.029

Human–artificial intelligence collaboration for comanaging epilepsy in real time

Nat Med  Zack Goldblum & Brian Litt
Key findings
An inpatient prototype links scalp and intracranial EEG to a secure LLM-based assistant that communicates bidirectionally through a smartphone app, processing biomarkers of sleep, medication, cognition and seizure probability, notifying patients of events and recording their feedback. Validated in 13 patients undergoing video-EEG monitoring (scalp n = 11; intracranial n = 2), the system used patient feedback alone — without expert intervention — to improve seizure-detector precision in a pseudoprospective simulation. The cohort is small and inpatient, so this is a proof-of-concept for closed-loop, patient-in-the-loop device management rather than validated clinical performance.
Model & method
Human inpatient study (n = 13); scalp/intracranial EEG feeding an LLM-based platform with app-based interaction and pseudoprospective detector retraining.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04680-4

Rare k-mers reveal centromere haplogroups underlying human diversity and cancer translocations

Cell  Yuichi Shiraishi & Seishi Ogawa
Key findings
Centromeres are among the least characterized genomic regions because their repetitive structure defeats conventional variant analysis. The authors show that rare k-mers capture lineage-specific centromeric variation defining discrete haplogroups, and build a tool (ascairn) to infer them from short-read WGS; applied to >3,300 genomes it reveals extensive global diversity and population-history signatures. In 142 oligodendroglioma cases with 1p/19q co-deletion, breakpoints mapped to the centromeres of chromosomes 1 and 19 with a clear positional relationship to kinetochore attachment sites, linking centromere haplotype structure to rearrangement susceptibility in this CNS tumor.
Model & method
Human population genomics (>3,300 genomes) plus 142 oligodendroglioma cases; rare-k-mer analysis of short-read WGS with breakpoint mapping.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.053

Pericyte loss and endothelial activation define distinct vascular states in cerebral small vessel disease

Acta Neuropathol  A Chagnot & A Montagne
Key findings
Quantitative single-vessel analysis of 11,409 human cortical microvascular fragments across increasing cerebral small vessel disease severity and ageing shows that PDGFRβ signal (pericyte coverage) falls markedly with severity in both grey and white matter, while endothelial PECAM-1 and VCAM-1 rise, reflecting endothelial activation. Unsupervised clustering resolved three vascular states — preserved pericytes/low activation, reduced pericytes without activation, and reduced pericytes with activation — indicating that pericyte loss and endothelial activation are partially dissociated processes with marked heterogeneity at intermediate severity. The data are cross-sectional postmortem, so temporal ordering remains to be established.
Model & method
Human postmortem cortex (20 cases); multiplex immunohistochemistry with spectral unmixing, automated single-vessel quantification and Gaussian mixture clustering.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03102-6

Huntington's disease vasculopathy is barrier-type-selective and arteriolar-dominant, and reversible by S1pr1 stimulation

Nat Commun  Krzysztof Kucharz & Martin Johannes Lauritzen
Key findings
In vivo two-photon imaging in R6/2 Huntington's-model mice reveals divergent barrier failure: surface blood–CSF-barrier vessels leak paracellularly, whereas parenchymal blood–brain-barrier vessels show increased vesicular transport and adsorptive-mediated transcytosis, with arterioles most affected. Barrier pathology was spatially matched to astrocyte activation in both mouse and human HD brain. An S1pr1 agonist reversed blood–CSF-barrier leakage and partially restored BBB function, selectively reducing arteriolar transcytosis without affecting capillaries or venules — redefining HD vascular pathology as vessel-type-specific and nominating S1pr1 modulation as a therapeutic lever. Causal and therapeutic evidence is mouse-based with human pathological correlation.
Model & method
R6/2 HD mouse model with human HD postmortem validation; in vivo two-photon microscopy of barrier permeability and S1pr1 agonist intervention.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77474-4

Global brain activity links subcortical degeneration to cortical tau across Braak regions in early Alzheimer's disease

Nat Commun  Yutong Mao & Xiao Liu
Key findings
Using multimodal ADNI data, infra-slow (<0.1 Hz) global brain (gBOLD) activity was related to nucleus basalis of Meynert volume and cortical tau in early AD. NbM degeneration was associated with reduced gBOLD activity and spatially co-localized tau that appeared in early Braak regions during the preclinical stage (cognitively unimpaired, abnormal CSF) and extended to more advanced Braak areas at the prodromal (MCI) stage. The authors propose infra-slow gBOLD activity as a functional mediator linking subcortical neuromodulatory degeneration to cortical tau spread. The evidence is cross-sectional and associational, so the mediation is a candidate mechanism rather than established causation.
Model & method
Human (ADNI cohort); resting-state fMRI gBOLD, structural MRI of NbM and tau-PET, with mediation/association analysis across disease stages.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77748-x

Incident versus prevalent progressive supranuclear palsy in the aging brain

Acta Neuropathol  Benjamin J Cushing & Peter T Nelson
Key findings
In the NACC neuropathology dataset (38 U.S. ADRCs; 6,845 autopsied participants, 297 with PSP pathology), PSP pathology was more common than other FTLD-tau subtypes and, among participants recruited while cognitively normal, appeared in 3.2% — higher than classic prevalence estimates of ~1:10,000 and often clinically atypical. Incident PSP cases (recruited normal) died older than prevalent cases (89.5 vs 76.3 years), were less likely to show stereotypical PSP features but more likely to have parkinsonism, and in a digital-pathology subsample had more tufted astrocytes and fewer neurofibrillary tangles in the putamen. The finding reframes PSP as a relatively common, underrecognized tauopathy of the aging brain, with the caveat that ADRC cohorts are a convenience sample.
Model & method
Human autopsy cohort (NACC/38 ADRCs) with a University of Kentucky digital-pathology subsample; longitudinal clinical–neuropathologic comparison stratified by recruitment cognitive status.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03100-8

Sphingolipid and ceramide associations with tau pathology vary across ethnoracial groups in postmortem brain

Alzheimers Dement  Annalise Schweickart & Jan Krumsiek
Key findings
In 547 postmortem brain samples from Hispanic American, non-Hispanic African American and White subjects — the largest multiethnic AD brain metabolomics cohort to date — 48 metabolites showed significant heterogeneity in their association with tau pathology (Braak stage), enriched for TCA-cycle intermediates and sphingolipid–ceramide lipids. Metabolite–tau associations were driven predominantly by the Hispanic American and non-Hispanic White groups, revealing ethnoracial-specific metabolic signatures of tau pathology and underscoring that emerging metabolic targets may not generalize across populations. Associations are cross-sectional and group-level.
Model & method
Human postmortem brain (n = 547, three ethnoracial groups); metabolome-wide association study of metabolite abundance versus AD neuropathology.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71690

Intrastriatal injection of amplified Lewy body dementia fibrils seeds LBD-structured α-synuclein and neurodegeneration in mice

bioRxiv  Bagade & Kotzbauer
Key findings
Amplified α-synuclein fibrils derived from three LBD autopsy cases were injected intrastriatally into mice carrying a human SNCA transgene on a Snca-null background. By 3 months, phospho-α-synuclein accumulated bilaterally in striatum, cortex and amygdala, with a reduction of substantia nigra dopaminergic neurons, Lewy-body-like inclusions in survivors, and impaired rotarod performance; fibril burden rose 3.6-fold between 1 and 3 months and then plateaued. Cryo-EM 2D class averages of fibrils re-amplified from mouse brain matched those from human LBD tissue, indicating the Lewy fold is propagated in vivo, though a high-resolution 3D structure could not be resolved. If it holds, this provides a human-conformation α-synuclein model for mechanism, imaging-biomarker and therapeutic studies; as a preprint the structural match rests on 2D class comparison.
Model & method
Humanized SNCA transgenic mice; intrastriatal amplified-fibril seeding, neuropathology, rotarod, and single-particle cryo-EM of re-amplified fibrils.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.06.757103

A squirrel monkey model of spontaneous cerebral amyloid angiopathy and amyloid-related imaging abnormalities

bioRxiv  Wadghiri & Scholtzova
Key findings
Geriatric squirrel monkeys, which spontaneously develop abundant CAA and ARIA, were imaged with 7T R2* mapping and T2*/FLAIR. Whole-brain and grey-matter R2* rose with age, while white-matter R2* increased then reversed in late life; T2*-weighted hypointensities corresponded histologically to CAA-positive iron-laden vessels with perivascular inflammation. The authors confirmed spontaneous ARIA-H and ARIA-E, with ARIA-E hyperintensities marked by reactive gliosis and BBB disruption and more advanced lesions by perivascular lymphocytic infiltration and myelin/axonal injury. This nominates R2* as a candidate antemortem marker of CAA severity and ARIA risk relevant to anti-amyloid immunotherapy; findings are descriptive in a non-human primate model.
Model & method
Squirrel monkeys across age groups; 7T multi-gradient-echo MRI with quantitative R2* mapping and MRI–histopathology correlation.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.08.757401

Spontaneous hemodynamic oscillations during sleep coordinate directional CSF flow in the subarachnoid space and ventricles

bioRxiv  Chen & Lewis
Key findings
Using 7T MRI that simultaneously measures cortical hemodynamics and slow subarachnoid CSF flow during human sleep, the authors show that spontaneous infra-slow hemodynamic oscillations are coupled to spatially structured, directional CSF flow across both ventricular and subarachnoid compartments. Hemodynamic oscillations and fourth-ventricle bulk flow track brain-wide CSF movement at different phases, consistent with a single coordinated cycle. Compared with cardiac pulsation, hemodynamic-driven flow was smaller in instantaneous amplitude but produced greater cumulative displacement — and thus greater transport potential — per oscillation. The work extends sleep-associated CSF dynamics from the fourth ventricle to flow around cortical tissue, relevant to clearance in brain aging; it is observational human imaging.
Model & method
Human sleep study; 7T MRI simultaneously capturing cortical hemodynamics and subarachnoid/ventricular CSF flow.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.07.757422

Normative transcriptomic clues to thalamic developmental vulnerability in C9orf72-FTLD

medRxiv  So & Borroni
Key findings
C9orf72 repeat expansions are the commonest genetic cause of frontotemporal lobar degeneration, and smaller thalamic volumes appear even in asymptomatic young-adult carriers, suggesting a neurodevelopmental contribution. Pairing normative transcriptomic reference data with neuroimaging, this study examines whether specific thalamic nuclei show selective vulnerability in C9orf72 expansion carriers and relates that vulnerability to regional gene-expression patterns. The approach links thalamic structural findings to developmental transcriptomic signatures rather than purely degenerative ones; as a preprint, inferences about developmental origins are associational.
Model & method
Human neuroimaging of C9orf72 expansion carriers integrated with normative brain transcriptomic reference atlases, analyzed at the level of thalamic nuclei.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.09.26365172

Separation, isolation and membrane lipid profiling of selectively vulnerable and resilient neurons

bioRxiv  Sadek & Maesako
Key findings
To attack selective neuronal vulnerability directly, the authors developed a labeling method using two cell-impermeable dyes to distinguish neurons that degenerate from those that resist a stressor, then separated them by FACS. As proof of concept under oxidative stress, lipidomic analysis showed that membrane lipid composition differed between vulnerable and resilient neurons. The method is a technical platform — demonstrated in culture on a single stressor — intended to enable molecular comparison of vulnerable versus resilient neurons for diagnostic and therapeutic discovery, rather than a disease-specific mechanistic result.
Model & method
Cultured neurons under oxidative stress; dual cell-impermeable dye labeling, FACS separation and membrane lipidomics.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.06.757005
