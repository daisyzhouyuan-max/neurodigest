### Age-dependent structural evolution of a functional amyloid contributes to memory impairment in Drosophila
**Songxian Ou & Ruben Hervas | Cell**

**Why it matters:** Functional amyloids are usually treated as static end-states, but the Orb2 amyloid that stores long-term memory in flies progressively remodels with age. Translation-promoting assemblies predominate early in life, whereas aging shifts the ensemble toward structurally distinct, destabilization-resistant conformers with reduced translational activity, coinciding with memory impairment. Boosting memory-related chaperones shifted old Orb2 back toward youthful, translation-competent states and restored memory, while forcing aged-like states in young flies caused premature memory loss. This links a specific amyloid conformational trajectory — not amyloid presence per se — to cognitive aging.

**Model/system & primary method:** Drosophila melanogaster across adult lifespan; in vivo–derived Orb2 amyloid cryo-EM structures with chaperone manipulation and memory assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.10.009

### LAMP2A gene therapy mitigates Parkinson's disease-related pathology in nonhuman primates
**Marie-Laure Arotcarena & Maria Xilouri | Science Advances**

**Why it matters:** Chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA) clears α-synuclein through its lysosomal receptor LAMP2A, and prior rescue evidence was limited to rodents. Here, AAV-driven LAMP2A overexpression in the primate substantia nigra protected nigral dopaminergic neurons against α-synucleinopathy induced by injection of patient-derived Lewy body extracts, delayed formation and release of pathological α-synuclein species, and conferred motor benefit. Demonstrating efficacy in a primate, disease-relevant context substantially strengthens the translational case for augmenting CMA as a disease-modifying strategy.

**Model/system & primary method:** Nonhuman primates receiving intranigral PD patient-derived Lewy body extract; AAV-mediated LAMP2A overexpression with neuropathology and motor assessment.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeg1787

### Exome sequencing identifies population-specific genetic risk factors for progressive supranuclear palsy in Asia
**Pin-Shiuan Chen & Chin-Hsien Lin | Movement Disorders**

**Why it matters:** PSP genetics has been defined almost entirely in European populations. Whole-exome sequencing of 549 East and Southeast Asian PSP patients against 1,492 Taiwan Biobank controls found heterozygous pathogenic or likely-pathogenic variants in 5.3% (including 12 novel variants), with rare variants of uncertain significance in a further ~23%; novel variants were functionally evaluated in cellular and Drosophila models. The work reveals population-specific risk architecture for this primary tauopathy and broadens its mutational landscape, though the use of biobank rather than PSP-screened controls is a design limitation.

**Model/system & primary method:** 549 Asian PSP patients vs 1,492 controls; whole-exome sequencing with functional validation in cellular and Drosophila models.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70595

### SNP rs11618521 modifies NANOG-mediated expression of KPNA3 and the age at onset of spinocerebellar ataxia type 3
**Hezhou Han & Thorsten Schmidt | Acta Neuropathologica**

**Why it matters:** Nuclear localization of ataxin-3 drives spinocerebellar ataxia type 3 (SCA3), and the importin KPNA3 is central to that pathology. This study identifies a KPNA3-promoter SNP (rs11618521) as a modifier of SCA3 age at onset and of KPNA3 promoter activity, and shows the transcription factor NANOG exerts allele-specific control over that promoter. Pharmacological NANOG inhibition reduced KPNA3 levels and ameliorated aggregate formation, nominating the NANOG–KPNA3 axis as both an onset modifier and a candidate therapeutic node; the rescue evidence rests on cellular rather than in vivo models.

**Model/system & primary method:** SCA3 patient genetics plus promoter-reporter and aggregation assays in cell models; pharmacological NANOG inhibition.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03051-0

### The neurobiology of menopause: implications for brain aging and Alzheimer's risk
**Lisa Mosconi | Neuron**

**Key findings:** This review frames the menopausal transition as a neurobiological inflection point in women's brain aging and Alzheimer's vulnerability, arguing the brain is an active estrogen-sensing organ embedded in a lifespan ovarian–brain axis that is established embryonically and reconfigured at menopause. It synthesizes candidate mechanisms — altered hormone signaling, bioenergetics, synaptic plasticity, neurovascular function, inflammation and AD pathology — links menopausal timing to AD risk, and weighs estrogen therapy and the case for midlife risk stratification. A synthesis rather than primary data.

**Model/system & primary method:** Narrative review integrating human and preclinical neuroendocrine and AD literature.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.015

### Polygenic risk scores for LATE-neuropathologic change: implications for clinical utility
**Jung-Min Pyun & the ADNI | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** In ADNI participants aged ≥75, a polygenic risk score for limbic-predominant age-related TDP-43 encephalopathy (LATE-PRS) was associated with pathologically confirmed LATE (OR 2.29), independent of AD pathology. Among cognitively impaired individuals, higher LATE-PRS predicted faster executive-function decline in amyloid-PET-negative people. Because LATE clinically mimics and frequently co-occurs with AD yet lacks dedicated biomarkers, a genetic index offers a potential handle on this co-pathology — though the neuropathology sample is small (n = 99).

**Model/system & primary method:** ADNI; LATE-PRS tested against neuropathology (n = 99) and longitudinal cognition (n = 538), stratified by amyloid PET.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71824

### Chronic kidney disease and blood-based biomarkers of Alzheimer's disease: the Brain in Kidney Disease study
**Kerry M. Sheets & Anne M. Murray | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** In 443 participants, lower eGFR was strongly and inversely associated with serum brain-derived tau (BD-tau): mean levels rose from ~9 pg/mL at eGFR ≥60 to ~55 pg/mL at eGFR <15, and greater albuminuria was likewise associated with higher BD-tau. Because BD-tau is advanced as an AD-specific blood biomarker, this is a cautionary result — kidney function is a substantial confounder that must be accounted for when interpreting blood-based neurodegeneration markers. Cross-sectional.

**Model/system & primary method:** 443 participants (Brain in Kidney Disease study); serum blood-based biomarker assays across eGFR strata.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71940

### MRI-derived glymphatic-related composite captures brain and cognitive vulnerability associated with cardiovascular risk in older adults
**Zhan Wang & Jing Yu | Alzheimer's & Dementia**

**Key findings:** A multimodal MRI composite integrating choroid plexus volume and free-water fraction, the DTI-ALPS index, BOLD–CSF coupling, and basal-ganglia and deep white-matter perivascular spaces was associated with higher cardiovascular risk and, in turn, with thinner cortex in AD-vulnerable regions and poorer cognition in older adults. The index packages several putative glymphatic proxies into a single vulnerability measure linking vascular risk to brain and cognitive outcomes, but associations are correlational and the cohort is small (92 at baseline, 68 at 6-month reassessment).

**Model/system & primary method:** 92 older adults (68 followed to 6 months); multimodal MRI composite versus ASCVD risk, cortical thickness and cognition.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71961

### Motor cortex disinhibition is dissociated from clinical motor features in Parkinson's disease: longitudinal evidence
**Claudia Ammann & Guglielmo Foffani | Movement Disorders**

**Key findings:** Longitudinal transcranial magnetic stimulation across three cohorts (total n = 95) confirmed that short-interval intracortical inhibition (SICI) deficits in Parkinson's disease neither track clinical motor severity nor progress with disease stage; instead, motor-cortex disinhibition related to olfactory dysfunction, a dopamine-independent prodromal feature. The dissociation argues that primary motor cortex disinhibition is not a straightforward correlate of dopaminergic motor impairment, reframing a frequently cited cortical physiology marker in PD.

**Model/system & primary method:** Three PD cohorts (n = 95); 2-year longitudinal TMS measuring SICI.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70517

### A model of brain function to predict tau across Alzheimer's disease and related disorders
**Nick Corriveau-Lecavalier & the ADNI | Nature Communications**

**Key findings:** A machine-learning framework grounded in a computational model of brain function predicted AD-type tau (flortaucipir-PET) from FDG-PET across AD and related disorders. Trained on 1,088 participants and validated in three independent cohorts (including ADNI, n = 295), it predicted regional tau at the participant level (R² ≈ 0.34–0.71) and classified low versus high tau in clinical scenarios. The approach offers a route to infer tau burden from the more widely acquired FDG-PET where tau-PET is unavailable.

**Model/system & primary method:** 1,088 training participants plus external cohorts; computational brain-function model with machine learning mapping FDG-PET to flortaucipir-PET.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78300-7

### Neuronal and glial transcriptional states vary with proximity to tangle-bearing neurons in the human entorhinal cortex
**S. L. Boulger & P. M. Matthews | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Combining 6,000-plex spatial transcriptomics with PHF1 tangle immunofluorescence (167,176 cells, 9 donors) and imaging mass cytometry (170,195 cells, 62 donors), the authors measured each cell's distance to the nearest tangle. Tangle-bearing layer 2–3 excitatory neurons occupied a late trajectory of rising microtubule-associated, synaptic and V-ATPase expression with elevated p62 and K48-ubiquitin; critically, tangle-free neurons increasingly shared this stressed state the closer they lay to a tangle, and nearby glia showed higher disease-associated scores (astrocytes with reduced GFAP). Because more neurons die than form tangles, the gradient suggests a tangle-proximal field effect on vulnerability. Cross-sectional human tissue; associations are by spatial proximity, not lineage tracing.

**Model/system & primary method:** Postmortem human entorhinal cortex; spatial transcriptomics plus imaging mass cytometry with tangle-distance mapping.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.27.762234

### Phenotypic screening in Parkinson's iPSC-dopamine neurons identifies the FASN–SREBP1 axis as a potential therapeutic target
**M. C. Caiazza & R. Wade-Martins | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Using impaired intracellular calcium release as a disease-relevant phenotype in GBA-N370S patient iPSC-derived dopaminergic neurons, a 4,108-compound phenotypic screen identified fatty acid synthase (FASN) as the most enriched target, with compounds' ability to rescue calcium release correlating with biochemical anti-FASN potency. The result nominates the FASN–SREBP1 lipogenic axis as a therapeutic node in GBA-related PD and demonstrates calcium-phenotype screening in iPSC-dopaminergic neurons for target discovery. Genotype-specific (GBA-N370S) and in vitro.

**Model/system & primary method:** GBA-N370S patient iPSC-derived dopaminergic neurons; 4,108-compound calcium-phenotype screen.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.27.761977

### The human circular Tau 12→7 RNA is translated in mouse neurons and promotes tau aggregation
**N. Bagheri & S. Stamm | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The human MAPT gene generates circular RNAs not expressed in rodents. AAV expression of circTau(12→7) in mouse brain underwent rolling-circle translation to protein in a strikingly neuron-specific manner despite a ubiquitous promoter, and produced tau aggregates resembling neurofibrillary tangles that incorporated endogenous mouse tau. The data support a model in which neuron-specific translation of a human tau circRNA seeds aggregation — a candidate human-specific contributor to tauopathy. Overexpression model.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse brain via AAV, with HEK293 comparison; circRNA expression, translation and tau-aggregation readouts.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.26.761947

### Multiscale transcriptomic architecture of the ALS motor cortex
**A. Goel & G. Alterovitz | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Across approximately 600 postmortem motor-cortex transcriptomes from the Target ALS resource, pathway-level gene-set variation analysis and consensus clustering resolved seven reproducible ALS subtypes with distinct inflammatory, damage-response, metabolic, glial and neuronal/synaptic signatures. Restricting analysis to a single pathology-relevant region avoids confounding regional variation with subtype structure and offers a molecular stratification framework for a clinically heterogeneous disease. Postmortem and descriptive.

**Model/system & primary method:** ~600 postmortem ALS motor-cortex transcriptomes (Target ALS); gene-set variation analysis with consensus clustering in pathway space.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.24.761595

### TMEM106B C-terminal fragment aggregation exacerbates α-synuclein and TDP-43 proteinopathy phenotypes
**R. Riordan & B. Kraemer | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** A C-terminal fragment of TMEM106B accumulates in lesions across neurodegenerative disease, and TMEM106B variation drives dementia risk. In bigenic C. elegans, co-expressing aggregating TMEM106B C-terminal fragment with α-synuclein or TDP-43 worsened neuronal degeneration and pathogenic protein accumulation relative to single proteinopathies. The findings position the TMEM106B fragment as a cross-seeding amplifier that could aggravate co-occurring proteinopathies and blunt therapies. Invertebrate model.

**Model/system & primary method:** Transgenic and bigenic C. elegans; neuronal degeneration and protein-aggregation assays.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.29.762961
