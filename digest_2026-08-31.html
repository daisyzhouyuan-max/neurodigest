1Longitudinal plasma phosphorylated tau217 classification and the association with Alzheimer's disease progression

Alzheimers Dement  Guoyu Lan & Tengfei Guo
Why it matters
In a multi-cohort study of 2,117 individuals, baseline plasma p-tau217 cutoffs for amyloid-PET positivity remained 86–95% accurate over up to 5 years of follow-up, and most p-tau217-positive individuals stayed stable while an "intermediate zone" group progressed to positivity at high rates (44–78%). Stable- and progress-to-positive profiles tracked with faster tau accumulation, hippocampal atrophy, and incident dementia, supporting p-tau217 as a scalable longitudinal risk-stratification tool rather than a single-timepoint screen.
Model & method
Human multi-cohort longitudinal study; plasma p-tau217 immunoassay benchmarked against amyloid-β PET.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71783

2AGG repeat expansion and aggregation of BIN1 in multiple system atrophy

Brain  Kodai Kume & Hideshi Kawakami
Why it matters
Long-read sequencing of a familial multiple system atrophy (MSA) proband identified an AGG repeat expansion in BIN1, the first candidate genetic driver reported for this fatal, sporadic α-synucleinopathy. Expansions >80 repeats were significantly enriched in pathologically confirmed MSA brains versus controls (13.4% vs 0%), and correlated with BIN1-positive glial cytoplasmic inclusions and increased insoluble BIN1; the association was weaker and not statistically significant in the larger clinically diagnosed cohort, so replication in independent pathology-confirmed series is needed.
Model & method
Human genetics (long-read whole-genome sequencing, clinical and pathology-confirmed patient cohorts) with postmortem neuropathology and biochemical fractionation.
https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag194

3Neuron-derived SPP1 instructs microglia to limit degeneration

Sci Adv  Song Li & Tatjana C Jakobs
Why it matters
SPP1 (osteopontin) secreted by stressed neurons reprograms microglia toward a neuroprotective, autophagy-competent state after injury in mouse models of glaucoma and optic nerve damage, preserving neuronal survival and visual function. SPP1 is elevated in human/primate glaucomatous retina and correlates with neuronal resilience, and in postmortem AD brain neuronal SPP1 tracks with neuronal survival despite defective microglial autophagy around plaques; in human iPSC co-cultures SPP1 enhanced microglial Aβ clearance. The causal injury-response work is mouse/retina-based, with human data limited to correlation.
Model & method
Mouse glaucoma/optic-nerve-injury models, human and primate glaucomatous retina, postmortem AD brain tissue, human iPSC-derived microglia–neuron co-culture.
https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee4940

4Leucine-rich repeat kinase 2 impairs the release sites of Parkinson's disease vulnerable dopamine axons

Nat Commun  Chuyu Chen & Loukia Parisiadou
Why it matters
Using intersectional genetic mouse models, this study shows pathogenic LRRK2 is enriched in the specific SNc dopamine-neuron subtypes most vulnerable in Parkinson's disease and disrupts presynaptic active-zone organization by increasing RAB3 phosphorylation and impairing its interaction with RIM1/RIM2, reducing spontaneous and evoked striatal dopamine release in vivo. This defines a cell-autonomous, subtype-specific synaptic mechanism that precedes overt neuronal loss and nominates early synaptic deficits as a therapeutic target — evidence to date is confined to mouse models.
Model & method
Intersectional genetic mouse models, proximity-labeling proteomics, in vivo striatal dopamine release measurements.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75194-3

5Characterizing shared and distinctive molecular phenotypes across motor regions in ALS with and without TDP-43 pathology in a veteran cohort

bioRxiv  Doyle, P. H. & Orr, M.
Why it matters
Spatially resolved profiling of postmortem primary motor cortex and cervical/lumbar spinal cord from ALS patients (pTDP-43-positive and -negative) and controls found convergent senescence-associated transcriptomic and proteomic signatures in vulnerable motor neurons across both pathology groups, alongside region- and pathology-specific differences in RNA processing, mitochondrial function, proteostasis, and inflammation pathways. The findings support a model in which motor neurons adopt a persistent stress-adaptive senescence state that permits short-term survival at the cost of progressive dysfunction, nominating senescence pathways as a therapeutic target — this is cross-sectional postmortem human tissue and a preprint, so causal and temporal claims are not established.
Model & method
Postmortem human ALS motor cortex and spinal cord (veteran cohort); targeted bulk transcriptomics and GeoMx digital spatial profiling of individual motor neurons.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.28.747944

A bifunctional optical reporter for tracking estrogen response dynamics in neurons

Neuron  Alexandra L Cara & J Edward van Veen
Key findings
The authors developed neuro-seeER, a fluorescent bifunctional reporter that tracks estrogen receptor-dependent estrogen response dynamics in vitro and longitudinally in behaving mice. Using repeated "snapshot" photometry, they detected coordinated exogenous-estrogen responses across multiple hypothalamic sites and captured endogenous estrogen response dynamics across the estrous cycle, revealing individual differences in response timing — a tools/methods advance rather than a disease-mechanism finding.
Model & method
Mouse in vivo fiber photometry (with in vitro validation) using an estrogen-receptor-dependent fluorescent reporter.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.07.031

Extracellular matrix remodeling upregulates hippocampal neurogenic niche stiffness and impairs neurogenesis in Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimers Dement  Weiqi Sun & Jie Zheng
Key findings
The hippocampal dentate gyrus subgranular zone stiffens as early as 3 months in 5×FAD mice, driven by ECM remodeling; artificially stiffening this niche in wild-type mice suppressed adult neurogenesis, while softening it in 5×FAD mice with glycosaminoglycanases (HAase1) preserved neurogenesis. Single-nucleus sequencing and conditional knockdowns implicate an integrin-β1/YAP1 mechanotransduction axis, and matched ECM/neurogenesis changes were seen in postmortem AD tissue. Causal manipulation is mouse-only; the human data are correlative.
Model & method
5×FAD mice, atomic force microscopy stiffness mapping, single-nucleus RNA sequencing, viral conditional knockdown/overexpression; postmortem human AD hippocampus.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71784

Core activation program and selective regional responsiveness of microglia during aging and parabiosis

Cell Rep  Huma Naz & Guoyan Zhao
Key findings
Single-cell profiling across four mouse brain regions identifies a reproducible core gene program driving microglial activation and marked region-specific heterogeneity in how microglia respond to aging and to parabiosis-mediated exposure to young or old blood. The cerebellum was consistently the most responsive region to parabiosis while the striatum was largely unresponsive, pointing to region-specific vulnerability that should inform where microglia-targeted rejuvenation strategies are likely to work.
Model & method
Mouse parabiosis (young/old blood exchange) with single-cell RNA sequencing across cerebellum, cortex, hippocampus, and striatum.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117919

CELF2 promotes 4R Tau splicing via nuclear clustering and drives cognitive dysfunction in tauopathy models

Nat Commun  Xin Li & Lizhen Chen
Key findings
The RNA-binding protein CELF2 promotes pathogenic 4R-tau exon-10 splicing through an intrinsically disordered hinge domain that drives multivalent nuclear clustering with NOVA2 and SFPQ; a single conserved residue (D388) is required for this assembly and for splicing activity. In vivo, CELF2's assembly capacity correlated with 4R-tau expression and with locomotor and cognitive performance, linking condensate formation by an RNA-binding protein to the tau-isoform imbalance central to tauopathy.
Model & method
In vitro splicing and condensate-formation assays combined with in vivo behavioral (locomotor/cognitive) testing in a tauopathy model.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76241-9

Temporal progression of auditory brainstem dysfunction and behavioral phenotypes in mouse models of tauopathy

J Neurosci  Ann M Nguyen & Xiangmin Xu
Key findings
In PS19 (P301S tau) mice, auditory brainstem response (ABR) abnormalities — reduced, delayed neural responses to sound — appeared by 3 months, before any detectable anxiety-like or motor changes. Adding the ApoE4 allele accelerated and sustained ABR deficits alongside emerging behavioral impairment by 7 months, suggesting ABR could serve as an early, noninvasive biomarker of tau-driven neurodegeneration and that ApoE4 modulates the pace of circuit dysfunction. Findings are limited to a single transgenic tau model.
Model & method
PS19 and ApoE4/PS19 mice; auditory brainstem response recordings paired with elevated-plus-maze and rotarod behavioral assays.
https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0511-26.2026

Centrally Acting Medications, Chronic Pain, and Orthopedic Surgical History Among Former American Football Players

Neurology  Alexa Puleio & as the DIAGNOSE CTE Research Project
Key findings
In the DIAGNOSE CTE cohort of 236 former professional and collegiate American football players and unexposed controls, centrally acting medication (CAM) use and average chronic pain scores — but not orthopedic surgery count — were independently associated with higher odds of neurobehavioral dysregulation and, for CAMs, lower MoCA scores, after adjusting for age, education, race, and years played. Neither CAMs nor pain were associated with consensus-diagnosed cognitive impairment. This is an important confound for interpreting cognitive/behavioral symptoms in CTE research, since polypharmacy and chronic pain — not just repetitive head impact exposure — track with neurobehavioral symptoms.
Model & method
Human cross-sectional cohort study (n=236) with logistic and linear regression against traumatic encephalopathy syndrome (TES) research criteria.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218475

Mitochondrial DNA copy number in neurodegenerative diseases: a global meta-analysis of 156 comparisons across 76 studies

bioRxiv  Mathews, R. & Havird, J. C.
Key findings
A systematic review and meta-analysis of 76 studies (156 comparisons) found no overall difference in mitochondrial DNA copy number (mtDNA-CN) between people with and without neurodegenerative disease, but heterogeneity across studies was extreme (I²=99.5%), explained mostly by the specific disease diagnosed: Alzheimer's disease showed a 21% mtDNA-CN decrease while Parkinson's disease showed none. Decreases were larger at older ages and in earlier-published studies, suggesting the field's prevailing assumption of universal mtDNA-CN decline in neurodegeneration is an oversimplification carried forward from early, possibly biased, studies — a caution for using mtDNA-CN as a general biomarker without disease-specific validation.
Model & method
Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of published human observational studies; no new experimental data generated.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.25.747144

Hyperactivation of the AXL-ICD/SIRT2 axis by Amyloid-β impairs astrocytic autophagic flux and exacerbates neuroinflammation

bioRxiv  Kim, T. Y. & Lee, C. J.
Key findings
Amyloid-β drives γ-secretase-dependent cleavage of astrocytic AXL, generating a nuclear intracellular domain (AXL-ICD) that phase-separates and, via SIRT2-mediated chromatin remodeling, initially promotes autophagy gene transcription; excessive AXL-ICD, however, paradoxically hyperstabilizes microtubules, impairs autophagosome–lysosome fusion, and drives H2O2 accumulation and astrogliosis. This axis is active in astrocytes of postmortem AD brain, and a peptide that disrupts the AXL-ICD/SIRT2 interaction restored autophagic flux and reduced plaque burden and astrogliosis in APP/PS1 mice — a mechanistically detailed and potentially druggable pathway, though the therapeutic peptide has only been tested in mice. Note: this is a v2 revision; the preprint was first posted in late August 2026 (~1 week before this digest window), with the current version posted 2026-08-31.
Model & method
Postmortem human AD astrocytes and APP/PS1 mice; biochemical/imaging assays of AXL-ICD phase separation and autophagic flux, peptide-based pathway disruption.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.23.746508

RGC-specific reversal of lipid peroxidation drives neuroprotection and vision restoration in optic nerve ischemia by targeting GPX4

bioRxiv  Yang, M. & Liao, Y. J.
Key findings
In human NAION (nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy) retina and a validated mouse model, ischemic injury triggers marked phospholipid peroxidation in retinal ganglion cells (RGCs). RGC-specific, and especially mitochondria-targeted, overexpression of GPX4 restored axonal and mitochondrial bioenergetics, suppressed oxidative stress, and preserved RGC survival, axonal transport, and visual function; the clinically tested GPX mimetic Ebselen reproduced these protective effects. This nominates lipid-peroxide detoxification as a translatable therapeutic strategy for a currently untreatable ischemic optic neuropathy, though clinical efficacy remains to be tested.
Model & method
Human NAION retinal tissue and a validated mouse NAION model; AAV-mediated GPX4 overexpression, real-time multiparametric in vivo imaging, pharmacologic rescue with Ebselen.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.25.747113

PD GENEration: An International Parkinson's Disease Genetic Research Study

medRxiv  Ghosh Galvelis, K. & The Parkinson's Foundation PD GENEration Study,
Key findings
This report describes the implementation and six-year scale-up of PD GENEration, a Parkinson's Foundation/GP2-partnered program offering no-cost clinical genetic testing and counseling to people with Parkinson's disease. The program has enrolled over 32,000 participants across 10 countries since 2019, using CAP/CLIA-certified whole-genome sequencing against a core PD gene panel with optional expanded panels, and emphasizes open genomic data sharing to accelerate precision-medicine trial recruitment. This is a descriptive/implementation report rather than a hypothesis-driven finding, but is notable as large-scale infrastructure that will shape future PD genetics research. Note: this is a v2 revision; the preprint was first posted around 2026-05-20 (roughly three months before this digest window), with the current version posted 2026-08-28.
Model & method
Human observational, multi-country clinical genetic-testing and counseling program; whole-genome sequencing.

Mitochondrial dysfunction as a hallmark of brain senescence in telomerase-deficient mice

bioRxiv  Palomares, D. & Suelves, N.
Key findings
In telomerase-deficient mice (a model of telomere-attrition-driven cellular senescence), proteomic profiling — more than transcriptomic — identified impaired electron transport chain activity and reduced energetic status as the dominant dysregulated pathway in the aging brain, despite preserved ETC complex integrity and mitochondrial content, indicating a functional rather than structural mitochondrial deficit. Senescent primary neurons showed increased ROS and reduced ATP, and affected mice showed region-specific (subiculum, cortical layer V) rather than widespread reductions in neuronal density, supporting mitochondrial dysfunction as a candidate driver of selective regional vulnerability in brain aging.
Model & method
Telomerase-deficient (telomere-attrition) mouse model; brain proteomics/transcriptomics, isolated-mitochondria functional assays, primary neuron culture.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.25.746691
