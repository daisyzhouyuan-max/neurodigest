### Seeds from ALS patients homozygous for the SOD1 D90A mutation transmit two types of SOD1 aggregation and motor neuron disease
**Caitlin Henne & Peter M Andersen | Acta Neuropathol**

**Why it matters:** Strengthens the prion-like propagation hypothesis for SOD1-linked ALS by showing that seed material from human D90A-homozygous patients — not just transgenic mice — transmits two distinct SOD1 aggregate strains and shortens survival when inoculated into hSOD1G85R mice. Demonstrates that self-templating pathogenic conformers exist in human patient tissue even in a genotype previously considered atypical because it usually requires biallelic inheritance. Reinforces strain-dependent phenotypic diversity as a feature of SOD1 ALS pathogenesis with direct relevance to therapeutics aimed at blocking aggregate templating.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem spinal cord seed material from 6 D90A-homozygous ALS patients and 4 controls, inoculated intraspinally into adult hSOD1G85R transgenic mice; survival and aggregate strain typing.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-026-03078-3

### Cross-species single-cell atlas of the striatum defines cell-type and subregion disease vulnerabilities
**Raleigh M Linville & Myriam Heiman | Cell**

**Why it matters:** Provides the first large-scale, cross-species single-nucleus atlas resolving striatal subregional cell-type specialization, with direct disease relevance: pairing transcriptomic and somatic CAG repeat-expansion measurements identifies which striatal neuron subtypes are most vulnerable in Huntington's disease. Also maps human-enriched opioid receptor expression and antipsychotic drug action sites, a resource useful for both movement-disorder and neuropsychiatric drug targeting. Notable human-mouse divergence in subregional gradients cautions against uncritical extrapolation from mouse striatal circuitry to human disease mechanisms.

**Model/system & primary method:** Single-nucleus RNA-seq across 109 human and 22 mouse striatal samples (dorsal and ventral); integration with GWAS, pharmacology, and somatic repeat-expansion data.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.006

### Whole-exome sequencing in individuals with obsessive-compulsive disorder and chronic tic disorders identifies 36 large-effect risk genes
**Belinda Wang & Matthew W State | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** The largest whole-exome sequencing study of OCD and chronic tic disorders to date (3,964 individuals, 2,418 trios) expands high-confidence large-effect risk genes from 4 to 36, a substantial leap for disorders that have lagged behind other psychiatric conditions in tractable genetic targets. Risk genes converge transcriptionally in postnatal cerebellum and pre/postnatal cortex and striatum and overlap with broader neurodevelopmental disease genes, arguing for shared developmental circuitry across OCD, tics, and other neurodevelopmental conditions. Provides concrete molecular entry points for mechanistic follow-up and drug-target identification in a space that has had very few.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human whole-exome sequencing; case-trio de novo and rare-variant burden analysis; transcriptomic/network convergence analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02419-5

### State-dependent signal flow hierarchy in the human cerebral cortex
**Younghyun Oh & Seok-Jun Hong | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Introduces integrated effective connectivity (iEC), a new neuroimaging framework that recovers directed, hierarchical signal flow across the human cortex from noninvasive data and validates against histologically defined feedforward/feedback pathways. Shows the cortical processing hierarchy is not static but reorganizes by brain state — flattening during externally oriented processing and steepening during internally focused states — linking macroscale connectivity dynamics to interoceptive engagement. A methodological advance with potential utility for characterizing hierarchy disruption in neuropsychiatric and neurodegenerative conditions, though this study itself is descriptive/methodological rather than disease-focused.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human resting/task fMRI; integrated effective connectivity modeling validated against histological connectivity data.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02389-8

### YTHDC1 functions as a molecular chaperone to suppress ALS-linked hnRNPA1 mutants from aggregation
**Kang Ren & Liangqian Huang | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Identifies YTHDC1, an RNA-binding protein, as a new class of ATP-independent molecular chaperone that prevents and reverses protein aggregation via an acidic low-complexity segment, independent of its RNA m6A-binding activity. YTHDC1 maintains liquid-like condensates of the ALS-linked protein hnRNPA1, delays fibrillization of disease-associated hnRNPA1 mutants, limits their stress-granule sequestration, and rescues neurite growth defects the mutants cause in primary neurons. Defines a previously unrecognized proteostasis pathway and nominates YTHDC1 chaperone activity as a potential therapeutic target for ALS and related proteinopathies, though the evidence here is cellular/biochemical and primary-neuron based rather than from an in vivo disease model.

**Model/system & primary method:** Biochemical reconstitution and condensate/aggregation assays; primary neuron culture expressing ALS-linked hnRNPA1 mutants.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77016-y

### Cofilin 1 Is an Extracellular Vesicle-Associated Plasma Biomarker of Parkinson's Disease in Humans
**Deepthy Chandran & Madhusoodanan Urulangodi | Ann Neurol**

**Key findings:** Plasma extracellular vesicle (EV)-associated cofilin 1 (CFL1) is identified as a PD-specific biomarker, elevated in PD versus controls (AUC 0.87) and able to distinguish PD from progressive supranuclear palsy (AUC 0.914); levels were independent of age, disease stage, and motor severity. CFL1 is enriched in L1CAM-positive (putatively neuronal-origin) EVs and detected in both human and mouse PD brain tissue, suggesting a possible pathogenic role rather than a purely passive marker role. A blood-based, minimally invasive diagnostic candidate, though the abstract does not detail longitudinal stability or an independent replication cohort.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human plasma EV proteomics with ELISA validation, ROC/precision-recall diagnostic analysis; immunohistochemistry in human and mouse PD brain tissue.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78348

### The Effect of LRRK2 and GBA1 Mutations on Survival in Early- and Late-Onset Parkinson's Disease
**Raz Rubin & Avner Thaler | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** In 2,039 Ashkenazi PD patients genotyped for LRRK2 and GBA1 variants, GBA1-PD carried increased mortality risk (HR 1.52) while LRRK2-PD carried reduced mortality risk (HR 0.76) relative to idiopathic PD; median survival across all groups (18–20 years from onset) was shorter than population norms. Early-onset PD (<50) showed longer disease duration but lower survival and younger age at death (67.8 vs 80.2 years) than late-onset PD, a pattern consistent across genetic subgroups. One of the largest genotype-stratified survival analyses in PD to date, with direct relevance to genetic counseling, though it is registry-based and restricted to a single-ancestry (Ashkenazi) cohort.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cohort study (n=2,039); GBA1/LRRK2 genotyping linked to national death registries; Cox regression survival analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70494

### Brain-derived plasma p-tau217 shows enhanced dynamic range for Alzheimer's disease neuropathological change
**Marisa N Denkinger & Nicholas J Ashton | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In 288 neuropathologically characterized end-of-life plasma samples, brain-derived (BD) p-tau217 and BD-based ratios showed larger dynamic range than standard plasma p-tau217 and improved discrimination of AD neuropathologic change (BD p-tau217/BD tau AUC 0.89 vs. 0.82 for standard p-tau217); BD p-tau217/p-tau217 best tracked continuous tangle burden (R² = 0.68) and best predicted CDR-SB decline (R² = 0.26). Suggests a brain-specific tau fraction can reduce the off-target elevations of plasma p-tau217 seen in some non-AD conditions while improving prognostic sensitivity. Cross-sectional and autopsy-anchored — strong neuropathological validation, but not a prospective clinical-utility trial.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human end-of-life plasma (n=288) with neuropathological gold-standard staging (NIA-AA, Thal phase, Braak stage, tau-PET); automated immunoassay for brain-derived p-tau217.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71773

### Female vulnerability to microvascular transport dysfunction emerges early across the Alzheimer's disease continuum
**Manxi Xu & Guolin Ma | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Using quantitative transport mapping (QTM)-derived cerebral perfusion velocity, microvascular transport deficits emerge as early as subjective cognitive decline (including in the hippocampus), preceding the MCI/AD-stage declines seen with conventional cerebral blood flow. QTM shows significant sex × diagnosis interactions not seen with CBF, with reductions specifically in females that track plasma NfL/GFAP and partially mediate the hippocampal volume–cognition relationship. Positions microvascular transport dysfunction as an early, sex-differentiated contributor along the AD continuum rather than a late-stage epiphenomenon, though the cross-sectional design (n=182) cannot establish whether microvascular change precedes or follows early neurodegeneration.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multi-delay arterial spin-labeling MRI across the AD continuum (n=182); QTM velocity vs. CBF, mediation analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71811

### Gender-associated differences in serum metabolite profiles among patients with Parkinson's Disease
**Marino, C. & Usiello, A. | medRxiv** *v2 revision of a preprint first posted October 2025*

**Key findings:** NMR-based serum metabolomics in 245 PD patients (121 idiopathic, 124 carrying LRRK2/TMEM175/PARK2/PINK1/PARK7/GBA1 variants) and 137 sex-matched controls finds sex, rather than genetic subtype, to be the dominant determinant of the circulating metabolic signature. Male PD patients show reduced glutamate and elevated tryptophan alongside altered energy-metabolism intermediates; female PD patients show altered threonine, choline, phospholipid, fatty-acid, and ketone-body profiles. Idiopathic and genetic PD converge on largely shared metabolic phenotypes within each sex, suggesting sex should be a stratification variable in PD metabolomic biomarker work — though the cross-sectional design cannot establish whether these differences are causal, compensatory, or downstream consequences of disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human serum, ¹H-NMR metabolomics; cross-sectional case-control comparison stratified by sex and genotype.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.30.25339159

### Returning APOE and pTau-217 Results: the eSMARTER Randomized Noninferiority Clinical Trial
**Langbaum, J. B. & Bradbury, A. R. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** In a randomized noninferiority trial (651 participants, 2:1 digital vs. clinician-mediated), a patient-centered digital platform for disclosing APOE genotype and plasma pTau-217 results was noninferior to clinician-mediated telehealth disclosure on AD-related knowledge, anxiety, and disease-specific distress at 1–7 days post-disclosure; no significant between-group differences emerged after pTau-217 disclosure either. As biomarker and genetic risk testing for AD moves into routine and direct-to-consumer contexts, this suggests scalable digital disclosure models can substitute for clinician-mediated sessions without measurable psychological harm — an operationally important finding for clinics anticipating disclosure volume they cannot staff conventionally.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human randomized controlled trial (decentralized, US-based, n=651 randomized); noninferiority design comparing digital vs. clinician-mediated genetic/biomarker disclosure.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.27.26361535

### Evaluating Cognitive Impact of Traumatic Brain Injury and Risk for Post-Traumatic Epilepsy
**Zink, T. & Sun, H. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Longitudinal cognitive testing (15 measures) in TBI patients followed to 18 months post-injury, compared against post-traumatic epilepsy (PTE) patients and healthy controls, finds that processing-speed/working-memory and executive-function measures largely recover over time in uncomplicated TBI, while verbal memory (HVLT-R delayed recall) remains chronically impaired. TBI patients who fail to show the expected recovery trajectory in typically-recovering domains more closely resemble the PTE cognitive profile, suggesting stalled cognitive recovery as a candidate marker of elevated PTE risk. Small, single-cohort sample (TBI n=32 at baseline, PTE n=20) and retrospective PTE ascertainment limit generalizability and causal interpretation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human prospective/retrospective cohort (TBI, PTE, healthy control); longitudinal neuropsychological battery; linear mixed-effects modeling.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.30.26361760
