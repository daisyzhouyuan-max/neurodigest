1Genome-wide analysis of common structural variants in Alzheimer's disease identifies a risk-associated regulatory CCG repeat in FAM193B

Nat Neurosci  Han Cao & Nancy Y Ip
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Most AD genetic architecture has been read from SNP arrays, leaving the contribution of structural variants largely unmeasured. Using long-read (n = 11) and short-read (n = 3,190) sequencing in a Chinese cohort, the authors show common SVs add to AD heritability beyond common SNPs and pin down an expanded CCG repeat in the FAM193B promoter with a genome-wide significant effect (OR = 2.20 per 10-repeat increase, P = 5.5 × 10⁻¹³). The repeat raises FAM193B expression and mediates the pleiotropic link between brain FAM193B and AD risk; in postmortem brain and cultured neurons, elevated neuronal FAM193B tracks with DNA-damage and cell-death pathways, offering a plausible mechanism rather than a bare association.
Model & method
Human long- and short-read genome sequencing with repeat-expansion association, expression quantitative analysis, and postmortem/neuronal culture validation.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02481-z

2The histone variant H2A.Z regulates sex differences in Alzheimer's disease pathophysiology

Neuron  Luca A Hategan & Iva B Zovkic
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
AD disproportionately affects women, and this work offers a chromatin-level mechanism rather than a correlate. In postmortem human hippocampus and 5xFAD mice, H2A.Z occupancy falls in female but rises in male AD; in females the protein redistributes away from transcription start sites despite higher total H2A.Z. Critically, the manipulation is bidirectional and stage-dependent: H2A.Z.1 depletion improved memory and pathology in females at early and late stages, yet impaired early-stage memory in males before helping them late. The finding reframes a chromatin factor as a sex- and stage-specific lever on AD, with a clear caution that any therapeutic targeting would need to be sex-aware.
Model & method
Postmortem human hippocampus and 5xFAD mice; H2A.Z chromatin occupancy profiling plus genetic depletion with behavioral and pathological readouts.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.019

3Striatal astrocyte μ-crystallin (Crym) loss contributes to early-stage Huntington's disease pathophysiology

Neuron  Matthias Ollivier & Baljit S Khakh
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
The study moves a striatal astrocyte subpopulation from a behavioral curiosity to an HD disease modifier. μ-crystallin (Crym) protein and transcript are reduced in human HD tissue, CSF, and mouse models, and selective astrocytic μ-crystallin restoration in an HD mouse improved astrocyte and neuronal morphology, electrophysiology, gene expression, and early compulsive/repetitive behaviors. The rescue is honestly bounded — late-stage motor deficits were largely unchanged — but ~32% of the rescued astrocytic genes overlap those dysregulated in human HD, indicating conserved molecular correction and a candidate early-stage target.
Model & method
Human HD tissue/CSF and HD mouse models; astrocyte-targeted μ-crystallin restoration with molecular, electrophysiological, and behavioral assessment.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.027

4Astrocyte SMN restoration and forskolin treatment improve SMA synaptic pathology

Ann Neurol  Emily Welby & Allison D Ebert
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Afferent synapse dysfunction is an early feature of spinal muscular atrophy, but the cellular basis has been unclear. Using patient and control iPSC-derived astrocytes in mono- and co-culture, the authors localize an intrinsic defect to astrocyte processes: SMA astrocytes show reduced actin-associated cell-surface N-glycoproteins, lower filopodia density and CDC42-GTP after stimulation, and decreased phosphorylated ERM proteins, with concurrent synapse loss at the peri-synaptic astrocyte–synapse interface. The work implicates the astrocyte, not only the motor neuron, in SMA synaptic pathology and nominates a forskolin/cAMP-directed and SMN-restoration strategy. Evidence is in human iPSC culture, so circuit-level and in vivo relevance remain to be shown.
Model & method
Patient and control iPSC-derived astrocyte monocultures and motor-neuron co-cultures; cell-surface proteomics, super-resolution imaging, and electrophysiology.
https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78390

5A water-pinning hot spot drives templated tau aggregation

Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A  Chung-Ta Han & Songi Han
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Templated, prion-like propagation of tau fibrils underlies spread in tauopathies, yet what makes naïve tau add in-register to a growing fibril is poorly defined. Tracking jR2R3-P301L tau by rapid ¹H-¹⁵N SOFAST-HMQC NMR, the authors provide evidence for a localized "water-pinning" hot spot — structured solvation water near the P301L site — that nucleates the initial monomer–fibril contact and enforces in-register stacking. This offers a physicochemical, rather than purely sequence-based, determinant of seeding competence and a conceptually new handle on blocking tau templating.
Model & method
In vitro recombinant jR2R3-P301L tau; solution NMR (SOFAST-HMQC) of hydration and monomer–fibril contacts.
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2611158123

Brain DNA methylation, APOE genotype and Alzheimer's disease neuropathology in a large postmortem cohort

Nat Aging  Guanhong Miao & Jinying Zhao
Key findings
EPIC v2 methylation profiling of 1,250 postmortem prefrontal cortex samples (Rush cohorts, ~930,000 CpGs) identified 107 differentially methylated probes associated with AD neuropathology, 38 replicated in an independent cohort (n = 139). DNA methylation partially mediated the APOE effect on AD diagnosis (proportion mediated 8.6%, P = 2 × 10⁻⁶), and 39 implicated genes (e.g., RIN3, LGMN, LHPP) were differentially expressed across neurons and astrocytes. The mediation is statistical and cross-sectional, but the scale and replication strengthen DNA methylation as a conduit for APOE-related risk.
Model & method
Human postmortem prefrontal cortex; epigenome-wide association across multiple neuropathologic measures with cross-cohort replication and mediation analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-026-01243-7

Investigating molecular and hemodynamic effects of LSD in the human brain

Nat Neurosci  Drummond E-Wen McCulloch & Gitte Moos Knudsen
Key findings
Simultaneous PET-MRI in seven healthy volunteers established, for the first time in vivo, the dose–occupancy relationship of LSD: high-affinity 5-HT2AR occupancy accompanied by increased global cerebral blood flow and internal carotid artery flow and decreased global connectivity, with the connectivity drop negatively related to CBF. An anticlockwise hysteresis between plasma drug levels and subjective effects points to atypical pharmacodynamics, and a separate psilocybin cohort (n = 25) highlighted shared and divergent CBF/connectivity effects. Sample size is small, as expected for a controlled PET-MR occupancy study.
Model & method
Healthy human volunteers; simultaneous PET-MRI target-engagement imaging with pharmacokinetic–pharmacodynamic modeling.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02462-2

Mapping high-resolution, multidimensional phase diagrams of near-physiological protein condensates

Nat Commun  Tanushree Agarwal & Jonathon Nixon-Abell
Key findings
A droplet-microfluidics platform (ExVivo PhaseScan) couples mammalian lysate-based reconstitution with scalable analysis to build high-resolution phase diagrams of compositionally complex condensates, applied to stress granules and nucleoli. A machine-learning morphology pipeline revealed how ALS-linked FUS mutations remodel condensate properties, capturing liquid-to-solid transitions of mutant FUS within both stress granules and nucleoli. The approach bridges the gap between oversimplified recombinant systems and intractable live cells, giving a controllable readout of the aberrant phase behavior central to ALS proteostasis.
Model & method
Mammalian cell lysate reconstitution in droplet microfluidics; phase-diagram mapping with ML-based condensate morphology analysis of wild-type and mutant FUS.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77696-6

Endothelial cell–microglia crosstalk mediates heart failure-associated synaptic loss and cognitive impairment in male mice

Nat Commun  Mengdan Wang & Shuo Zhang
Key findings
In a pressure-overload (transverse aortic constriction) plus plasma-transfer model in 8-month-old male mice, circulating factors from heart failure induced senescence of brain endothelial cells, which drove synaptic loss and cognitive decline via endothelial–microglial crosstalk. Elevated circulating TGFβ2 acted on endothelial TGFBR2 to induce SPARC through a non-canonical MEK/ERK-MYC pathway; endothelial SPARC then engaged microglial TLR4 to promote synapse engulfment. Endothelial Tgfbr2 or Sparc deletion, microglial Tlr4 deletion, and pharmacological TLR4 inhibition each preserved synapses and cognition. Causal evidence is strong but limited to male mice.
Model & method
Male mouse transverse aortic constriction with plasma transfer; cell-type-specific genetic deletion and pharmacological TLR4 inhibition with synaptic and behavioral readouts.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76687-x

Shedding light on left hippocampal mGlu5 in Alzheimer's disease

Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A  Charleine Zussy & Laurent Givalois
Key findings
Wild-type mice show a physiological left > right asymmetry in hippocampal mGlu5 expression that is lost in two AD models (J20 and 5xFAD). Using the light-controllable negative allosteric modulator Alloswitch-1, selective inhibition of left — but not right — hippocampal mGlu5 restored working and short-term memory in AD mice, arguing that altered mGlu5 reflects a dysregulated, pharmacologically tractable signaling state rather than simple receptor loss. The work introduces hemispheric lateralization as a design consideration for mGlu5-directed AD therapeutics; evidence is mouse-only.
Model & method
J20 and 5xFAD mouse models; optopharmacological (Alloswitch-1) lateralized mGlu5 inhibition with memory testing.
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2535413123

Consistency of serial CSF α-synuclein seed amplification assay results in the Parkinson's Precision Medicine Initiative

Mov Disord  David G Coughlin & Parkinson's Precision Medicine Initiative
Key findings
Most α-synuclein seed amplification assay (aSyn-SAA) reports are cross-sectional; this analysis of 1,238 PPMI participants (PD = 633, prodromal = 563, HC = 42) with repeat CSF sampling over a median 2.0 years establishes strong intraindividual stability. Among aSyn-SAA+ PD, 96% stayed positive and 92% of negatives stayed negative; aSyn-SAA+ prodromal participants remained positive in 99% of cases. The high test–retest consistency supports aSyn-SAA as a stable biological marker for trial enrichment and staging, with conversion from negative to positive being uncommon over this interval.
Model & method
Human longitudinal CSF cohort (PPMI); serial Amprion aSyn-SAA with consistency and conversion-rate analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70509

Plasma lipid remodeling across preclinical burden, disease onset, and early motor severity in Huntington's disease

Mov Disord  Mohamed Bendimerad & Juan Manuel Chao de la Barca
Key findings
Targeted metabolomics/lipidomics in 93 HTT mutation carriers (54 premanifest, 39 early manifest) linked systemic lipid changes to disease stage. Rising CAG-Age Product scores were accompanied by coordinated enrichment of EPA (20:5) and DHA (22:6) phospholipids with triglyceride depletion, whereas clinical onset was marked by triglyceride enrichment and palmitate-containing lipids. The signatures track preclinical burden and motor severity, nominating plasma lipid remodeling as an accessible readout of HD progression; the cohort is modest and associations are correlative.
Model & method
Human premanifest and early-manifest HD plasma; targeted metabolomics/lipidomics with lipid-class and acyl-chain enrichment analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70531

Astrocyte-specific dsRNA–ADAR1 signalling links innate immunity to complement activation in Parkinson disease

bioRxiv  Liu, Y. & Fu, Y.
Key findings
Combining immunofluorescence of postmortem PD cingulate cortex with single-nucleus RNA-seq from three human datasets, the authors report a higher proportion of dsRNA-positive astrocytes (but not neurons) in PD, with ADAR1 p150 induction in astrocytes correlating with astrocytic α-synuclein burden. Network analysis found complement gene expression selectively coupled to dsRNA-sensing pathways in astrocytes, whereas interferon/NF-κB programs were shared across cell types, suggesting a constitutive astrocytic dsRNA→complement circuit engaged by disease-associated dsRNA. Findings are observational and association-based; the proposed model is explicitly testable rather than demonstrated causally.
Model & method
Human postmortem PD cortex immunofluorescence integrated with single-nucleus RNA-seq and modular gene-scoring/network analysis.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.06.757209

Oligodendrocyte stress response as a driver of vascular dementia

bioRxiv  Merour, E. & Popko, B.
Key findings
A chronic cardiometabolic model (high-fat diet plus hypertension) produced progressive oligodendroglial loss, myelin thinning, and VaD-like cognitive, anxiety, and memory deficits in mice. The integrated stress response (ISR) emerged as a cell-autonomous driver: pharmacological ISR inhibition restored myelination, and oligodendrocyte-specific deletion of the ISR kinase PERK fully protected against pathology and behavioral deficits. ISR activation was also detected in oligodendrocytes from human cerebrovascular disease, positioning oligodendroglial ISR as a candidate therapeutic target. Causal data are mouse-based with supporting human histology.
Model & method
High-fat-diet/hypertension mouse model; pharmacological ISR inhibition and oligodendrocyte-specific PERK deletion, with human postmortem validation.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.12.758545

Progranulin deficiency promotes neuroinflammation through cathepsin B-mediated activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome

bioRxiv  Aggarwal, G. & Nguyen, A. D.
Key findings
Across Grn-deficient mice, human microglial cells, and patient fibroblasts, progranulin loss increased inflammasome components (procaspase-1, NLRP3, cleaved IL-1β) and promoted NLRP3 oligomerization. Pharmacological inhibition or siRNA knockdown of cathepsin B reduced inflammasome assembly and cytokine output, implicating a lysosomal cathepsin B–NLRP3 axis downstream of progranulin haploinsufficiency. A progranulin-raising antisense oligonucleotide corrected the inflammatory phenotype in heterozygous GRN fibroblasts, offering a mechanistic link between lysosomal dysfunction and neuroinflammation in FTD-GRN and a therapeutic rationale. Evidence spans mouse and human cells in vitro.
Model & method
Grn-deficient mice, GRN-knockout human microglia, and patient fibroblasts; inflammasome biochemistry with cathepsin B inhibition/knockdown and ASO rescue.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.11.758389

A rapid, genetically tractable human iPSC-derived model for studying tissue-resident macrophage biology in Alzheimer's and other diseases

bioRxiv  Lee, H. & Goate, A. M.
Key findings
A simplified 19-day protocol generates high-purity (>90% CD45+CD11b+) iPSC-derived macrophage-like cells (iMacs) from cryopreservable EMP-like intermediates using M-CSF alone. iMacs upregulate phagocytic/lysosomal programs and scavenger and AD-risk genes (CD36, CD163, MRC1, TREM2), show enhanced uptake of amyloid-β and other substrates versus iPSC-derived microglia, and display stronger TREM2-driven SYK/PLCγ2 signaling. They tolerate lipid-mediated siRNA knockdown, enabling rapid perturbation of AD risk genes — a scalable, genetically tractable platform for efferocytosis and TREM2 biology relevant to AD and aging. As a methods preprint, physiological fidelity to brain-resident macrophages remains to be benchmarked.
Model & method
Human iPSC-derived macrophage differentiation; transcriptomic profiling, phagocytosis/TREM2 signaling assays, and siRNA genetic perturbation.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.08.757653

5-HT1A antagonist WAY-100635 prevents chronic neurodegeneration and immune activation after TBI

bioRxiv  Das, A. & Gao, X.
Key findings
In a mouse controlled-cortical-impact model targeting visual cortex, daily post-injury WAY-100635 (5 mg/kg, starting 1 h after injury for 6 weeks) preserved retinal ganglion cell survival and function, improved visual acuity and contrast sensitivity, and maintained visually evoked cortical responses. Diffusion MRI showed selective normalization of optic-tract microstructure, and perilesional cortex retained neurons and synaptophysin-positive synapses with markedly reduced microglial/astroglial activation. Cavity volume was unchanged, consistent with preferential protection of circuitry connected to the lesion. The study builds on a prior stem-cell RGC screen linking 5-HT1A antagonism to cAMP-dependent mitochondrial biogenesis; group sizes are small (5–8 per injury group) and male-only.
Model & method
Male mouse controlled cortical impact; post-injury 5-HT1A antagonism with electrophysiology, diffusion MRI, and histopathology.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.11.758371
