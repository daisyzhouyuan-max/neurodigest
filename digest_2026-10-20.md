### Imaging nanoscopic co-assemblies of α-synuclein and Tau in human samples
**Yu P Zhang & David Klenerman | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** αS–Tau mixed aggregates are often invoked to explain co-pathology, but their prevalence and biophysical character have not been measured directly in human tissue. Adapting single-molecule pull-down (SiMPull), the authors find αS–Tau co-assemblies make up roughly 10–70% of total αS aggregates across post-mortem brain, soluble brain fractions, serum, CSF and saliva — and, notably, are present at comparable levels in Parkinson's disease and controls. Co-assemblies from control soluble fractions were more proteinase-K resistant than those from PD, and although Tau accelerated αS aggregation in simple in vitro systems, that effect vanished in complex matrices. The data argue against a single uniformly toxic αS–Tau species and reframe how mixed aggregates should be interpreted in fluid biomarkers.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem brain, CSF, serum and saliva (PD and control); single-molecule pull-down with two-colour single-molecule imaging, plus in vitro aggregation assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77728-1

### An acetylcholine-inhibited sleep-active hippocampal interneuron orchestrates memory consolidation
**Yili Feng & Min Xu | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** Sharp-wave ripples drive sleep-dependent memory consolidation, but the inhibitory machinery that biases ripples toward sleep has been unclear. The authors identify a sparse population of Chrm2 (M2 acetylcholine receptor)-expressing GABAergic interneurons in mouse hippocampus that — unlike other labelled interneuron classes active in waking and REM — fire preferentially in NREM sleep and quiet wakefulness, when cholinergic tone is low. These cells impose a widespread, infra-slow (~0.015 Hz) inhibitory rhythm; silencing them in dorsal CA3 or ventral hippocampus selectively impaired spatial or social memory, and their activity bidirectionally gated ripple occurrence. The work supplies a genetically defined handle linking neuromodulatory state to ripple generation and consolidation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse hippocampus; genetically targeted recording and bidirectional manipulation of Chrm2+ interneurons with ripple and memory assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.031

### Spatiotemporal remodeling in habenula marks the onset of depression-related phenotypes
**Patricia Molina & Manuel Mameli | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** The lateral habenula (LHb) is implicated in stress-related behavioural change, but the timing of its contribution has been obscure. Combining a spatially resolved transcriptomic atlas of the LHb with longitudinal two-photon calcium imaging of the same neurons, the authors show that chronic stress drives topographically organized, temporally staged transcriptional remodeling alongside progressively heightened LHb excitatory responses to aversive stimuli. These neuronal and molecular changes tracked the emergence of depression-related behaviour and were reversed by ketamine; chemogenetically limiting LHb activity, or giving ketamine, at the onset of the changes produced antidepressant effects. The study ties a defined spatiotemporal LHb signature to the transition into a depression-related state, though the causal manipulations and atlas are from male mice only.

**Model/system & primary method:** Male mice, chronic stress; spatial transcriptomics, longitudinal in vivo two-photon calcium imaging, chemogenetics and ketamine.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02488-6

### Cortex-wide imaging in naturally behaving mice with 1-gram wireless mesoscopes
**Yuanlong Zhang & Qionghai Dai | Nature**

**Why it matters:** Miniaturized microscopes have been limited by narrow fields of view and the tether required for data transfer. Airscope is a 1.0-gram wireless mesoscope giving 4 µm resolution across a 6 mm field at 12.5 Hz; an aspheric-lens design shortens total track length 2.84-fold versus prior miniscopes while matching benchtop one-photon neuron yield, and a wireless link supports cable-free calcium imaging from >8,500 neurons per animal (a ~25-fold increase). The authors use it for multi-region dorsal-cortical recording during naturalistic, multi-animal behaviour — decoding competition outcomes between paired animals and recording brain–spinal interactions during fear conditioning. It makes untethered, cellular-resolution, cortex-wide recording in freely behaving mice practical.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mice; wireless one-photon mesoscope calcium imaging during free behaviour, paired with automated behavioural analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11293-x

### An amygdala-mediated simulation-to-decision cascade aligns interbrain dynamics to solve social dilemmas
**Jun Ni & Yina Ma | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Using simultaneous intracranial EEG from pairs of epilepsy patients playing a real-time iterated prisoner's dilemma, the authors show that amygdala interbrain representational alignment (similarity of fine-grained spectral patterns across partners) emerges selectively during mutual cooperation and predicts trial-by-trial outcomes. Early θ-suppression alignment reflected rapid history-sensitive coordination; later low-frequency suppression with γ increases supported committed action. Cooperation relied on an amygdala simulation-to-decision cascade — a model-based estimate of the partner's choice (tracked by low-frequency suppression) linked to self-decision via θ–γ coupling. Conditional cooperators relied on partner tracking, unconditional cooperators on dispositional uncertainty tolerance.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human epilepsy-patient dyads; simultaneous intracranial EEG during an iterated prisoner's dilemma with computational modelling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02473-z

### Distinct patterns of whole-body coding in human motor cortex and posterior parietal cortex
**Kelly Kadlec & Richard A Andersen | Neuron**

**Key findings:** Recording single neurons from hand areas of motor cortex (MC) and posterior parietal cortex (PPC) in tetraplegic humans attempting movements across the body, the authors found a population response to every tested movement in both regions, but with distinct organization. The MC hand knob responded more selectively to hand movements, with single neurons tuned to one effector or to two effectors of the same limb; PPC neurons showed mixed selectivity, representing combinations of effectors and all effectors strongly. The results suggest a gradient from effector-general representation in PPC to regional effector specificity in MC, bearing directly on how effectors should be decoded in brain–machine interfaces.

**Model/system & primary method:** Tetraplegic human participants; chronic single-neuron recordings in MC and PPC during attempted whole-body movements.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.030

### Cortical Cholinergic Pathway Degeneration Reflects Cognitive Impairment and Dementia in Parkinson's Disease
**Nicola M Slater & John C Dalrymple-Alford | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** In 108 people with Parkinson's disease and 41 controls, the authors used anatomically constrained tractography (HARDI) to extract the medial and two lateral cortical cholinergic projection pathways from the nucleus basalis of Meynert. Lower cognitive performance was associated with higher mean diffusivity across all pathways, most prominently the perisylvian component of the lateral pathway; participants with dementia additionally showed increased free-water fraction and reduced fractional anisotropy and fibre density/cross-section across pathways. The findings link structural degeneration of cortical cholinergic projections to cognitive decline in PD, though the design is cross-sectional and cannot establish whether pathway damage precedes decline.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human PD cohort stratified by cognitive status; diffusion MRI tractography of NBM cholinergic pathways with neuropsychological assessment.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70584

### Automated Magnetic Resonance Volumetry to Measure Multiple System Atrophy Disease Progression in Clinical Trials
**Andrea Quattrone & M‐STAR Study Group | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** Using 1-year longitudinal MRI from the placebo arm of the M-STAR trial (129 MSA patients: 76 cerebellar, 53 parkinsonian) against 184 healthy controls, the authors benchmarked automated regional volumetry as a trial endpoint. Infratentorial structures showed the largest longitudinal volume loss in both subtypes, and an annualized cerebellum–pons–putamen volumetric index was the most powerful progression marker (effect size −1.81), requiring only ~55 patients per arm to detect a 30% treatment effect and reducing required sample size by ~60% relative to clinical scales. The work supports MRI-derived atrophy rates as sensitive, trial-ready endpoints for MSA.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human MSA clinical-trial cohort; automated longitudinal MRI volumetry with effect-size and sample-size modelling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70585

### Microglia and border-associated macrophages play opposite roles in cerebrovascular and leukocyte responses under systemic inflammation
**Ana Rita Brás & Ádám Dénes | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Sustained systemic inflammation altered microglia–vascular interactions, recruited leukocytes to cerebral vessels and blunted hypercapnia-induced vasodilation in mice. Microglia and border-associated macrophages (BAMs) acted in opposition: BAM depletion (clodronate) increased leukocyte recruitment, whereas microglia loss (PLX5622, or CSF1R^ΔFIRE/ΔFIRE mice lacking microglia but retaining BAMs) markedly reduced it; conditional microglial IL-1 deletion likewise inhibited recruitment. Only microglial absence impaired hypercapnia-induced vasodilation, implicating endothelial IL-1R1 and microglial IL-1α, with two-photon imaging showing IL-1α-dependent leukocyte–artery interactions. The study dissects distinct brain-macrophage contributions to neurovascular and neuroimmune responses, in male mice.

**Model/system & primary method:** Male mice, systemic inflammation; genetic and pharmacological macrophage depletion, conditional IL-1 deletion and in vivo two-photon imaging.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77787-4

### Insulin regulates activity-dependent mitochondrial depolarization and calcium dynamics in dentate granule cells
**Beatriz Ferrán & Carlos Manlio Díaz-García | J Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Probing insulin action in dentate granule cells of adult female mice, the authors found that insulin (revealed only when insulin-degrading activity was inhibited) increased Akt phosphorylation via the neuronal insulin receptor without changing resting mitochondrial membrane potential. During neuronal activity, insulin limited activity-evoked mitochondrial depolarization and reduced cytosolic Ca²⁺ accumulation through an insulin-receptor-dependent, Akt-independent mechanism, and accelerated clearance of mitochondrial Ca²⁺ transients — with no change in excitability or cytosolic ATP. The results identify endogenous insulin degradation as a gatekeeper of hippocampal insulin signalling and connect central insulin to mitochondrial and Ca²⁺ homeostasis relevant to brain insulin resistance in aging.

**Model/system & primary method:** Adult female mouse hippocampal slices/dentate granule cells; live imaging of mitochondrial membrane potential and Ca²⁺ with insulin-receptor manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0291-26.2026

### Analysis of dementia strategic plans in US states
**Matthew Nesvet & Kathleen T Unroe | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** This systematic content analysis of US state dementia strategic plans finds five consistently underspecified policy areas in recent plans and marked inconsistency in how states monitor whether plans are implemented or effective. The work maps the current policy landscape for dementia care and risk reduction and highlights gaps between stated recommendations and mechanisms for accountability; it is a descriptive policy analysis rather than a study of clinical or biological outcomes.

**Model/system & primary method:** US state dementia strategic plans; structured document content analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71814

### A systems approach uncovers drivers of the multifocal emergence of an astrocyte state contributing to cognitive decline in Alzheimer's disease
**Comandante-Lou, N. & De Jager, P. L. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** From a unified single-nucleus atlas of 867 brains, the authors prioritize a non-canonical reactive astrocyte state (Ast10; SLC38A2^high SMTN^high CACNA1D^high) as a link between neuropathology and cognitive decline. Ligand–receptor modelling nominated PLXNB1, JAM2 and SEMA4D as drivers, validated in mouse and human astrocytes and across two spatial modalities, with Ast10 distributed multifocally through the neocortex. In CSF from a SEMA4D-blocking antibody trial, Ast10 markers were pharmacologically reduced and their reduction tracked cognitive stabilization, suggesting a modifiable target. Cautious interpretation is warranted: the trial-linked evidence is correlational marker tracking, not a powered clinical outcome. This is a revision of a preprint first posted February 2025.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human single-nucleus/spatial transcriptomic meta-analysis (867 brains) with mouse and human astrocyte validation and trial CSF marker analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.02.24.639868

### Redox surveillance of stress granules prevents TDP-43 aggregation
**Yan, X. & Hyman, A. A. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The authors report that stress granules are redox-active compartments: they contain elevated hydrogen peroxide in living cells, and minimal G3BP1–RNA condensates generate hydrogen peroxide and reactive radicals in vitro without enzymatic oxidases. A locally recruited peroxiredoxin–thioredoxin system controls this environment; disrupting redox surveillance increased local oxidation and drove TDP-43 demixing and aggregation, whereas targeting peroxiredoxins to stress granules suppressed it. The work proposes local redox control as a determinant of protein state within stress granules, with implications for TDP-43 proteinopathies, though the evidence is largely cell-based and in vitro.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cell lines and reconstituted G3BP1–RNA condensates; redox biosensors, in vitro condensate assays and TDP-43 aggregation readouts.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.16.759411

### BNIP3L/NIX-mediated mitophagy limits Alzheimer disease pathology and preserves neuronal function
**Li, Y. & Rasmussen, L. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** The authors identify BNIP3L/NIX-dependent mitophagy as a neuronal quality-control pathway constraining AD-associated proteotoxicity. Loss of BNIP3L/NIX (or its C. elegans orthologue DCT-1) impaired basal and stress-induced mitophagy, disrupted mitochondrial homeostasis, worsened Tau and amyloid-β pathology and compromised synaptic and behavioural function across human neuronal and in vivo models; restoring NIX/DCT-1 activity improved function under proteotoxic stress, lowered Tau phosphorylation in human neurons and suppressed seeded Tau aggregation in primary mouse neurons. The convergent loss- and gain-of-function data across species make a reasonably strong mechanistic case, pending peer review and in vivo mammalian validation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived neurons, C. elegans and mouse neurons; genetic manipulation of BNIP3L/NIX/DCT-1 with mitophagy, Tau/Aβ and functional assays.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.18.759942

### Female microglia show heightened responses to aging and amyloid pathology in mice and humans
**Cox, J. E. J. & Ocanas, S. R. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Examining cell-type-specific responses to aging and amyloid-β in mouse brain (single-cell transcriptomics, proteomics, flow cytometry, microscopy) and comparing to human microglia from ROSMAP, the authors find microglia are a primary source of sex differences: females showed greater disease-associated microglia proportions and reactivity, with enhanced neuroinflammatory and interferon signalling. Sex-biased transcriptional responses were substantially conserved between mouse and human, including shared reactive-microglial states and regulatory networks, with amyloid precursor protein (APP) a central network node. The cross-species concordance strengthens relevance to human AD, though causal tests of the sex-biased programs are not yet established.

**Model/system & primary method:** Aged and amyloid-model mice plus human ROSMAP microglia; single-cell/single-nucleus transcriptomics, proteomics and flow cytometry.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.13.758897

### Plasma neurofilament light links mortality to neuroaxonal injury beyond clinical decline and Alzheimer disease plasma biomarkers
**Lorenz, L. M. C. & Holstege, H. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Following 559 individuals aged 92–107 across three oldest-old cohorts for up to 16 years, the authors tested which plasma biomarkers predict mortality. NfL was the only biomarker consistently associated with mortality (HR 1.21–1.38), whereas AD-specific markers (Aβ42/40, pTau-181) did not, despite reflecting post-mortem AD neuropathology. NfL remained predictive independently of cognition and activities of daily living, which mediated only modest fractions. The authors conclude that at extreme age, mortality is more closely tied to neuroaxonal injury than to upstream AD pathology — an observational association in a select oldest-old population.

**Model/system & primary method:** Three oldest-old human cohorts (Dutch centenarians, two Danish population cohorts); plasma biomarker panels with longitudinal mortality analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.17.26365798
