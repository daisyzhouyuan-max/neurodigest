### Gliomas phenocopy an inborn error of metabolism to drive neuronal activity and tumor growth
**Kalil G Abdullah & Samuel K McBrayer | Cell**

**Why it matters:** Human high-grade glioma tissue accumulates the creatine-pathway intermediate guanidinoacetate (GAA) ~100-fold over control brain, mimicking the metabolic state of GAMT deficiency, an inborn error of metabolism. Rather than converting GAA to creatine, glioma cells secrete it, and GAA excites surrounding neurons by activating GABA-A receptors that are depolarizing in tumor-associated neurons with dysregulated chloride homeostasis. Depleting tumoral GAA reduced electrochemical activity, neuron-glioma interactions and tumor aggressiveness, nominating GAA synthesis as a metabolic target that acts through the neuronal microenvironment.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human HGG metabolomics; mouse glioma models; electrophysiology of glioma-associated neurons; genetic and pharmacologic GAA depletion.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.037

### Quality control of glycogen through direct ubiquitylation by RNF213
**Matthew C J Yip & Felix Randow | Nature**

**Why it matters:** The study defines a previously unrecognized quality-control pathway that protects the brain from insoluble polyglucosan bodies, the aberrant glycogen that drives disorders such as Lafora and adult polyglucosan body disease. The E3 ligase RNF213 selectively ubiquitylates poorly branched glycogen, and its CBM20 carbohydrate-binding domain restrains activity toward normal glycogen; disrupting that binding causes gain-of-function ubiquitylation of physiological glycogen. Ubiquitylated polyglucosan recruits SQSTM1, TAX1BP1 and optineurin for autophagic clearance, and mice lacking RNF213 ligase activity accumulate polyglucosan in cerebellum, pons and hippocampus. Cryo-EM of RNF213 bound to maltoheptaose provides the structural basis, establishing an essential role for non-protein ubiquitylation in astrocytes.

**Model/system & primary method:** RNF213 ligase-dead knock-in mice; engineered polyglucosan-producing cells; cryo-EM; epistasis with LUBAC.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11139-6

### Microglia deploy TAM receptors to kill motor neurons in a mouse model of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis
**Youtong Huang & Greg Lemke | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** The work provides causal genetic evidence that microglia hasten motor neuron death in ALS by phagocytosing still-living neurons. In SOD1-G93A mice, motor neuron surfaces display the "eat-me" signal phosphatidylserine, a ligand for the microglial TAM receptors Axl and Mer, and microglial lysosomes fill with cholinergic neuronal remains. Germline or microglia-restricted deletion of Axl and Mertk extended lifespan, preserved cholinergic motor neurons and protected neuromuscular synapses, with reduced neuronal debris in the spinal cord. This reframes microglial phagocytosis as an active driver of degeneration and identifies TAM receptors as a therapeutic target, though evidence is confined to a single mouse model.

**Model/system & primary method:** SOD1-G93A mice; germline and microglia-conditional Axl/Mertk knockouts; survival, motor neuron and neuromuscular junction quantification.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76728-5

### The longevity gene APOE2 enhances pericyte function and reduces lipid droplets
**Rais Reskiawan A Kadir & Lisa M Ellerby | Brain**

**Why it matters:** APOE2 lowers Alzheimer's risk and associates with longevity, but its cerebrovascular action was unclear. Humanized APOE2 knock-in mice showed higher pericyte markers and better blood-brain barrier integrity than APOE3 or APOE4 mice, and isogenic APOE2 iPSC-derived pericytes provided greater barrier integrity in a reconstituted human BBB, resisted senescence and accumulated less amyloid-beta. Proteomic and lipidomic profiling tied these effects to more efficient lipid processing (increased degradation, beta-oxidation and transport) and fewer lipid droplets, and recombinant APOE2 rescued pericyte function in APOE3 and APOE4 cells. The mechanistic link is correlative-to-causal in cells but relies on in vitro models for the human evidence.

**Model/system & primary method:** Humanized APOE2/E3/E4 knock-in mice; CRISPR isogenic iPSC-derived pericytes; human in vitro BBB model; untargeted proteomics and lipidomics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag311

### Intracranial recordings in humans reveal differential contributions of medial and lateral orbitofrontal cortex to approach-avoidance decision-making
**Clara Kwon Starkweather & Robert T Knight | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Stereo-EEG recordings from the human orbitofrontal cortex during a gamified approach-avoidance task revealed two anatomically distinct signals preceding approach: increased activity in the medial OFC and decreased activity in the lateral OFC. Before decisions, these signals rapidly alternated between discrete pro-approach and pro-avoidant states, suggesting a functional architecture in which medial and lateral OFC compartments compete in real time to arbitrate reward-versus-punishment conflict.

**Model/system & primary method:** Humans with OFC stereotactic EEG electrodes; task-based intracranial electrophysiology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02444-4

### Visualizing synaptic disruptions in the release and regulation of dopamine hotspots in Huntington's Disease
**Sarah J Yang & Markita P Landry | J Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Using near-infrared catecholamine nanosensors (nIRCats) to image striatal dopamine release at bouton-scale resolution in R6/2 Huntington's disease mice, the authors found that dorsal striatal dopamine release declines with disease progression, driven chiefly by a loss in the number of dopamine release sites ("hotspots") together with reduced release fidelity. The D2-receptor antagonist sulpiride increased hotspot fidelity in wild-type but not late-disease HD striatum, indicating that D2-mediated regulation of dopamine release becomes compromised late in disease. The work is limited to ex vivo brain slices in one HD model.

**Model/system & primary method:** R6/2 HD mouse striatal slices; nIRCat near-infrared dopamine imaging; pharmacology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1862-24.2026

### Anti-CD40L prevents meningeal B cell aggregates and mitigates progressive autoimmune encephalomyelitis
**Sofia Tyystjärvi & Thomas Korn | Brain**

**Key findings:** In the opticospinal encephalomyelitis (OSE) mouse model, which spontaneously forms meningeal tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) resembling those in progressive multiple sclerosis, single-cell RNA-seq showed the CD40L-CD40 pathway dominating T-B interactions in the meningeal niche. Systemic anti-CD40L antibody cleared meningeal TLS and improved the progressive disease phase, whereas anti-CD20 B cell depletion did not. Effects persisted after treatment withdrawal without rebound, and the intervention broadly remodeled the CNS antigen-presenting compartment, including myeloid cells and astrocyte subsets, offering a mechanism to target compartmentalized inflammation that drives progression independently of relapses.

**Model/system & primary method:** OSE (MOG TCR × BCR transgenic) mice; meningeal scRNA-seq; anti-CD40L vs anti-CD20 treatment.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag274

### Genetic and molecular evidence linking CTSH to Alzheimer's disease pathophysiology
**Cynthia Picard & PREVENT-AD research group | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Integrating genomic, transcriptomic and proteomic data from CSF and brain across three cohorts, the study shows that the CTSH coding variant rs2289702 acts as a cis-regulatory variant altering cathepsin H mRNA and protein levels, with the T allele associating with better cognition and lower amyloid burden. CSF CTSH tracked total tau, p-tau181, neuronal markers and multiple glial and complement-related inflammatory proteins, positioning this lysosomal protease as a genetically regulated contributor to early neurodegenerative, synaptic and inflammatory change. Evidence is associative and human-cohort based.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human CSF and post mortem brain multi-omics across three cohorts; genetic association and co-expression analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71833

### MARBLE: An in vivo MRI marker of limbic-predominant age-related TDP-43 encephalopathy neuropathologic change (LATE-NC)
**Mahir Tazwar & Konstantinos Arfanakis | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** LATE-NC is common in older adults but diagnosable only at autopsy. Training machine-learning models on brain MRI and neuropathology from 918 community-based older adults, the authors built MARBLE, a fully automated in vivo marker that predicted LATE-NC with an AUC of 0.92 in an independent group scanned within three years of death. Higher MARBLE scores tracked higher LATE-NC stage and a lower level and faster decline of cognition, most strongly in episodic and semantic memory and perceptual speed, offering a scalable imaging surrogate for trial enrichment.

**Model/system & primary method:** Community-based older adults (n=918); machine learning on MRI with autopsy-confirmed neuropathology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71832

### Efficacy and Safety of DL-3-n-Butylphthalide in Spinocerebellar Ataxia Type 3
**Xiaokai Shen & Hong Jiang | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** In a 12-month randomized, placebo-controlled trial of 116 patients with spinocerebellar ataxia type 3, oral DL-3-n-butylphthalide (NBP, 200 mg three times daily) produced modest but statistically significant benefit over placebo on both primary endpoints (SARA difference -0.86, 95% CI -1.56 to -0.16; SCAFI difference 0.16, 95% CI 0.01-0.31). Adverse-event rates did not differ significantly and no serious events occurred, though drug-related AEs trended higher with NBP. The effect sizes are small and the study is a single-trial first-in-disease signal, but it is preliminary human evidence of clinical benefit in an otherwise untreatable neurodegenerative ataxia.

**Model/system & primary method:** Randomized 1:1 placebo-controlled human trial (mITT n=116); 12-month SARA and SCAFI outcomes.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70499

### SNCA triplication shapes neuronal extracellular vesicle biology and promotes microglial activation in patient-derived iPSC-based models of Parkinson's disease
**Boumpoureka, I. & Krueger, R. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Comparing PD-patient iPSC-derived midbrain dopaminergic neurons carrying SNCA triplication against gene-corrected and knockout isogenic controls, the authors report that alpha-synuclein overexpression reshapes neuronal extracellular vesicle (nEV) biology: SNCA-4x neurons released more and smaller nEVs enriched in alpha-synuclein, mitochondrial DNA and PARK7/DJ-1 mRNA. These nEVs were taken up more efficiently by isogenic microglia and drove stronger pro-inflammatory activation (raised TNF and IL-1beta, altered morphology), consistent with DAMP-mediated signaling. The findings are in vitro but nominate EV-associated alpha-synuclein, mtDNA and PARK7 as candidate mechanistic and biomarker features.

**Model/system & primary method:** PD-patient iPSC-derived midbrain dopaminergic neurons and microglia (SNCA-4x, gene-corrected, knockout); EV isolation and functional uptake assays.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.09.750343

### mTOR disrupts cerebrovascular RNA regulatory networks in Alzheimer's disease
**Makhlouf, H. & Galvan, V. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Testing how mTOR signaling links to cerebrovascular dysfunction in AD, the authors treated hAPP(J20) mice with rapamycin from 6 to 12 months and found partial restoration of disease-suppressed RNA-regulatory proteins in the cerebrovasculature, including hnRNP D1 (AUF1) and hnRNP E1 (PCBP1). The rescue extended to PS19 tauopathy mice, indicating a shared amyloid- and tau-driven program, and hnRNP E1 and hnRNP L showed inverse relationships with Braak stage in human AD brains. The work suggests a reversible, mTOR-dependent RNA regulatory program in the neurovascular unit, though human data are correlative.

**Model/system & primary method:** hAPP(J20) and PS19 mice with rapamycin; cerebrovascular RNA-binding protein profiling; human AD brain correlation with Braak stage.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.15.750223

### Intergenerational instability of the C9orf72 hexanucleotide repeat
**Rautila, O. S. & Tienari, P. J. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Analyzing 539 unselected parent-offspring pairs plus 152 pairs carrying the risk-tagging SNP, the authors detected intergenerational length changes only in alleles above 20 repeats. Expansions in the 20-40 repeat range were strongly paternally biased (34% in paternal vs 11% in maternal transmissions), most changes were small (1-4 repeats), and one large paternal jump from 27 to 73 repeats was observed. The data support a model in which intermediate C9orf72 alleles can expand across generations, offering a mechanism for apparently sporadic ALS/FTD via parental premutation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human genetics; 539 + 152 parent-offspring pairs; two repeat-primed PCR methods.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.10.750611

### NaV1.7-dependent peripheral sensitization drives chronic pain in Parkinson's disease
**Nelson, T. S. & Khanna, R. J. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In a brain-restricted 6-OHDA model, nigrostriatal degeneration produced persistent hyperexcitability of small dorsal root ganglion neurons with increased sodium current attributable to NaV1.7. Safinamide, one of the few drugs reported to relieve PD pain, directly inhibited a NaV1.7-dependent current and reversed pain-like behavior. Disrupting CRMP2 regulation of NaV1.7 pharmacologically normalized DRG excitability, and genetic disruption of the CRMP2 regulatory sequence prevented pain for up to 30 weeks without altering the motor phenotype. Human PD DRG transcriptomics showed selective changes in excitability genes, identifying CRMP2-NaV1.7 as a target for Parkinsonian pain, with causal evidence from the mouse model.

**Model/system & primary method:** Brain-restricted 6-OHDA mice; DRG electrophysiology; pharmacologic and genetic CRMP2/NaV1.7 disruption; human PD DRG transcriptomics.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.09.750469

### Regional choroid plexus calcifications and their associations with aging, brain structure, and disease
**Ba Gari, I. & Jahanshad, N. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Using quantitative susceptibility mapping in 30,012 UK Biobank participants, the authors quantified choroid plexus calcification (CPcal) across the lateral, third and fourth ventricles. CPcal rose with age, was greater in males, and associated with endocrine/metabolic, psychiatric, nervous-system and circulatory disorders, most consistently in the third ventricle and independent of ventricular volume, with the strongest links for diabetes and tobacco use. Maternal family history of ADRD tracked with greater lateral-ventricle CPcal, and CPcal correlated with cortical and subcortical volumes and white matter microstructure. This positions QSM-derived CPcal as a scalable, radiation-free imaging phenotype of brain aging and neurodegenerative risk; associations are cross-sectional.

**Model/system & primary method:** UK Biobank (n=30,012); QSM-based multi-ventricular calcification quantification; cross-sectional association analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.10.750525
