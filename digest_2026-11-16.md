### The aging human brain exhibits reduced hemodynamic and cerebrospinal fluid flow waves during sleep
**Sydney M Bailes & Laura D Lewis | Nature Aging**

**Why it matters:** Large CSF flow waves during NREM sleep are thought to support brain clearance, but whether this physiology degrades in human aging was unknown. Imaging CSF flow and hemodynamics during nighttime sleep in young (18–39) and older (60–84) adults, the authors found sleep-dependent CSF flow was reduced in older people and tracked lower EEG slow-wave power, diminished global hemodynamic oscillations, and gray-matter atrophy. Sensory and vasoactive stimuli showed both neural and cerebrovascular changes contribute. Because the decline appears in normal aging before disease onset, it identifies a candidate upstream mechanism linking sleep disruption to neurodegenerative risk, though the evidence is cross-sectional and associative.

**Model/system & primary method:** Healthy younger and older adults; simultaneous accelerated fMRI of CSF flow and hemodynamics during overnight sleep, with sensory/vasoactive stimulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-026-01270-4

### Single-nucleus multiomic profiling uncovers epigenetic dysregulation driving hippocampal vulnerability in Alzheimer's disease
**Quyuan Tao & Lei Han | Science Advances**

**Why it matters:** Regional and cell-type selective vulnerability is a defining feature of AD, but its regulatory basis is unclear. Pairing single-nucleus RNA-seq and ATAC-seq in human hippocampus, the authors linked chromatin accessibility to transcription across >1000 disease-associated cis-regulatory interactions. Excitatory cornu ammonis (CA) neurons showed disproportionate regulatory disruption — increased accessibility coupled to activation of apoptosis programs — whereas dentate gyrus neurons were comparatively resistant. Glial regulatory networks centered on SPI1/KLF4 in microglia and STAT3 in astrocytes. Comparison with prefrontal cortex revealed region-specific immune divergence, and a substantial fraction of AD-associated regulatory elements were human-specific, with risk variants enriched in microglial elements.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem hippocampus (with prefrontal cortex comparison and cross-species integration); paired single-nucleus RNA-seq and ATAC-seq with regulatory-network inference.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee4760

### Guam disease features chronic traumatic encephalopathy-like tau seeds in brain and spinal cord
**Nil Saez-Calveras & Ying Lu | Brain**

**Why it matters:** The etiology of Guam ALS/parkinsonism-dementia complex (ALS/PDC), a tau-dominant multiproteinopathy, remains unresolved. Using FRET-based biosensor cells, the authors confirmed potent tau seeding activity in ALS/PDC brain and, notably, extended it to spinal cord. Alanine mutational scanning showed the aggregate-incorporation profile of ALS/PDC tau resembles that of chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE)-type tau, consistent with cryo-EM evidence that ALS/PDC filaments adopt the CTE fold. The finding places a geographically restricted neurodegenerative disease within a defined tau strain family and nominates a shared prion-like seeding mechanism, though it does not itself identify the environmental trigger.

**Model/system & primary method:** Postmortem Guam ALS/PDC brain and spinal cord; tau biosensor-cell seeding assays with alanine-scan strain profiling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag405

### Enhancer-targeted CRISPR-A rescues haploinsufficiency and mutant phenotypes in organoid models of autism
**George T Chen & Daniel H Geschwind | Nature Neuroscience**

**Why it matters:** Roughly 15% of ASD genetic risk comes from de novo haploinsufficient mutations, where a single wild-type allele persists. The authors mapped enhancers of two high-confidence genes, CHD8 and SCN2A, and used CRISPR activation targeting those enhancers (eCRISPR-A) in human iPSC-derived excitatory neurons and cortical forebrain organoids. eCRISPR-A drove a sustained, cell-type-selective increase in CHD8 and SCN2A expression specifically in mutant cells, restoring gene-expression levels and rescuing mutation-associated transcriptomic and physiological phenotypes. By exploiting endogenous regulation and the remaining wild-type allele, the approach offers a dosage-sensitive therapeutic strategy for haploinsufficiency disorders, so far demonstrated only in vitro.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human pluripotent stem cell-derived excitatory neurons and cerebral forebrain organoids; enhancer-targeted CRISPR activation (eCRISPR-A).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02504-9

### Prognostic value of plasma brain-derived phosphorylated Tau
**Valentina Ghisays & Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative | Alzheimers & Dementia**

**Why it matters:** Plasma brain-derived pTau217 (BD-pTau217) may be more AD-specific than total pTau217, but its prognostic value was untested. In cognitively unimpaired ADNI participants, BD-pTau217 and total pTau217 showed similar discrimination for clinical progression (full-follow-up AUC 0.66 each). Among initially amyloid-negative individuals, however, BD-pTau217 better predicted near-term progression to amyloid-PET positivity (tdAUC 0.82 vs 0.69 at 2.5 years; 0.77 vs 0.64 at 4 years). It conferred no advantage for predicting clinical progression. The result positions BD-pTau217 as an early marker of incipient amyloid accumulation rather than of dementia onset — a distinction that matters for trial enrichment.

**Model/system & primary method:** Cognitively unimpaired ADNI cohort (amyloid-PET progression n=175; clinical progression n=508); baseline NULISAseq plasma biomarkers with Cox models and time-dependent AUCs.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71983

### Tau ubiquitination and phosphorylation in relation to Alzheimer's disease in older adults
**Shahram Oveisgharan & David A Bennett | Alzheimers & Dementia**

**Key findings:** Using proteomic quantification of tau and its post-translational modifications in Rush Memory and Aging Project participants, the authors found tau ubiquitination positions were correlated with and related to greater tau-tangle burden, whereas phosphorylation at only some positions was. Statistically, ubiquitination mediated the association between tau phosphorylation and tangles, and the two modifications together mediated 56.5% of the amyloid–tangle association. The data are observational and correlational, but they implicate tau ubiquitination as a candidate node linking soluble, phosphorylated tau to insoluble tangle formation downstream of amyloid.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem cohort (Rush Memory and Aging Project); quantitative proteomics of tau modifications with pathological and cognitive correlation and mediation analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71985

### Tau conveys intrinsic hyperactivity of VTA dopamine neurons but an inability to sustain burst firing
**William M Kennedy & Michael J Beckstead | Alzheimers & Dementia**

**Key findings:** VTA dopamine dysfunction is implicated in neuropsychiatric symptoms of AD. Comparing 3xTg-AD (amyloid+tau), APP^NL-G-F (amyloid-only), and a viral p-tau model, the authors found 3xTg-AD dopamine neurons burst less in vivo yet spiked faster between bursts, and ex vivo could not sustain firing during depolarization. Inducing p-tau expression reproduced these firing aberrations, whereas amyloid-only neurons did not. The work assigns tau, rather than amyloid, a specific role: p-tau makes individual dopamine neurons intrinsically hyperexcitable while paradoxically impairing the bursting that drives transmission — potentially reducing dopamine output in vivo and marking an early-intervention window.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse AD models (3xTg-AD, APP^NL-G-F, viral p-tau); in vivo and ex vivo electrophysiology of VTA dopamine neurons.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71981

### Cortical macro- and microstructural alterations in multiple system atrophy and their clinical correlates
**Jèssica Pardo & Bàrbara Segura | Movement Disorders**

**Key findings:** Although MSA is primarily a subcortical synucleinopathy, cortical involvement is recognized neuropathologically but hard to capture by imaging. In 29 patients and 35 matched controls, cortical mean diffusivity (cMD) revealed widespread microstructural change, predominantly increases, that was more extensive than cortical-thickness differences and correlated with clinical severity and cognition. The findings support cMD as a more sensitive marker of cortical microstructural integrity than thickness in MSA, with the usual caveats of a modest single-cohort sample and cross-sectional design.

**Model/system & primary method:** Patients with MSA versus matched controls; T1-weighted and diffusion MRI with cortical thickness and mean-diffusivity analyses against clinical and cognitive measures.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70602

### Disentangling the role of adaptive immunity in Alzheimer's disease
**Marco Zattoni & Ludwig Aigner | Molecular Neurodegeneration**

**Key findings:** This review synthesizes evidence that the adaptive immune system is an active contributor to AD, beyond the traditional focus on innate microglial responses. The authors trace how T- and B-cell responses change across disease stages in human studies and animal models, emphasizing temporal dynamics, and critically weigh areas of consensus and controversy where model, stage, and methodological differences drive divergent findings. They assess immunomodulatory strategies and argue that therapeutic windows and routes of administration are decisive for efficacy — a framing relevant to the design of immune-targeted AD trials.

**Model/system & primary method:** Narrative review integrating human and animal-model evidence on adaptive immunity in AD and associated therapeutic strategies.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-026-00985-7

### Caregiver well-being after multisite implementation of the Care Ecosystem
**Elan L Guterman & Katherine L Possin | Alzheimers & Dementia**

**Key findings:** Collaborative dementia-care models help in randomized trials, but whether benefits survive at-scale implementation was uncertain. In a pragmatic multisite study of 1186 dementia patient–caregiver dyads across six US health systems receiving the navigator-led Care Ecosystem, caregiver depression (PHQ-9) was modestly lower at 12 months (mean change −0.48; 95% CI −0.71 to −0.25), with parallel improvements in anxiety, neuropsychiatric-symptom distress, and caregiver strain. Effects are small in absolute terms and the design lacks a concurrent randomized control, but the consistency across outcomes at scale supports real-world deployment of structured dementia care.

**Model/system & primary method:** Pragmatic multisite implementation study (1186 dyads, six US health systems); linear mixed-effects models of caregiver-reported outcomes over 12 months.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.72005

### E2F4 gene therapy reverses established synaptic and cognitive deficits and remodels learning-associated signaling in Alzheimer's disease mice
**C Sanchez-Puelles & J M Frade | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Strategies that reverse, rather than prevent, established AD deficits are scarce. The authors delivered a p38MAPK phosphorylation-resistant human E2F4 variant (hE2F4DN) by AAV to neurons of homozygous 5xFAD mice after functional impairment was already present, and report restored hippocampal LTP and memory, reduced amyloid-β and phospho-tau, and prevention of hippocampal neuronal loss. Proteomic and phosphoproteomic profiling showed remodeling of metabolic, redox, neuroimmune, synaptic-vesicle, and GSK3β-associated pathways, with broader learning-associated signatures after fear conditioning. As mouse-only evidence in an aggressive amyloid model, the therapeutic claim awaits replication and translation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Homozygous 5xFAD mice; AAV-delivered hE2F4DN with electrophysiology, behavior, and integrated (phospho)proteomics.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.13.766444

### The adaptor protein DAB2 cell-autonomously links APOE with disease-associated microglia (DAM)/TREM2 state dysregulation associated with Alzheimer's disease
**D A Salih & J Hardy | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Reelin signaling and its adaptors interact genetically with APOE4 to modify AD risk, but the cellular locus was unclear. Integrating human brain transcriptomics, a Reln-deficient mouse, and DAB1/DAB2 knockdown in APOE-isogenic iPSC-microglia, the authors found microglial DAB2 was dysregulated with increasing amyloid pathology and its transcriptional networks correlated with disease- and lipid-associated microglial states expressing dementia-risk loci (TREM2, GRN, SORL1). DAB2 knockdown shifted microglia away from DAM/TREM2 states toward pro-inflammatory NF-κB programs, and APOE4/4 microglia showed blunted responses — evidence for pathway-level genetic interaction. The causal direction in human disease remains inferential.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human brain transcriptomics, Reln-deficient mice, and APOE-isogenic iPSC-derived microglia with DAB1/DAB2 knockdown.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.12.766141

### PSEN1 loss-of-function variants do not cause early-onset Alzheimer's disease
**A Farinas & M D Greicius | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Whether PSEN1 pathogenicity acts through loss- or gain-of-function bears directly on therapeutic strategy. Following a report of early-onset AD in carriers of a predicted loss-of-function stop-gain (p.K109*), the authors identified 45 carriers of PSEN1 LoF variants predicted to trigger nonsense-mediated decay across three large sequencing databases; none had recorded early-onset dementia, and 17 were over 65 without dementia. Re-analysis of p.K109* revealed a cryptic splice site predicted to yield a small in-frame deletion rather than true LoF. The data argue PSEN1 LoF variants as a class do not cause EOAD, consistent with a gain- or change-of-function mechanism — though based on registry phenotyping with incomplete clinical follow-up.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human genetics; carrier ascertainment across ADSP, UK Biobank, and BioVU with variant-effect and splice re-analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.13.26367865

### Excitatory neurons downregulate synaptic proteins before motor onset and glia reduce neuronal support in Huntington disease mice
**R Alvarez Pardo & B Alvarez-Castelao | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Whole-tissue proteomes obscure how distinct cell types respond in Huntington's disease. Using cell-type-specific metabolic labeling in living R6/1 mice across disease stages, the authors measured proteomes of cortical and hippocampal excitatory neurons, cerebellar inhibitory neurons, and glia separately. Hippocampal excitatory neurons showed broader, more coordinated regulation already before motor onset, while the cortical response emerged only at the most severe stage; all neuronal populations progressively upregulated ubiquitin–proteasome proteins, and glia showed reduced neuronal-support programs. The work resolves region- and cell-type-specific proteostatic trajectories in HD, with the caveat that R6/1 is an aggressive fragment model.

**Model/system & primary method:** R6/1 Huntington's disease mice; in vivo cell-type-specific metabolic protein labeling with mass spectrometry and network analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.12.766120

### Wearable-based sleep and activity tracking detects Parkinson's disease and associated pathological hallmarks
**A Moustaklis & S Hadjidimitriou | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** The authors derived sleep and activity metrics from free-living wrist accelerometry and trained a model (SAM-PD) to distinguish people with PD (n=133) from controls (n=26), reaching AUROC 0.93 and generalizing without retraining to an independent European cohort recorded on a different device (AUROC 0.95). REM sleep duration, walking cadence, and low-level acceleration features were most predictive, and the model related to dopaminergic deficit and α-synuclein pathology. The imbalanced case–control design and small control group temper the headline accuracy, but the cross-device generalization is a meaningful step toward passive digital screening.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human cohorts with wrist accelerometry; machine-learning classifier trained on sleep/activity metrics with external validation.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.13.26367931
