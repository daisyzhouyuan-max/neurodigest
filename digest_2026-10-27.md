### Microglial NUS1 deficiency drives dopaminergic neurodegeneration through intercellular iron dysregulation in Parkinson's disease models
**Lele Liu & Haigang Ren | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** NUS1 (encoding NgBR) is a PD risk gene, and this work places its pathogenic action in microglia rather than neurons. NgBR-deficient mouse and human microglia undergo ER stress and PERK–ATF4–NUPR1 signaling that biases them toward LCN2-mediated iron efflux over cytokine release; iron-loaded Holo-LCN2 (but not iron-free Apo-LCN2) is taken up by dopaminergic neurons via SLC22A17, causing iron overload and ferroptosis. It reframes a genetic PD risk factor as a problem of microglia-to-neuron iron trafficking and nominates the LCN2–SLC22A17 axis as a druggable node.

**Model/system & primary method:** Microglia-specific NgBR-deficient mice and human microglia; pharmacological and genetic manipulation of the LCN2–SLC22A17 axis and ferroptosis, with in vitro and in vivo rescue.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02499-3

### Chronic progressive demyelination induces a microglial state with lysosomal dysfunction
**Stefan A Berghoff & Mikael Simons | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Comparing acute versus chronic demyelination across genetic and toxic mouse models isolates a microglial state specific to chronic myelin damage — sustained pro-inflammatory activation, lysosomal lipid sequestration, reduced lipid-droplet formation, and a metabolic shift toward cholesterol synthesis with diminished sterol efflux. Crucially, a desmosterol mimetic with 24-dehydrocholesterol-reductase–inhibitor activity reversed this state, lowered CCL4, reduced myelin damage, and improved motor performance in a leukodystrophy model, identifying a tractable target for chronic demyelinating disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** Acute and chronic demyelination mouse models (genetic and toxic) with transcriptomics; pharmacological reversal with a desmosterol mimetic in a leukodystrophy model.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02489-5

### Phase 3 randomized trial of latozinemab in frontotemporal dementia due to GRN mutations
**Adam Simmons & Arthur J Mayorga | Alzheimers Dement**

**Why it matters:** INFRONT-3 is the first phase 3 efficacy readout for any investigational therapy in FTD-GRN. Latozinemab, an anti–sortilin monoclonal designed to raise progranulin, produced sustained elevation of plasma and CSF PGRN in symptomatic carriers but did not slow progression on clinical or biomarker endpoints, with no new safety signals. The dissociation between a successful target-engagement biomarker and a null clinical outcome is an important cautionary result for the PGRN-elevation strategy.

**Model/system & primary method:** Randomized, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial (NCT04374136); 60 mg/kg IV every 4 weeks for 96 weeks in symptomatic and at-risk heterozygous LOF GRN carriers, with an open-label extension.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71947

### Spatial multi-omics reveals disease-associated oligodendrocytes and metabolic dysregulation in Parkinson's disease mouse models
**Ziting Zhu & Wenlong Zhang | Nat Commun**

**Why it matters:** Integrating single-nucleus RNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics and spatial metabolomics across disease stages defines a disease-associated oligodendrocyte lineage marked by elevated Il33 and Kirrel3 that expands with α-synuclein pathology. Bidirectional manipulation of oligodendrocyte Il33 alters microglial ST2 activation, insoluble α-synuclein burden and motor performance, and both IL33 and ST2 are elevated in human PD midbrain and CSF — implicating oligodendrocyte-to-microglia IL33–ST2 signaling as an active contributor, not a bystander, in synucleinopathy. Causal manipulations are mouse-only and the mouse work used male animals.

**Model/system & primary method:** Male A53T α-synuclein transgenic mice and AAV-α-synuclein overexpression; snRNA-seq plus spatial transcriptomics and metabolomics, with human midbrain and CSF validation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78051-5

### Remak bundle size, structure, and function are regulated by the Schwann cell basal lamina
**Monique Lillis & Jonah R Chan | Neuron**

**Key findings:** Blocking Schwann-cell secretion of type IV collagen left myelin normal but disrupted Remak bundles — reduced bundle density, enlarged bundles, improperly ensheathed axons, and reduced mechanosensitivity — via decreased postnatal Schwann-cell proliferation rather than integrin signaling. Inducing supernumerary Schwann cells rescued both architecture and mechanosensitivity, establishing that the SC-secreted basal lamina sets Remak Schwann-cell number and bundle organization, with implications for peripheral neuropathy and neuropathic pain.

**Model/system & primary method:** Genetic prevention of type IV collagen secretion from Schwann cells in mice; ultrastructural and mechanosensory phenotyping with Schwann-cell number manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.034

### Human brain cellular xenografts in neuroimmunology and beyond
**Nicola Fattorelli & Renzo Mancuso | Immunity**

**Key findings:** This review synthesizes human brain cellular xenograft (HBCX) models — human iPSC-derived neurons, glia or organoids transplanted into immunodeficient mice — as an in vivo platform for human-specific phenotypes inaccessible to conventional in vitro or rodent systems. Given pronounced species differences in microglia and other brain macrophages and their centrality to neurodegenerative disease, it frames HBCX as a route to interrogate human neuroimmunology and to support functional genomics, drug testing and biomarker discovery, while cataloguing current limitations.

**Model/system & primary method:** Review of human pluripotent-stem-cell xenotransplantation into immunodeficient mice.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2026.09.024

### Glymphatic dysfunction drives high-urea-induced neurodegeneration in Parkinson's disease
**Shengyao Zhang & Jianhua Ran | Mov Disord**

**Key findings:** In urea-transporter-B knockout mice, elevated brain urea upregulated astrocytic DPP6 and activated PKCη signaling, driving AQP4 endocytosis and loss of membrane polarity. The resulting impairment of glymphatic clearance accelerated α-synuclein spread and dopaminergic neurodegeneration; inhibiting the DPP6–PKCη axis pharmacologically or genetically rescued AQP4 localization and the downstream pathology, linking urea metabolic disturbance to synucleinopathy through astrocytic polarity. Causal evidence is mouse-only.

**Model/system & primary method:** UT-B knockout high-urea mouse model; pharmacological and genetic targeting of DPP6/PKCη with glymphatic-clearance and α-synuclein-propagation readouts.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70565

### Real-world clinical, biomarker, and imaging outcomes of lecanemab in Alzheimer's disease: a multicenter longitudinal cohort study in Jiangsu, China
**Limoran Tang & Yun Xu | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** In 181 Chinese AD patients on biweekly lecanemab, a cognitive response (by disease-stage-specific CDR-SB thresholds) emerged at month 6 and intensified by month 12, largely confined to mild cognitive impairment and mild dementia; APOE ε4 positivity and more severe impairment predicted lower response odds. Plasma p-tau217 and Aβ42/40 shifted as early as month 3, preceding clinical change, and ARIA incidence was 7.2%, all asymptomatic. This is observational real-world data without a placebo arm, but it extends lecanemab evidence to a Chinese cohort and supports early plasma biomarkers as response indicators.

**Model/system & primary method:** Multicenter prospective cohort (n=181); penalized models, ROC analysis and exploratory month-3 plasma biomarker analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71936

### Tau extent is associated with lower cognition in Down syndrome-related Alzheimer's disease
**Matthew D Zammit & Michael S Rafii | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** The second-generation tau PET tracer 18F-MK6240 was evaluated for the first time in Down syndrome, a population at high risk of early-onset AD. MK6240 showed mesial-temporal binding shortly after amyloid onset, paralleling 18F-Flortaucipir but without its early-region off-target binding, and greater tau extent correlated with lower episodic memory and overall cognition across harmonized cohorts. The work supports MK6240 as a sensitive tau biomarker in DS and strengthens the case for including this population in early intervention trials.

**Model/system & primary method:** Cross-sectional tau PET in Trial-Ready Cohort-Down Syndrome (18F-MK6240) harmonized against ABC-DS (18F-Flortaucipir), related to cognitive endpoints.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71925

### Short-term outcomes, longer-term disability and relapse risk in LGI1-antibody encephalitis
**Sudarshini Ramanathan & International LGI1-antibody Encephalitis Consortium | Brain**

**Key findings:** In 181 patients with LGI1-antibody encephalitis, immunotherapy — corticosteroids, plasma exchange and rituximab, but not antiseizure medications, IVIg or steroid-sparing agents — was strongly associated with seizure resolution, while oral prednisolone discontinuation preceded seizure recurrence. Each month of delayed immunotherapy raised one-year disability risk by 16%, rituximab exposure cut future relapse risk by 93%, and persistent serum LGI1-IgM (not IgG) at one year flagged relapse risk; the authors also calibrate an mRS threshold against quality-of-life data. Findings are retrospective and real-world, pending randomized trials.

**Model/system & primary method:** Multicentre international retrospective cohort; Cox, Andersen-Gill and generalized linear mixed-effects models with QoL-calibrated disability thresholds.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag370

### Vascular drivers of cerebrospinal fluid movement and enlarged perivascular spaces
**James D LeFevre & Angela L Jefferson | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** This review focuses specifically on the vascular mechanisms that drive perivascular fluid dynamics and how their dysfunction may produce enlarged perivascular spaces, an emerging marker of cerebral small-vessel disease linked to protein accumulation and cognitive decline. It proposes an integrated vascular-ePVS hypothesis in which early vascular dysfunction impairs waste clearance and raises ePVS burden, connecting early-life vascular health to later proteinopathy and cognitive decline and framing vascular targets for cognitive preservation in AD and related dementias.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mechanistic review of vascular contributions to perivascular fluid dynamics and ePVS formation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71927

### Cerebrospinal fluid alpha-synuclein seed amplification assay positivity identifies a prognostically distinct subgroup in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis
**Obara, K. & Mattsson-Carlgren, N. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Across 443 ALS patients from two cohorts, strong CSF α-synuclein seed-amplification-assay positivity was uncommon (5.4%; 8.4% including weak positives) and no more frequent than in Aβ-negative cognitively unimpaired controls. However, strong positivity was associated with shorter tracheostomy-free survival after adjustment for established predictors including NfL (adjusted HR 2.08) and modified the NfL–ALSFRS-R decline relationship; neuropathology in 5 cases was concordant. This provides in vivo evidence that α-synuclein co-pathology may contribute to prognostic heterogeneity in ALS, though effects rest on a small strong-positive subgroup.

**Model/system & primary method:** CSF RT-QuIC α-synuclein SAA in two ALS cohorts (n=248 Nagoya, n=195 Bologna) benchmarked against BioFINDER groups; cohort-stratified Cox and mixed-effects survival/decline models.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.23.26366349

### Proteogenomics captures a midlife biological state of Alzheimer's disease susceptibility
**Perry, A. & Gamazon, E. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Combining multimodal neuroimaging with plasma proteomics in UK Biobank, the authors derived a circulating "neurostructural proteome" reflecting brain structure that predicted future cognitive decline, incident dementia and later-life AD pathology across independent cohorts spanning two decades. Genetic and single-nucleus analyses showed convergence with AD genetic architecture, enriched in astrocyte, microglia and endothelial pathways beyond APOE — pointing to a midlife, blood-detectable molecular signature of AD susceptibility preceding conventional biomarkers. Associations are observational.

**Model/system & primary method:** UK Biobank multimodal MRI plus plasma proteomics; cross-cohort prediction with genetic and single-nucleus transcriptomic convergence analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.22.26366029

### Postsynaptic TAK1-GluN2B axis supports hippocampal synaptic integrity and cognitive function during aging
**Liu, Y. & He, K.-W. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Conditional reduction of the kinase TAK1 in forebrain excitatory neurons impaired memory and hippocampal LTP, lowered dendritic spine density and suppressed excitatory transmission; disrupting TAK1 kinase activity reproduced the deficits. TAK1 localized postsynaptically with PSD-95 and phosphorylated GluN2B at S920, and phosphodeficient/phosphomimetic mutants supported S920 as the effector. Synaptic TAK1 fell in the aged hippocampus, and re-expressing it in aged mice restored spine density, plasticity and memory — defining a postsynaptic TAK1–GluN2B axis relevant to age-related hippocampal vulnerability.

**Model/system & primary method:** Forebrain-excitatory conditional TAK1 mouse models; electrophysiology, PSD phosphoproteomics, GluN2B mutant analysis and aged-mouse rescue.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.25.761716

### Spatial mapping of α-synuclein seeding activity and pathology using immunofluorescence-integrated quiescent seed amplification
**Mao, H. & Xu, P. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** IF-QSAA couples in situ quiescent seed amplification with multichannel immunolabeling on a single brain section, recovering spatial coordinates that homogenate-based seed assays discard while measuring the functional seeding competence that pS129 immunostaining cannot. Across preformed-fibril-inoculated mouse brains and human PD tissue it revealed white-matter seeding enrichment, resolved hemispheric asymmetry earlier than pS129, and recovered seeding from archival pS129-negative tissue, while mapping selective neuronal and microglial associations. A methods advance co-mapping functional seeding with molecular histopathology. This is a revision of a preprint first posted March 2025.

**Model/system & primary method:** Multiplexed immunofluorescence-integrated QSAA in PFF-inoculated mouse brain and human PD tissue; thioflavin-T seeding readout with spectral unmixing.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.03.26.645411

### Improving DASH adherence after midlife promotes better late-life cognition and brain health: the SG70 Study
**NG, E. K. K. & Zhou, J. H. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In 892 participants with diet records from 1993–2025 nested in the Singapore Chinese Health Study, higher DASH adherence in both midlife (44–55 y) and early elderhood (61–73 y) was associated with better late-life verbal memory; midlife adherence tracked greater white-matter volume in association tracts, and early-elderhood adherence tracked lower free water and white-matter hyperintensities. White-matter volume and free water mediated diet–memory associations, and participants whose diet improved from mid-to-late life had better memory and brain health than persistently low adherers — suggesting post-midlife dietary improvement still supports cognitive resilience. Observational with mediation analysis.

**Model/system & primary method:** Longitudinal human cohort (n=892) with repeated DASH scoring, late-life multidomain cognition and neuroimaging (morphometry, WMH, free-water markers); mediation analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.23.753372
