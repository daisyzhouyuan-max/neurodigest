1Noncoding functions of FMR1 transcript in riboregulation of human neurodevelopment

Neuron  Soraya O. Sandoval & Xinyu Zhao
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Fragile X syndrome is attributed to loss of FMR1 and its protein product FMRP, but this work isolates a protein-independent function of the FMR1 transcript itself. FXS patient neurons (which lose the transcript) are hyperexcitable and morphologically immature, whereas FMRP-knockout neurons that retain the transcript are not — a discrepancy that tracks to metabolism, since only FXS neurons show elevated glycolysis. The FMR1 RNA riboregulates lactate dehydrogenase A by blocking LDHA mRNA from binding IGF2BP2, and re-expressing the transcript alone, without FMRP, rescues the glycolytic and cellular deficits. The result reframes FMR1 as a dual-function RNA and implies the transcript, not only the protein, is a therapeutic target.
Model & method
Human iPSC-derived FXS and FMRP-KO neurons; multi-omics, metabolic flux assays, and RNA–protein interaction mapping.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.10.015

2Distinct prelimbic microcircuits control sensory and affective dimensions of neuropathic pain

Nature Neuroscience  Zheng Gan & Rohini Kuner
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
Deactivation of medial prefrontal cortex is a shared feature of chronic pain, but its microcircuit basis has been unclear. Longitudinal two-photon imaging of mouse prelimbic cortex after nerve injury shows that all classes of GABAergic interneuron become sensitized and switch from unimodal to polymodal responses. Crucially, the sensory and affective dimensions of neuropathic pain are routed through two separable microcircuits — PV⁺–Rbp4⁺ neurons versus Prox1⁺–Cux2⁺/Ntsr1⁺ neurons — that drive distinct projection outputs. The study gives a circuit-level account of how prefrontal deactivation exacerbates pain and dissociates its sensory from its emotional components, a potential substrate for dimension-selective neuromodulation.
Model & method
Mouse prelimbic cortex; in vivo longitudinal multiphoton imaging with genetically targeted interneuron-class circuit interrogation.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02498-4

3Chondroitin sulfate proteoglycan 4 participates in the internalization of arginine-rich dipeptide repeat proteins

PNAS  Alexandra B. Sutter & Robert G. Kalb
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
C9orf72 repeat expansion is the most common genetic cause of ALS and frontotemporal dementia, and the toxic arginine-rich dipeptide repeat proteins (poly-PR, poly-GR) spread cell-to-cell by undefined routes. Using µMap proximity labeling and RNAi screening, the authors identify CSPG4 as a cell-surface factor for poly-PR uptake: blocking glycosaminoglycan synthesis or knocking down/ablating CSPG4 reduces internalization and protects neurons from PR20 toxicity. In a dual-reporter system, neuron-to-neuron PR50 transmission was CSPG4-dependent, as was uptake of transmissible protein from conditioned media. The work nominates a receptor pathway for DPR propagation and a candidate handle on prion-like spread, though the evidence is entirely in vitro.
Model & method
Cell lines and cultured neurons; µMap proximity labeling, RNAi screens, and a dual-reporter neuron-to-neuron transmission assay.
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2534149123

4Identification of Type 1 and Type 2 α-synuclein seeds by CSF seed amplification assay in patients with movement disorders

Neurology  Sandrina K. Weber & Brit Mollenhauer
Schematic drawn from the reported findings — not a figure from the paper.
Why it matters
A seed amplification assay that distinguishes Lewy-body-associated (type 1) from MSA-associated (type 2) α-synuclein seeds was applied to 1,059 consecutive movement-disorder clinic patients — a routine clinical rather than a research cohort. Seeds were detected in 54%, with type 1 predominating; positivity was high in Parkinson's disease (85%), dementia with Lewy bodies (93%), MSA (85%) and isolated REM-sleep behavior disorder (80%). Type 2 seeds were largely specific to MSA (80% of MSA seeds; only 2.6% of non-MSA patients), so detecting type 2 outside clinically defined MSA may flag atypical presentations. Type 1 seeds associated with worse olfaction and more hallucinations. The data provide clinical reference frequencies and support in vivo seed typing for diagnosis and trial stratification; cross-sectional and single-center.
Model & method
Human CSF, cross-sectional movement-disorders cohort (n=1,059); α-synuclein seed amplification assay with clinical correlation.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218763

An open agentic AI co-scientist for clinical and biomedical discovery

Nature Medicine  Kaleigh F. Roberts & Justin T. Reese
Key findings
OpenScientist is an open-source agentic AI system that executes scientist-defined analyses end to end. Across five expert-evaluated biomedical case studies it quantified Alzheimer's blood-biomarker performance, proposed a reproducible mechanism linking tau pathology to altered lysosomal acidification, and built a plasma-proteomic survival model validated in a held-out cohort, completing in minutes analyses that would otherwise take months. It can run air-gapped on open models for sensitive clinical data. For neurodegeneration researchers it is of interest as an auditable, reproducible tool for biomarker and mechanism exploration, rather than as a biological finding in itself.
Model & method
Open-source agentic LLM framework; expert evaluation across five biomedical case studies, including AD biomarker and tau-pathology analyses.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04808-6

A spatiotemporal atlas of maturation and aging of the male monkey hypothalamic–pituitary–testicular axis

Cell Stem Cell  Chao Liu & Wei Li
Key findings
A multi-omic atlas — spatial ATAC-RNA sequencing combined with single-nucleus and single-cell RNA-seq — of the cynomolgus monkey hypothalamic–pituitary–testicular axis across juvenile, pubertal, adult and old stages. On the neural side, hypothalamic KNDy neurons shift between quiescent-like and excitatory-like transcriptional states and spatially reorganize with astrocytes in a pattern that tracks axis maturation; with aging, the hypothalamus shows senescence-associated changes while the pituitary loses organizational structure across endocrine populations. The atlas provides a primate reference for hypothalamic neuroendocrine maturation and aging.
Model & method
Cynomolgus monkey HPT axis across four life stages; spatial ATAC-RNA sequencing with snRNA-seq and scRNA-seq.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2026.10.014

Robustness of plasma p-tau217 diagnostic thresholds for Alzheimer disease across clinical populations and laboratory environments

Neurology  Jean-Louis Bayart & Bernard J. Hanseeuw
Key findings
Across a Brussels derivation cohort and three external validation cohorts (Paris memory clinic, Bordeaux early-onset dementia, monogenic FTLD), a single Lumipulse plasma p-tau217 threshold (~0.193 pg/mL) transferred across centers, analyzers and reagent lots. p-tau217 gave the best performance (AUC 0.96 for amyloid status), and a p-tau217/Aβ42 ratio added nothing; sensitivity/specificity for clinical-biological AD were ~93%/93% (Paris) and ~96%/92% (Bordeaux). Gray-zone values (15–25% of patients) were resolved by second-line CSF in ~87% of cases, and false positives were enriched in FTLD with ALS. The results support plasma-first diagnostic pathways with confirmatory testing; cross-sectional and recruited in specialized Western European centers.
Model & method
Human plasma, multicenter cross-sectional study (n=411 plus validation cohorts); Lumipulse p-tau217 against amyloid/tau-PET and CSF reference standards.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218784

In vivo topographic associations between tau pathology, atrophy, and symptom domains in progressive supranuclear palsy

Neurology  Yuko Kataoka & Hironobu Endo
Key findings
In 58 PSP patients and 52 controls, florzolotau (¹⁸F) tau-PET and structural MRI mapped tau deposition and atrophy onto PSP symptom domains. Ocular-motor scores tracked tau in the midbrain tegmentum and globus pallidus; gait and midline scores tracked tau in the globus pallidus, thalamus and subthalamic nucleus; limb-motor scores tracked tau in primary motor and somatosensory cortex together with parietotemporal atrophy. Path analysis was consistent with subcortical tau driving local neuronal loss and ocular-motor/gait deficits, while neocortical tau contributes to limb-motor impairment through both local and more distributed effects. The findings support anatomically distinct tau–neurodegeneration pathways; cross-sectional and single-center.
Model & method
Human PSP cohort; florzolotau tau-PET, T1-weighted MRI, and path analysis.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218693

Genome-wide association and multi-omics analysis of sporadic Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease in a Chinese population

Alzheimer's & Dementia  Ruhan A & Xiao-Ping Dong
Key findings
The first case-control GWAS of sporadic CJD in an East Asian population (143 cases, 301 Chinese-ancestry controls) identified eight loci with suggestive association and, through QTL-based multi-omics prioritization, candidate genes in lipid metabolism, ER proteostasis, cytoskeletal regulation and membrane transport (including SSH1, PLAAT3, DNAJC10 and CDS1). Several loci showed population-specific allele frequencies relative to European cohorts. The study extends prion-disease genetic architecture beyond European ancestry and nominates lipid metabolism and ER protein quality control as susceptibility pathways, with the caveats of a small sample and signals at suggestive rather than genome-wide significance.
Model & method
Human case-control GWAS with QTL-based multi-omics gene prioritization.
https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71999

Localization mechanisms and function of postsynaptic Kv7 (KCNQ) channels and their reduction in KCNQ2 epileptic encephalopathy

PNAS  Sahil Malpotra & Hee Jung Chung
Key findings
Beyond their established axonal role, Kv7.2/Kv7.3 channels are shown at and near the postsynaptic density within hippocampal dendritic spines, where their hyperpolarizing current reduces NMDA-receptor calcium influx. Spine localization requires Kv7.2/Kv7.3 heteromerization and binding to AnkyrinG-190, the only AnkyrinG isoform in spines. KCNQ2 variants that cause developmental and epileptic encephalopathy markedly reduce spine localization of Kv7 channels and their suppression of NMDAR activity. The work reveals a postsynaptic Kv7 population that constrains excitatory synaptic transmission and implicates its dysregulation in KCNQ2-associated encephalopathy.
Model & method
Hippocampal neurons; channel localization imaging, electrophysiology, and analysis of disease-causing KCNQ2 variants.
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2606522123

Expanding the amyotrophic lateral sclerosis genetic spectrum via systematic screening of adult-onset neurologic disease genes

Neurology  Arianna Manini & Nicola Ticozzi
Key findings
Whole-genome sequencing of 856 ALS/PLS patients screened 453 adult-onset neurologic-disease genes. Nearly 20% carried pathogenic or likely-pathogenic variants or repeat expansions, and 4.8% carried variants in non-ALS/FTD genes spanning ataxias, neuropathies, myopathies, movement disorders, mitochondrial disease and hereditary spastic paraplegia. Variant carriers were enriched for family history and earlier onset, and pathway enrichment extended to mitochondrial, metabolic and proteostasis genes. The authors argue for broader diagnostic gene panels to support precision medicine in ALS; the absence of a control cohort limits formal assessment of variant enrichment.
Model & method
Human ALS/PLS cohort (n=856); short-read whole-genome sequencing with short-tandem-repeat expansion screening.
https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218712

α-Synuclein structural heterogeneity associates with distinct cytopathologies in multiple system atrophy

bioRxiv  X. Xu & M. Enomoto
Key findings
Cryo-EM resolved α-synuclein filaments from two regions of a single neuropathologically confirmed MSA brain — medial temporal lobe, with predominantly neuronal inclusions, versus cerebellar white matter, with exclusively oligodendroglial inclusions — at 3.0 Å and 2.0 Å. Medial-temporal filaments contained a previously undescribed protofilament fold, PF-IIIA, paired with canonical PF-IIB2, whereas cerebellar filaments adopted the canonical MSA Type II structure. The result suggests α-synuclein filament conformation varies with regional and cellular (neuronal versus oligodendroglial) context within one brain, bearing on disease heterogeneity. As a single-case study, generalization remains to be established.
Model & method
Human MSA brain; region-resolved cryo-electron microscopy of α-synuclein filaments.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.13.766536

The ANKLE2–VRK1 pathway promotes neuronal resilience by preserving proteostasis during aging

bioRxiv  N. L. Link & H. Newlin
Key findings
In Drosophila, adult-onset neuronal knockdown of Ankle2 — a developmental regulator that inhibits the kinase VRK1 — shortens lifespan, impairs motor function, accelerates neuronal loss, and causes CNS-wide accumulation of ubiquitinated proteins with reduced proteasome activity; lifelong reduction produces Parkinson-like phenotypes. Human ANKLE2 rescues the fly phenotypes, and reducing VRK1/Ballchen reverses the ubiquitin accumulation and behavioral deficits, indicating the developmental pathway remains functionally important in aged neurons. The work provides a mechanistic link from neurodevelopmental-disease genes to age-associated proteostasis decline and tau-relevant neurodegeneration, in an invertebrate model.
Model & method
Drosophila with adult-onset and lifelong neuronal knockdown; proteostasis assays, behavior, and cross-species rescue.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.13.766461

Age-dependent phosphorylation of estrogen receptor β regulates homodimer formation and impairs repression of the amyloid precursor protein promoter

bioRxiv  Y. Yildiz & T. R. Pak
Key findings
Using FRET from fluorescence-lifetime measurements of ERβ phosphomutants, phosphorylation at S176, S200 and Y488 reduced ERβ homodimer formation, and phosphorylation at S200 and Y488 abolished ERβ-mediated repression of the APP promoter. The findings offer a molecular explanation for why estrogen's neuroprotection wanes with age and after menopause: age-dependent ERβ phosphorylation disrupts its genomic signaling and de-represses APP. The evidence is biochemical, and the link to amyloid is at the level of promoter regulation rather than in vivo pathology.
Model & method
ERβ phosphomutant proteins; FRET/fluorescence-lifetime imaging and APP-promoter reporter assays.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.13.766544

Age-dependent changes in the putamen transcriptome and proteome of a transgenic minipig model of Huntington's disease

bioRxiv  E. Kamenna & J. Klima
Key findings
RNA-seq and label-free proteomics of putamen in transgenic HD minipigs versus wild-type at 4 and 8 years found few changes at 4 years but 3,324 differentially expressed genes at 8 years, with concordant transcriptome and proteome pathway shifts. D2 medium spiny neuron markers declined; mitochondrial and oxidative-phosphorylation pathways reversed from positively enriched at 4 years to negatively enriched at 8 years; immune and complement pathways rose, and altered splicing events increased nearly fivefold. There was no accumulation of soluble mutant huntingtin and the transgene's repeat does not expand somatically, leading the authors to interpret the reduced neuronal markers as loss of neuronal identity rather than cell death — a model of the slow early disease phase. Single tissue, large-animal model.
Model & method
Transgenic HD minipig putamen at 4 and 8 years; RNA sequencing, label-free proteomics, and mutant-huntingtin immunoassay.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.13.766462

Cell-state mapping reveals a reversible neuroblast accumulation in the aging mouse hippocampus

bioRxiv  N. B. Rasetto & A. F. Schinder
Key findings
Lineage tracing combined with single-nucleus RNA-seq of adult-born granule cells in the aging mouse hippocampus revealed that aging causes accumulation of postmitotic neuroblasts, a stage-specific bottleneck in the neurogenic trajectory. Voluntary running relieved the bottleneck, restoring progression toward maturation and reducing apoptosis at the neuroblast stage. The work identifies postmitotic neuroblasts as a stage of heightened vulnerability in aging and points to exercise as a restorative intervention. This is a revised version of a preprint first posted in April 2026; mouse model.
Model & method
Aging mouse hippocampus; lineage tracing with single-nucleus RNA-seq and a voluntary-running intervention.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.23.717949
