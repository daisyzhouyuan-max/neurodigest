### Prion-like transmission of human tau strains in the mouse brain
**Sofia Lövestam & Sjors H W Scheres | Nature**

**Why it matters:** This supplies the missing pillar of the prion hypothesis for tauopathy — that a strain keeps its structure when it passes to a new host. Tau filaments extracted from Alzheimer's disease or corticobasal degeneration brains, injected into wild-type mice, templated the assembly of filaments made of *mouse* tau that, by cryo-EM, adopted the same fold as the human seed. Because distinct disease folds are faithfully propagated, the work establishes that templated seeding — not merely aggregation — transmits conformational identity, and validates the ordinary mouse as a tractable system for studying how specific tau folds drive disease-specific pathology.

**Model/system & primary method:** Intracerebral seeding of wild-type mice with AD- and CBD-derived tau filaments; cryo-EM structure determination of the resulting mouse tau filaments.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11061-x

### A TMEM119–tankyrase axis restrains microglial phagocytosis to maintain memory and enables pharmacological forgetting
**Suhua Sun & Hai Qi | Neuron**

**Why it matters:** The study identifies the molecular setpoint for how aggressively microglia prune synapses and shows it can be moved with a drug. TMEM119, a microglia-specific protein, sequesters tankyrase; this stabilizes axin, limits free APC and thereby caps the actin nucleation needed for phagocytosis. Acute microglial ablation of TMEM119 in adult mice exaggerated synapse elimination and destabilized established fear memories — a phenotype reproduced by an intravenously delivered, membrane-permeable peptide that competitively breaks the TMEM119–tankyrase interaction. The axis is thus a pharmacologically accessible rheostat on synapse loss, relevant both to disorders of excess synaptic pruning and, provocatively, to deliberate memory modification. Causal evidence is mouse-only.

**Model/system & primary method:** Adult mouse microglia-specific TMEM119 ablation and fear-memory assays; mechanistic cell biology of the tankyrase–axin–APC–actin pathway; in vivo delivery of a disrupting peptide.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.001

### A population code for semantics in human hippocampus
**Melissa Franch & Jay A Hennig | Nat Neurosci**

**Why it matters:** Recording from human hippocampal neurons during natural speech listening, the authors show that word meaning is carried in distributed population activity rather than by single-word cells, and that it survives controls for phonemics and grammar. Distances between neural population responses tracked semantic distances, population activity aligned most closely with GPT-2 embeddings, and response variability correlated with LLM-derived polysemy — evidence that the hippocampal code is contextualized, much like a language-model embedding. The result gives a neurocomputational account of semantic encoding in humans and a concrete bridge between single-neuron data and representation geometry in large language models.

**Model/system & primary method:** Intracranial single-unit recordings from human hippocampus during narrative listening; encoding-model analysis compared against LLM (GPT-2) embeddings.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02436-4

### Cranial bone marrow-derived monocytes promote neuroinflammation in chronic traumatic brain injury
**Zhichao Lu & Wei Shi | Sci Transl Med**

**Why it matters:** The paper traces age-worsened decline after chronic TBI to a specific, druggable cell source, and takes it from mouse mechanism to a first human test. Chronic TBI drove persistent aberrant myelopoiesis in the cranial bone marrow — expanding inflammatory monocytes/macrophages that migrated into brain parenchyma and sustained neuroinflammation, worsening with age. Mechanistically, age-related PPARα deficiency disrupted lipid metabolism in these cells and, via H3K4me3-marked chromatin, locked in an inflammatory state; the PPARα agonist fenofibrate corrected the metabolic defect and reduced brain infiltration. In a proof-of-concept randomized trial of 40 older adults with chronic TBI, fenofibrate lowered plasma neurofilament light and improved cognition — a small but directionally concordant human signal atop the mouse mechanism.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse chronic-TBI models with cell tracing, multiomics and adoptive transfer; fenofibrate intervention; proof-of-concept randomized clinical trial (n=40 older adults).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aef5898

### Osteoclasts intercept meningeal lymphatic paths for dural metastasis
**Rui Zeng & Yudan Chi | Nature**

**Why it matters:** Beyond its oncology focus, this is a meningeal-lymphatic biology paper of interest to anyone working on CNS waste clearance. Using a new mouse model of dural metastasis plus patient imaging, the authors show that tumour progression impairs meningeal lymphatic drainage, and that cancer-associated osteoclasts localizing at the skull–tumour interface are responsible: osteoclast-derived angiopoietin-2 disrupts the lymphatic network. Selectively targeting osteoclasts restored meningeal lymphatic structure and function and augmented anti-tumour immunity. The finding adds osteoclast–angiopoietin-2 signalling as a modifiable regulator of meningeal lymphatic function, with therapeutic implications for dural metastasis and, more broadly, for conditions of meningeal drainage failure.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse dural-metastasis model with clinical imaging; single-cell RNA sequencing of the tumour microenvironment; osteoclast-targeting intervention.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11082-6

### The default mode network exhibits circuit-specific selective vulnerability despite widespread amyloid burden in an animal model of Alzheimer's disease
**Samuel J Brunwasser & Keith B Hengen | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** Testing whether DMN vulnerability in AD is merely a consequence of amyloid or reflects an intrinsic circuit susceptibility, the authors made long-term, multi-region single-unit recordings in APP/PS1 mice with widespread Aβ. Dysfunction was restricted to the within-DMN circuit (retrosplenial→anterior cingulate), while communication from the same source to an out-of-DMN target (primary visual cortex) was spared — dissociating circuit identity from global plaque load and favouring intrinsic DMN susceptibility. The deficit was most pronounced during sleep. Evidence is from an amyloid-only mouse model.

**Model/system & primary method:** APP/PS1 mice; chronic multi-region extracellular single-unit recordings with sleep-state analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76952-z

### Electronic profiling of neuronal extracellular vesicles enables early prediction of Parkinson's disease
**Tianrui Chang & Sahika Inal | Sci Adv**

**Key findings:** An organic electrochemical transistor assay measures total, aggregated and Ser129-phosphorylated α-synuclein proteoforms in serum L1CAM+ neuronal extracellular vesicles, reaching low-femtomolar sensitivity and sidestepping the red-blood-cell α-synuclein background that confounds blood assays. In 66 individuals including prodromal and diagnosed PD, combining the three proteoform readouts separated PD from controls with 90.9% accuracy (88.6% sensitivity, 90.9% specificity). The platform is a promising minimally invasive route to early PD detection, pending validation in larger, longitudinal cohorts.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human serum (n=66, incl. prodromal PD); organic electrochemical transistor multiparametric immunoassay of neuronal-EV α-synuclein proteoforms.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee4525

### ZBP1-mediated Treg cell dysfunction exacerbates neuroimmune dysregulation in Alzheimer's disease under metabolic stress
**Shanshan Huang & Houguang Zhou | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Linking metabolic stress to AD-relevant neuroimmune failure, the authors show that high-fat-diet/high-glucose–high-palmitate conditions impaired regulatory T-cell stability and upregulated ZBP1, activating apoptotic and necroptotic signalling; dysfunctional Tregs were associated with greater glial activation and neuronal injury. ZBP1-deficient Tregs survived better, were more immunosuppressive, and — on adoptive transfer into HFD-fed 5xFAD mice — conferred greater neuroprotection than wild-type Tregs. ZBP1 is nominated as a target to strengthen Treg-based therapy under metabolic stress. Mechanistic work in mice and cell co-cultures.

**Model/system & primary method:** HFD-fed 5xFAD mice, metabolically stressed Treg co-cultures, and adoptive Treg transfer; flow cytometry, RNA-seq, PET-CT and electrophysiology.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71896

### Endothelial Malat1 promotes post-stroke angiogenesis and neurovascular recovery via the Tek axis
**Tianqing Xiong & Ke-Jie Yin | Brain**

**Key findings:** The lncRNA Malat1 acts as an endothelial driver of post-stroke repair: endothelium-specific Malat1 overexpression enhanced angiogenesis, restored cerebral blood flow and improved sensorimotor and cognitive recovery after ischemic stroke in mice. FACS-based endothelial RNA-seq identified Tek (Tie2) as a key target that co-localized and directly interacted with Malat1; AAV-mediated endothelial Tek knockdown abolished the pro-angiogenic and reparative effects, defining a Malat1–Tek axis for cerebrovascular remodeling and nominating Malat1 as a restorative stroke target. Mouse genetic evidence.

**Model/system & primary method:** Endothelium-specific Malat1-overexpression mice with ischemic stroke; endothelial RNA-seq and AAV-mediated Tek knockdown.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag338

### Exposome-wide association study and predictive risk score for incident Alzheimer's disease and related dementias
**Chenshuang Li & Chirag J Patel | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Across 499,992 UK Biobank participants and 193 exposures in five domains, an exposome-wide scan identified 76 ADRD-associated exposures after Bonferroni correction over a median 15.3-year follow-up (8,881 incident ADRD cases). An XGBoost model incorporating the exposome reached AUC 0.718, outperforming demographics plus APOE alone (0.678), and the derived exposomic risk score stratified risk (top vs bottom quartile HR 2.97). The results argue that broad environmental profiling adds predictive value beyond genetics for dementia-prevention strategies — an observational, single-biobank analysis.

**Model/system & primary method:** UK Biobank cohort (~500k); exposome-wide Cox regression with XGBoost prediction and an exposomic risk score.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71842

### An amygdala to anterior hypothalamic circuit gates stress sensitivity
**Zachary T Pennington & Denise J Cai | Nature**

**Key findings:** Whole-brain activity mapping in previously stressed mice flagged the anterior hypothalamic nucleus (AHN) — a largely overlooked region — as showing heightened stress reactivity and stronger coupling to a threat network. Miniscope imaging showed AHN activity scales with negative valence, and prior stress enlarged the valence-sensitive AHN population. Inhibiting AHN neurons blunted, and exciting them promoted, stress responses; silencing amygdala inputs to the AHN abolished sensitized responses. The work defines an amygdala→AHN pathway that encodes negative valence and sets stress sensitivity after adversity.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse whole-brain c-Fos mapping, in vivo Miniscope calcium imaging, and projection-specific optogenetic/chemogenetic manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11075-5

### Spatiotemporal clonal architecture of the newborn mouse forebrain
**Guohua Yuan & Tomasz J Nowakowski | Nature**

**Key findings:** Lentiviral barcoding combined with spatial transcriptomics across cortex, hippocampus, striatum, thalamus and olfactory bulb yields a clonal lineage atlas of the newborn mouse forebrain. The data reveal distinct generation of ventral versus dorsal medium spiny neurons and unexpected lineage links among late-born MSNs, striatal astrocytes and olfactory-bulb neurons. A developmental principle emerges: glutamatergic neurons and astrocytes are clonally related and stay in their region of origin, whereas GABAergic neurons share lineage with oligodendrocyte precursors and disperse widely; broadly dispersed GABAergic clones are enriched for neurodevelopmental-disorder genes.

**Model/system & primary method:** Newborn mouse forebrain; lentiviral clonal barcoding with spatial transcriptomic registration.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11064-8

### A two-hit mechanism triggers autoimmune hair loss
**Jun Cui & Ting Chen | Nature**

**Key findings:** A neuro-epithelial-immune circuit, not immune dysregulation alone, initiates autoimmune alopecia. Transient Treg depletion caused CD8+ T-cell destruction of hair-follicle stem cells only during anagen, when sympathetic nerve activity is physiologically high; sympathetic denervation prevented disease, whereas psychological stress or optogenetic sympathetic activation rendered resistant telogen skin susceptible. Mechanistically, noradrenaline acting through stem-cell ADRB2 reactivated endogenous retroviruses, engaging AIM2 inflammasome and pathogenic CD8+ responses; blocking AIM2 or ERV reverse transcription was protective. The study is a clean demonstration that physiological neural activity can license tissue-specific autoimmunity — of interest for neuroimmune mechanisms generally.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse models of Treg depletion across the hair cycle; sympathetic denervation, optogenetic activation, and genetic/pharmacological ERV and AIM2 blockade.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11098-y

### CLOK: a chemigenetic multicolor labeling system to visualize neuronal birthdate and circuit integration
**Giulia Faini & Minoru Koyama | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** CLOK is an in vivo birthdating method built on HaloTag and its palette of fluorescent ligands, color-coding neurons and subcellular structures by birthdate. In developing zebrafish visual and motor systems it tracked functional maturation of newborn neurons and resolved distinct early- versus late-born maturation trajectories, and it couples to calcium/voltage imaging and optogenetics for age-specific circuit interrogation. A flexible addition to the developmental-neuroscience toolkit for linking neuronal age to circuit integration in a behaving vertebrate.

**Model/system & primary method:** Developing zebrafish; HaloTag-based multicolor birthdate labeling combined with functional imaging and optogenetics.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02423-9

### Autism-associated SCN2A deficiency disrupts cortico-striatal circuitry in human brain assembloids
**Xiaoling Chen & Yang Yang | Neuron**

**Key findings:** In human cortico-striatal assembloids, heterozygous SCN2A (NaV1.2) protein-truncating variants impaired long-range cortical axonal projections, reduced striatal spine density and weakened excitatory cortico-striatal transmission, with genotype-defined models implicating both cortical-projection failure and intrinsic striatal vulnerability. Paradoxically, SCN2A-deficient neurons showed increased intrinsic excitability and elevated spontaneous network firing. Viral (CAV2) delivery of human SCN2A rescued cellular and circuit deficits, supporting gene replacement as a therapeutic strategy for SCN2A-related autism. Findings derive from an in vitro human model.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iPSC-derived cortico-striatal assembloids with genotype-defined SCN2A variants; electrophysiology, connectivity assays and CAV2-mediated gene rescue.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.031

### Purinergic receptor activation rectifies autism-associated endothelial dysfunction
**Julie Ouellette & Baptiste Lacoste | Neuron**

**Key findings:** In the 16p11.2 deletion mouse model of autism, the deletion caused a selective bioenergetic failure in brain endothelial cells, with reduced intracellular ATP. ATP supplementation rescued endothelial function in vitro via P2Y2 purinergic receptors; activating P2Y2 restored cerebrovascular reactivity ex vivo, activity-dependent cerebral blood flow in vivo, and rescued deletion-associated behaviours. The work positions neurovascular metabolic reprogramming via purinergic signalling as a candidate therapeutic avenue for autism, on mouse evidence.

**Model/system & primary method:** 16p11.2-deletion mice (juvenile and adult, male); endothelial bioenergetics, ex vivo arteriole reactivity, in vivo cerebral blood flow and behaviour.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.09.009

### Assessment of upper-limb motor recovery after stroke using a wrist-worn accelerometer digital biomarker
**Ryan Wang & Sunghoon I Lee | Sci Transl Med**

**Key findings:** The Digital Arm Performance Scale (DAPS) was built from ~23,000 hours of wrist-accelerometer data across 215 healthy, subacute- and chronic-stroke participants by decomposing continuous arm motion into movement segments and aggregating features with a linear mixed-effects model. DAPS showed strong reliability, validity and responsiveness, and power analysis suggested it could cut the sample size needed for upper-limb trial endpoints by more than 60% versus conventional scales — a low-burden, naturalistic measure for stroke rehabilitation trials and practice.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human stroke and control cohort; wrist-worn accelerometry with segment-level feature extraction and mixed-effects modeling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.adw3644

### Depletion of border-associated macrophages impairs CSF flow and exacerbates extranuclear mutant Huntingtin aggregation in the zQ175DN mouse model of Huntington disease
**Jiang, Y. & Duan, W. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In zQ175DN knock-in HD mice, the CD206+Lyve1+ subset of leptomeningeal border-associated macrophages (BAMs) declined selectively with age (by 16 months), tracking a drop in CSF flow velocity and perivascular influx. Intracerebroventricular clodronate depletion of BAMs in 10-month-old mice worsened CSF tracer distribution and, critically, increased *extranuclear* mutant huntingtin aggregates while leaving intranuclear inclusions and glial activation unchanged — suggesting the added aggregate burden is a consequence of impaired waste clearance rather than altered gliosis. The data nominate BAMs as non-neuronal regulators of brain-border clearance and a potential HD target. Causal evidence rests on chemical ablation in one mouse model.

**Model/system & primary method:** zQ175DN HD mice; i.c.v. clodronate-liposome depletion of border-associated macrophages with CSF-flow tracing and mHTT aggregate quantification.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.24.754172

### Selective nigrosome vulnerability in Parkinson's disease revealed by histology-anchored ultra-high-field quantitative MRI
**Li, R. & Doan, T. P. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** To avoid the circularity of defining nigrosomes from the same disease contrast then measured, the authors warped calbindin-validated histological nigrosome masks into a cohort-specific 7-Tesla multimodal qMRI template (28 PD patients, 28 matched controls). PD shifted nigrosomal susceptibility, R2* and R1 upward relative to the nigral matrix, concentrated in nigrosomes 1, 2 and 4, while whole-nigra analysis showed no group difference; nigrosome 1/4 susceptibility tracked contralateral motor asymmetry. The multimodal signature likely reflects secondary iron-associated glial processes more than neuronal loss itself. The approach recovers the post-mortem map of nigral vulnerability in vivo. Cross-sectional, modest n.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human 7T multimodal quantitative MRI with histology-derived probabilistic nigrosome masks; PD-vs-control subregional analysis.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.24.754224

### Transcriptomic profiling uncovers mis-splicing and fusion transcripts in ALS
**Xu, H. & Sadri-Vakili, G. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** A reanalysis of NYGC ALS Consortium bulk RNA-seq across five post-mortem tissues found the ALS transcriptome is defined primarily by splicing failure rather than gene-expression change: aberrant splicing events, especially intron retention, outnumbered differentially expressed genes by an order of magnitude and disproportionately hit RNA-binding proteins, consistent with collapse of their autoregulatory loops. Disruption localized to glia in sporadic cases but neurons in C9ORF72 cases, with sex-specific effects. A de novo pipeline uncovered ALS-specific RNA fusions not explained by genomic structural variants; one (FAM69A-EVI5) was confirmed in ALS cerebellum by a junction-specific assay, and long-read Iso-Seq corroborated multiple fusions. (v2 revision of a preprint first posted February 2026.)

**Model/system & primary method:** Human post-mortem ALS tissue; reference-based and de novo RNA-seq with whole-genome sequencing, PacBio Iso-Seq and BaseScope validation.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.02.05.26345503

### Genetic variation in EIF2AK3 alters astrocyte-dependent stress tolerance in neurons
**Ghura, S. & Jordan-Sciutto, K. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Using knock-in mice carrying the common PERK haplotype B (EIF2AK3; ~28% global frequency, associated with several neurodegenerative diseases), the authors found PERK-B altered the kinetics of the integrated stress response: astrocyte-containing — but not neuron-only — PERK-B cultures showed elevated eIF2 phosphorylation at 4–6 h after ER stress. Low-dose PERK activation left PERK-B neurons more vulnerable to NMDA-induced death, and neuron–astrocyte coculture showed this impaired acquired stress tolerance depended on astrocyte genotype. Effects were transient and modest, but argue PERK-B contributes to cell-non-autonomous, astrocyte-mediated stress vulnerability relevant to neurodegeneration. In vitro mouse cultures.

**Model/system & primary method:** Novel PERK-B knock-in mouse neuronal and neuron–astrocyte cocultures; ER-stress and NMDA-excitotoxicity assays with ISR readouts.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.29.755429

### Multimodal evidence for impaired glymphatic brain clearance as a mediator of "inflammaging" in schizophrenia
**Tan, X. & Gaebler, A. J. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Integrating in vivo neuroimaging, omics and post-mortem single-nucleus RNA-seq, the study reports convergent evidence that impaired glymphatic clearance mediates "inflammaging" in schizophrenia — positioning a brain waste-clearance deficit upstream of age-related neuroinflammatory signatures. The multimodal design strengthens the association, but the mediation is cross-sectional and correlational, and the schizophrenia focus should be read as a disease context for glymphatic/inflammaging mechanisms rather than evidence of causation. Of interest for the overlap between clearance failure and brain aging.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human multimodal study: in vivo neuroimaging, omics, and post-mortem single-nucleus RNA-seq with mediation analysis.
