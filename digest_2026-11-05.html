CSF prion-seeding activity marks a state of incipient conversion in E200K prion disease carriers

medRxiv  T. H. Mok & S. Mead
Key findings
In 94 PRNP E200K mutation carriers followed longitudinally across international natural-history cohorts, CSF real-time quaking-induced conversion (RT-QuIC) — a direct readout of prion-protein amyloid seeding — turned positive before clinical onset in every one of the 11 carriers who converted, at a median of 2.0 years prior to symptoms (IQR 1.3–3.3; range 0.2–4.8), while carriers who stayed asymptomatic remained RT-QuIC-negative throughout follow-up. Landmark analyses tied a positive result to a sharply elevated short-term conversion risk, positioning CSF seeding positivity as a biomarker-defined prodromal state useful for risk stratification and preventive-trial enrollment. This is the most direct human evidence to date that silent proteopathic seeding signals near-term disease; the inference rests on only 11 converters in a single genetic form of prion disease.
Model & method
Humans (94 E200K carriers, longitudinal); CSF RT-QuIC seeding assay with landmark survival analysis.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.03.26367164
Preprint · not peer reviewed

FGF signaling drives mature oligodendrocytes into a disease-associated state that disrupts myelin

bioRxiv  M. K. Weigel & J. B. Zuchero
Key findings
Disease-associated oligodendrocytes (DAOs) — a conserved reactive transcriptional state seen in multiple sclerosis and Alzheimer's — were shown to be actively inducible rather than merely a marker of damage. A screen of disease-linked secreted factors identified FGF1 and FGF2 as potent inducers of a DAO-like program in purified oligodendrocyte cultures, accompanied by myelin-membrane shedding; in vivo, activating oligodendrocyte FGF signaling was sufficient to elicit DAO programs and myelin disruption, while inhibiting FGF signaling suppressed DAO induction in a mouse model of multiple sclerosis. Post-mortem MS tissue showed a concordant DAO-like signature coupled to increased FGF/FGFR engagement, arguing that mature oligodendrocytes are active drivers of myelin pathology. Causal manipulation is mouse-and-culture based; the human data are correlative.
Model & method
Purified oligodendrocyte cultures, oligodendrocyte-specific FGF gain/loss-of-function in mice (MS model), and human MS post-mortem tissue; factor screening plus transcriptional profiling.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.03.764025
Preprint · not peer reviewed

Golgi mannosidase and glycosylation enzymes are depleted in C9orf72 ALS/FTD glia

bioRxiv  B. N. Ondatje & R. Sattler
Key findings
Using human post-mortem tissue together with an iPSC-derived glia-crosstalk model, the authors found reduced Golgi mannosidases MAN1A1 and MAN1A2 — alongside additional glycan-processing enzymes — specifically in C9orf72 ALS/FTD-associated microglia and astrocytes. The work links impaired Golgi and glycan metabolism in non-neuronal cells to several established ALS disease axes, including lysosomal proteostasis failure, extracellular-matrix alteration, disrupted secretion and altered cell–cell signaling, pointing to glial glycosylation as a candidate contributor to neuroinflammation in C9orf72 disease. The findings are descriptive and associative, drawn from a single genetic subtype; whether the enzyme loss is causal for motor-neuron degeneration is not established.
Model & method
Human ALS/FTD post-mortem tissue and iPSC-derived microglia/astrocyte co-culture; transcriptomic and enzyme-expression profiling.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.30.763135
Preprint · not peer reviewed

Disentangling Braak-stage transcriptional signatures of Alzheimer's with an interpretable generative model

bioRxiv  A. W. Li & S.-I. Lee
Key findings
ADVISE, an interpretable deep generative framework, separates Braak-stage-associated transcriptional signal from dominant background biological variation in single-nucleus RNA-seq of human prefrontal cortex, then uses explainable-AI attribution to prioritize the genes and pathways carrying the progression signal. Across global, sex-specific and cell-type analyses it recovered convergent programs in synaptic transmission, HSF1-linked proteostatic stress, and SLIT/ROBO axon guidance, and nominated ROBO2 as a progression-associated candidate in excitatory neurons. CRISPR activation of ROBO2 in hiPSC-derived excitatory neurons lowered the phospho-tau-to-total-tau ratio, providing experimental support for a prioritized target rather than a purely computational hit. The causal direction in human disease remains open; the validation is a single perturbation in a reductionist neuronal model.
Model & method
Human prefrontal-cortex snRNA-seq with a variational disentanglement model; CRISPRa validation in hiPSC-derived excitatory neurons.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.02.763789
Preprint · not peer reviewed

Monoaminergic metabolite shifts track loss of hippocampal neurogenic coupling in ageing and Parkinson's dementia

bioRxiv  K. J. Mathews & K. L. Double
Key findings
In post-mortem human hippocampus from young controls, aged controls and Parkinson's disease dementia (PDD) cases, neurogenic markers DCX and MKI67 were markedly reduced throughout the hippocampus in PDD, while GFAP/GFAPδ were elevated in aged controls but not in PDD. Dopamine and serotonin levels were unchanged, but the dopamine metabolite DOPAC fell and the serotonin metabolite 5-HIAA rose along the hippocampal axis in PDD, and both metabolites were significant predictors of DCX and MKI67 expression independent of diagnostic group in hierarchical regression. The coupling between stem-cell markers and proliferative output, preserved in young controls, was absent in both aged controls and PDD, suggesting monoaminergic turnover tracks stage-specific failure of neuronal proliferation. The evidence is cross-sectional and correlational in human tissue, so causation cannot be inferred.
Model & method
Human post-mortem hippocampus (young, aged, PDD); neurogenesis-gene expression, monoamine/metabolite quantification and hierarchical regression along the longitudinal axis.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.03.764096
