### Aberrant excitatory neuronal ERBB4 promotes Alzheimer's disease pathology
**Se Young Lee & Won-Suk Chung | Nature**

**Why it matters:** In AD mouse models, astrocytes/microglia shift toward eliminating excitatory (not inhibitory) synapses early in disease, but the authors show this phagocytic shift alone is not what drives synapse loss. Instead, single-nucleus RNA-seq identifies a population of "early-responsive excitatory neurons" (EREN) marked by ectopic ERBB4 expression as one of the earliest transcriptional changes in AD models. Neuron-selective Erbb4 deletion blocks abnormal network activity, synapse loss, reactive gliosis, amyloid deposition and cognitive deficits; conversely, Erbb4 overexpression in wild-type neurons reproduces the core AD-like phenotype in the absence of amyloid plaques, downstream of mTOR signaling. Mediation analysis of human AD transcriptomic data supports a role for neuronal ERBB4 linking amyloid to tau propagation and cognitive decline.

**Model/system & primary method:** AD mouse models (conditional Erbb4 deletion/overexpression in excitatory neurons); single-nucleus RNA-seq; human AD transcriptomic mediation analysis. Causal evidence is mouse-only; human data are correlative/mediation-based.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10964-z

### Autism mutations rewire protein interaction networks to drive neurodevelopmental pathology
**Belinda Wang & Nevan J Krogan | Science**

**Why it matters:** A systematic affinity purification-mass spectrometry map of protein-protein interactions for 100 high-confidence ASD risk genes (>1800 interactions), integrated with AlphaFold structural modeling, shows that independent pathogenic missense mutations converge on shared protein complexes rather than acting through unrelated pathways. As a validated example, patient-derived FOXP1 variants disrupt its interaction with FOXP4, altering cortical neurogenesis and neural activity in human brain organoids. This gives a mechanistic, network-level framework connecting ASD genetic heterogeneity to convergent cellular dysfunction and candidate therapeutic nodes.

**Model/system & primary method:** Affinity purification-mass spectrometry PPI mapping of 100 ASD genes; AlphaFold-based mutation impact prediction; validation in human iPSC-derived brain organoids.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ady4523

### Viral mimetic triggers haemorrhagic transformation in a childhood stroke model via neutrophil elastase
**Aditya Rayasam & Zinaida S Vexler | Brain**

**Why it matters:** Childhood arterial ischemic stroke is mechanistically distinct from adult stroke, and viral infection is a known clinical trigger with no prior animal model. Using the TLR3 agonist poly(I:C) to simulate viral priming before transient MCA occlusion in juvenile mice, the authors show priming alters brain microvascular gene expression and sensitizes endothelial/inflammatory responses, leading to increased neutrophil accumulation, neutrophil elastase activation and NETosis, and marked hemorrhagic transformation after stroke. Pharmacologic neutrophil elastase inhibition at the time of occlusion substantially reduced hemorrhagic transformation and injury, identifying a concrete, druggable mechanism linking antecedent viral infection to stroke severity in children.

**Model/system & primary method:** Juvenile mouse model combining poly(I:C) viral priming with transient middle cerebral artery occlusion; gene expression profiling of brain microvascular cells; pharmacologic neutrophil elastase inhibition.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag292

### Loss of laminin-γ1 in pericytes impairs microvascular function and cognition
**Jingsong Ruan & Yao Yao | Cell Rep**

**Why it matters:** Blood-brain barrier disruption tracks with lesion burden and cognitive dysfunction in cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD), but a causal driver has been unclear. Selective deletion of laminin-γ1 in pericytes (not smooth muscle cells or fibroblasts) causes age-dependent BBB disruption through both paracellular and transcellular routes, along with pericyte loss, thickened basal lamina, increased brain stiffness, impaired brain clearance, synaptic loss and cognitive impairment — reproducing several hallmark features of CSVD in a single genetic manipulation and pointing to pericytic laminin as a specific mechanistic node.

**Model/system & primary method:** Pericyte-specific laminin-γ1 conditional knockout mice; BBB permeability assays, brain stiffness measurement, behavioral cognitive testing.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117904

### Lesion-Level Subtypes of White Matter Hyperintensity Evolution Beyond Spatial Location
**Raul Gonzalez-Gomez & Yasser Iturria-Medina | Neurology**

**Why it matters:** White matter hyperintensities (WMH) are usually quantified as a single global burden measure, but this longitudinal study (3,224 MRI scans, 403 participants spanning normal aging through AD and PD) shows lesion-level heterogeneity is biologically meaningful. Unsupervised clustering of 2,107 lesions' longitudinal trajectories identified three coexisting subtypes: a stable subtype unrelated to atrophy, an unstable subtype linked to weight gain (metabolic vulnerability), and an unstable subtype linked to brain atrophy, older age and vascular risk (pulse pressure). Notably, global WMH burden lost its association with atrophy once the atrophy-linked subtype was accounted for, and the clustering replicated in an independent external cohort — arguing that lesion composition, not burden alone, is the more informative readout for cerebrovascular contributions to neurodegeneration.

**Model/system & primary method:** Longitudinal human multimodal MRI (structural, diffusion, resting-state) across cognitively normal, MCI, AD and PD; unsupervised lesion-trajectory clustering with external cohort validation. Observational/correlative; causality not established.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218472

### Granule cells reorient cortical trajectories to separate contexts
**Martha G Garcia-Garcia & Mark J Wagner | Nature**

**Key findings:** Learning two distinct skills with shared temporal structure requires both generalizing across contexts and keeping them distinct. Simultaneous imaging of layer 5 pyramidal tract neurons (cortex) and cerebellar granule cells (GrCs) in mice during parallel learning shows the cortex maintains stable, generalized low-dimensional activity across tasks, while GrC population activity coherently "rotates" its low-dimensional trajectory between tasks — separating contexts without cortical representations expanding or scrambling. Separation between GrC trajectories was strongest in expert (well-trained) mice, suggesting a division of labor in which cortex provides invariant dynamics and the cerebellum reformats them for context-specific output.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mice performing two temporally-matched skills in parallel; simultaneous two-photon imaging of cortico-cerebellar pathway (L5PT and granule cells).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10946-1

### Endocannabinoids facilitate reward engagement through retrograde gain control
**David J Marcus & Michael R Bruchas | Nature**

**Key findings:** Endocannabinoid (eCB) signaling is known to filter excitatory/inhibitory transmission via retrograde presynaptic action in vitro, but whether this shapes motivated behavior in freely moving animals was untested. Combining in vivo physiology, imaging, genetic manipulation and machine-learning-based behavioral analysis, the authors show dynamic eCB release in a genetically and anatomically defined thalamostriatal circuit drives behavioral engagement during reward seeking, establishing retrograde eCB gain control as a mechanism linking synaptic-level neuromodulation to reward-directed behavior in vivo.

**Model/system & primary method:** Freely moving mice; in vivo fiber photometry/imaging, genetic circuit manipulation (thalamostriatal pathway), machine-learning behavioral quantification.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10967-w

### Leptomeningeal fibroblasts promote glioblastoma progression by regulating cerebrospinal fluid dynamics
**Maoyuan Sun & Zhifeng Shi | Nat Commun**

**Key findings:** How CSF dynamics are altered by brain tumors was unclear. In male tumor-bearing mice, glioblastoma drives leptomeningeal perivascular fibrosis via NF-κB activation in leptomeningeal fibroblasts (the principal fibrotic source, with minimal pericyte contribution), impairing fluid transport/clearance and reducing intratumoral cytotoxic T cell infiltration. Inhibiting NF-κB signaling in these fibroblasts reduced fibrosis, restored fluid clearance, boosted cytotoxic T cell accumulation and restored responsiveness to PD-1 blockade — identifying leptomeningeal fibrosis as a mechanism coupling CSF/glymphatic dysfunction to tumor immune evasion, with relevance to CSF clearance biology more broadly.

**Model/system & primary method:** Male tumor-bearing mice; lineage tracing, single-cell analysis, pharmacologic NF-κB inhibition, immune checkpoint blockade combination.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76032-2

### Dynamic imaging markers of incident microhemorrhages and superficial siderosis in the A4 study: A longitudinal person-interval analysis
**Callum Hill & Christopher M Kipps | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Amyloid-related imaging abnormalities with hemosiderin deposition (ARIA-H) are a key safety concern for anti-amyloid therapies, but predictors of new hemorrhagic events were poorly characterized. In a person-interval analysis of 1,069 A4 study participants (3,647 intervals, 171 hemorrhagic events), both current microhemorrhage burden (OR 1.24–1.37) and recent microhemorrhage accumulation (OR 1.43–1.83) independently predicted short-term risk of new microhemorrhages/superficial siderosis, beyond baseline risk factors — supporting a dynamic, trajectory-based rather than purely static approach to ARIA-H risk stratification.

**Model/system & primary method:** Longitudinal human MRI in the A4 trial cohort (solanezumab, preclinical AD); discrete-time person-interval survival modeling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71792

### Genetic frontotemporal degeneration across the lifespan? A critical appraisal of the neurodevelopmental hypothesis
**Isis So & Elizabeth C Finger | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** A critical review of ~25 years of observational evidence proposing that genetic FTD-associated variants (C9orf72, GRN, MAPT) have neurodevelopmental effects, not just late-life neurodegenerative ones. The authors conclude that while presymptomatic biological and phenotypic changes are seen even in young adults with these variants, no causal human data currently support a true neurodevelopmental hypothesis — correlational findings remain open to alternative explanations. They outline what preclinical/human data would be needed to test potential links between early-life cellular responses to FTD variants and later neurodegeneration.

**Model/system & primary method:** Critical narrative review of preclinical and human genetic FTD literature (C9orf72, GRN, MAPT).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71754

### Methodological insights and clinical outcomes in ALS and FTLD: lessons from the SPIN cohort
**Álvaro Carbayo & Ricard Rojas-Garcia | Alzheimers Dement**

**Key findings:** Describes the SPIN-ALS-FTLD subcohort, a longitudinal multimodal platform (>1,000 participants; ~800 blood, 400 CSF, 800 DNA samples; imaging in >250 cases; 66 neuropathological studies) integrating clinical, cognitive, genetic, biofluid, imaging and neuropathological data for the ALS-FTLD clinico-genetic-neuropathological continuum within routine clinical care. Positioned as infrastructure for biomarker discovery and patient stratification rather than a single hypothesis-driven finding; value lies in enabling future multimodal analyses of this heterogeneous disease spectrum.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human longitudinal, multimodal observational cohort (clinical, biofluid, genetic, imaging, neuropathological data) embedded in routine ALS/FTLD care.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71777

### Vagal subcircuits differentially regulate sepsis-induced sickness and anxiety-related behaviors
**Lena Bourhy & Gabriel Lepousez | Cell Rep**

**Key findings:** The vagus nerve relays peripheral immune status to the brain via nodose ganglion sensory neurons projecting to the brainstem dorsal vagal complex (DVC), and severe inflammation (sepsis) can drive persistent brain dysfunction including anxiety. Using surgical and chemogenetic gain/loss-of-function in a mouse cecal ligation and puncture sepsis model, the authors show DVC neuron activation (not upstream nodose neuron activation) exacerbates sickness behavior and drives lasting anxiety-like behavior, whereas subdiaphragmatic vagotomy reduces acute brain activation and partially attenuates anxiety in sepsis survivors — localizing sepsis-associated behavioral dysfunction to a specific brainstem relay rather than the vagal afferent arm broadly.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse cecal ligation and puncture (CLP) sepsis model; chemogenetic and surgical (vagotomy) manipulation of nodose/DVC subcircuits; behavioral phenotyping.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117896
