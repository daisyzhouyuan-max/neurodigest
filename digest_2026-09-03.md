### Intracellular complement factor H protects neurons during CNS inflammation
**Christina Mayer & Manuel A Friese | Nature**

**Why it matters:** Using single-nucleus RNA-seq of retinal ganglion cells from people with multiple sclerosis, the authors found that complement factor H (CFH) expression tracks with neuronal resilience to inflammatory degeneration — but the protective activity is intracellular and independent of CFH's canonical secreted, complement-regulatory role. CFH localizes to the ER, limits ROS accumulation and lipid peroxidation, and its effect requires the C-terminal SCR20 domain but not complement component C3. This is a non-canonical, cell-intrinsic neuroprotective mechanism validated in both mouse models and human tissue, with direct relevance to inflammatory neurodegeneration beyond MS.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human MS retinal ganglion cell snRNA-seq plus mouse CNS inflammation models; domain-deletion and complement-independence experiments.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10981-y

### Somatic mutations reveal the ontogeny of microglia in human ageing
**Julia A Belk & Siddhartha Jaiswal | Nature**

**Why it matters:** Using naturally accumulated somatic mutations as clonal lineage tags, the authors show that bone-marrow-derived myeloid cells infiltrate the aging human brain in all 20 individuals examined and can constitute a large fraction of the microglial pool — challenging the assumption that human microglia are exclusively embryonically seeded and self-renewing, as in mice. Cohort analysis further links most forms of clonal hematopoiesis to reduced Alzheimer's disease risk, tying microglial replenishment dynamics to disease susceptibility.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem brain (20 older individuals), somatic mutation-based and mitochondrial DNA single-cell lineage tracing; epidemiological clonal hematopoiesis cohort analysis.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10939-0

### Metabolic reallocation in spinal cord oligodendrocytes drives chronic pain in mice through neuronal Aβ42 production
**Yannick Fotio & Daniele Piomelli | Sci Transl Med**

**Why it matters:** After peripheral injury, spinal oligodendrocytes down-regulate myelin synthesis and up-regulate lipid biosynthesis, and this metabolic reallocation compromises axonal integrity, elevates BACE1, and drives neuronal accumulation of insoluble amyloid-β42 during the window when pain becomes chronic. BACE1 inhibition or antibody-mediated Aβ42 clearance prevented persistent pain, and oligodendrocyte-specific deletion of NAAA (the metabolic node) blocked Aβ42 release altogether. An unexpected mechanistic bridge between a peripheral-injury model and amyloidogenic processing, nominating the NAAA→Aβ42 axis as a disease-modifying target — evidence is mouse-only.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse hindpaw formalin and sciatic nerve ligation models; snRNA-seq, lipidomics, pharmacological BACE1 inhibition and 4G8 antibody clearance, oligodendrocyte-specific NAAA knockout.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aea8738

### Proteomic signatures of APOE ε4 across human tissues and cell types in Alzheimer's disease

**[PREPRINT — NOT PEER REVIEWED]**

**Li, X. C. & Shvetcov, A. | bioRxiv**

**Why it matters:** Pooling plasma, CSF, brain multi-omic, and iPSC-derived astrocyte/neuron proteomic data across three large cohorts (ROSMAP, AMP-AD, ADNI; combined n>3,600), the authors identify a consistent APOE ε4-associated plasma proteomic signature enriched for immune processes, independent of AD diagnosis, that a classifier trained on plasma data could also detect in independent CSF proteomics. APOE ε4 carriage tracked with higher Braak and CERAD scores, but bulk brain transcriptomic/proteomic changes were limited and poorly concordant across molecular layers — the ε4 signal is much clearer in biofluids and cell-type-resolved iPSC models than in bulk brain tissue, arguing for cell-type-resolved multi-omic designs going forward.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human plasma, CSF, brain tissue (multi-cohort, n>3,600) and iPSC-derived astrocytes/neurons; multi-omic proteomic/transcriptomic profiling and machine-learning classification.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.27.746928

### 3D epigenome of glial cell types in developing human cortex
**Ian R Jones & Yin Shen | Nature**

**Key findings:** Comprehensive 3D epigenomic profiling (expression, chromatin accessibility, methylation, chromatin interactions) of four glial populations from mid-gestational human cortex identifies cell-type-specific candidate regulatory elements. Machine learning prioritized 112 schizophrenia risk variants within glial regulatory elements, and the authors confirmed in vivo that the rs4449074 risk allele disrupts an outer radial glia enhancer; outer radial glia elements were also enriched for human-accelerated genomic regions relative to chimpanzee, some with activity differences linked to neurodevelopmental genes.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human mid-gestational neocortex; multi-omic 3D epigenomics with transgenic mouse validation of regulatory elements.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10987-6

### Connectome analysis of a cerebellum-like circuit for sensory prediction
**Krista E Perks & Nathaniel B Sawtell | Nature**

**Key findings:** Connectomic mapping of a cerebellum-like circuit in electric fish reveals inhibitory and disinhibitory input pathways and structured recurrent connectivity that together solve a distributed credit-assignment problem for canceling predictable sensory input. A computational model constrained by electrophysiology shows how multiple, spatially distributed plasticity sites cooperate to produce fast, accurate, noise-robust sensory cancellation — a structural blueprint for how distributed synaptic plasticity supports learning more generally.

**Model/system & primary method:** Electric fish cerebellum-like circuit; serial-section connectomics combined with cell-type-specific electrophysiology and computational modeling.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10690-6

### Glutamate concentration tunes AMPA receptor function through conductance-state occupancy
**Gokulakrishna Banumurthy & Jacques I Wadiche | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** At mouse interneuron synapses, lowering synaptic glutamate concentration reduces hallmark AMPAR properties (rectification, polyamine block, Ca2+ permeability) independent of subunit composition, by shifting receptor occupancy across conductance states with differential spermine affinity. This means glutamate concentration itself — not just subunit stoichiometry — is a key, previously unrecognized variable shaping how excitatory synapses encode information, complicating standard biophysical inference of AMPAR composition from these same properties.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse interneuron synapses; patch-clamp electrophysiology of native and isolated AMPARs with numerical simulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02414-w

### Mitochondrial calcium influx-driven bioenergetics selectively enable drug addiction
**Jun Gao & Xin Pan | Nat Neurosci**

**Key findings:** Opioids and methamphetamine, but not natural rewards, trigger mitochondrial calcium influx via the mitochondrial calcium uniporter (MCU) specifically in dopaminergic terminals of the nucleus accumbens during high-intensity activation, fueling rapid ATP production that sustains drug-evoked dopamine release. Genetic or pharmacological MCU inhibition selectively blunted drug-induced dopamine release and addictive behaviors while sparing natural reward, nominating MCU as an addiction-selective therapeutic target that spares normal reward signaling.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse nucleus accumbens dopaminergic terminals; optogenetics, fiber photometry, genetic/pharmacological MCU manipulation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02421-x

### Elevated translocator protein in skull bone marrow is linked to human chronic pain
**Mehrbod Mohammadian & Marco L Loggia | Sci Transl Med**

**Key findings:** Integrated PET/MRI in 125 people with chronic low back pain or knee osteoarthritis versus 22 controls shows elevated TSPO (a myeloid-cell density marker) in skull bone marrow, more pronounced in osteoarthritis, correlating with pain and pain-comorbid symptoms in partially distinct spatial patterns; limited immunohistochemistry in a chronic-pain donor supported the imaging finding. This nominates skull marrow as a previously overlooked neuroimmune hub relevant to chronic pain — the immunohistochemical support is preliminary (single donor comparison).

**Model/system & primary method:** Human PET/MRI cohort (n=125 chronic pain, n=22 controls) with limited postmortem immunohistochemistry.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aed8729

### Activation of transposable elements is linked to a region- and cell type-specific interferon response in Parkinson's disease
**Raquel Garza & Johan Jakobsson | Sci Adv**

**Key findings:** Bulk and single-nucleus RNA-seq of postmortem PD brain across four regions shows region- and cell-type-specific transposable element (LINE-1, ERV) activation in microglia and neurons of substantia nigra and putamen but not amygdala or prefrontal cortex, correlating with an innate immune/interferon signature in the same regions. The interferon–TE link was mechanistically confirmed in hPSC-derived microglia and neurons, implicating TE derepression as a contributor to region-selective neuroinflammation in PD.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem PD brain (4 regions), bulk/single-nucleus RNA-seq; hPSC-derived microglia and neurons for mechanistic validation.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aed2952

**Sienna S Drake & Alyson E Fournier | J Neurosci**

**Key findings:** This review synthesizes evidence for an accelerated aging and cellular senescence-like phenotype in MS brain cells, both in patients and preclinical models, and its interaction with age and hormonal status in driving worse prognosis and declining treatment response in older MS patients. **Model/system & primary method:** Narrative review of human MS and preclinical animal model literature on cellular senescence and aging.

### Neuroinflammation induces aberrant activation of Wnt signaling in astrocytes and impairment of cholesterol homeostasis and myelin integrity
**Jiannan Zhang & Kang Zheng | Cell Rep**

**Key findings:** Inflammation-driven Wnt activation in astrocytes raises intracellular cholesterol (via SREBF2) while suppressing APOE expression and cholesterol efflux, starving oligodendrocytes of the extracellular cholesterol needed to drive MYRF and myelin gene expression. In transgenic mice, defective oligodendrocyte differentiation and myelination caused by astrocytic Wnt activation was partially rescued by pharmacologically boosting ApoE expression, defining an inflammation→Wnt→cholesterol→myelin pathway with a candidate therapeutic entry point for demyelinating disease.

**Model/system & primary method:** Transgenic mouse models of neuroinflammation; astrocyte/oligodendrocyte co-culture and pharmacological ApoE rescue.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117871

### T Cell-Mediated Targeting of Interneurons in Mice Shapes Hippocampal Remodeling and Epilepsy
**Daniel S Galvis-Montes & Julika Pitsch | Ann Neurol**

**Key findings:** A new mouse model expressing ovalbumin selectively in CA1 interneurons alongside OVA-specific CD8+ T cells reproduces key features of GAD65-antibody autoimmune encephalitis: CD8+ T cell infiltration and interneuron targeting cause acute seizures, followed by a chronic phase of spontaneous recurrent seizures, hippocampal sclerosis, sustained gliosis, reduced Reelin expression, and granule cell dispersion arising from displacement (not aberrant neurogenesis). This causally links cell-type-specific autoimmune targeting of inhibitory neurons to temporal lobe epileptogenesis.

**Model/system & primary method:** Transgenic mouse model of interneuron-specific CD8+ T cell-mediated autoimmunity; histology, seizure monitoring, proliferation assays.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78349

### Repairing the Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis Trial Credibility Gap
**Michael Benatar & Thomas I Cochrane | Neurology**

**Key findings:** This perspective argues that a "clinical trial industrial complex" has incentivized recurring methodological fallacies in early/mid-phase ALS trials — misused biomarkers, mismatched design and objectives, selective reporting, over-reliance on post hoc and open-label extension data — that generate premature efficacy claims failing to replicate in phase 3, with direct harm to patients. The authors call for stricter adherence to reporting standards, clearer separation of hypothesis-generating from hypothesis-testing analyses, and more disciplined phase 2 triage.

**Model/system & primary method:** Perspective/commentary on ALS clinical trial design and reporting practices; no primary data.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218471

### A Two-Stage Plasma Biomarker Algorithm Reduces Amyloid PET Use and the p-Tau217 Gray Zone
**Parankusham, H. & Frank, B. | medRxiv**

*v3 revision of a preprint first posted February 2026 (as "GRAD: A Two-Stage Algorithm for Resolving Diagnostic Uncertainty in the Plasma p-tau217 Gray Zone"); this version carries a new title and expanded author list.*

### Single nucleus RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics reveal unique functionalities of gray matter versus white matter oligodendrocytes in aging and Alzheimer's Disease
**Voth, J. P. & Keene, C. D. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** Profiling prefrontal white matter from 48 human brain donors (young adulthood through late life, low vs. high AD neuropathologic change) with snRNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics, the authors find white-matter oligodendrocytes mount stronger immune programs with aging and pathology (cytokine signaling, antigen presentation) while gray-matter oligodendrocytes' synapse/neurotransmitter signature declines with aging and high pathology. High-pathology white matter also showed amplified stress/proteostasis signatures (chaperones, ferritin) and an expanded population inferred to progress along a differentiation→lipid-remodeling→stress/ISR-reactive trajectory, consistent with a senescence-like oligodendrocyte state in diseased white matter.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human postmortem prefrontal white matter, 48 donors; single-nucleus RNA-seq, CosMx spatial transcriptomics, pseudotime trajectory inference.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.29.747923

### Linoleic Acid-Lyso PG Axis promoting lipid droplet-mitochondria tethering by stabilizing Noncanonically Mitochondrial PPARβ/δ to Ameliorate Microglial Dysfunction in subarachnoid hemorrhage
**Zou, L.-X. & Li, W. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** CSF linoleic acid (LA) was elevated and strongly discriminated aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) patients (n=30) from controls (n=10; AUC 0.997). In hemoglobin-exposed mouse microglia, LA restored phagocytosis, mitochondrial membrane potential and ATP production via a lysophosphatidylglycerol(16:0)–PPARδ axis: LPG(16:0) stabilizes a mitochondrial-membrane pool of PPARδ that tethers lipid droplets to mitochondria and supports fatty acid oxidation. LA treatment reduced neuroinflammatory injury and partially rescued anxiety-like behavior and spatial memory in a mouse SAH model — causal evidence for the pathway is mouse-only; the human data are correlative.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human CSF metabolomics (SAH patients vs. controls) plus mouse blood-injection SAH model and primary microglia; lipidomics, mitochondrial/phagocytosis assays, structural modeling, behavioral testing.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.26.747434

### Tau isoforms modulate the axon initial segment controlling axonal trafficking and neuronal excitability
**Arnaiz Yepez, C. & Falzone, T. L. | bioRxiv**

**Key findings:** In mouse primary neurons and hiPSC-derived neurons, tau isoform balance (independent of total tau levels) regulates axon initial segment (AIS) maturation: tau deficiency or 3R-tau predominance delays Ankyrin G accumulation and AIS stabilization, and shifting hiPSC-derived neurons toward 3R-tau reduces sodium currents, impairs action potential firing, and alters lysosomal transport within the AIS. Since aberrant exon-10 splicing (the 3R/4R ratio) is a defining feature of primary tauopathies, this links isoform imbalance mechanistically to altered neuronal excitability and trafficking, independent of tau aggregation.

**Model/system & primary method:** Mouse primary neurons and hiPSC-derived neurons; super-resolution DNA-PAINT imaging, electrophysiology, live axonal trafficking assays.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.01.745409

### Markerless Motion Capture Reveals Movement Abnormalities in Isolated REM Sleep Behavior Disorder
**Wegner, P. & Faber, J. | medRxiv**

**Key findings:** Single-camera markerless motion capture of 93 walking videos from three clinical sites, analyzed with pose estimation and machine-learning classifiers (best: tsfresh+XGBoost, AUROC 0.739), distinguished people with isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (a prodromal PD stage) from healthy controls, outperforming the MDS-UPDRS III clinical score when trained across sites. SHAP analysis identified hip-related kinematic features as key discriminators, more closely tracking regional dopaminergic deficits than the clinical scale — a low-cost, scalable candidate digital biomarker for prodromal PD, based on a modest retrospective sample.

**Model/system & primary method:** Human iRBD patients vs. controls (n=93 videos, 3 sites); single-RGB-camera pose estimation and machine-learning classification benchmarked against MDS-UPDRS III.

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.28.26361609
